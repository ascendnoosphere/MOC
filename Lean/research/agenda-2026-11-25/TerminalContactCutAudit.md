# Contact cuts: what rank does not imply, and what strict maximality does

Let \(r\) be a pivot and \(\mathcal C\) its contact labels. For a finite label set \(I\), call it a common cut if
\[
  r^{-1}q(j)\in I\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad j\in I
  \qquad(q\in\mathcal C).
\]
This condition means every contact matches columns in \(I\) to rows in \(r(I)\). When there is no proper nonempty common cut, the group generated by the pivot-relative contact permutations acts transitively. Equivalently, the bipartite union of the contact assignments is connected after the pivot edges identify the row and column copies. This is weaker than connectivity by individual tied transpositions.

## Rank alone does not give cut connectivity

The simple two-imaginary-outlier family in `TwoOutlierZeroSupport.lean` has the exact exposed contact set \(\{q:q(d)=d\}\) for positive spectator sums and imaginary height product greater than every crossed spectator product. Pick positive distinct spectators and positive outlier heights. All matching factors are nonzero, and the spectra are simple. Every pivot-relative contact still fixes \(d\), so the contact assignment union cannot connect the outlier to the other labels.

The functional rank nevertheless meets the terminal threshold from order five on. Among the spectator labels, choose an anchor \(o\). Use the pivot differences relative to the identity for each swap \((o,i)\), and for each directed three-cycle \(o\mapsto i\mapsto j\mapsto o\) with \(i,j\) distinct spectators different from \(o\). In a zero linear combination, the singleton marker \(i\mapsto j\) first isolates that three-cycle. No other selected label has the same directed move between two distinct nonanchor spectators. These coefficients vanish. The singleton marker \(o\mapsto i\) then isolates each remaining swap. Therefore those differences are independent; there are \((n-2)^2\) of them, which is at least \(2n-2\) for \(n\ge5\). This is an exact realized support example, not a strict determinant-gap witness.

## Strict contact maxima do cross every cut

Suppose a strict witness has a nontrivial common cut \(I\). Change the spectra by
\[
  a_i(z)=a_i+z\,1_{r(I)}(i),\qquad
  b_j(z)=b_j-z\,1_I(j).
\]
Each old contact fixes all its own factors, so the entire complex contact product stays fixed. Some matching \(s\) has nonzero product at \(z=0\): if every product were zero, the real signed determinant formula would force the determinant to vanish, contrary to strictness.

If \(s\) already crosses the cut, its product along the motion has positive degree. Each unchanged factor is nonzero, and each changing factor is linear with slope \(+1\) or \(-1\). If \(s\) preserves the cut, swap one of its columns in \(I\) with one outside \(I\). Only the two exchanged factors change with \(z\), and the other factors remain nonzero. In both cases there is a nonconstant matching coordinate while the pivot coordinate is constant.

A nonconstant gap on this exact polynomial profile curve admits the checked affine half-plane continuation over the parameter line. If the gap is constant instead, the varying deficit polynomial attains a negative real value and connectedness gives the extra contact. Thus any strict witness with a common cut can gain a contact, keeping its mixer, direction and pivot.

Lean checks this in `PolynomialProfileContact.lean`. The conclusion has no spectral simplicity or nonzero matching-product premise. It rules out the disconnected high-rank example as a possible *maximal strict* contact pattern, but it neither proves a tied-swap spanning family nor closes the terminal determinant inequality.
