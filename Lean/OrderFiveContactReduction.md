# General contact reduction and the next order-five problem

Status: the translation obstruction, contact transfer on a supplied
fixed-gap curve, and finite iteration have Lean proofs. The construction
of that curve through each small-contact profile is a mathematical step
that this library does not yet formalize. No order-four result is needed
for the general contact argument. Section 5 uses the published double-dimer
identity and is not a Lean theorem. The library does not prove all of order five.

Source and attribution: Zheng, *The Marcus–de Oliveira conjecture for
n = 4*, [preprint DOI](https://doi.org/10.13140/RG.2.2.36790.25923),
Theorem 3.6 and Appendix C.5. The following proof replaces the
order-four-specific matching-cubic calculation by an elementary
rank-two kernel normalization. It also uses the compact-curve maximum
principle argument of Zheng's Lemma 3.5 and the established signed
determinant law in MOC/Weights.lean. See ZhengOrderFourReview.md for
the audit status of the preprint.

## 1. Exact matching profiles and their dimension

Use row-to-column permutations on \(\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\), the inverse of
the convention in MOC/Weights.lean. Define
\[
X_n=\{p\in\mathbb C^{S_n}:\ 
p_\sigma=\prod_i(a_i+b_{\sigma(i)})
\text{ for some }a,b\in\mathbb C^n\}.
\]
This set is an irreducible complex affine algebraic cone of dimension
\(2n-2\) when \(n\ge2\).

Here is a proof without relying on the invariant theory of a specific
number of points. A kernel \(K=(a_i+b_j)\) has rank at most two. Conversely,
the matching profile of any complex kernel \(K\) of rank at most two
is in \(X_n\). If it has no nonzero matching product, its profile is the
zero profile. Otherwise every row and column is nonzero. A rank-one
kernel has a constant matching profile, which is realized by additive
spectra. For rank two write \(K_{ij}=u_i^T v_j\), using nonzero
two-dimensional factors. Choose coordinates so that the first component
of each \(u_i\) and the second component of each \(v_j\) are nonzero.
Such a basis exists: choose a vector avoiding finitely many kernels of
the row factors, and also avoiding finitely many lines generated by
the column factors, and extend it to a basis. In these coordinates
\[
K_{ij}=\ell_i r_j(a_i+b_j),\qquad \ell_i r_j\ne0.
\]
The product of all row and column factors is common to every matching.
Absorb this product by simultaneously scaling both spectra by a complex
\(n\)-th root. This proves the equality of additive profiles and rank-two
kernel profiles.

The repository already proves Euclidean closure of this exact set
in MOC/MatchingProfileClosed.lean. For clarity an elementary proof is
as follows. For a convergent sequence of matching profiles with nonzero
limit, pass to a subsequence with a common permutation of maximum
modulus. Relabel it as the identity; its product \(\lambda\) tends to
a nonzero limit. Divide each row of each kernel by its diagonal entry.
The resulting kernel has diagonal one and all permutation products
of modulus at most one. Thus the product of absolute entry values on
every simple directed cycle is at most one (extend that cycle by fixed
points to a permutation). Define \(d_j\) as the maximum product of
absolute entry values over directed paths ending at \(j\), including
the empty path of value one. Deleting a repeated-vertex cycle cannot
decrease the product; hence this maximum is finite and attained on a
simple path. It satisfies \(d_j\ge d_i|K_{ij}|\) for the normalized
kernel. Replace its entries by \(d_iK_{ij}/d_j\).
This diagonal similarity preserves rank, diagonal one, and every
matching product, and all entries now have modulus at most one.
Take a convergent subsequence of these bounded kernels. The limit
still has rank at most two and has the limiting normalized matching
profile. Multiply one row by the limiting \(\lambda\); its profile is
the original limit. The zero limiting profile is also in \(X_n\).

The polynomial map from the affine space of additive spectra has
constructible image by Chevalley's theorem, and a Euclidean-closed
constructible subset of complex affine space is Zariski closed:
each dense locally closed stratum is Euclidean dense in its
irreducible algebraic closure, so all these closures lie in the
Euclidean-closed set. Its image is irreducible, because its domain is
irreducible. It is a cone since a common scalar on matching products
can be absorbed by scaling the spectra by an \(n\)-th root.

To obtain the dimension, restrict to the nonempty open set where every
matching product is nonzero, equivalently every kernel entry is nonzero.
Normalize the anchor row and column to one by diagonal row/column
scaling:
\[
\widetilde K_{ij}=\frac{K_{ij}K_{00}}{K_{i0}K_{0j}},\qquad
\widetilde K_{0j}=\widetilde K_{i0}=1.
\]
The lower-right \((n-1)\)-square block of \(\widetilde K-1\) has rank
at most one, by the Schur complement of the anchor entry. Denote it by
\(M\). Extend \(M\) to have zero anchor row and column. The profile has
the form
\[
p_\sigma=\lambda\prod_i(1+M_{i,\sigma(i)}).
\tag{1}
\]
Conversely, every such normalized rank-one block and nonzero scalar
gives a rank-two kernel profile. In the nonzero-profile chart,
\(\widetilde K\) is recovered uniquely from \(p\): for each \(i,j\ne0\)
choose a permutation \(\sigma\) assigning \(0\mapsto0,i\mapsto j\),
and let \(\tau\) exchange those two images while keeping all other
assignments. Then
\[
\widetilde K_{ij}=p_\sigma/p_\tau.
\tag{2}
\]
The scalar \(\lambda\) is recovered using any matching product and
(1). The rank-at-most-one block has dimension \(2(n-1)-1\) (a rank-one
outer product has two factors and one common scaling redundancy).
Together with \(\lambda\) this gives dimension \(2n-2\).

## 2. Translation-line lemma in arbitrary order

Let \(\mathbf1\) be the constant profile of value one.
Suppose \(p\in X_n\) and the entire affine line
\(p+t\mathbf1\) belongs to \(X_n\) for all \(t\in\mathbb C\).
Then there are \(c\in\mathbb C\) and an \(n\times n\) complex matrix
\(Q\) such that
\[
\boxed{p_\sigma=c+\sum_i Q_{i,\sigma(i)}
\qquad\text{for all }\sigma.}\tag{3}
\]
In particular every such profile satisfies the determinant inclusion,
for every relative unitary.

Proof. For large positive real \(t\), all the matching coordinates of
\(p+t\mathbf1\) are nonzero. Use (2) on that profile:
\[
\widetilde K_{ij}(t)
=\frac{t+p_\sigma}{t+p_\tau}
=1+\frac{p_\sigma-p_\tau}{t}+O(t^{-2}).
\]
Write \(Q_{ij}=p_\sigma-p_\tau\) using the fixed chosen completion for
\((i,j)\) and \(Q=0\) on the anchor row and column. The above expansion
holds for every position, with finitely many fixed constants.
Let \(e\) denote the identity permutation. Equation (1) gives
\[
\lambda(t)=\frac{t+p_e}{\prod_i\widetilde K_{ii}(t)}
=t+c+O(t^{-1}),\qquad
c=p_e-\sum_iQ_{ii}.
\]
Therefore, for every permutation,
\[
t+p_\sigma
=\lambda(t)\prod_i\widetilde K_{i,\sigma(i)}(t)
=t+c+\sum_iQ_{i,\sigma(i)}+O(t^{-1}).
\]
Subtract \(t\) and take the limit. This proves (3).

For a fixed relative unitary \(U\), the real signed determinant law
in MOC/Weights.lean gives real coefficients
\[
w_\sigma=\Re\left(
\frac{\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_iU_{i,\sigma(i)}}{\det U}
\right),\quad
\sum_\sigma w_\sigma=1,\quad
\sum_{\sigma:\sigma(i)=j}w_\sigma=|U_{ij}|^2.
\tag{4}
\]
These coefficients need not be nonnegative. They nevertheless give the
complex determinant as \(\sum_\sigma w_\sigma p_\sigma\).
If (3) holds, the determinant is
\(c+\sum_{ij}|U_{ij}|^2Q_{ij}\). Birkhoff's theorem produces a
nonnegative probability law with the same singleton marginals, so
the same determinant is a convex combination of the matching products.
This proves the last assertion. QED.

Unlike the order-four proof using the Hessian of the invariant cubic,
this translation-line argument is valid in every order and involves
only finite products and an expansion at infinity. It also covers
degenerate spectra and zero matching products at the original point.

## 3. General contact-reduction theorem

**Theorem.** Fix a finite order \(n\ge2\) and a complex unitary \(U\).
If the hull inclusion fails for this \(U\), then it fails for the same
\(U\) with at least \(2n-2\) contact permutations. Different contacts
can have the same complex matching product.

Use strict separation and absorb the support direction by a common
spectral scaling. Work in the minimum-support convention. Let \(p\)
be the matching profile, let \(\pi\) attain the minimum real part, and
let \(T\) be the \(k\) matching labels with that real part. Let \(w\)
be the real signed determinant law for the fixed \(U\). Define
\[
D(p)=\sum_\sigma w_\sigma(p_\sigma-p_\pi).
\]
The starting profile has \(\Re D(p)<0\).

Suppose \(k<2n-2\). In the matching-profile variety \(X_n\), fix the
\(k-1\) complex differences \(p_\tau-p_\pi\) for
\(\tau\in T\setminus\{\pi\}\). Also fix the complex value of
\(D(p)\). There are \(k\) affine linear conditions. Each irreducible
component through the starting point has dimension at least
\(2n-2-k\ge1\). The last condition keeps the determinant violation
strict. It replaces the normalization of the sum of the real gaps.

Some difference \(p_\sigma-p_\pi\) varies on this component.
Otherwise the component lies on a translation line in the constant
profile direction. A positive-dimensional closed algebraic subset of
that line is the whole line. Section 2 would give the hull inclusion,
which contradicts the strict violation.

Cut the component by general affine hyperplanes through the starting
point to obtain a curve on which that difference still varies. For a
component of dimension \(d\), use \(d-1\) hyperplanes. The same
hyperplanes cut the proper level set of the varying difference to
local dimension zero. The dimension theorem then supplies a curve
component through the point that is not in this level set.

Normalize and compactify this curve. Remove the finitely many points
over infinity. On the remaining connected curve, the old differences
are constant and all differences \(H_\sigma=p_\sigma-p_\pi\) are
holomorphic. If all real gaps stayed nonnegative, each transform
\(1/(1+H_\sigma)\) would be bounded by one. These transforms extend
over the removed points. On the compact connected surface they are
constant, contrary to the variation of a difference.

Thus some real gap is negative at a point on the punctured curve.
The minimum of the gaps for labels outside \(T\) is continuous.
It starts positive and is negative at that point. At an intermediate
point it is zero. This adds a contact, keeps all old contacts, and
keeps \(D(p)\) fixed. Every point of the affine curve is an exact
spectral profile. Repeat until there are at least \(2n-2\) contacts.

The proof does not require a new maximizing mixer. The mixer stays
fixed. The dimension and curve construction are mathematical steps;
the Lean results do not yet supply this construction.

## 4. Consequence for n = 5

There are \(120\) matching labels. Their products can coincide. Any violation has a witness
with at least eight contact permutations.

By the minor expansion and complementary minors, the support
functional on the unitary group uses only the singleton and
two-subset image indicators; larger subsets are complementary.
There are 25 singleton and 100 two-subset indicator functions,
plus the constant. Their span has dimension 42. The permutation representation on the two-subsets
decomposes into the irreducibles of dimensions 1,4,5, and its matrix
coefficient span has dimension \(1^2+4^2+5^2=42\).

Thus the corresponding order-five minor polytope has 120 vertices
and affine dimension 41. The matching cone itself has complex
dimension eight. The full conjecture is equivalent to proving
nonnegative determinant gaps for every order-five complex unitary
and every realizable profile with at least eight zero projected gaps.

The eight-contact condition is a reduction, not a proof. The remaining task is an argument on realizable spectral profiles, not a census of label subsets.

The order-four geometric realification argument does not extend
formally: ten general points of \(\mathbb P^4\) do not lie on an
elliptic normal quintic. Indeed the family of embedded elliptic
normal quintics has dimension 25 (one elliptic modulus and the
24-dimensional projective linear group); adding ten marked points
gives dimension at most 35, smaller than the 40-dimensional space
of ten ordered points of \(\mathbb P^4\). This rules out a direct
copy of that method for generic configurations, not the
realification conjecture itself. The contact theorem above avoids
needing any order-five realification result.

## 5. A proved structured class in order five and higher: real Cauchy mixers

Zheng's Appendix C.5 uses a nonnegative network identity for
Cauchy-type real orthogonal matrices in order four. The dense
Cauchy argument extends to all n, without needing order-four MOC.

**Theorem.** Suppose the relative unitary is equivalent by diagonal
unitary left and right factors and row/column permutations to a
real orthogonal Cauchy matrix
\[
O_{ij}=\frac{u_i v_j}{r_i-s_j},
\]
where all factors are real nonzero, all \(2n\) real nodes \(r_i,s_j\)
are distinct. Then there is a probability law on \(S_n\), depending
only on the mixer, which simultaneously represents every squared
minor. In particular MOC holds for all complex spectra for such
a mixer in order five and every finite order.

Proof. Diagonal phases do not change squared moduli of minors;
relabeling only relabels the law. Work with \(O\).
The \(2n\) marked columns of \([I_n,O]\) lie on a real rational
normal curve. Explicitly let
\(v(t)=(1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1})^T\). The polynomial vector with entries
\(u_i\prod_{k\ne i}(r_k-t)\) is \(T v(t)\) for an invertible real
matrix \(T\), since these Lagrange polynomials are independent.
At \(t=r_i\) it is a nonzero multiple of coordinate vector \(e_i\);
at \(t=s_j\) it is a nonzero multiple of column \(j\) of \(O\).
Hence the marked columns can be written \(c_\ell T v(t_\ell)\)
with real nonzero scalars \(c_\ell\), for the \(2n\) distinct
nodes \(t_\ell\).

Orthogonality of \(O\) says
\[
[I,O]\operatorname{diag}(I,-I)[I,O]^T=0.
\]
After applying \(T^{-1}\) and its transpose, it yields
\[
\sum_{\ell=1}^{2n}\epsilon_\ell c_\ell^2 t_\ell^k=0
\quad(0\le k\le 2n-2),
\]
where \(\epsilon_\ell=1\) for coordinate columns and \(-1\) for
columns of \(O\). Arrange the nodes increasingly and let
\(\chi(t)=\prod_\ell(t-t_\ell)\).
The \((2n-1)\times2n\) Vandermonde matrix in this equation has
one-dimensional kernel generated by
\((1/\chi'(t_\ell))_\ell\) (Lagrange interpolation of polynomials
of degree at most \(2n-2\)). These coefficients alternate in
sign. Hence coordinate-column and \(O\)-column labels alternate.

Rescaling the columns by their nonzero factors and changing
coordinates identifies the configuration in this alternating
order with a totally positive Vandermonde point of
\(\mathrm{Gr}(n,2n)\). Use the nonnegative complementary
Plücker-product identity of T. Lam, *Dimers, webs, and positroids*,
J. Lond. Math. Soc. 92 (2015), 633--656,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/jdv039),
Theorems 2.1 and 3.1, in the form recalled as (C.12) in Zheng's paper:
\[
\Delta_S\Delta_{S^c}
=\sum_{\mu\ {\rm noncrossing},\ 
       \text{each pair meets }S,S^c}
 W_\mu,\quad W_\mu\ge0,\quad |S|=n .
\]
Here the weights \(W_\mu\) depend on the fixed totally positive
point, not on \(S\), and \(\mu\) runs over noncrossing perfect
matchings of the \(2n\) boundary labels. Every such matching pairs
odd positions with even positions, since the number of vertices
between the endpoints of any chord is even. In the alternating
order this makes \(\mu\) a permutation between the coordinate
and \(O\)-column labels.

Let \(S_0\) be the coordinate labels. Every noncrossing matching
is compatible with \(S_0\); consequently
\(\sum_\mu W_\mu=\Delta_{S_0}\Delta_{S_0^c}>0\).
For row set \(I\) and column set \(J\) of the same size, use
\(S=(S_0\setminus I)\cup J\), where the column labels in \(J\)
are from the \(O\) block. Compatibility is exactly the condition
that the matching permutation maps \(I\) onto \(J\).
The ratio of complementary Plücker products is unchanged in
absolute value by the common coordinate change and column
scalings. Jacobi's complementary-minor identity for real
orthogonal \(O\) identifies this ratio with
\(|\det O[I,J]|^2\). Therefore the weights
\(W_\mu/(\Delta_{S_0}\Delta_{S_0^c})\) give the required single
nonnegative probability law. The determinant minor expansion
then proves MOC for all complex spectra. QED.

This proof uses Lam's published identity as a mathematical input.
It does not assert that every real orthogonal mixer is of this type,
nor that arbitrary complex order-five mixers can be realified.

## 6. Lean results and the next step

- [GeneralContactReduction.lean](MOC/GeneralContactReduction.lean) proves
  the translation obstruction. Infinitely many realizable translates
  already force an affine sum of singleton terms. It also proves the
  analytic transfer for a supplied infinite fixed-gap curve.
- [ContactCurveStep.lean](MOC/ContactCurveStep.lean) turns that transfer
  into a strict increase of the contact set. [ContactIteration.lean](MOC/ContactIteration.lean)
  proves that repeated increases reach the target.
- [RealizableContactKernels.lean](MOC/RealizableContactKernels.lean) proves
  the normalized chart and shows how to recover its entries from
  matching ratios. Its general pivot chart also covers profiles with
  zero coordinates.
- [ContactWeightPositivity.lean](MOC/ContactWeightPositivity.lean) gives
  nonnegative laws for certain grouped objectives. It also shows that
  a large contact set does not make all signed determinant weights
  nonnegative.
- [OrderFiveContact.lean](MOC/OrderFiveContact.lean) proves the block
  reduction. If a mixer with a proper block split or with a row or
  column of degree at most two gives an order-five failure, order four
  fails. The file also proves the eight-contact equivalence under the
  one-contact transfer premise for mixers with no zero entries.

- [AffineContactStep.lean](MOC/AffineContactStep.lean) constructs the next
  contact directly on an affine profile line that keeps the same gap.
  It gives an explicit test when only one spectral entry can vary.
- [SpectralMinorDecomposition.lean](MOC/SpectralMinorDecomposition.lean)
  checks which expectations suffice for a spectral objective. Along a
  one-entry complex pencil, the hull test for every entry is equivalent
  to one law on at most five permutations.

- [OrderFivePairMarkers.lean](MOC/OrderFivePairMarkers.lean) proves
  that order-five doubleton image masses alone determine every spectral
  determinant objective for fixed weights. It does not give the weights.
- [NestedContactObjective.lean](MOC/NestedContactObjective.lean) constructs
  a nonnegative law when the objective splits into scores on the images
  of two nested subsets.
- [TripleFibreContact.lean](MOC/TripleFibreContact.lean) bounds a
  three-label fibre by four contacts at nonzero height for simple spectra.

- [TripleFibreAmbient.lean](MOC/TripleFibreAmbient.lean) applies the
  fibre bound in any order. In order five it fixes two assignments.
- [SubsetAssignmentSupport.lean](MOC/SubsetAssignmentSupport.lean)
  checks a bound from an assignment certificate on subsets for one
  objective. It does not require a common law for all spectra.
- [FiniteCurveGrowth.lean](MOC/FiniteCurveGrowth.lean) proves uniform
  root growth for monic polynomial families and proves that polynomially
  bounded holomorphic functions off a finite set agree with polynomials.
  It also proves that a proper holomorphic factorization off finitely
  many points contradicts irreducibility. The next analytic step is
  to derive such factors from a disconnected cover by simple roots.

The missing Lean step is the geometric construction from Section 3.
Produce an infinite curve of exact profiles through a strict witness.
Keep its old complex contact differences and its complex determinant
gap fixed. After that step, the stated transfer and iteration apply.
The eight-contact inequalities themselves still require a proof.
