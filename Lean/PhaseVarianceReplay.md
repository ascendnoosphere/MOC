# Phase, marker, and stability checks for Cycles 03 and 04

## Verified in Lean

* Cycle 04 Paths 02 and 14, and the corresponding Cycle 03 phase-contractivity target: [UnitaryPhaseCuts.lean](MOC/UnitaryPhaseCuts.lean) proves the closed-polydisc bound directly, without a maximum-modulus argument. If \(V_{ij}=U_{ij}z_{ij}\), with \(|z_{ij}|\le1\), then the trace of \(VV^*\) is at most \(n\). The arithmetic/geometric-mean inequality on its nonnegative eigenvalues gives \(|\det V|\le1\). Taking the normalized determinant at both \(z\) and \(\bar z\) isolates the real-signed weights and proves the continuum phase-cut inequality. This is not positivity of the individual signed weights.
* Cycle 04 Path 11 and the Cycle 03 moment-lift issue: [PhaseGramJet.lean](MOC/PhaseGramJet.lean) proves the formal Gram-series quartic identity. When the off-diagonal linear coefficients vanish, all degrees 1–3 of its determinant vanish. In degree 4 only permutations moving exactly two positions contribute. Each is a transposition; for the Hermitian phase Gram series its contribution is minus the square modulus of the corresponding quadratic entry coefficient. The theorem gives the exact sum and the nonpositivity conclusion for every genuine \(U\). It is a statement about \(\det G\), not yet the full complex-centred signed fourth moment.
* Cycle 04's independent threshold variance target: [ThresholdVariance.lean](MOC/ThresholdVariance.lean) proves the finite-law gap bound, its sharp integer-lattice version, and the comparison between sums of squared level increments and squared sums. [RankOneLattice.lean](MOC/RankOneLattice.lean) also assembles the physical projection-minor identity and proves the genuine rank-one lattice bound for nested integral thresholds. The stronger bound at the adjacent achievable scores remains open.

## Independent symbolic replay of the pentagon phase-null example

Use the adjacency matrix \(C\) of an undirected loopless \(d\)-regular graph on \(n\) vertices and the flat reflection \(U=I-(2/n)\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\). The first Gram variation vanishes: each row of \(C\) has sum \(d\), and the two diagonal terms in the weighted difference cancel by symmetry. For distinct \(i,j\), writing \(\kappa_{ij}\) for the number of common neighbours,
\[
\sum_k (C_{ik}-C_{jk})^2 U_{ik}U_{jk}
= \frac8{n^2}(d-\kappa_{ij})-\frac4n C_{ij}.
\]
This comes from the disagreement count \(2(d-\kappa_{ij})\) and the two special terms \(k=i,j\); no permutations are enumerated. The actual Gram acceleration is the negative of this expression. On the pentagon \(d=2\); adjacent pairs have \(\kappa=0\) and distance-two pairs have \(\kappa=1\). The acceleration is \(4/25\) and \(-8/25\), respectively, so the squared Frobenius norm is \(32/25\) and the mean score is \(4d/n=8/5\).

For this real mixer every normalized signed weight is real. The centered determinant series \(F_0\) has constant 1, linear coefficient 0 by the singleton score mean, and real quadratic coefficient 0 because the Gram determinant has no quadratic coefficient. Therefore \([t^4]|F_0|^2=2[t^4]\operatorname{Re}F_0=m_4/12\). The swap formula gives \([t^4]\det G=-\|G''(0)\|_F^2/8\), hence \(m_4=-48/25\). This is a paper replay of the specific signed fourth moment from the checked Gram mechanism. It rules out classical arbitrary-score fourth-moment positivity, not the Marcus–de Oliveira conjecture.

For complex \(U\), vanishing of the Gram quadratic coefficient alone does not imply that the **complex** quadratic coefficient of \(F_0\) vanishes. A separate argument gives that cancellation: \(M=(C\circ U)U^*\) is Hermitian on a phase-null direction, and \(N=(C^2\circ U)U^*\) has \(\operatorname{tr}N=\|(C\circ U)\|_F^2=\operatorname{tr}(M^2)\). The second derivative of the determinant at the identity then gives complex cancellation after centering. This analytic last step is not imported by the Gram-jet module.

## Fourier, harmonic, and literature boundaries

* Tadej and Życzkowski, [Defect of a unitary matrix](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2008.02.036), [author copy](https://chaos.if.uj.edu.pl/~karol/pdf/TZ08.pdf): the phase defect is infinitesimal and bounds possible fibre dimension. A first-order phase-null direction need not integrate; the pentagon is a second-order obstruction.
* Frappier, [Inequalities for entire functions of exponential type](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1984-073-4), [publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/8D6BEFCD2A4F5AF6B600DE338C56F516/S000843950006896Xa.pdf/inequalities-for-entire-functions-of-exponential-type.pdf), equation (1): the Bernstein derivative bound supports the Cycle 03 interval **upper** variance argument. No entire-function derivative inequality is imported by the algebraic modules.
* Dritschel, [Factoring non-negative operator valued trigonometric polynomials in two variables](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00208-024-02895-9): the nonnegative result is genuinely two-variable. The multivariate factorization quoted there needs strict positivity; contact cuts touch zero. Nonnegative Toeplitz quadratic forms can be deduced by integrating a nonnegative phase function against a modulus square, but the completeness of such tests in marker coordinates is not proved. Phase cuts need not lie in the subset-marker span. This track does not certify a finite harmonic or Toeplitz dictionary.
* Lyons, [Determinantal probability measures](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10240-003-0016-0): projection DPP marginals supply the separate threshold laws. The independent model has relaxed support, possibly strictly larger than the actual permutation-score set. Its lattice bound does not settle the true adjacent-score question.
