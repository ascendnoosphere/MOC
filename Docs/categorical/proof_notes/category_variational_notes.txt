1. NOTATION.
For a unitary U in U(n), let its complete squared-minor kernel be

 R(U)[I,J]=|det U[I,J]|^2,      |I|=|J|, I,J subsets of {1,...,n}.

All Boolean weights are retained. A common permutation law means
R(U)=sum_pi lambda_pi R(P_pi), lambda_pi>=0, sum lambda_pi=1.
Such a law proves MOC simultaneously for every pair of complex spectra,
by the exterior-minor expansion of det(diag(a)+U diag(b)U*).

Let P and Q be partitions of the coordinate labels. U respects P when it
is block diagonal on the P-blocks, and V respects Q similarly. Form the
bipartite incidence multigraph G(P,Q): its left vertices are P-blocks,
its right vertices are Q-blocks, and coordinate i is an edge joining the
unique two blocks containing i. Two labels in the same P/Q intersection
give parallel edges. A pair of parallel edges is a cycle of length two.

THEOREM 1 (EXACT UNAVERAGED COMPOSITION).
The following are equivalent for fixed partitions P,Q:

 (i) G(P,Q) is a forest as a multigraph.
 (ii) R(UV)=R(U)R(V) for every U respecting P and V respecting Q.

In particular, under (i), if the individual blocks of U and V have common
permutation laws, UV has a common law obtained by multiplying the two
probability distributions on permutations.

2. PROOF THAT A FOREST ELIMINATES ALL INTERMEDIATE INTERFERENCE.
Fix row and column subsets I,J of equal cardinality. Cauchy--Binet gives

 det(UV)[I,J]=sum_{|K|=|I|} det U[I,K] det V[K,J].

A nonzero term must satisfy the occupancy equalities

 |K intersect P_alpha|=|I intersect P_alpha| for every P-block,
 |K intersect Q_beta|=|J intersect Q_beta| for every Q-block.

Interpret K as a subset of graph edges. These equations prescribe its
degree at every graph vertex. In a forest, a 0/1 edge subset with specified
vertex degrees is unique if it exists: subtract two solutions and remove
leaves successively; the incident edge difference at a leaf must be zero,
and induction removes the entire forest.

Thus the Cauchy--Binet sum contains at most one nonzero summand. Squaring
its absolute value yields exactly

 |det(UV)[I,J]|^2=sum_K |det U[I,K]|^2 |det V[K,J]|^2.

This includes the case where no admissible K exists, when both sides are
zero. It holds at every Boolean weight simultaneously, proving (ii).
The argument uses no reality assumption and allows arbitrary complex
phases, arbitrary singular minors, and arbitrary block sizes.


4. POSITIVE COMPOSITION OF COMMON LAWS.
If R(U)=sum_p lambda_p R(P_p) and R(V)=sum_q mu_q R(P_q), Theorem 1 gives

 R(UV)=sum_{p,q} lambda_p mu_q R(P_p P_q).

All weights are nonnegative and sum to one. In the row convention
P_p[i,p(i)]=1, P_p P_q=P_(q composed with p). Hence this is an explicit
distribution, independent of every spectral parameter.

Every 3x3 unitary has a common law: a Birkhoff decomposition of its squared
entry matrix gives the weight-one law, and complementary minors give the
same law at weight two. Weights zero and three are both the scalar one.
Thus arbitrary complex U(3) seeds are permissible in this theorem, in
addition to U(2) seeds and any larger blocks with separately proved laws.

5. OVERLAPPING THREE-SITE BLOCKS AND HYPERTREE CIRCUITS.
In particular, if U acts on sites S and V on sites T with |S intersect T|
<=1, their block-partition incidence graph is a forest. Hence exact
composition holds, even when both U and V are nontrivial arbitrary
three-site unitaries. The example S={1,2,3}, T={3,4,5} is connected and is
not a direct sum of its two gates.

More generally, construct a circuit from gates whose supports are
hyperedges. Require each new support to meet each previously connected
component in at most one coordinate. Equivalently, the bipartite incidence
graph of sites and gate supports is a forest (after discarding trivial
one-site gates). At every step the current unitary is block diagonal on
the old connected components. The new gate and those components have
a forest incidence graph, so Theorem 1 applies inductively.

If every gate has a common law, the entire circuit has the product law.
Consequently every circuit of arbitrary complex U(2) and U(3) gates on
such an incidence forest satisfies MOC for all complex spectra in every
dimension, with one spectrum-independent distribution on permutations.

Pairwise intersections of gate supports having size at most one are NOT
enough: three supports may form a cycle by intersecting in three different
coordinates. The required condition concerns the full incidence graph,
or equivalently intersection with already connected components.

This extension allows internally arbitrary three-site mixing at a newly
attached clique. Such a gate need not decompose into two-site gates each
joining previously separate components: a general U(3) seed has intrinsic
three-site interference. Its complete seed law, rather than a claim that
its internal interference vanishes, is used in the composition proof.

6. FULL EXACT RATIONAL FIVE-DIMENSIONAL EXAMPLE.
Take

 H1=(1/9)[[7,-4,-4],[-4,1,-8],[-4,-8,1]],
 H2=(1/7)[[6,-2,-3],[-2,3,-6],[-3,-6,-2]].

They are orthogonal Householder reflections, and every entry is nonzero.
Embed H1 on sites {1,2,3} and H2 on {3,4,5}, calling the resulting matrices
U and V. Their product is

 W =
 [[ 7/9, -4/9,  -8/21,   8/63,   4/21],
  [-4/9,  1/9, -16/21,  16/63,   8/21],

Thus this is an explicit full coefficient identity, proving the MOC

9. A SINGLE MATCHING CYCLE: EXACT RANK-ONE INTERFERENCE.
Now let n=2r, and let P,Q be perfect matchings of the coordinate labels
whose union is one alternating cycle. Parallel matching edges are allowed
when r=1. Let U and V be arbitrary complex unitary two-site layers on P and
Q, respectively. Thus there are 2r two-site gates in total.

The cycle has two complementary alternating coordinate sets K0,K1, each
meeting every P-pair and every Q-pair in exactly one coordinate. Choose an
ordering (p0,p1) of every P-pair and (q0,q1) of every Q-pair. Define

 f_P(I)=(-1)^(number of selected second P-coordinates),

when I selects exactly one coordinate in every P-pair, and f_P(I)=0
otherwise. Define f_Q similarly. Their supports lie entirely in weight r.
Let I0 contain all first P-coordinates and J0 all first Q-coordinates, so
f_P(I0)=f_Q(J0)=1. Define the actual complex amplitude

 kappa=det U[I0,K0] conjugate(det U[I0,K1])
       *det V[K0,J0] conjugate(det V[K1,J0]).

For a diagonal phase D with entries exp(i theta_j), put
theta=sum_{j in K0}theta_j-sum_{j in K1}theta_j and c=Re(exp(i theta)kappa).
Then the entire complete kernel obeys the exact identity

              R(UDV)=R(U)R(V)+2c f_P f_Q^T.                  (C1)

All interference is at the middle exterior degree and has matrix rank one.

Proof. A nonunique intermediate occupancy set on a cycle must differ by
the alternating cycle flow. For its two 0/1 solutions to be distinct, every
cycle vertex must have occupancy one; hence |I|=|J|=r, with I a P-transversal
and J a Q-transversal, and the two possibilities are K0,K1. Otherwise the
intermediate subset is unique and there is no cross term.

For a P-transversal I, the product
det U[I,K0] conjugate(det U[I,K1]) equals a fixed complex scalar times
f_P(I). Indeed block determinant expansion factors it over P-pairs. The
row regrouping signs cancel between the two determinants. In each local
unitary, switching the selected row changes the factor u_i,k0 conjugate
(u_i,k1) to its negative, by orthogonality of the two columns. The analogous
product for V changes only by f_Q(J), by row orthogonality. Taking I0,J0
fixes the common scalar to kappa. The two cross terms are conjugate, and
the diagonal phase contributes exp(i theta), proving (C1).

If gate g has mixing probability p_g=|offdiagonal entry|^2, then

              |kappa|=product_g sqrt(p_g(1-p_g)).             (C2)

This follows immediately from the same local factors. If a gate is
deterministic, kappa=0 and exact composition holds for every phase.

10. SIGNED OUTCOME IDENTITY AND A POSITIVE CYCLE COMPOSITION THEOREM.
Index the 2^(2r) classical gate outcomes by bit strings s in {0,1}^{2r}.
A zero means identity and a one means swap at that gate. Let pi_s be the
resulting permutation in the same two-layer order, and let

 w_s=product_g p_g^(s_g)(1-p_g)^(1-s_g),
 chi_s=(-1)^(sum_g s_g),
 sigma=f_P(K0) f_Q(K0) in {+1,-1}.

Then

             sum_s chi_s R(P_pi_s)=2 sigma f_P f_Q^T.         (C3)

Proof. The signed product over the first matching is
A_P=product_{e in P}(I-R(P_swap_e)). It annihilates every Boolean basis
state containing both or neither coordinate of some pair. On the remaining
middle-weight transversal states it is f_P f_P^T, since each two-coordinate
factor is [[1,-1],[-1,1]]. Likewise A_Q=f_Q f_Q^T. Their product is
f_P(f_P^T f_Q)f_Q^T. There are exactly two simultaneous transversals K0,K1;
complementation multiplies each f by (-1)^r, so their two products agree
and sum to 2 sigma. Expanding A_P A_Q gives the left side of (C3).

Combining the phase-averaged classical law with (C1)-(C3) yields an exact
permutation expansion for the individual phase:

        R(UDV)=sum_s [w_s+sigma c chi_s] R(P_pi_s).           (C4)

Its weights sum to one. Therefore it is a common positive law whenever

                     w_s+sigma c chi_s>=0 for all s.        (C5)

This is a proved all-spectra MOC-preserving composition criterion with
explicit weights. It allows genuine nonzero interference on a closed cycle.
A convenient symmetric sufficient condition is

 |c|<=product_g min(p_g,1-p_g).                              (C6)

When every gate is nondeterministic, write
kappa=|kappa| exp(i phi0). Then (C6) is

 |cos(theta+phi0)| <=
       product_g sqrt(min(p_g,1-p_g)/max(p_g,1-p_g)).         (C7)

Consequences:
* For arbitrary gate probabilities there is a nonempty open phase interval
  around quadrature where full common-law composition is valid.
* At exact quadrature c=0, the individual kernel equals its phase average.
* If every gate is balanced, p_g=1/2, condition (C7) holds at every phase.
  Thus arbitrary complex balanced matching-cycle circuits have common laws
  for every phase, in all even dimensions.
* For several disjoint cycle components, impose the condition separately
  on each cycle and take product laws; path components are covered by the
  incidence-forest theorem. Thus arbitrary two matching layers admit
  nontrivial, explicitly described phase regions with a common law.

Failure of (C5) only means this particular signed correction is not a
positive law. It does not disprove existence of a different law or MOC.

11. A DETERMINANT-SPECIFIC CYCLE GLUING THEOREM.
For arbitrary complex spectra a,b, formula (C1) gives the scalar defect

 det(D_a+UDV D_b V*D*U*)
   -sum_s w_s product_i(a_i+b_{pi_s(i)})

 =2c [product_{(p0,p1) in P}(a_{p1}-a_{p0})]
      [product_{(q0,q1) in Q}(b_{q0}-b_{q1})].              (C8)

To check this, insert the middle-weight rank-one defect into the exterior
determinant expansion. The sum over I of a_(I-complement) f_P(I) factors
into product_P(a_p1-a_p0), and the sum over J of b_J f_Q(J) factors into
product_Q(b_q0-b_q1).

Consequently, if even one P-pair has equal a-eigenvalues, OR one Q-pair has
equal b-eigenvalues, the actual determinant equals the phase-averaged
positive permutation mixture for every phase and every gate probability.
This removes the cycle defect for that spectral class without requiring
the whole kernel to compose. Other eigenvalues are completely arbitrary.

The unrestricted all-phase inequality for biased gates would require the
classical support margin to dominate the right side of (C8). Neither (C4)
nor the generic phase-average identity establishes that domination.

12. EXACT NONZERO-INTERFERENCE CYCLE EXAMPLE.
In dimension four take U as the layer on pairs (1,2),(3,4), and V as the
layer on pairs (1,3),(2,4), every local gate being

                     [[3/5,4/5],[-4/5,3/5]].

Insert D=diag((7+24i)/25,1,1,1). Here every p_g=16/25 and
the right side of (C7) is (3/4)^4=81/256>7/25, so the theorem applies.
With K0={1,4}, K1={2,3}, I0={1,3}, J0={1,2}, one gets

 kappa=-20736/390625,
 c=-145152/9765625,
 sigma=1.

Thus c is nonzero: this example is not merely a zero-interference phase.
The exact verifier category_cycle_verify.py uses Gaussian rational pairs
and checks all 70 equal-size minor pairs, the rank-one defect (nonzero at
16 minor pairs), and the 16 positive corrected permutation weights.
Their minimum is 18873/9765625 and they sum to one.

This is a full complex four-dimensional all-spectra certificate for a
closed-loop circuit with nonzero coherent interference.

13. COMBINING THE POSITIVE CYCLE AND OVERLAP THEOREMS.
Treat each matching-cycle circuit satisfying (C5) as one common-law seed.
Such seeds can have arbitrarily many sites and nonzero interference.
Attach them, together with arbitrary U(3) seeds, along an incidence
hyperforest as in Section 5. Theorem 1 composes their positive laws exactly.
This gives connected all-dimensional circuits containing arbitrarily many
closed coherent cycles, as long as the cycle gadgets meet only through
acyclic single-coordinate attachments. No averaging over their internal
phases is needed.

For a balanced cycle the explicit law is especially simple:

 lambda_s=2^(-2r)[1+sigma cos(theta+arg kappa) chi_s].

Thus the entire coherent phase dependence is reproduced by a classical
bias of the parity of the local swap choices. At the two extreme phases
one uses only even or only odd outcomes; at quadrature both parities have
equal weight. This explains how nonzero cycle interference can coexist
with a full common permutation law, and why demanding zero interference
would discard valid positive composition families.
