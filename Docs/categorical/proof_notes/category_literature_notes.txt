1. Primary results with an actual mathematical connection.

(A) Nested projection determinantal measures.
Russell Lyons, Determinantal Probability Measures, Publ. Math. IHES98(2003),
167-212. https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0204325
Theorem6.2: nested subspaces H1 subset H2 give stochastic domination of their
projection determinantal measures. Finite Strassen coupling gives nested
random subsets. This applies directly to column subspaces of a unitary, hence
to its squared minors. See the derived flag law in section2 below.
Question10.1 asks for more: a disjoint coupling of PH1 and PH2 whose union
has law PH1+H2. The paper's positive ground-set<=5 result does not establish
arbitrary dimensions or labelled Boolean cubes.
Primary source refs: turn49view0; theorem find turn51view0; question turn51view1.

(B) Strong Rayleigh / stable polynomial theory.
Julius Borcea, Petter Branden, Thomas Liggett, Negative dependence and the
geometry of polynomials, JAMS22(2009),521-567.
https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.2340
Proposition3.5 puts positive-contraction DPPs in the strongly Rayleigh class.
Theorem4.13: 0<=K<=L<=I implies stochastic domination, without assuming KL=LK.
Theorem4.20: partial symmetrization by a transposition preserves multiaffine
complex stability. These are rigorous tools for nested marginals and averaged
permutation operations. They do not supply compatibility among crossing
subset labels; the actual-unitary labelled-square obstruction proves that
such a compatibility conclusion cannot follow in this generality.
Primary source refs: turn49view1; finds turn51view2 and turn51view3.

2. Derived flag law, with every singleton marginal included.

THEOREM. Let U be any n-by-n complex unitary. Fix any column ordering
j1,...,jn and Jk={j1,...,jk}. There is a probability law on permutations
sigma:[n]->[n] such that, simultaneously,
  Pr(sigma(Jk)=I)=|det U[I,Jk]|^2 for every k and every |I|=k,
  Pr(sigma(j)=i)=|U[i,j]|^2 for every i,j.
The same law also reproduces all complementary flag minors.

PROOF. Let Qk be projection onto span{U[:,j1],...,U[:,jk]}. Its projection
DPP has masses muk(I)=|det U[I,Jk]|^2 on k-element sets. Theorem6.2 yields
adjacent nested couplings kappak between muk and muk+1. Disintegrate each
finite coupling into a Markov transition Kk(I,I')=kappak(I,I')/muk(I), choosing
an arbitrary conditional law on zero-mass I. Compose these transitions,
starting at S0=empty, to obtain S0 subset S1 subset ... subset Sn=[n] with
Sk distributed as muk. Define sigma(jk) to be the unique element of
Sk minus Sk-1. This is a permutation and gives the first formula.
For singletons use nesting, not an extra coupling:
 Pr(sigma(jk)=i)=Pr(i in Sk)-Pr(i in Sk-1)
               =(Qk-Qk-1)[i,i]=|U[i,jk]|^2.
Taking complements and using the unitary complementary-minor identity gives
the complementary flag statement. QED.

Consequently, every complex function on permutations in the span of all
singleton indicators and one flag's subset indicators has its squared-minor
evaluation in the convex hull of its permutation values. This is a genuine
common positive law on a specified test space. The law may depend on the
chosen flag. It cannot be glued indiscriminately to other flags: the audited
labelled-square example gives an exact obstruction.

3. Exact boundary of the flag test in dimension4.

Fix source partition12|34. The flag test space is generated by all singleton
indicators and by 1_{sigma({1,2})=I} for the six output pairs I. Its dimension
is12. The full two-row matching-function space has dimension14.
For z_sigma=product_j(a_{sigma(j)}+b_j), exact elimination gives
 z belongs to this fixed flag space iff
 (b1-b2)(b3-b4)(a1-a2)(a3-a4)=0 and
 (b1-b2)(b3-b4)(a1-a3)(a2-a4)=0.

A representation-theoretic derivation is short. Terms of degrees0,1,3,4 in
the subset expansion lie in the singleton space. The only new representation
at degree2 is the two-dimensional(2,2) module. On the three unordered pair
partitions12|34,13|24,14|23 the a-coefficient is the centered vector of
 (a1a2+a3a4, a1a3+a2a4, a1a4+a2a3),
and similarly for b. The(2,2) matrix coefficient is their outer product.
Fixing the flag restricts the b-direction to the line on which the second
and third coordinates agree. Their difference is(b1-b2)(b3-b4). The two
independent differences of the a-vector are the displayed a-products.
Hence either the b-direction is the flag line or the a-vector vanishes.
The latter means at least three a-values coincide. Generic spectra therefore
require b1=b2 or b3=b4, so this flag theorem alone does not add a generic
four-point spectral MOC proof. For a real support functional Re(eta z), the
two equations become the real parts of eta times those products.
