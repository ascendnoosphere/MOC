3. A faithful fixed-spectral repair has completely computable topology.

Fix a single matching vector z, with pairwise distinct entries and a
two-dimensional hull P. Define

 K(z)={A subset S_n: some eta!=0 has A subset argmax_pi Re(eta z_pi)}.

This retains the common spectral witness lost in the existential union.
Its maximal simplices are the full label sets lying on the exposed edges
of P. Adjacent edge simplices meet at their one common extreme-point
label; nonadjacent edge simplices are disjoint. Labels lying strictly in
the interior of P are not vertices of this complex.

PROPOSITION. K(z) is homotopy equivalent to a circle.
Proof. On every edge simplex choose a linear retraction onto the interval
joining its two endpoint labels, sending each intermediate collinear
label to its affine position on that interval. The straight-line homotopy
from identity to this retraction stays in the simplex and fixes its two
endpoints. Because different edge simplices meet only at fixed endpoints,
the homotopies fit together. The resulting deformation retract is the
polygonal cycle. QED.

If P is a segment, a perpendicular support direction exposes every label
at once, so K(z) is a full simplex and contractible. The same is true
when P is one point. Thus the fixed-spectral contact complex has only the
circle/contractible alternatives for elementary planar reasons. It does
not depend on U or on det(D_a+U D_b U*), so its topology cannot distinguish
unitaries satisfying or violating the desired inclusion.

For one FIXED direction eta, the local contact complex is simply the full
simplex on its active labels. The proved high-contact theorem says that
a hypothetical counterexample can be moved to a point with at least2n-2
distinct active labels. This supplies a large simplex, which is acyclic;
it does not imply nonzero homology in any positive degree.

4. Adding the determinant yields a contractible visible contact complex.

For a fixed z and a point d outside P, the strict separating directions are

 V(d)={eta in S^1: Re[eta(d-z_pi)]>0 for all pi}.

Before intersecting S^1 these directions form an open convex cone in R^2.
Since the cone is nonempty and contains no line, V(d) is one open angular
interval of length at most pi. The contact faces encountered while eta
runs through this interval form one consecutive chain of vertices and
edges on the boundary of P. The simplicial complex they generate is
contractible: collapse each edge simplex as above to obtain a path, a
single interval, or a point. When d is in P there are no such directions.

Therefore merely restricting contacts to violation directions does not
produce a homological contradiction: when a violation exists, its visible
contact complex is contractible, as it is for any exterior point of any
convex polygon. The nonemptiness of V(d) is exactly separation, not a new
unitary-specific obstruction.
