WHY THESE CHECKS PROVE COMPLETENESS.
The abstract oriented simplices, with their checked formal boundary
cancellation, map affinely into the boundary of the actual convex polytope
P. The resulting top-dimensional geometric chain is closed. Degenerate
simplices contribute zero top-dimensional multiplicity, but are retained
when checking formal boundary cancellation. Every nondegenerate contribution
has the same orientation relative to the interior point c, and at least
one is nonzero. Thus the pushforward chain is a nonzero positive chain.

The boundary of a full-dimensional convex13-polytope is an oriented
connected12-sphere. A closed top-dimensional geometric chain on it has
constant integer multiplicity away from a lower-dimensional subdivision.
One can see this by taking a common refinement of the finitely many affine
simplex images with a boundary triangulation: cancellation at each interior
ridge makes the coefficients of adjacent top cells agree. Connectedness
propagates the equality, and positivity makes the common multiplicity
strictly positive. Equivalently this is the degree argument for radial
projection from c to the sphere. The chain therefore covers every open
facet of P. Because every simplex image lies in a listed supporting facet,
an unlisted facet would have an uncovered open subset, a contradiction.
The82 facets are consequently complete.

This is why checking only the82 supporting inequalities and ranks would
not have sufficed, whereas the checked closed positive chain does suffice.
