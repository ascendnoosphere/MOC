STANDALONE HIGH-CONTACT LOCALIZATION FOR THE COMPLEX MOC
Independent dimension, realization and deformation audit, 2026-10-17.

This proves a necessary form of any counterexample. It does not assert
that such high-contact configurations are impossible.

THEOREM.
Let n>=4 and fix U in U(n). Suppose some finite complex spectra a,b and
eta in C give the strict support violation

 g=Re[eta det(D_a+U D_b U*)]
      -max_(pi in S_n) Re[eta product_i(a_i+b_pi(i))] >0.

Then there are finite spectra a',b' for the SAME U and SAME eta such that:

 (i) the support violation is exactly g;
 (ii) all n! complex matching products are pairwise distinct;
 (iii) at least 2n-2 of them attain the maximal real support value.

In particular, every possible n4 counterexample can be replaced by one
having at least six distinct matching products on a supporting line.
No claim of uniqueness, minimality, or novelty in the literature is made.

The proof uses standard algebraic-geometry facts (dimension of fibers,
Chevalley's constructibility theorem, dimension drop by equations, and
normalization of an algebraic curve) but not the prior volume's global
invariant-quotient realization theorem.

1. A standalone dimension calculation.

Let R be the irreducible affine variety of n-by-n complex matrices of rank
at most2, and let

 Phi(C)_pi=product_i C_(i,pi(i)).

Let Y be the Zariski closure of Phi(R). The rank2 locus of R has dimension
4n-4: write C=XY with X of size n-by-2 and Y of size2-by-n, both full rank.
This parameter space has dimension4n, and its fibers are the free changes
of basis (X,Y)->(XT,T^-1 Y), T in GL2, of dimension4.

Restrict to the nonempty open locus R0 of rank2 matrices with all entries
nonzero. If Phi(C')=Phi(C), then C' also has all entries nonzero, since
every entry occurs in a nonzero matching product. For distinct rows i,k
and distinct columns j,l, divide the products of two permutations that
agree elsewhere and exchange the indicated assignments. This recovers

 C_ij C_kl/(C_il C_kj)

from Phi(C). All such crossratios therefore agree for C and C'. Taking
row1 and column1 as anchors gives

 C'_ij/C_ij=r_i s_j

for nonzero row and column factors r_i,s_j. The equality of matching
products is precisely product_i r_i product_j s_j=1.

Conversely, every such row/column scaling preserves both rank and Phi.
The scaling group with this product constraint has dimension2n-1, and
its one-dimensional stabilizer is r_i=t, s_j=t^-1 for every i,j. Thus
each fiber over Phi(R0) has dimension2n-2. The dimension-of-fibers theorem
gives

 dim_C Y=(4n-4)-(2n-2)=2n-2.

Y is irreducible because R is. It is an affine cone because scaling C
scales every matching product by the same nth power, and every complex
scalar has an nth root.

2. A dense open set of actual finite spectra, without global surjectivity.

Every C in R0 has a factorization C_ij=x_i y_j with nonzero row covectors
x_i in C^2 and nonzero column vectors y_j in C^2. Make a common generic
GL2 basis change so that all second coordinates (x_i)_2 and all first
coordinates (y_j)_1 are nonzero. This is possible because each forbidden
zero is a proper algebraic condition and there are only finitely many.

Put r_i=(x_i)_2, s_j=(y_j)_1,
 a_i=(x_i)_1/(x_i)_2, b_j=(y_j)_2/(y_j)_1.
Then

 C_ij=r_i s_j(a_i+b_j),
 Phi(C)=rho (product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)))_pi,
 rho=product_i r_i product_j s_j !=0.

Choose lambda in C with lambda^n=rho. Replacing a,b by lambda a,lambda b
absorbs rho. Thus every point Phi(R0) is an actual finite spectral product
vector, with no extra scalar left over.

By Chevalley, Phi(R0) is a dense constructible subset of Y, so it contains
a nonempty Zariski-open subset O0 of Y. Every point of O0 is therefore
actual finite spectra. The actual spectral image is invariant under
nonzero scalar multiplication. Hence

 O1=union_(c in C*) c O0

is a nonempty conic Zariski-open subset of Y, still consisting entirely
of actual finite spectral vectors.

Fix the four permutations obtained from a base permutation by independently
swapping assignments in row pairs {1,2} and {3,4}; all other assignments
stay fixed. Their matching coordinates satisfy on Y

 z00 z11=z10 z01.

Let Gamma=z00-z10-z01+z11. This is not identically zero on Y: on additive
spectra it is, up to sign,

 (a1-a2)(a3-a4)(b_j1-b_j2)(b_j3-b_j4)
                     product_(i>=5)(a_i+b_pi(i)).

For any two different permutations pi,sigma, z_pi-z_sigma is likewise
not identically zero on Y. Indeed their additive product polynomials
have different sets of irreducible factors a_i+b_j.

Intersect O1 with Gamma!=0, all z_pi!=0, and all z_pi-z_sigma!=0. The result
O is nonempty, conic and Zariski-open in irreducible Y, and all its points
are actual finite spectra with pairwise distinct matching products.

The additive spectral map is dominant onto Y by the preceding argument.
Its preimage of O is therefore nonempty Zariski-open, hence Euclidean
dense in C^(2n). Strict violation is an open condition. We may perturb
the initial spectra into this preimage, keeping U and eta unchanged.
If the resulting positive violation is g1, scale all eigenvalues by the
positive real number (g/g1)^(1/n). All matching products and the determinant
then scale by g/g1, restoring exactly the original gap g. Since O is
conic, the perturbed and rescaled point remains in O.

3. The determinant as a linear function on Y.

For fixed U, the identity

 det(D_a+U D_b U*)=det(D_a U+U D_b)/det U

gives

 d_U(z)=sum_pi c_pi z_pi,
 c_pi=sgn(pi) product_i U_(i,pi(i))/det U,
 sum_pi c_pi=1.

Thus d_U is a well-defined holomorphic linear function of the matching
coordinate vector. The coefficients may be complex; this proof does not
require them to be real or nonnegative. On every actual spectral point
of O, d_U is exactly the determinant for the SAME prescribed unitary U.

4. One deformation step adding an active matching product.

Suppose the current point z belongs to O, has gap g, and has k<2n-2 active
labels. Choose an active label pi0. On Y impose the k complex linear
equations

 d_U(z)-z_pi0=P0:=(d_U-z_pi0)(current),
 z_pi-z_pi0=(z_pi-z_pi0)(current),
                         for every other currently active label pi.

The first condition preserves Re(eta P0)=g. The others preserve all old
support ties. Because dim Y=2n-2, the local dimension of their common
fiber at the current point is at least2n-2-k>=1. There is a nonconstant
irreducible algebraic curve through that point in the fiber.

Take the smooth compact normalization S of this curve. Remove its
finitely many points at infinity and the finite preimage of Y\O. The
latter set is finite because the curve contains a point of O and a
proper closed subset of an irreducible algebraic curve is finite. Let
S' be the remaining connected Riemann surface. A connected Riemann
surface remains connected after deleting finitely many points.

The pullbacks of all matching coordinates are meromorphic on S and
holomorphic on S'. For inactive labels put

 delta_pi=Re[eta(z_pi0-z_pi)].

These are harmonic. The active deficits are identically zero. Let Omega
be the connected component containing the starting point of the open
set where every inactive deficit is strictly positive.

If Omega has a boundary point in S', at that point every deficit remains
nonnegative and at least one new deficit is zero. Thus the number of
active labels strictly increases. The point lies in O, so it has actual
finite spectra, all products remain distinct and Gamma remains nonzero.
The first fiber equation preserves the exact positive gap g, with U and
eta unchanged.

It remains to prove that this boundary must exist. If it did not, Omega
would be nonempty, open and closed in connected S', hence Omega=S'.
Every delta_pi would then be nonnegative on S'. A meromorphic function
on compact S whose real part is nonnegative outside finitely many points
has no pole: a nonzero leading pole term takes both positive and negative
real parts in every punctured disk. It is consequently holomorphic on
compact S and constant. Apply this to eta(z_pi0-z_pi). Since eta!=0,
all complex differences z_pi-z_pi0 are constant.

The entire matching-coordinate curve would thus have the form

 z(q)=m+c(q)1,

with c(q0)=0 at the initial point. Its fixed quartet equation becomes

 0=(m00+c)(m11+c)-(m10+c)(m01+c)
   =c Gamma(m).

Gamma(m)!=0 because m lies in O. Hence c is identically zero, contradicting
the nonconstant algebraic curve in matching-coordinate space.

The boundary exists and supplies the next point. Repeat the step while
k<2n-2. The number of active labels strictly increases, so after finitely
many steps the asserted threshold is reached. All products stay distinct,
the spectra remain finite, and U, eta and g remain exactly the same. QED.

5. Exact remaining obligation.

This theorem localizes a hypothetical counterexample to a support line
containing at least2n-2 distinct matching products. It neither proves
positivity on that locus nor identifies its possible face configurations.
Those are genuine remaining mathematical questions. In particular, the
argument cannot be described as an unrestricted MOC proof.
