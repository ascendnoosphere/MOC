UNIVERSAL WEIGHTED-MINOR COMPENSATION: ANALYTIC PROOF
Research certificate, 2026-10-17. This proves one inequality and one residual
nine-contact face; it is not an unrestricted MOC4 proof.

THEOREM. For every complex U in U(4), put rho(I,J)=|det U[I,J]|^2 and
f=rho(3,3)+rho(2,4)+rho(12,13)-rho(13,23), using one-based indices.
Then, for every tau>0,
  f + tau rho(23,34) + tau^{-1} rho(13,13) >= 0.             (W)
Equivalently f >= -2 sqrt(rho(23,34)rho(13,13)).

The equivalence follows by minimizing over tau>0 (and taking limits when
one minor vanishes). The proof first establishes (W) on SO(4), then uses
the previously proved equality of convex hulls of real-orthogonal and
complex-unitary common-minor profiles (supplied volume N013). It does NOT
apply Jensen directly to the nonlinear square-root expression.

FOUR-CIRCLE LEMMA.
Let X,Y,U,V>=0, X+Y=U+V=1. Let
 a^2+x^2=X^2, b^2+y^2=Y^2, c^2+u^2=U^2, d^2+v^2=V^2.
Define
 p=X+U-1=X-V=U-Y,
 t=-x+y+u+v,
 f=ab+cd+XU+YV-xu+yv.
Then f+|p t|>=0.

PROOF OF THE LEMMA.
By continuity it suffices to take positive radii and p != 0. The map
 (X,Y,U,V)->(Y,X,V,U),
 (a,x;b,y;c,u;d,v)->(b,y;a,x;d,-v;c,-u)
 preserves f and maps (p,t) to (-p,-t). Thus assume p>0.

First prove f>=0 on t=0. A minimum exists on this compact constraint set.
If all four cosine coordinates a,b,c,d vanish, then x=eps1 X,
y=eps2 Y,u=eps3 U,v=eps4 V and
 f=XU(1-eps1 eps3)+YV(1+eps2 eps4)>=0.
Otherwise the gradient of t on the four circles is nonzero: its components
are, up to harmless choices of angle orientation, (-a,b,c,d).
Consequently Lagrange multipliers mu,lambda1,...,lambda4 exist at a
constrained minimum, with
 b=lambda1 a,       -u-mu=lambda1 x,
 a=lambda2 b,        v+mu=lambda2 y,
 d=lambda3 c,       -x+mu=lambda3 u,
 c=lambda4 d,        y+mu=lambda4 v.                       (S)
The first equations imply that a,b either are both zero or both nonzero;
the same holds for c,d.

If a=b=0 and c,d are not both zero, x,y cannot have opposite signs:
t=0 would give |u+v|=X+Y=1, forcing |u|=U,|v|=V and c=d=0.
Flipping all four vertical coordinates does not change f or t=0, so take
x=X,y=Y. Put alpha=U-u and beta=V+v. Both are nonnegative. The equation
t=0 implies U+u=2X-beta and V-v=2Y-alpha. Hence
 f=cd+X alpha+Y beta
 >= X alpha+Y beta-sqrt(alpha beta(2X-beta)(2Y-alpha))
 >= X alpha+Y beta-2 sqrt(XY alpha beta) >=0.
Here 2X-beta=U+u>=0 and 2Y-alpha=V-v>=0; their product is at most4XY.

If c=d=0 and a,b are not both zero, u,v cannot have the same sign:
t=0 would force |x-y|=1 and then a=b=0. Flip all vertical signs to take
u=U,v=-V. Put alpha=X-x,beta=Y-y. Then
 2X-alpha=2U-beta, 2Y-beta=2V-alpha,
and
 f=ab+U alpha+V beta
 >= U alpha+V beta-sqrt(alpha beta(2U-beta)(2V-alpha))
 >= U alpha+V beta-2 sqrt(UV alpha beta)>=0.

It remains to consider a,b,c,d all nonzero. Set k=lambda1,l=lambda3;
then lambda2=1/k,lambda4=1/l. If kl!=1, solving the four vertical
equations in(S) gives
 x=mu(1+l)/(1-kl), u=-mu(1+k)/(1-kl), y=kx, v=-lu,
and therefore t=-2mu. Since t=0, mu=0 and x=y=u=v=0. Thus
 f>=XU+YV-XY-UV=p^2>=0.
If kl=1, the first/third vertical equations force mu(1+l)=0 and
mu(1+k)=0. If mu!=0, then k=l=-1 and again t=-2mu, impossible.
So mu=0, u=-kx,v=y/k, and t=0 gives (k+1)(y-kx)=0.
For k!=-1 this yields Y=|k|X,V=U/|k|, hence XU=YV, contradicting
p=XU-YV>0. For k=-1, b=-a,d=-c,u=x,v=-y. The norm equations give
X^2-Y^2=U^2-V^2; since both radius sums equal1, X=U,Y=V. Substitution
then gives f=0. This proves the entire t=0 boundary assertion.

Now suppose f+p|t| has a negative global minimum. It cannot have t=0.
Flipping all vertical signs preserves f and reverses t, so assume t>0.
In a neighborhood of this point the objective is f+p t. Equations(S)
hold with mu=p. Individual-circle second variations give lambda_i<=0.
At a vanishing cosine pair (a,b)=(0,0), the second variation in these
two cosine coordinates has matrix [[-lambda1,1],[1,-lambda2]]; hence
lambda1 lambda2>=1. At (c,d)=(0,0), likewise lambda3 lambda4>=1.

If both pairs are nonzero, the preceding solution of(S), including the
kl=1 exceptional case, gives t=-2p, impossible.

If a=b=0 and c,d are nonzero, write lambda3=-k,lambda4=-1/k with k>0.
Then u=(x-p)/k,v=-k(y+p). Direct algebra gives
 lambda1 lambda2-1 = -p(t+2p)/(xy).
Its required nonnegativity forces xy<0. If x=X,y=-Y, then
 t=-1+u+v<0 because c,d are nonzero. If x=-X,y=Y, then
 |u|=(X+p)/k<U and |v|=k(Y+p)=kU<V.
Thus k>(X+p)/U=(V+2p)/U but k<V/U, impossible.

If c=d=0 and a,b are nonzero, write lambda1=-k,lambda2=-1/k, k>0.
Then x=(u+p)/k,y=-k(v+p), and direct algebra gives
 lambda3 lambda4-1 = p(t+2p)/(uv).
Its required nonnegativity forces uv>0. If u=-U,v=-V, then
 t=-x+y-1<0. If u=U,v=V, then |x|=(U+p)/k<X and
 |y|=k(V+p)=kX<Y. Hence k>(U+p)/X=(Y+2p)/X and k<Y/X,
again impossible.

Finally, when all cosine coordinates vanish, the displayed corner
formula already gives f>=0. This exhausts all possibilities and proves
f+p|t|>=0. Continuity supplies zero radii and p=0. QED.

FROM THE CIRCLE LEMMA TO SO(4).
Write O=L(q)R(r) with unit real quaternions, in the conventions of N090.
Set
 X=q0^2+q2^2, Y=q1^2+q3^2, U=r0^2+r2^2, V=r1^2+r3^2,
 a=q0^2-q2^2, x=2q0q2,
 b=q3^2-q1^2, y=2q1q3,
 c=r0^2-r2^2, u=2r0r2,
 d=r3^2-r1^2, v=2r1r3.
Exact quaternion minor identities give
 f=ab+cd+XU+YV-xu+yv,
 det O[23,34]=(-x+y+u+v)/2=t/2,
 det O[13,13]=X+U-1=p.
Thus the lemma gives f>=-2|det O[23,34]det O[13,13]|.
Weighted AM-GM proves(W) for each tau, and convexification proves it for
all complex unitary matrices. In particular, the result has no phase
assumption or restriction on the unitary's zero pattern.

APPLICATION TO ONE NINE-CONTACT FACE.
In the old82-ray nonnegative-permutation common-minor cone, consider
 g=a r3+b r12+c r21+d r30+f78, with a,b,c,d>=0.
Its mixed ray f78 is the row/column relabeling of f given by
 alpha=(0,1,3,2), beta=(3,1,0,2), with transformed permutation
 pi -> alpha o pi o beta. The two compensation rays become
 r21=rho(23,34), r30=rho(13,13) in standard-f coordinates.
Let A=1+a,B=1+b. The exactly reconstructed necessary spectral nonic is
 A^2 B^2(1+c)[(A+d)(B+d)-d(1+c)(1+d)].
For an actual spectral cost this polynomial vanishes. Since A,B>=1,
this forces d>0 and
 c=[AB+d(A+B-1)]/[d(1+d)]>=1/d.
Consequently
 g(U)>= f78(U)+(1/d)r21(U)+d r30(U)>=0
for every complex unitary U. The removed a r3,b r12 and
(c-1/d)r21 terms are nonnegative ordinary squared minors.
An arbitrary nonnegative coefficient lambda on f78 causes no gap: if
lambda=0 the cost is already an ordinary-minor sum; if lambda>0, divide
the entire actual cost by lambda and apply the normalized argument.
Positive scaling preserves actuality and support inequalities.
This closes that entire coefficient face once the stated necessary
spectral-nonic identity is imported; no converse lifting claim is used.

APPENDIX: THREE-COMPENSATOR PROPAGATION.
For every mixed ray, its three relabeled compensator pairs are the three
edges of a triangle on three ordinary squared-minor values x,y,z. Thus
  f>=-2 min(sqrt(xy),sqrt(xz),sqrt(yz)).
Let a,b,c>=0 and S=a+b+c. A sufficient condition for
  f+a x+b y+c z>=0
is, for each coefficient w among a,b,c,
  if w<=1: S>=2;
  if w>=1: w(S-w)>=1.
Proof: order the three values so x is smallest and y is second smallest.
Then min(sqrt(xy),sqrt(xz),sqrt(yz))=sqrt(xy), and z>=y gives
 a x+b y+c z-2sqrt(xy)>=y[a t^2-2t+(b+c)], t=sqrt(x/y) in[0,1].
If y=0 the conclusion is immediate. Otherwise the quadratic's minimum
is S-2 when a<=1, and b+c-1/a when a>=1. Applying the stated conditions
for whichever coefficient is attached to the smallest value proves the
claim. At w=1 the two formulas agree.
This is a propagation rule, not a claim that every spectral cost meets
its hypotheses.
