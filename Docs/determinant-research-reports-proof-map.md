# Determinant proof routes and obstructions

For complex normal matrices $A$ and $B$ of the same order, the
Marcus–de Oliveira conjecture asks whether $\det(A+B)$ lies in the
real convex hull of the products obtained by matching their eigenvalues.
Count eigenvalues with multiplicity. Write $a_i$ and $b_j$ for the two
lists and $U$ for the unitary change of eigenbasis. A matching product
is $P_\sigma=\prod_j(a_{\sigma(j)}+b_j)$ for a permutation $\sigma$.
The unrestricted conjecture remains open.
[Conjecture](../Lean/MOC/Core.lean#L33) · [Proof tree](../README.md#proof-tree)

Each route gives a determinant identity, a conditional implication, or
an obstruction. The links give the hypotheses of the Lean statements.
Public paper arguments and open implications have their own scope.
[Other research routes](research-attempts-proof-map.md)

## Determinant, rank, and coefficient tests

| Rank of the pair-sum kernel | For any complex unitary $U$, the determinant in spectral coordinates is an entrywise-product determinant divided by $\det U$. ## Compression, phase, and realization

| Subject | Relation to the determinant problem | Lean source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fractional-linear transport | Let $f$ be an invertible fractional-linear map with no poles at $a_i$ or $-b_j$. Replace $a_i$ by $f(a_i)$ and $b_j$ by $-f(-b_j)$. At each fixed unitary, this rescales the determinant and every matching product by the same nonzero factor. The rectangular comparison with one extra column gives an exact size reduction. | [Fractional covariance](../Lean/MOC/ProjectiveTransport.lean#L61) · [Pole chart](../Lean/MOC/ProjectivePole.lean#L15) · [Rectangular equivalence](../Lean/MOC/PrincipalCoisometry.lean#L228) |
| Compression with rank-one defect | A rank-one defect permits a one-variable quadratic disk test. The disk maximum is explicit. | Singularity of a normal sum | For any two lists, some relative unitary gives a singular sum if and only if the convex hulls of the lifted lists $a_i$ and $-b_j$ intersect. | Determinant modulus | A unitary entrywise-product determinant is bounded in modulus by a largest matching modulus. A maximizing bound can be sought on the support of the unitary. The centered determinant inequality for all centers remains equivalent to the conjecture. | [Entrywise modulus](../Lean/MOC/DeterminantModulusBound.lean#L53) · [Support-sensitive bound](../Lean/MOC/DeterminantModulusBound.lean#L91) · [Normal sums](../Lean/MOC/DeterminantModulusBound.lean#L141) · [Monomial inequality](../Lean/MOC/NewtonPolytopeModulus.lean#L103) · [Centered monomial obstruction](../Lean/MOC/NewtonPolytopeModulus.lean#L128) |
