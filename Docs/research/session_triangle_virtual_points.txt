Matching-product convex geometry for the triangle budget

Let P_i=product_k(alpha_k+beta_{pi_i(k)}) with lexicographic
permutation indices. Set

  L=P_1+P_6+P_14+P_23-P_0,
  H=max_i Re(eta P_i),
  lambda=Re(eta L)-3H,
  p=H-Re(eta P_18), q=H-Re(eta P_14), r=H-Re(eta P_9).

Thus, for fixed alpha,beta and all complex eta, the triangle-budget
inequality C(p,q,r)>=lambda is equivalent to the following concrete
plane-convex-hull assertions. They are not proved here.

For each choice of i in {18,14,9}, with j,k the other two, let

 W_i(t)=[4t L+4t^2 P_i+P_j+P_k]/[12t+4t^2+2],  0<=t<=1/2.

Then all W_i(t) must lie in conv{P_0,...,P_23}. At t=0 they are
the pair midpoints and lie in the hull automatically. All three
curves meet at

 W_*=[2L+P_18+P_14+P_9]/9  when t=1/2.

Derivation: the exact dual of the pair-certificate budget is the
minimum of p*y_18+q*y_14+r*y_9 over y>=0 with y_i*y_j>=1/4.
An optimizer is (1/2,1/2,1/2) or a permutation of
(t,1/(4t),1/(4t)), 0<t<=1/2. The inequality lambda<=sum y_i*g_i
for all eta is exactly the support-function condition that the
associated virtual point belong to the complex-plane convex hull.
The t=0 endpoint is the limit of the star family. This is an exact
equivalent geometric problem for the triangle budget; it does not
establish the candidate.

A stronger finite sufficient condition is inclusion of W_* and the
three Bezier control points

  V_i=(L+P_j+P_k)/5.

To see this, write x=2t. Up to a positive denominator, W_i is the
quadratic Bezier combination of W_i(0), V_i, and W_* with positive
weights 2, 5, 9 respectively. Each W_i(0) is already in the hull.
The four inclusion statements are equivalent, for all eta, to

  2lambda<=p+q+r,
  lambda<=p+q, lambda<=p+r, lambda<=q+r.

These four inequalities are stronger than the triangle budget and
are not asserted to be true. A finite random hull search is exploratory
only; in particular an actual example violating one of the pair-sum
inequalities would refute the finite strengthening but not necessarily
the triangle budget itself.

Finite normal-cone test and two-contact reduction

For fixed alpha,beta suppose the convex hull of the 24 complex products
is two-dimensional. For each vertex, its outward normal cone is
generated by the normals of its two incident edges. Inside that cone,
H=Re(eta P_vertex), so every gap g_i and lambda are linear in eta.
The function C(p,q,r) is concave and positively homogeneous: combining
feasible edge-budget allocations and using concavity of sqrt(uv)
proves this directly. Hence C-lambda at any eta in the vertex cone
is at least the same nonnegative linear combination of its values on
the two edge normals. Therefore the triangle inequality for all eta
is equivalent to checking its finitely many outward hull-edge normals.

Each edge normal makes at least two permutations contacts. Thus, to
prove the candidate for every actual gap it suffices to prove it for
actual gaps with at least two permutation contacts. The possibly
one-dimensional or singleton hulls are limits of two-dimensional
hulls, and the gaps, lambda and C are continuous, so they follow by
perturbing alpha,beta and passing to the limit. This is a rigorous
reduction to two-contact gaps, not a proof of their triangle budget.

For the specific alpha,beta in actual_gap_outside_ordinary_cone.txt,
code/research/verify_f34_triangle_hull_edges.py uses exact Gaussian
integer products and checks the capacity inequality on all nine
outward hull-edge directions. Thus that fixed alpha,beta satisfy the
triangle inequality for every complex eta, not merely for the single
eta in the original example. This is one family, not a global proof.
