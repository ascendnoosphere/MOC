1. Full-height extraction of the true four-space

Use the orthonormal tetrahedral L, L^T L=I and L^T1=0, and the universal
quadratic profile psi(H)_j=vec(H)^T M_j vec(H) in the fourteen matching
coordinates. For invertible H put

 S=L H^(-T) L^T, tau=cof00(S),
 c_pi(T)=sgn(pi)prod_i T_i,pi(i).

Direct calculation gives c(S), evaluated on the matching basis, equal
to psi(H)/(det H)^2. Consequently the projected chart derivatives have
rank nine and

           W5=ker Dpsi_H^T,       dim W5=5, e in W5.

The full-height derivative is

 d= (d/dt)[c(S+t11^T)/tau] at t=0,
                 d.e=16.

Thus K4=W5 intersect d-perp has dimension four and excludes e. The
replay also differentiates c(T) in ALL SIXTEEN matrix entries and
checks rank ten and the same kernel K4. This prevents discarding the
tenth tangent direction or silently working with the five-space.

At the two anchors, every coefficient of the fourteen gradient-quadrics
restriction is computed exactly. The cubic itself restricts identically
to zero, checked by the four polynomial Euler-derivative identities.
For every basis vector g of K4, M(g)vec(H)=0 as well. Together these
are direct coordinate checks of K4 contained in the secant on the
nonempty Q!=0 chart, hence by closure on the whole four-space.

3. Why the generic entry curve is the quadric-ruling elliptic quartic

The primary input is the HMSV gradient-ideal theorem: the fourteen
partial derivatives of the skew cubic generate the ideal of M8.
The Segre construction and quadric-pencil description are also explicit
in HMSV, arXiv:1103.5255v1, Theorem 3.4 and Lemma 3.5, with the
geometric construction stated in the introduction. The local source
files were read directly:

 docs/references/m8_hmsv/0809.1233v1/8pts-090708.tex
 docs/references/m8_hmsv/1103.5255v1/8pts-032711.tex

Primary URLs:
 https://arxiv.org/abs/0809.1233
 https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.5255

There is also a useful direct matrix proof of the relevant incidence.
For a finite critical source z_pi=prod(a_i+b_pi(i)),

             sum_pi c_pi(T) z_pi=det(D_a T+T D_b).     (1)

Therefore the sixteen conormal conditions at S read

        (a_i+b_j) adj(D_a S+S D_b)_(j,i)=0.           (2)

If all cross brackets a_i+b_j are nonzero, (2) is equivalent to

                       rank(D_a S+S D_b)<=2.          (3)

At rank two take a basis r,s of the LEFT kernel of this matrix. Then

 Q(X)=(r.X)(D_a s.X)-(s.X)(D_a r.X)                   (4)

vanishes at all eight columns of [I|S]. The associated two-by-two matrix
of linear forms has ruling ratios a_i at the first four columns and
-b_j at the last four. Thus the critical source recovers its quadric
AND its chosen ruling. Changing the left-kernel basis only rescales Q.

Conversely, a smooth quadric through the eight columns gives two ruling
projections, i.e. eight points on P1 for each ruling. Normalize one
projection to finite values (a_i,-b_j). Its two defining linear rows
give two independent left kernel vectors in (3), so its critical
matching profile lies in K4. Equations (3)--(4) give inverse maps on
the nonempty rank-two/cross-nonzero open set.

The replay constructs a rational hyperbolic decomposition of R0 above,
extracts BOTH finite ruling octuples, checks (3) has rank exactly two,
checks every cross bracket is nonzero, checks both profiles lie on
Q0=Q1=0 in K4, and recovers R0 up nonzero rational scale. One recovered
scale is 805/25992. Hence the inverse is an actual computation, not
only a dimension count.

For clarity about the generic degree: let C be the smooth intersection
of the two original quadrics in P3. The two-ruling cover of its quadric
pencil is Pic^2(C). A degree-two line bundle L gives the ruling map
C->P(H0(L)^*); the complementary bundle O_C(1) tensor L^-1 gives the
other ruling. The four solutions 2L=O_C(1) give the singular pencil
members. This recovers the double cover of the pencil, branched at the
four determinant roots.

For labels X_i,X_j on C, the bracket of their two ruling evaluations
is the determinant section of

              H0(L) -> L|X_i directsum L|X_j.

It vanishes precisely at L=O_C(X_i+X_j), the reduced degree-one theta
divisor on Pic^2(C). A matching product contains four such determinant
sections. All matchings are sections of the SAME degree-four bundle,
since each label occurs once. Thus the ruling-source map has at most
four independent coordinates by Riemann--Roch.

At the exact smooth anchor it is nonconstant and the matrix inverse
(4) makes it birational onto its image. That image lies on the computed
smooth irreducible quartic in K4 and therefore is that quartic; its
span is all P3. Nonvanishing of the corresponding four-section minor
persists on a nonempty Zariski open of H. The complete degree-four
linear system on a genus-one curve embeds it as an elliptic normal
quartic. Its quadratic ideal in P3 has dimension two and generates the
ideal. The primary HMSV gradient-ideal theorem now implies that the
fourteen restricted critical quadrics have span at most two generically;
the exact rank-two witness gives equality on the generic open set.

This proves the generic elliptic entry-curve description. It does not
assert smoothness at every invertible H, as the next example disproves.
Over the reals, identify the ruling cover with Pic^2(C), not with C
without a choice of real point. Here the original C contains the real
columns e_i, so such a point is available. No real-form conclusion is
drawn from equality of the complex j invariant alone.

4. A complete rational future test on the smooth generic fibre

Let E=(Q0=Q1=0) and x in K4 be a real point outside E. The unique
pencil quadric through x is

        Qx=(x^T Q1 x) Q0-(x^T Q0 x) Q1.              (5)

When Qx is nonsingular split (inertia (2,2)), its two ruling generators
through x are real. Choose u,v in the tangent plane x-perp modulo x.
Their isotropic directions are u+r v, where

      A+2Br+Cr^2=0,  A=u^TQx u, B=u^TQx v, C=v^TQx v.

Intersect each generator line with either independent base quadric.
The line discriminant Delta(r) reduces modulo this quadratic to
R0+R1 r. The two discriminant values have rational trace and norm

 T=2R0-2B R1/C,
 N=R0^2-2B R0R1/C+A R1^2/C.                          (6)

A negative line discriminant is exactly a nonreal conjugate pair of
critical endpoints. Away tangencies, one conjugate secant occurs when
N<0, two when N>0 and T<0, and none when N>0 and T>0. Thus T,N give a
rational test; radicals are unnecessary. If Qx has Lorentz signature
(3,1) or (1,3), it has no real ruling generator, hence no conjugate
critical secant through a real x.

Why this tests ALL critical endpoints, rather than only endpoints
assumed to be in K4: the fourteen gradient restrictions span two, so
their differential H_F(x)|K4 has rank at most two. If the global
H_F(x) has rank twelve, its two-dimensional kernel therefore lies
inside K4. Every critical secant decomposition x=p+q satisfies

                       H_F(x)(p-q)=0.

Consequently p,q both lie in K4. The same proves completeness for
conjugate endpoints x+iy,x-iy. The replay checks global Hessian rank
twelve and exact kernel containment at its real chamber samples.

This replaces an unnecessary generic-Terracini assumption by a finite
linear-algebra check. At Hessian rank drops the extra kernel directions
must be retained, as in the degenerate example below.

4A. A stronger uniform endpoint theorem for ACTUAL rank-eight costs

There is an actual-source argument that does not require Hessian rank
twelve. Let A=-eta M(z), so Re A=M(g), and suppose rank Re A=8. The
source theorem gives rank_C A<=4. Therefore rank A=4 and its complex
column space and its conjugate have zero intersection. If real h lies
in ker Re A, then Ah=-bar A h lies in that intersection. Thus Ah=0.

Suppose h=vec(H) with H invertible. The source quadratic has the form

 q_z(H)=-u^T adj(H^T A0+B0 H^T)v.

After constant invertible changes taking u,v to the third basis vector,
this is a nonzero multiple of det ell(H), where ell maps C9 to Mat2.
Rank M(z)=4 forces ell to be surjective. The polar form of the two-by-
two determinant is nondegenerate, so M(z)h=0 implies ell(H)=0.

To interpret this condition, write K=H^(-T). In the orthogonal basis
(e=1/2,L), the matrix D_a S+S D_b is

             [[0, u^T K], [K v, A0 K+K B0]].

Multiplying on both sides by diag(1,K^-1) preserves rank and gives
the bordered matrix [[0,u^T],[v,H^T A0+B0 H^T]]. After u,v are changed
to e3, ell(H)=0 is exactly the vanishing of its interior two-by-two
block. The first three rows are then supported in the last column,
so the whole four-by-four bordered matrix has rank at most two.

Hence rank(D_a S+S D_b)<=2. Its adjugate vanishes, so every one of
the sixteen source-normal derivatives in (2) vanishes. The actual
complex critical source therefore lies in the FULL K4, not only W5.

Consequently, for every actual cost of inverse-pencil rank eight whose
real kernel matrix H is invertible, the real and imaginary source
endpoints belong to the computed entry curve E. The two-quadric ruling
criterion is complete for such actual rank-eight candidates even if a
global cubic Hessian rank condition was not separately checked. Odd
or lower rank costs do not satisfy the disjoint-column-space step and
require the larger source intersection discussed in Section7.

6. H=I: double conic, negative-copy component, and rank boundaries

At H=I the restricted critical ideal is generated by

 q0=2x0x3-8(x1-x2)^2,
 q1=(x1+x2)^2.

Its binary determinant is 32s^3t. The scheme is a double conic in the
plane l=x1+x2=0, with reduced equation x0x3-16x1^2=0.
The original eight-point pencil through [I|I-J/4] has common kernel1;
its base curve is four concurrent lines. These are different degenerations
of the two elliptic descriptions, and neither is a smooth elliptic curve.

For b=-a one has

        D_a(I-J/4)+(I-J/4)D_b=[D_a,I-J/4], rank<=2.

Thus the negative-copy critical sources lie on the reduced entry conic;
their nonconstant one-parameter matching image equals that conic. Its
linear span is the three-plane l=0. On this plane M has a uniform
four-dimensional common kernel: the three symmetric off-diagonal
matrices and I. Consequently rank M<=5, with generic rank five.

One exact principal-eight-minor identity on the full K4 is

 det M[1:9,1:9]
   =-(x1+x2)^6*(x0x3-4x1^2+8x1x2-4x2^2)/67108864.

The negative-copy ordinary theorem from the Grace branch therefore
handles the costs generated by this entry-conic component. Covering
EVERY actual cost satisfying only M(g)vec(I)=0 would additionally
require excluding critical secants with endpoints outside K4 at the
rank-dropped singular secant points. No such blanket exclusion is
asserted here. In particular, the rank-twelve kernel argument of
Section4 must not be applied at a Hessian rank drop.

Section4A nevertheless proves that an ACTUAL rank-eight cost cannot
have kernel matrix H=I: its endpoints would lie in the reduced conic,
whose real span has inverse-pencil rank at most five. Thus any actual
cost in this projected I-kernel space belongs to a lower-rank boundary.

7. A source-column criterion useful at odd-rank boundaries

Suppose the actual complex source matrix has rank four and factor it
as M(z)=CC^T, C of size9 by4. Put L=im C. Then

        rank Re M(z)<=7  iff  L intersect conjugate(L) !=0.         (7)

Indeed Re M(z)=1/2[C,bar C]diag(I4,I4)[C,bar C]^T. If the eight columns
are independent its rank is eight; if dependent their span has dimension
at most seven. The intersection is conjugation-stable and thus contains
a real vector. Rank seven versus a further drop depends on the quadratic
form on the column relation, not on a parity rule. This gives a direct
source interpretation of the odd rank-seven controls in the other branch.

What remains

The smooth-fibre geometry now has an explicit K4 construction, two
quadrics, a rational future criterion, and source/quadric inversion.
It does not by itself build a full C0 Gram for every neutral or future
point. Singular normals and Hessian rank drops require retaining their
larger kernels. The neutral past control above shows exactly why the
future condition and the full C0 condition must remain distinct.
