General rank reduction. Let L:Sym_2(R)->R^m be linear with rank at most 2 and
let g!=0. If some Y>=0 satisfies L(Y)=g, then some rank-one Y>=0 also does.
If Y already has rank one, nothing remains. If Y is positive definite, the
nullspace of L contains a nonzero symmetric direction D. Choose the sign of
t so Y+tD eventually ceases to be PSD, and move from t=0 to the first
boundary point. It remains PSD and has rank at most one; it cannot be zero
because its L-image is g!=0. Therefore PSD feasibility is equivalent to
rank-one feasibility under these hypotheses.

A strict separating functional does not follow merely from infeasibility:
linear images of the PSD cone need not be closed. For example
 L([[u,v],[v,w]])=(u,v), g=(0,1) is infeasible, but is the limit of images
of positive semidefinite matrices [[epsilon,1],[1,1/epsilon]] as epsilon
approaches zero. Thus the separator displayed above is an additional exact
certificate, rather than an automatic consequence of the rank lemma.

The global pencil-sign argument for the all-ones direction generalizes to
any fixed v such that H_v has inertia (2,2,10): H_x v=H_v x and a real
lift obeys Q_v(y)=Q_v(x). If Q_v(x)!=0, a definite H_v restriction on the
2-dimensional Hessian kernel is impossible for the same two-index reason;
rank-12 neutrality then gives [t^2]det(H_x+tH_v)<=0, and lower ranks give
zero. A Q_v=0 extension by actual-spectral continuity requires that Q_v is
not identically zero on spectral parameters; it should not be presumed for
an arbitrary direction v. An exact common spectral anchor with all 34
ordinary-ray Q_v nonzero was independently computed and sent to the team.
