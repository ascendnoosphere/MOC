FULL FIVE-PARAMETER CLOSURE OF THE (0,7,10,18,22) CAP
31 October 2026

The previously open unequal branch is excluded. Combined with the existing
equal-weight and d=0 proofs, this closes the entire nonnegative family

  g = f34 + a r0 + b r7 + c r10 + d r18 + e r22,
  a,b,c,d,e >= 0.

The conclusion concerns actual spectral support gaps: their unitary support
inequality holds. It does not assert that every arbitrary coefficient vector
in this cap is nonnegative on U(4). Unrestricted complex MOC4 remains open.

The main observation is the exact identity P=e^2 A(c+p(c+1)/e). The large
five-variable polynomial is a shifted quadratic in one root coordinate.
This identity proves uniqueness of the relevant root and supplies a global
path to one rational anchor. No subdivision or sampled sign inference enters
the proof.

1. Inputs already proved in the repository

Use the 14-coordinate spectral cubic and its Hessian H. Let E=H(ones14).
An actual gap has x=g-h ones14, F(g)=h Q(g), det H(x)=0, and

  [z^2] det(H(x)+z E) <= 0.                              (1)

The last inequality is the audited necessary pencil-sign theorem in
actual_pencil_sign.txt. Its proof uses the neutral inertia of actual rank-12
Hessians and the (2,2,10) inertia of E, and includes rank drops by continuity.

For this cap the exact pencil is det(H(g)-h E)=4 L(h) R(h), with L linear
and R quadratic. Put

  p=(a+1)(b+1), q=p+d(a+b+1),
  F=-(c+1)(p+e)<0, Q=e(d-1)-1,
  J=p(c+1)+e(c+1-ab),
  P=Q L(F/Q)/(c+1).

The expression P is polynomial. The full determinant identity gives

  L(F/Q)=(c+1)P/Q, R(F/Q)=-(c+1)P/Q.

Thus an actual positive-height point has Q<0 and P=0. Q=0 or h=0 is
impossible since F=hQ and F<0. Negative-height support is covered by the
previously audited external-half-plane theorem. The existing proofs
eight0710_equal_closure.txt and eight0710_d0_closure.txt close a=b and d=0,
respectively, including their nonnegative boundaries. It remains to exclude

  P=0, Q<0, d>0, a!=b, a,b,c,e>=0.                       (2)

2. The shifted quadratic

Define

  T=ab+d(a+b)-d^2-2d-2,
  S=(ab-1)(d^2+1)-(a-1)(b-1)d,
  A(w)=q w^2-p T w-p S.

Exact expansion gives

  P=A(c)e^2+p(c+1)A'(c)e+p^2(c+1)^2 q
   =e^2 A(c+p(c+1)/e).                                 (3)

Since P at e=0 equals p^2(c+1)^2 q>0, every point in (2) has e>0.
Set w=c+p(c+1)/e>c>=0. Conversely, a positive root w of A and 0<=c<w
determine the unique positive coefficient e=p(c+1)/(w-c).

Two identities control the roots:

  A(ab-1)=-ab d(a-b)^2 <= 0,
  S=(ab-1)(d-1)^2+d(p-4).                               (4)

Whenever A has a positive root, S>0 and this root is unique and simple.
To see this, first suppose ab>=1. Then a+b>=2 sqrt(ab), so p>=4;
equality occurs only at a=b=1. Away from that exception (4) gives S>0.
At the exception A=q w^2-pT w has T=-(d^2+1)<0, hence no positive root.
If ab<1, (4) places ab-1<0 between A's roots (possibly at an endpoint).
The existence of a positive root therefore forces the other root to be
negative, and again S>0. The roots have opposite signs since their product
is -pS/q. In particular w depends continuously on a,b,d throughout S>0.

3. Every point in (2) connects to c=0,d=1

Hold a,b,d,w fixed and decrease c to zero, resetting e=p(c+1)/(w-c).
The numerator of Q is affine:

  (w-c)Q=[p(d-1)+1]c+p(d-1)-w.                         (5)

At c=0 it is negative. For d<=1 this follows from w>0; for d>1 the
coefficient of c is positive, so it follows from its assumed negativity
at the starting point. Both endpoint values are negative, hence the entire
segment retains Q<0. This step preserves P=0,d>0,a!=b and e>0.

Now keep c=0 and a,b fixed, and move d to 1, following the positive root w.
For d<=1, S/d=(ab-1)(d+1/d-2)+(p-4). If ab<1 this increases as d increases
to 1, so remains positive. If ab>=1, positivity follows directly from (4).
Q<0 is automatic for d<=1. In either case p>4 at the endpoint.

For d>1, let

  K(d)=(a+b+2)d^2+(ab-a-b+1)d-2ab-a-b.

The exact factorization

  A(p(d-1))=p[p(d-1)+1]K(d)                             (6)

shows that Q<0 at c=0 is equivalent to K(d)<0: the comparison argument
p(d-1)>0 lies below the unique positive root. Also

  K(1)=4-p,
  K'(1+t)=ab+a+b+5+2(a+b+2)t>0, t>=0.

Decreasing d towards 1 keeps K negative. It also keeps a positive root
available: A is negative at the positive comparison argument, and its
upward leading coefficient gives a larger positive root. The root lemma
then gives S>0. Consequently the path retains Q<0 throughout, and p>4
at d=1. This proves the path for both ranges of d; no assertion that d can
always decrease to zero is used.

At c=0,d=1 the remaining domain is exactly

  a,b>=0, (a+1)(b+1)>4, a!=b.

There is an elementary piecewise-affine path to an anchor in each ordering.
If a<b, increase b to B=max(b,4), holding a fixed; this increases p and
preserves a<b. Next decrease a to zero, holding b=B; throughout this
segment p>=(B+1)>=5 and a<b. Finally decrease b to 4 with a=0; again
p>=5. This reaches (a,b)=(0,4). If a>b use the symmetric three segments
to (4,0). The positive root can explicitly be written

  w=(pT+sqrt(p^2 T^2+4qpS))/(2q),

so it and e vary continuously on these paths. No logarithmic change of
coordinates or implicit root-selection assumption is needed.

4. The sign cannot change along these paths

On P=0 both pencil factors vanish at h0=F/Q, so

  a2=[z^2]det(H(g-h0 ones14)+zE)=4 L_h R_h(h0).         (7)

The sign from h0-z does not alter the product of the two first derivatives.
The unequal-branch nondegeneracy proof shows that both derivatives are
nonzero on (2), including a=0 or b=0. Its argument is recalled here to
specify precisely what the continuation requires.

If L_h=0, then L(0)=d p(c+1)J=0, whence ab>c+1 and
e=p(c+1)/(ab-c-1). Substitution instead gives

  L_h=d p(a-b)^2(c+1)^2/(ab-c-1)>0,

a contradiction. The discriminant of R is e^2 D. For 0<d<=1 substitute
d=1/(1+t); (1+t)^4 D has 282 nonnegative coefficients and its a=b=c=e=0
part is (t+2)^2>0. For d>1 and Q<0 write d=1+t,e=1/(t+u), t,u>0;
(t+u)^2 D has 486 nonnegative coefficients and its a=b=c=0 part is
(t+1)^2(t^2+tu+2t+2u+1)^2>0. These exact polynomial certificates cover
all nonnegative a,b,c boundaries. Thus R_h(h0) cannot vanish, even if
the quadratic coefficient of R vanishes and R becomes linear.

At either rational anchor

  (a,b,c,d,e)=(0,4,0,1,10) or (4,0,0,1,10),

one has F=-15,Q=-1,h0=15,L_h=-5,R_h(h0)=-600 and a2=12000>0.
Equation (7) is continuous and nonzero along every path above. Therefore
a2>0 everywhere in (2), contradicting (1). This excludes the entire
previously unresolved branch.

The theorem follows with the two previously proved boundary families and
the height cases in Section 1. A positive coefficient of f34 is normalized
to one by scaling; coefficient zero is an ordinary-minor nonnegative sum.

5. Reproduction and scope

  python code/research/verify_eight_contact_full_0710.py --full-pencil

The verifier reconstructs F,Q and optionally verifies the full 14-by-14
determinant identity from the repository's exact spectral model. It checks (3)--(6), both
nondegeneracy certificates, and both anchors; it also evaluates the new
anchor directly as a Hessian determinant. The handwritten root and path
arguments above supply the continuum step. A finite collection of samples
is not used for that step.

Dependencies: closing_moc4_model_verify.py and its certificate;
actual_pencil_sign.txt and its audits; eight0710_equal_closure.txt;
eight0710_d0_closure.txt; the audited negative-height theorem. The optional
full-pencil mode rechecks the previously proved factorization on all 38,760
nonnegative integer six-tuples of coordinate sum at most 14. Both sides are
polynomials of total degree at most 14: the Hessian pencil is a 14-by-14
affine matrix, and the degrees of L and R are checked directly. This grid is
unisolvent. In the basis product_j binomial(x_j,alpha_j), evaluation is
triangular by total degree, with identity diagonal blocks. Thus agreement
at these exact integer nodes proves the polynomial identity, including
all real and complex parameter values. Bareiss divisions are checked
exactly. The grid is an algebraic identity certificate, not a numerical
sample of the positivity statement.

This is a full closure of one canonical five-parameter family and its
relabelings. Six- and seven-contact families, and other eight-contact
families, remain. No percentage of unrestricted MOC4 is inferred from the
number of closed contact types, and no claim of literature priority is made.
