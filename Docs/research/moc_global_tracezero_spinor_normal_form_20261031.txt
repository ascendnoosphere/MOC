1. BOTH SPECTRA CAN BE MADE TRACELESS, FOR EVERY n>=2

Write c_j=-b_j and assume the2n labels a_i,c_j are all distinct.
Let P(t)=prod(t-a_i), Q(t)=prod(t-c_j), D=P-Q. Distinct labels imply
D!=0; write d=degree(D)<=n-1. The critical polynomial is

  W=P'Q-PQ'=D'Q-DQ'.

Its degree is n+d-1>=n-1>=1, with leading coefficient
(d-n)*lead(D), so it has a finite complex root t. No root of W is one
of the labels: W(a_i)=P'(a_i)Q(a_i)!=0 and
W(c_j)=-P(c_j)Q'(c_j)!=0.

At such a t,
  sum_i1/(a_i-t) = sum_j1/(c_j-t).
Let s be their common average and m(z)=1/(z-t)-s. Then
  a_i'=m(a_i), b_j'=-m(c_j)
have BOTH sums zero. All labels remain finite and distinct.

For any fractional linear m(z)=(rz+s)/(tz+u) with Delta=ru-st!=0,
the noncommuting matrix identity is
  m(A)-m(-B)=Delta*(tA+uI)^-1*(A+B)*(-tB+uI)^-1.
Consequently the determinant and every permutation product acquire
the SAME nonzero scalar factor. MOC is invariant under this operation.
For the critical-centroid m above, that factor is (-1)^n/(P(t)Q(t)).

Thus a theorem for ALL traceless pairs proves unrestricted MOC for
all disjoint simple sources. Continuity of determinant and the finite
permutation polygon extends it to all sources. This is a uniform
normalization of the original problem, not an assumption of real
spectra, circle geometry, or repeated eigenvalues.

2. THE n4 SPINOR QUARTIC

For a trace-zero4-tuple, set
  X=(a0+a1,a0+a2,a0+a3).
Then a=(X0+X1+X2, X0-X1-X2, -X0+X1-X2, -X0-X1+X2)/2.
Write
  q(C)=[2tr((C C^T)^2)-(tr(C C^T))^2]/16
for an arbitrary COMPLEX3x3 matrix C; transpose is not conjugation.
The quaternion left/right multiplication matrices L_i,R_j give
  H(C)=-(1/2)sum_ij Cij L_i R_j,
  H(diag X)=diag(a), det H(C)=q(C)
as a54-term polynomial identity, audited for all9 complex entries.
Every realSO4 conjugation acts on C by C->R C S^T, R,S in SO3.

The audited whole-U4-to-SO4 squared-minor convexification is in
  docs/theorems/profile_geometry/algebra_unitary_realification.md.
It makes an inequality for every realSO4 sufficient for unrestricted

Therefore the remaining whole4x4 theorem can be written
