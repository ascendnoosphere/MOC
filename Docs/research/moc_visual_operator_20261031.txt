1. Stronger bounds, different reasons for failure, and a genuine weak pattern

Image 01 combines four panels with explicitly different populations.

The operator replay has 4,167 noncontrol n=4 small_complete records. The
one-vector Gram certificate succeeds in 542 (13.01%), versus 51 (1.22%)
for the Frobenius-only certificate. All 51 Frobenius successes are among
the Gram successes. This is an increase of 491 observed certificates,
not a new theorem that 13% of arbitrary inputs are easy.

The more useful distinction is why the other 3,625 tests do not certify:

  2,266 / 4,167: a positive origin-centered triangle disk exists, but
                the Gram bound is too large;
  1,359 / 4,167: no positive origin-centered triangle disk exists.

For clustered spectra, 794 of 833 inputs belong to the second category;
only three certify. Improving the determinant modulus bound cannot repair
an origin-centered-disk argument when the relevant hull does not contain
such a disk. The picture identifies a geometric limitation, not a numerical
optimization failure. Conversely, all 833 paired-cancellation n=4 cases
have a positive disk, and 559 fail because the bound remains too large.
Those two families need different improvements.

Panel C uses all 5,994 structured-reflector observations, one per family
at every integer dimension 2 through 1000. Global-maximum scaling labelled
5,260 hulls degenerate. Recorded subset selection recovers 5,216 of these,
and the final inside count is 5,950. The visible red/green separation is a
conditioning artifact in a particular numerical representation. It is not
evidence that the underlying determinant is leaving the permutation hull.
These remain floating-point classifications, not interval proofs.

The apparently flat underflow curve also has a concrete explanation: all
813 underflow flags are from the paired-cancellation family. They first
appear at n=185 and occur throughout most of the later dimensions. With six
families per dimension, a roughly one-sixth pooled plateau is largely a
sampling-composition effect. It is not a universal underflow probability.

Panel D fixes reflector rank at four and restricts n>=100. For Gaussian
spectra, Spearman correlation between n and

  log(|det|/|identity matching product|)/4

is -0.76187 across 301 cases. For near-real spectra it is +0.02847 across
300 cases. The near-real binned medians stay close to a horizontal line,
while the raw scatter remains substantial. This is a useful null result:
the observed dimension trend does not transfer uniformly between spectral
families. It does not refute eventual fixed-rank asymptotics. The very small
imaginary scale in the near-real generator can produce a long finite-size
regime. Fixing rank avoids the additional confounding from its 2/4/8 schedule.

Visual inspection: the failed-test categories remain legible even for the
small clustered and anisotropic success counts. Their almost empty blue
bars are shown rather than hidden by selecting successful families.

2. The exact neighborhood is a picture of bounds, not an observed determinant

Image 02 uses the previously verified nonordinary four-dimensional example.
Its left panel draws only the three exact matching products and several
rigorously justified disks, in units of 10^9. It does not put a fabricated
point at the unknown determinant.

At the center, the matching triangle contains an origin disk of radius
more than 10^9, while the Gram estimate proves |det|<10^8. Across every
spectral perturbation with entrywise complex modulus at most 0.01 and every
unitary perturbation with operator norm at most 0.03, the triangle continues
to contain a disk of radius greater than 970,000,000, while the determinant
modulus stays below 800,000,000. Thus the uniform radial clearance is at
least 170,000,000. The illustrated triangle is the unperturbed triangle;
the dashed 0.97 disk is the guaranteed common inner disk after perturbation.

The right panel separates a continuous upper-bound formula from the simpler
exact constants used in the certificate. At unitary radius 0.001, the recorded
bound is 200,000,000; at radius 0.03 it is 800,000,000. These orange markers are
bounds, not determinant samples. The curve eventually crosses the green
inner-disk radius, which marks the limit of this sufficient argument, not
the appearance of a MOC counterexample. The shaded interval indicates the
reported 0.03 neighborhood rather than claiming all parameters beyond it fail.

The designated support costs stay outside the ordinary-minor cone throughout
the spectral neighborhood. This is full convex-hull containment for the
stated neighborhood of mixers, not a proof for every unitary at those spectra.
The rational verifier passed; there is no claimed Lean formalization.

Visual inspection: the small central blue disk represents |det|<0.10 in
billions. Both the legend and subtitle say that no determinant point is
plotted. After revision, the legends no longer overlap the explanatory text.

3. Positive pinching fails often, including an exact visible exterior point

Image 03 uses 4,095 shape-qualified n=4 inputs. This denominator differs from
4,167: 72 rows fail the axis-ratio guard. Diagonal pinching sends the expression

  product_i (a_i + sum_j |U_ij|^2 b_j)

outside the original matching hull in 910 of the 4,095 retained cases.
The effect is concentrated among clustered spectra: 488/625 Haar inputs
and 70/208 near-permutation inputs fail the replacement, versus 61/626 and
7/208 for Gaussian inputs. These are descriptive generator-conditioned counts.

The exact right-hand panel uses Walsh/2 with

  a=(1,0,1+i,1+2i), b=(2-i,2+i,-2+i,-2i).

The blue polygon is the complete 24-product matching hull. The actual
determinant -1+29.5i is the green star and has positive matching weights
1/34, 193/272, 71/272 on indices 0, 1, 3. The red point is the different, pinched
expression 645/256+135i/64. It lies strictly across the supporting line

  37x-69y=-540,

with support excess 124845/256. This gap is measured in the unnormalized
support functional, not as Euclidean distance. The image makes the logical
error visible: a positive conditional expectation on matrices changes B's
spectrum and does not preserve this determinant-hull conclusion.

Visual inspection: the red point is plainly separated from the hull, while
the green star lies within its explicitly drawn positive triangle. The
caption prevents interpreting the red point as a counterexample to MOC.

4. Large dimension exposes source-specific regimes and a failed sparse rule

Image 04 uses all 7,560 existing dense/reflector local-block replays, n=4…1000.
It shows every finite value of

  log10(|det|/R) = -disk_log_slack/log(10),

where R is the best recorded disk supplied by a restricted three-permutation
rule: identity, one swap, and a second disjoint swap in a four-index block.
This is not the best arbitrary triangle among all sampled permutations.
Below 0 is a successful numerical certificate. Above 0 is an insufficient
bound, not a MOC violation. If the selected triples provide no positive disk,
the ratio is undefined and the case appears in the panel's “no disk” count.
Vertical scales vary between panels and this is stated on the image.

Full plotted counts:

  Family       Reflector pass/total   no disk   Dense pass/total   no disk
  Gaussian          955/997              34          246/317          53
  Disk              943/997              33          not sampled
  Clustered         937/997              58          243/315          54
  Near-real          58/997              30           49/315          50
  Cancellation        0/997               9          158/316          48
  Wide radii        656/997              35          201/315          64

At n>=256 the Gaussian and clustered cases all certify: 745/745 reflectors
and 153/153 dense cases in each family. Disk reflectors also give 745/745.
But near-real gives only 51/745 and 30/153, and paired-cancellation reflectors
still give 0/745. Dimension does not repair this particular selection rule
uniformly. Wider sampled hulls can contain the same target even when every
local three-product certificate fails.

The conspicuous three bands in the cancellation-reflector panel are not
three mysterious populations. They are reflector ranks 2, 4, 8. Over n>=600,
the respective median log10 ratios are 4.32530, 8.94368, 19.23488, with 133, 134, 134
cases. The labels were added after viewing the first draft. They expose the
rank schedule that a pooled smooth fit would obscure.

An essential source confound is now printed directly on this image.
Reflector cancellation uses b=-a+0.03c, aligned with the identity matching.
Dense cancellation uses b=-a[permutation]+0.025c, with shuffled pairs and a
different a generator. Thus the identity-based sparse rule has fundamentally
different reference products. The dense/reflector cancellation contrast
cannot be assigned to the mixer alone. Near-real imaginary scales also differ
(0.001 versus 0.025), as do clustered centers and log-radius ranges. Raw family
names are retained in metrics.json; grouping them into six panels is only
for display. Gaussian is the cleanest spectral-generator comparison here.

The apparent paradox between cancellation underflow and failure of local
triangles is therefore informative. The absolute determinant may be tiny
relative to randomly reassigned products, yet enormous relative to a triangle
that changes only four identity pairs. Underflow and local-certificate failure
measure different reference scales. They are compatible observations.

Visual inspection: all large positive cancellation ratios and near-real
failures remain visible. The rank labels are readable, the zero line separates
certificate success from insufficiency, and undefined ratios are counted.
The disk panel explicitly says dense Haar was not sampled rather than
leaving that missing series unexplained.

Data coverage and limits

The script reads all 20,127 frozen original records and checks the hash before
and after. It joins existing derived records by record_id; all IDs match.
The original source counts are small_complete 9364, large_reflector 5994,
dense_haar 1578, adversarial 3189, and two singleton exact/control records.

The local-block replay contains 15,412 records overall. The 7,852 small cases
are not in the large-dimension figure; they include 1,338 controls and produce
19 successes. The other atlas lanes own the small-case geometry. The operator
figures use their explicitly stated n=4 subsets. The plots therefore do not
pretend that 20,127 observations are independent unrestricted draws or that
all of them have the same diagnostic fields.
