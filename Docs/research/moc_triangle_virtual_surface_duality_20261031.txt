1. Primal and dual, including degenerate budgets

For beta=(beta1,beta2,beta3)>=0, define
 C3(beta)=max{-sum_{i<j} Gamma_ij : Gamma>=0, diag Gamma=beta}.

The feasible primal set is nonempty and compact. Its exact value is

The SDP dual is

 D(y)=[[y1,1/2,1/2],[1/2,y2,1/2],[1/2,1/2,y3]].

The factor1/2 is essential: off-diagonal entries occur twice in the trace
pairing. Strong duality follows from strict dual feasibility, e.g y=(2,2,2),
and finite feasible primal. If all beta_i>0 the dual infimum is attained.
It need not be attained on degenerate budgets: beta=(1,0,0) has C3=0,
but every finite feasible y has y1>0. One may approach y1=0 only with
other components tending to infinity. Thus MIN must be replaced by INF
in a statement covering all spectra/contacts. This does not affect any
universal inequality quantified over every finite feasible y.
