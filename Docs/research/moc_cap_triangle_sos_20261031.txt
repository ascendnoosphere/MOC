2. Exact conditional triangle-Gram theorem

Put b=(beta18,beta23,beta28). There is a positive-semidefinite real 3x3
matrix Gamma with diagonal b and sum of its three off-diagonal entries -t
if and only if

 T(b) := sum b - max(0, sqrt(b_max)-sum_{other two} sqrt(b))^2 >= 2t.    (1)

Proof. Realize Gamma as the Gram matrix of three vectors of lengths
sqrt(b_i). Their sum has squared norm sum b - 2t. The possible norms of
their sum form the closed interval

 [max(0,largest length - sum other lengths), sum all lengths].

The desired norm is at most the upper endpoint because t>=0. It is
attainable exactly when its square is at least the lower endpoint squared,
which is (1). This includes zero lengths and degenerate Gram matrices.

If (1) holds, the Boolean identities give

 g = t f^2 + sum_{j outside {18,23,28}} beta_j r_j^2
             + (r18,r23,r28) Gamma (r18,r23,r28)^T.                  (2)

The two delta contributions cancel. Therefore g is a real sum of squares
of matching functions. The established corrected real matching-moment
kernel for SO4 then makes every such actual support cost nonnegative at
every real orthogonal determinant profile. The existing n4 profile bridge
transfers this linear support conclusion to complex unitary profiles.

Equivalently, four lengths sqrt(b18),sqrt(b23),sqrt(b28), and
sqrt(sum b-2t) can form a closed quadrilateral. This formulation does not
provide the missing actual-spinor identity realizing the four lengths.

There is also a radical-free version. Put A=sum b-2t,
S2=b18*b23+b18*b28+b23*b28, P3=b18*b23*b28. Then (1) is equivalent to

 A>=0 AND [t^2<=S2 OR 4A P3-(t^2-S2)^2>=0].

Indeed the Brahmagupta product for those four lengths is
4(S2-t^2)+8 sqrt(A P3). For nonnegative lengths its nonnegativity is
equivalent to the quadrilateral inequality. Squaring is valid only on
the branch t^2>S2. This branch qualification is essential.

A still simpler sufficient condition is b_i b_j >= t^2 for some pair.
Then use a 2x2 Gram with off-diagonal entry -t and leave the third ray
diagonal. This pair condition is stronger than (1), not equivalent to it.
