A dimension-five fixed-spectrum Clifford kernel theorem
31 October 2026

Scope

This proves the full signed fixed-column-spectrum moment kernel positive
semidefinite for every real orthogonal 5 by 5 mixer and every complex
spectrum, including repeated and zero entries. Its rank is at most16.
It does not prove MOC or extend the real orthogonal mixer to a complex
unitary mixer. Historical novelty has not been established.

Theorem and division-free factor

Let O be real orthogonal of size5, b in C^5, and let I,K range over all32
subsets of {0,1,2,3,4}. Put

 c_pi=sgn(pi) product_i O_i,pi(i)/det(O),
 f_I(pi)=product_(i in I) b_pi(i),
 G_IK=sum_pi c_pi conjugate(f_I(pi)) f_K(pi),
 B=O diag(b) O^T, C=conjugate(B).

B,C are symmetric and commute. Work in the Euclidean Clifford algebra
e_i^2=1, e_i e_j=-e_j e_i for i!=j, with its usual orthonormal blade
coordinates. For increasing I, let reverse(e_I) reverse its factors and set

 v_I=sum_(|J|=|I|) det B[I,J] reverse(e_I)e_J.

Thus v_I has coordinates on the16 even subsets S (grades0,2,4), with

 (v_I)_S=sigma(I,J)det B[I,J], J=I symmetric_difference S,
 sigma(I,J)=(-1)^(|I|(|I|-1)/2 + #{(i,j) in I times J:i>j}),

and the coordinate is zero if |J|!=|I|. All minor row/column sets are in
increasing order. Define w_I as follows. Keep its grade0 and grade2
coordinates equal to those of v_I. For its grade4 coordinates:

 * If |I|=2, use conjugate((v_I)_S).
 * If |I|=3, write I=(i0<i1<i2), A=I^c and define a row r_C,I by

   (r_C,I)_ia=(-1)^(sum(I)+a) det C[I without ia,A], a=0,1,2,
   (r_C,I)_j=0 if j not in I.

   For S={0,1,2,3,4} without m, set

                    (w_I)_S=(-1)^m (r_C,I B)_m.              (1)

 * For the other subset sizes, every grade4 coordinate is zero.

Then, for every I,K,

                 G_IK=sum_(S even) conjugate((w_I)_S)(w_K)_S. (2)

Formula (1) is polynomial, without any inverse, in B and conjugate(B).
The rank bound and positive semidefiniteness follow directly from (2).

Row determinant bridge

Let R_IK have row i equal to e_i^T, C_i,*, B_i,* or (BC)_i,* according
as i is in neither subset, I only, K only, or both. Determinant expansion
before right multiplication by O^T gives exactly

                           G_IK=det R_IK.                   (3)

Indeed its row i is the row of O multiplied entrywise by1, conjugate(b),
b or |b|^2, and det(O^T)=det(O) cancels the original denominator.
This also covers det O=-1. Identity rows outside I union K may be deleted
together with their corresponding columns, without a sign.

Why the polynomial correction equals the polar correction

Index the grade4 coordinates by S=all without m and put
H[S,m]=(-1)^m, with its other entries zero. H is real orthogonal. The
grade4 Hodge row of v_I built from C, for |I|=3, is

                (v_C,I,grade4) H = r_C,I C.                 (4)

To verify (4), expand det C[I,I^c union {m}] along the column m. For
m in I, the total Clifford, Hodge and column-reordering sign is
(-1)^sum(I), independent of m. For m outside I, both sides vanish,
since the cofactor cross product annihilates the two columns C[I,I^c].

For invertible B put U=B C^-1 and M=H U H^T. Symmetry, C=bar(B), and
BC=CB imply U*U=I, so M is unitary. Equations (4) and BC=CB give

 conjugate(v_B,I,grade4) M
     =r_C,I C B C^-1 H^T=r_C,I B H^T.

This is precisely (1). Thus the polynomial factor is the polar-glued
Clifford factor, and its apparent singularity is removable explicitly.

Fourteen formal base identities

Temporarily let B,C be independent symmetric matrices, and let Q=BC-CB.
Formal conjugation exchanges B and C. Use the polynomial factor above
and define D_IK=det R_IK - sum_S (w_I with B,C exchanged)_S (w_K)_S.

For |I|+|K|<=5, neither empty, simultaneous row-label permutations and
Hermitian exchange reduce all pairs to the14 orbits below:

 (0),(1);       (0),(0);
 (0),(12);      (0),(01);
 (0),(123);     (0),(012);
 (0),(1234);    (0),(0123);
 (01),(23);     (01),(02);     (01),(01);
 (01),(234);    (01),(023);    (01),(012).

All identities below hold as formal polynomial identities in the30
independent entries of symmetric B,C, over every commutative ring.

1. If I=(0) and 0 not in K, D_IK=0. If 0 is in K, with 0 the first
index of its principal submatrix B_K, then

 D_(0),K=sum_(j in K without0) cofactor_(0,j)(B_K) Q_0j.      (5)

For K=(0) the sum is empty. Here the cofactor column position is its
position in K. Formula (5) proves all eight singleton orbits.

2. For the two-subset block,

 D_01,01=0, D_01,23=0,
 D_01,02=B_02(C_12 Q_01-C_11 Q_02+C_01 Q_12).                (6)

3. The disjoint two/three-subset case satisfies D_01,234=0.

4. For the one-overlap two/three case define

 p=B_02 B_33-B_03 B_23,
 r=B_03 B_22-B_02 B_23,
 t=B_02 B_13-B_03 B_12.

Then

 D_01,023=
 p(C_12 Q_01-C_11 Q_02+C_01 Q_12)
 +r(C_13 Q_01-C_11 Q_03+C_01 Q_13)
 +t(C_13 Q_02-C_12 Q_03+C_01 Q_23).                         (7)

5. For the two-overlap case put J=(0,1,2). The proposed Gram entry is
identically, even without imposing Q=0,

        det [ (CB)[0,J]; (CB)[1,J]; B[2,J] ].               (8)

Consequently its residual is the following explicit commutator multiple:

 D_01,012=det [ Q[0,J]; (BC)[1,J]; B[2,J] ]
          +det [ (CB)[0,J]; Q[1,J]; B[2,J] ].               (9)

This is just determinant multilinearity, replacing the two shared rows
one at a time. Writing A=BC, symmetry gives CB=A^T, and (9) simplifies
to the compact three-commutator certificate

 D_01,012=Q_01(B_02 A_12-B_12 A_20)
          +Q_02(B_12 A_10-B_02 A_11)
          +Q_12(B_02 A_10-B_12 A_00).                       (11)

In (8) the grade4 product also simplifies to
det C[01,34] det B[012,234], by the elementary three-dimensional cross
product annihilation identity. The exact verifier independently expands
the full16-coordinate Gram entry, verifies (8), and verifies (9).

Equations (5)-(9) establish the14 base cases when Q=0. Empty-subset cases
are immediate: w_empty has only scalar coordinate1, and the scalar
coordinate of w_K is det B[K,K], which also equals the row determinant.

Equivariance needed for the orbit reduction

Let P be a permutation of the five generators. Clifford functoriality
gives one real orthogonal map T_P on the16 even coordinates. Under
B'=P B P^T, C'=P C P^T, simultaneous relabelling I to P(I) sends v_I
to T_P(v_I). The signs used to sort e_(P(I)) occur twice, in the
reversed input blade and its image, and therefore cancel. Complex
conjugation commutes with the real map T_P, and grades are preserved.

The Hodge identification in dimension5 obeys the usual orientation rule:
on grade4 its transformation is det(P) times the vector transformation.
This orientation sign occurs twice in H U H^T and cancels, so the polar
correction, and hence the polynomial correction by continuity, is also
equivariant. Equivalently, direct cofactor expansion gives

 r_(P C P^T),P(I)=det(P) r_C,I P^T.

Thus the entire factor changes by ONE real orthogonal column map,
independent of I. Its Gram entries are invariant under simultaneous
relabelling. Hermitian exchange permits |I|<=|K|. These facts justify
every orbit reduction used above; no row-dependent unexplained phase
is discarded.

Complementation covers the other pairs

Assume B invertible and write d=det B, D=B^-1. Right multiplication
of R_IK by (BC)^-1 and commutation give

 det R_B(I,K)=|d|^2 det R_D(I^c,K^c).                       (10)

Jacobi's complementary-minor identity and the sign identity

 sigma(I,J)(-1)^(sum I+sum J)=sigma(I^c,J^c)

give v_B,I=d v_D,I^c in every Clifford coordinate. The sign identity
holds in every dimension: for k=|I|=|J|, the difference of its two
integer exponents is n(n-1)-k(2n-k-1), which is even.

On grades0 and2 the corrected factors therefore have multiplier d.
On grade4 note that M_D=M_B^-1. For |I|=2 the complementary row has
size3, so

 w_B,I,4=bar(d) w_D,I^c,4 M_B.

For |I|=3 its complementary row has size2, and exactly the same formula
holds. Other subset sizes have zero grade4 coordinates. Thus ALL rows
obey the SAME column transformation

 w_B,I = w_D,I^c diag(d I11, bar(d) M_B),

whose adjoint times itself is |d|^2 I16. The Gram entry therefore obeys
exactly (10). Every pair of total size above5 reduces to total size
below5. The14 base cases and the empty cases prove all1024 entries for
invertible B.

Finally replace any spectrum b by nonzero-entry spectra tending to it.
The row determinant and the factor (1) are both polynomials in b and
bar(b). Taking limits proves the identity with the same polynomial
factor for arbitrary zero and repeated spectra. No inverse or rank
assumption remains in the theorem or its asserted factor.
