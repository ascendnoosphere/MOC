1. Common radical and source complex structure

The5D complex kernels of Z and bar Z intersect in dimension at least1.
Their intersection is contained in ker Q_C, which has dimension1.
Thus their common kernel is C h and BOTH Re Z and Im Z kill h. The
complex source descends to the8D real radical quotient without an extra
assumption. Write its descended form Q+iR, Q real nonsingular, and
I=Q^{-1}R. Symmetry gives I^T Q=QI.

Both Id+iI and Id-iI have rank4. Their kernels are disjoint, since a
vector killed by both is zero. Each kernel has dimension4, so they
span the complexified quotient, with I-eigenvalues+i and-i. Therefore
I^2=-Id, without a diagonalizability assumption. In particular
Q(Ix,Iy)=-Q(x,y), so Q has signature(4,4).

2. Rulings are forced by the invertible-radical chart

Factor the rank4 complex quadratic as det F(H), with F:Mat3(C)->Mat2(C)
surjective. Its kernel equals ker Z. Every universal row and column

If such an image had dimension at most1, ker F would meet that
complexified3-plane in dimension at least2. Its conjugate kernel would
also meet it in dimension at least2. Their intersection would then
contain h. But every matrix in a row or column3-plane has rank at most1,
contrary to det H_h!=0. Hence every image is a maximal null2-plane.

For h_ij=vec(l_i^T l_j), F(h_ij) cannot be zero: h_ij is real, so it
would lie in ker Q=R h, again contradicting the invertible reshape.
If a row and column image2-plane coincided, the real5D sum of their
original3-planes would be Q-isotropic. A5D null space in a neutral
rank8 form on9 variables must contain its radical. But that sum consists
of matrices l_i^T v+u l_j, all of rank at most2; it cannot contain H_h.
Thus no row image coincides with a column image.

A maximal null2-plane of the2x2 determinant has either a fixed left

F(h_ij), they must have opposite rulings. Consequently all four rows

Rank0 would make F zero. If Lambda=r c^T had rank1, then

would not exclude the rank1 cofactor branch.

5. Universal selected Lorentz real form

A Q-selfadjoint complex structure has a canonical real model
  Q=diag(I4,-I4), I(x,y)=(-y,x).
One way to obtain it is to regard Q-iR as a nondegenerate complex
symmetric bilinear form for the complex structure I and diagonalize it
over C. Every real maximal null4-plane is a graph y=O x, O orthogonal.
I-stability is exactly O^2=-Id. These orthogonal complex structures on
R4 form the two quaternion sphere families: O is left or right
multiplication by a unit imaginary quaternion.

The independent verifier derives their pure spinors in the ACTUAL
wedge/contraction Clifford matrices used by the construction. It solves
for s(q)=S(1,q1,q2,q3) by coefficient identities modulo |q|^2-1 and
checks the resulting annihilation identities for every unit q. Both
families lie in the plus half-spin module, in this canonical convention.
Their coefficient-frame Chevalley Grams are EXACTLY
  S_left^T Bplus S_left=diag(2,-2,-2,-2),
  S_right^T Bplus S_right=diag(-2,2,2,2),
  S_left^T Bplus S_right=0.
This supplies an explicit universal Clifford proof of the two

The generic opposite-ruling result places all row pure lines in one

frame independence; this is an extra open condition, not a consequence

6. Closed-form source generator from a Clifford four-volume

They therefore define a full16D Clifford representation

Orthogonalizing a nondegenerate frame gives

an invertible Clifford generator inside the four-space makes the two
block traces opposite; one in its orthogonal complement makes them
equal. The degenerate case follows polynomially.

The volume anticommutes with C(s) for s in the four-space and commutes
for s in its orthogonal complement. Consequently T preserves ker A_s
