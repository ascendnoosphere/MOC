Primary theorem check: two separate generalized-circle spectra
31 October 2026

Verified from the ORIGINAL full paper:
 S. W. Drury, Essentially Hermitian matrices revisited,
 Electronic Journal of Linear Algebra 15 (2006), 285--296.
 Official full PDF:
 https://journals.uwyo.edu/index.php/ela/article/download/403/403/403

Theorem1.2, p285: MOC holds for Hermitian A and B a nonreal scalar
multiple of a Hermitian matrix.

Corollary1.3, p286: if the spectrum of A is contained in a circle C_A
and the spectrum of B is contained in a circle C_B, MOC holds. The two
circles are independent. Its proof explicitly treats coincident,
disjoint, tangent, and two-point-intersecting circles. It uses common
Mobius transformations and earlier results for the disjoint and
tangent cases. Thus this is not restricted to a single common circle.

The paper writes det(A-B); replacing B by -B gives the sum convention.
Lines are included by the Mobius/limit argument. For example, finite
points on the real axis are limits of points on circles with centres
iR and radii R; transform this construction affinely for any line.
Use such approximations independently for each spectrum and apply
the closure-under-limits statement in the paper. No openness claim
is involved.

Independent primary confirmation: Shitov2025's introduction explicitly
lists the sufficient condition that A's spectrum lies on a line or
circle and B's spectrum lies on a line or circle, independently, citing
Drury2006 and Drury2007.
 https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im9292

Consequence for the current boundary branch: a four-point spectrum
on one generalized circle and another four-point spectrum on any
other generalized circle already satisfy a published MOC theorem.
An inverse-pencil rank-seven mechanism or a full matching-SOS
certificate for that family could still be additional structure;
the MOC conclusion for the two-circle family must not be called new.

This does not cover arbitrary one or two common eigenvalue pairs
between A and -B. The separate zero-pair literature audit found no
verified primary theorem for that general condition.
