Independent review: mixed cancellation graph and 14-ray caps
2026-10-30

Verdict. The K48 cancellation, weighted capacities, and the union of the
ordinary cone with 48 simplicial 14-ray caps follow from the checked exact
certificates and the stated allocation argument. This is a global result
about the coefficient cone C82. It neither asserts that all actual spectral
costs lie in the ordinary cone nor proves MOC4.

The verifier was independently run from the repository root and passed in
1.17 seconds. Its integer/rational primal and dual checks cover all 1128 mixed
pairs via the 576 row/column actions, verify exact maximal ratios and their
common exposing duals, and replay the weighted and ordinary absorption
operations. The allocation theorem for arbitrary real coefficients is a
separate mathematical consequence of the displayed linear identities, not
an extrapolation from its rational example runs.

Recovery of identities as functions on U(4). The 34 ordinary permutation
vectors have rank 14. The two families of incidence equations are valid by
Cauchy--Binet on U(4), with complementary 2x2 minors identified by Jacobi;
the checker finds their span has rank 20, exactly the kernel dimension. Thus
any linear identity among the ordinary permutation profiles holds for the
corresponding unitary minor functions. The checker reconstructs each mixed
function as a signed sum of relabeled ordinary minor functions, so the same
conclusion applies to every mixed-pair and absorption certificate. These are
identities for every unitary, not only for permutation matrices.

Allocation and uniqueness. If no mixed coefficient exceeds half the total,
the antipodal interval pairing consumes all mixed coefficients. Otherwise
the dominant coefficient pairs with all others; interpolating the equal-pair
and maximum-ratio certificates permits any consumption between one and the
certified capacity per unit of the other ray. The residual is precisely the
positive part of alpha_d-sum_j T(d,j)alpha_j. If positive, all other mixed
coefficients were used at their maximum capacities, and the resulting
ordinary contributions have zero w_d-value. The 21 ordinary absorption
identities then either remove the residual or consume every ordinary ray with
w_d=1. In the latter case the remaining rays are f_d and the 13 zero-dual
ordinary rays, which are linearly independent by the exact rank check. This
proves the advertised simplicial cap and its unique coordinates. The common
duals have value -1 on their own mixed ray, capacity at least 1 on every other
mixed ray, and 0 or 1 on ordinary rays. Thus w_i+w_j is nonnegative on every
generator for i!=j and two different duals cannot both be negative. If none
is negative, the allocation cannot leave a mixed residual and the cost is
ordinary.

The common dual stored for f34 sums to 3 exactly, so w34(e)=3 for the
all-ones permutation vector e. Every other common dual is a permutation of
its 24 coordinates; hence w_i(e)=3 for all 48. This normalization is a
statement about duals on the 14-dimensional profile space and does not by
itself decide which actual spectral gap vectors lie in any cap.
