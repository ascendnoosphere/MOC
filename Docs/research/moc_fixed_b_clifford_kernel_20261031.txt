A positive fixed-spectrum coefficient kernel in dimension four
31 October 2026

The following is a complete four-dimensional kernel theorem. It gives joint
positivity for all row spectral derivatives, beyond positivity on separate
three-dimensional derivative planes. It does not itself prove MOC: converting
an actual nonnegative support gap into squares in the relevant feature spaces
requires another argument. The parent lane has in fact found an exact
obstruction to using just one fixed-column-spectrum square cone for that step.

Theorem

Let O be any real orthogonal 4 by 4 matrix, let b be any complex four-vector,
and index all16 subsets I of {0,1,2,3}. Define

 c_pi=sgn(pi) product_i O_i,pi(i)/det O,
 f_I(pi)=product_{i in I} b_pi(i),
 G_IK=sum_pi c_pi conjugate(f_I(pi)) f_K(pi).

Then G is Hermitian PSD and has rank at most8.

Here is an explicit eight-column Gram factor. Put B=O diag(b) O^T. In the
Euclidean Clifford algebra on four generators, e_i^2=1 and e_i e_j=-e_j e_i
for i!=j, write e_I in increasing order and reverse(e_I) in reverse order.
Set

 v_I=sum_{|J|=|I|} det B[I,J] reverse(e_I) e_J.

Every v_I has even Clifford degree, so its coordinates are indexed by
the eight even subsets S. Replace ONLY the coefficient of e_0123 by its
complex conjugate, calling the resulting vector w_I. Then

                 G_IK=sum_{S even} conjugate(w_I,S) w_K,S.       (1)

The top-grade conjugation is essential. Omitting it gives the wrong imaginary
part in the overlapping two-subset block for generic complex b. The formula
as stated is specific to dimension four; applying the same top-grade twist
in larger dimensions has not been justified.

Proof: reduce to symmetric commuting matrices

Let C=conjugate(B). Both B and C are symmetric and BC=CB, because they are
simultaneously diagonalized by the same real O. Determinant expansion gives
G_IK=det R_IK, where row i of R_IK is

  e_i^T          if i is in neither I nor K,
  C_i,*          if i is in I only,
  B_i,*          if i is in K only,
  (BC)_i,*       if i is in both.

Indeed, before right multiplication by O^T the selected row is the row of
O multiplied entrywise by1, conjugate(b), b, or |b|^2. Right multiplication
by O^T cancels the determinant denominator, including det O=-1. Rows outside
I union K are identity rows, so they may be removed together with their
matching columns without a sign.

The Clifford coefficient of det B[I,J] is

 sigma(I,J)=(-1)^(|I|(|I|-1)/2 + #{(i,j) in I times J:i>j})

in coordinate S=I symmetric_difference J. Thus all formulas in (1) are
explicit finite minor formulas, with no unexplained Clifford operation.

Nine base cases

First suppose |I|+|K|<=4 and neither subset is empty. Simultaneous index
permutations and Hermitian exchange I,K reduce every pair to the following
nine cases:

 (0),(0); (0),(1);
 (0),(01); (0),(12);
 (0),(012); (0),(123);
 (01),(01); (01),(02); (01),(23).

For this calculation temporarily regard B,C as independent symmetric matrices,
and let Q=BC-CB. Formal conjugation exchanges B and C. Define D_IK to be the
row determinant minus the proposed twisted Gram entry. Six of the nine
differences vanish identically, without requiring Q=0. The other three are

 D_{0,01}=-B_01 Q_01,

 D_{0,012}=-(B_01 B_22-B_02 B_12)Q_01
           +(B_01 B_12-B_02 B_11)Q_02,

 D_{01,02}=B_02(C_12 Q_01-C_11 Q_02+C_01 Q_12).

These identities follow by ordinary determinant expansion. The accompanying
verifier checks them as polynomial identities in20 independent formal entries
of B and C. Since Q=0 in the theorem, all nine entries agree. The empty-subset
case is immediate: only the scalar Clifford coefficient contributes.

Complementation covers every remaining pair

Suppose B is invertible; then C is too. Right multiply the row-flag matrix
R_IK by (BC)^(-1). Using BC=CB, its four row types become respectively
(BC)^(-1), B^(-1), C^(-1), I. These are exactly the row types for the
complementary pair I^c,K^c with B,C replaced by B^(-1),C^(-1). Consequently

 det R_B(I,K)=|det B|^2 det R_{B^(-1)}(I^c,K^c).                 (2)

Jacobi's complementary-minor identity, symmetry of B, and the elementary
four-index sign identity

 sigma(I,J)(-1)^(sum I+sum J)=sigma(I^c,J^c)

give, before the top-degree conjugation,

 v_B,I,S=det B v_{B^(-1),I^c,S}

for every even S. After conjugation, the multiplier is det B for S of
degree0 or2, and conjugate(det B) for S of degree4. Each multiplier is
independent of I and has modulus |det B|. The Gram side of (1) therefore
satisfies exactly the same complementary identity (2).

Any pair with |I|+|K|>4 is reduced to one with complementary size sum<4.
The base cases prove all256 entries when B is invertible. Replace b by a
sequence of pole-free complex vectors with no zero entry and take limits
to cover singular B and repeated eigenvalues. Both sides of (1) are
polynomials in b and conjugate(b), so this last passage is immediate.

This proves the theorem, including det O=-1 and all degenerate spectra.

The constant-column Schur complement gives the variance kernel

The empty-subset feature is identically1, so G_empty,empty=1. It follows
from the Schur complement of this entry that

 sum_pi c_pi |sum_I alpha_I f_I(pi)|^2
       >= |sum_pi c_pi sum_I alpha_I f_I(pi)|^2                 (3)

for every coefficient vector alpha. In particular every product
z_pi=product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)) and all its row-coordinate derivatives are
linear combinations of these fixed-b features. Their JOINT signed
Hermitian moment matrix is PSD. This gives all row derivatives at once;
interchanging a,b gives the corresponding all-column statement. It does
not permit freely combining the two positive kernels into one joint row-
and-column kernel without another proof.

Equation (3) is a genuine strengthening of the signed scalar variance
bound. A nonnegative support gap is a linear observable, and need not
admit a sum-of-squares identity in this one fixed-b space. Positivity of
G therefore is not promoted to an unrestricted MOC conclusion.
