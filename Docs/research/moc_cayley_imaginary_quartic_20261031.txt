Imaginary-coordinate quartic and the remaining cubic interference
31 October 2026

This calculation does not prove unrestricted MOC4. It locates a concrete
global bottleneck inside the existing full-range Sinkhorn/Cayley reduction.
The target remains arbitrary complex spectra and every U(4), with the original
permutation hull and no enlargement. The result below is algebraic, not an
inference from numerical sampling.

The existing canonical support polynomial is

 G(T)=q(T)^T M q(T),
 q(T)=(coords(T),coords(adj(T)),det(T)),

with T Hermitian 3 by 3 and M a canonical 19 by 19 Gram representative.
M need not be positive. Conjugation averaging always allows M to preserve
the plus/minus coordinate decomposition in PARITY_REDUCED_COMPLETION.md.
Write its canonical blocks A,B,D,u,e,g as in that report. Real-coordinate
indices are R=(11,22,33,Re12,Re13,Re23); imaginary indices are I=(Im12,Im13,Im23).
The parity blocks are (t_I,c_R) and (t_R,c_I,d). This representation is valid
for the original support polynomial before any positivity assumption.

1. Exact polynomial identities

Let X be any real symmetric 3 by 3 matrix, y any real three-vector, and set

 [y]_cross = [[0,-y3,y2],[y3,0,-y1],[-y2,y1,0]],
 T = X+i[y]_cross.

Then

 adj(T) = adj(X)-yy^T-i[Xy]_cross,                     (1)
 det(T) = det(X)-y^T X y.                             (2)

The minus sign in the imaginary adjugate term is essential for this cross
convention. Both identities follow by expanding cofactors, and the exact
verifier checks every coefficient with all nine real parameters symbolic.

Consequently, for every fixed X, the original degree-six polynomial G(T)
has degree at most FOUR in y. No term of degree five or six in y remains.
This uses the actual adjugate/determinant relations, not a free formal
cofactor relaxation.

Its homogeneous degree-four part is exactly

 P4(X;y) = coords(yy^T)^T D_RR coords(yy^T)
                  +g (y^T X y)^2.                   (3)

Here the coordinates of a real symmetric matrix have zero imaginary slots;
equivalently D_RR is the six-dimensional real-cofactor block. The other
parity block variables have degrees at most one in y, apart from d, whose
degree-two part is -y^T X y. The parity decomposition therefore gives (3)
without any assertion that the full M is positive.

2. Both the constant and leading term are nonnegative

Assume identity maximizes the ORIGINAL 24 permutation costs in the support
direction, so all original gaps Delta_pi are nonnegative and Delta_id=0.
The known real-Cayley theorem REAL_COFACTOR_BOUND.md gives

 P0(X)=G(X)>=0.

The same report proves a decomposition of the real-cofactor quadratic form
using baseline atoms, special atoms, and a nonnegative Laplacian remainder.
For a rank-one real symmetric matrix C=yy^T, adj(C)=0. All the z_e(C) terms
in those atoms therefore vanish, leaving only the nonnegative squares

 4 b_e(yy^T)^2,     4 s_e(yy^T)^2,

and the remaining diagonal-difference squares. Thus

 coords(yy^T)^T D_RR coords(yy^T)>=0.

Also g=(1/4)sum_(i<j)Delta_(ij)>=0. Equation (3) proves P4(X;y)>=0 for
every X,y. This conclusion uses only the supported formal common-minor
identities and inequalities, not the stronger actual spectral model.

3. Cubic interference still defeats endpoint positivity

Endpoint positivity does not close the original polynomial. This exact
formal mixed-ray example gives a reproducible control.

Use the fixed e,Y from SINKHORN_GLOBAL_REDUCTION.md and let

 y=t(-2/3,1/3,0),    X=0,
 V=(I-[y]_cross)(I+[y]_cross)^(-1),
 W=ee^T+Y V Y^T,
 U=W P, where P reorders columns as (3,1,0,2), zero based.

Every real t gives an actual real orthogonal U. For the canonical mixed cost

 f34(U)=|U33|^2+|U24|^2+|det U[12,13]|^2-|det U[13,23]|^2

(one-based minor labels), direct rational algebra gives

 f34(U)=6t^2(2t-3)(2t-1)/(5t^2+9)^2.                  (4)

At t=0 this is zero, so the chosen identity chart is a formal contact. The
Cayley denominator is (5t^2+9)^2/81, so its numerator is

 (2/9)t^2-(16/27)t^3+(8/27)t^4.                     (5)

Both its quadratic and quartic coefficients are positive. Nevertheless
f34(U)=-3/98 at t=1, and it is negative throughout 1/2<t<3/2. At t=2 it
is positive. The radial quadratic discriminant is exactly 64/729>0.
The verifier checks these rational identities independently of the
repository's historical Cayley/SOS code.

This is a FORMAL COST control. f34 is nonnegative on permutations but is
not asserted to be an actual spectral support gap. It is NOT a counterexample
to MOC. Its purpose is to show precisely why (3), the positive quadratic
block, and the established real-Cayley theorem cannot imply the desired
full inequality without an additional actual-spectral restriction.

4. A precise interference target on the pure-imaginary branch

For X=0, det(T)=0, adj(T)=-yy^T, and G(0)=0. Thus

 G(i[y]_cross)=A2(y)+B3(y)+D4(y),

where the subscripts denote homogeneous degrees and A2,D4 are nonnegative.
Along each fixed y and every real scalar t, this becomes

 t^2[A2(y)+t B3(y)+t^2 D4(y)].

It is nonnegative for every real t if and only if

 B3(y)^2 <= 4 A2(y)D4(y).                             (6)

This includes zero values of A2 or D4: the inequality then forces B3=0,
which is exactly the condition that a degree-one radial term cannot have
both signs. The displayed control violates (6). Proving (6) for every actual
supported spectral cost would close this entire three-real-parameter branch,
but it does not yet handle X!=0 and is not a restatement of unrestricted
MOC4 as a solved theorem. The product-linked actual spectral constraints
are the only plausible source of the missing control; arbitrary formal
nonnegative permutation costs provably do not supply it.
