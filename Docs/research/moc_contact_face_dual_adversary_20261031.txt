Contact-face dual adversary for actual matching SOS membership
31 October 2026

This note defines and tests a targeted adversary for the sufficient real
matching-SOS cone. It is not a MOC counterexample and does not establish
universal actual-cost membership. The method removes two flat directions
that made the earlier global dual searches uninformative.

Exact finite-dimensional reduction

Let V be a real orthonormal 24-by-14 evaluation frame for the matching
function space. Fix a set C of contact permutations and let N span the
nullspace of V_C. Put F=VN. A nonnegative cost g with g_C=0 can have a
PSD matching Gram only if its Gram annihilates every contact vector.
Consequently its certificate has the form
 g_pi=f_pi^T A f_pi, A>=0,
where f_pi is row pi of F. Every real vector y on the noncontact rows
with
 H_y=sum_pi y_pi f_pi f_pi^T>=0
therefore satisfies y.g>=0. The trace normalization trH_y=1 makes this
a compact matrix optimization problem after quotienting coefficient
directions that have zero moment matrix. The coefficient vector itself
is reconstructed in the orthogonal complement of that moment kernel.

This normalization avoids the global dual's zero bound from a contact
permutation: all its face feature vectors are zero and cannot satisfy
trH=1. For strict relative SOS feasibility, the normalized dual minimum
is positive rather than identically zero.

If H_y is positive definite, a finite full moment completion exists.
Extend y by zero at contacts and split the full 14-dimensional feature
space into contact span and contact orthogonal complement. Its moment
has blocks[A,B;B^T,D], with D=H_y>0. The contact point masses give a
matrix[S,0;0,0], where S>0 on the contact span. Choosing M sufficiently
large makes
 A+MS-BD^{-1}B^T>0.
Adding M at each chosen contact then gives a full positive moment.
This preserves y.g only when g vanishes at every chosen contact. The
code checks the direct contact residual before numerical zeroing. The
normalization sum is made positive before dividing; division preserves
the sign of the pairing. An exact candidate would need rational Schur
completion and exact contact identities, not just this numerical step.

Actual spectra and a fixed contact plane

The cofactor reduction uses a=(0,1,infinity,x), with product values
 z_pi=u_pi x+v_pi. Thus the 24 coefficient pairs form a point cloud in
R4. A fixed set of four affinely independent coefficient points gives
a supporting-plane candidate from the null vector of their three
differences. This enforces all four contact equalities throughout the
outer spectral search. The remaining four complex beta spinors use
eight real compact projective coordinates.

The code penalizes planes that cut the coefficient cloud. Separate
search guards keep all eight projective points separated, the cloud
four-dimensional, and noncontact gaps away from zero. These guards are
only search restrictions. They do not remove boundary cases from the
universal theorem that remains to be proved.

Canonical nonordinary targeting

For the canonical mixed ray r34, all chosen contacts are among its 13
zeros. The actual costs are required to satisfy u34(g)<0, using the
ordinary-cone facet identified in the parent computation. The optional
normalization g/(-3u34(g)) fixes the canonical cap coefficient instead
of letting the optimizer drift toward ordinary cost directions.

A second plateau remained after trace normalization: the face dual
minimum often approached a pure noncontact permutation moment. Its
purity was approximately 0.999997, so it merely selected the smallest
noncontact cost permitted by the search guard. The new dual cut
 y.r34<=-0.05
excludes that plateau. A strict initial point comes from the exact
global cap34 dual, restricted and trace-normalized to the contact face.
Contact completion preserves this negative cap pairing because r34
also vanishes at every selected contact.

The cut changes the barrier stationarity equation. If k is the face
dimension and kappa is the nonnegative cut multiplier, the reconstructed
positive Gram pertains to g+kappa*r34, not g alone. The code labels this
augmented cost explicitly; a positive cut-dual minimum is not being
misrepresented as an independent Gram certificate for g.

full normalized positive moment with negative r34 pairing. The positive
control g_i=||f_i||^2/max_i||f_i||^2 has the explicit normalized pairing
1/max_i||f_i||^2=1.777777777777777, which the oracle reproduces.
Control output is

For an actual canonical-cap target with contacts (1,6,12,22), the first
nonclassical face search reached an interior configuration with all

approximately 0.32837. Its direct contact residual was 5.9e-17. The face

Any negative feasible pairing on an actual supported gap would refute

Removing the arbitrary cap-strength cutoff

The final oracle uses the homogeneous section
 H_y>0, y.r34=-1,
instead of trace one plus a negative-cap cutoff. This is a full section
of all strictly positive face moments having negative r34 pairing.
There is no remaining arbitrary lower bound on that pairing relative
to the moment trace. Ordinary permutation moments have nonnegative
r34 pairing and cannot lie in this section.

This section loses no strict separating witness for a nonnegative cost
in the single-cap cell Gamma+R_+ r34. Indeed, write g=t*r34+gamma with
gamma in the ordinary SOS cone. If g and r34 both vanish on C, gamma
also vanishes there, so its ordinary Gram annihilates the contact
feature vectors. Any face moment with y.g<0 then has y.r34<0. Adding a
sufficiently small positive multiple of the uniform noncontact weights
makes H_y positive definite and preserves both strict inequalities.
Positive rescaling gives y.r34=-1. The exact single-cap decomposition
used in this argument is supplied by the parent mixed-ray census;
the numerical search itself is not a proof of that decomposition.

For this section, the barrier stationarity equation is
 g + lambda*r34 = mu*(f_i^T H_y^{-1} f_i)_i.
Thus the numerical central coefficient lambda belongs to an augmented
cost identity. Its reported stationarity residual must be taken into
account; the floating-point coefficient is not an exact objective
lower bound or an exact Gram certificate for the original cost.

Whitening a strict rational starting moment and orthonormalizing its
affine moment directions makes the Newton optimization stable enough
to explore this noncompact section. The returned y is checked directly
for positive moment eigenvalues and negative cap normalization. A
numerically negative actual supported gap would still require exact
replay of the moment and contact identities.

The oracle stops as soon as a directly feasible negative pairing is
found; it does not require optimization to converge before recording
a discovery. This branch is verified on the known nonactual ray r34:
the strict starting moment gives pairing -1, and full contact completion
gives a normalized negative pairing about -0.03131 with full moment
minimum eigenvalue about 5.82e-6. No central-path coefficient or barrier
gap is asserted for this early feasible stop. The nominal barrier gaps
in the numerical campaign records are diagnostics, not exact bounds.

Exact finite controls

The separate verifier
 code/research/moc_contact_face_exact_controls.py
constructs the matching projector with rational arithmetic. For the
contact set (1,6,12,22), its contact-null projector P_C has rank 10,
and its squared-entry Gram on the 20 noncontact rows has rank 20.
Consequently no coefficient-kernel directions are lost by the quotient
in this case. The maximum diagonal of P_C is exactly 9/16. Therefore
the explicit positive-control cost
 g_i=(P_C)_{ii}/(9/16)
has pairing exactly 16/9 with every trace-one face moment. The numeric
oracle reproduces this value.

Restricting the parent's strict rational cap34 dual to the face and
rescaling it gives an exact strict section point with y.r34=-1. The
verifier checks ten rational positive LDL pivots of its face moment.
It pairs with the known nonactual ray r34 at exactly -1. These are
controls of the reduction and normalization; r34 is not an actual
spectral support counterexample. The rational output is
 build/moc_contact_face_dual_adversary/exact_controls.json.

Final targeted continuations

With both actual cost and dual cap normalization, the face continuation
on contacts (1,6,12,22) reached the positive pairing 0.02088339447.
Its eight-point separation was 0.2839782 and its four-dimensional cloud
singular ratio was 0.3094492. The canonical actual cap coefficient was
0.2462376 before cost normalization. The smallest raw noncontact gap
reached the search guard 1e-6. Thus this run approaches an additional
hull contact, while the spectral collision and cloud-rank guards are
inactive. It does not approach the ordinary cap-normalization boundary.

The next contact is permutation 17. Replacing contact 22 by 17 and
repairing the spectra into the adjacent valid facet chamber gave a
positive pairing 0.02543473218. The repaired spectra moved only about
1.3e-6 in the eight real projective coordinates. This checks that the
first fixed-contact result was not simply reported without exploring
the adjacent hull chamber. Both local optimizations stayed positive;
neither establishes a global lower bound.
