Full five-parameter eight-contact closures: canonical orbits32 and36
=================================================================

For f=r34 and arbitrary nonnegative a,b,c,d,e, the following whole
families satisfy g(U)>=0 for every U in U(4):

  orbit32: g=f+a r0+b r17+c r18+d r23+e r27;
  orbit36: g=f+a r0+b r17+c r18+d r27+e r31.

Each canonical cap orbit has24 images. Their eight contacts are respectively
[6,7,8,9,17,20,22,23] and[6,8,9,14,17,20,22,23]. Positive mixed
coefficient is normalized to1; mixed coefficient0 is ordinary-minor positive.

Actual-spectrum criterion
-------------------------
For Q=D_1F, every actual gap at height h satisfies F(g)=h Q(g). Both
families have F<0, hence Q!=0. The checker reconstructs the full determinant
pencil and proves the exact identity

  det H_F(g-F(g)/Q(g)*1)=-4 N^2/Q(g)^2.

Actual Hessian singularity therefore forces N=0. The polynomials below
exclude this on the required coefficient regions, for every height sign.
Neither family requires the stronger actual-pencil sign theorem.

Orbit32
--------
Here F=-(e+1)(a+b+1), Q=-be-b-cd+ce-e-1, and

  N=(d+1)(e+1)(a+b+1)^2 P,
  P=a(c+1)(e+1)+(c+1+e)(1-cd)+c(c+1)e.

If cd<1, the middle term is strictly positive and all remaining terms are
nonnegative. Thus N>0 and there is no actual positive-mixed gap, regardless
of height. If cd>=1, then c>0 and the established weighted-minor theorem

  f+c r18+c^(-1)r23>=0

recovers g>=0, because d-c^(-1)>=0 and the other terms are ordinary rays.
This proves the entire five-parameter family, including cd=1.

Orbit36
--------
Here F=-(d+1)(e+1)(a+b+1), Q=-bd-be-b+cd-de-d-e-1, and

  N=(d+1)(e+1)^2(a+b+1)^2 P,
  P=(a+1)(c+1)+d[(a+c)(c+1)+1]>0.

Consequently there is no actual positive-mixed gap anywhere on this whole
family at any height. Coefficient boundaries and vanishing Q are covered:
Q=0 would contradict F<0 for an actual gap.

All elimination pivots define rational charts solely for computation.
Exact denominator clearing proves the displayed polynomial identities
through their chart boundaries. No generic coefficient assumption remains.
