Extensive analysis of the MOC campaign, 31 October 2026

The most consequential finding is a coverage failure. Among 4,095 examined
four-dimensional spectral pairs, every one of their 31,214 permutation-hull
edge costs passed the numerical ordinary-cone test. A known exact nonordinary
spectral example failed the same test as expected. The campaign explored many
spectra, mixers and dimensions, but its random four-dimensional spectra did
not reach the support-cost region responsible for the remaining proof problem.
More draws from the same generic generators would therefore add much less
information than a deliberately targeted nonordinary-support experiment.

The analysis also identifies useful structure within the data. Near-real raw
margins mostly measure flattening of the hull. Near-permutation boundary
proximity depends strongly on which matching product the determinant approaches.
Clustered spectra remain comparatively close to the boundary after conditioning.
Large-dimensional generic samples admit small inner-hull witnesses, but their
apparent radial contraction depends strongly on both the mixer and the sampled
permutation design. Several tempting universal extrapolations fail exact tests.

1. What was analyzed

The immutable source is

  build/moc_campaign_20261031/all_moc_data.jsonl

It contains 20,127 records and covers every integer dimension from 1 through
1000. Its SHA-256 is

  079d7550cedeb1b6743404140b1c94855e1065690ae214e9da0cf639e36e0ff0

The records have different roles:

  7,765 small-matrix noncontrol spectral draws;
  1,599 small-matrix controls and special loci, including unit-circle spectra;
  1,578 dense Haar draws;
  5,994 draws with products of 2, 4 or 8 Householder reflections;
  3,189 optimization starts reusing 532 spectral cases;
  one exact scalar control and one selected exact triangle certificate.

These numbers must not be added and described as independent unrestricted
random tests. Complete permutation hulls were available for the small cases
through n=9 and for the support-optimization cases. The large-dimensional
records use selected permutation products. Inclusion in their inner hull is
sufficient for membership in the full hull of that instance. Exclusion from an
inner hull is inconclusive. Floating-point inclusion is numerical evidence,
unless a separate exact certificate is supplied.

The original classifications remain unchanged. In particular, small cases
include 192 boundary/tolerance records and 2,004 numerically degenerate hulls;
44 reflector records were initially unresolved by their hull procedure.
Subsequent angular replays resolve 17 of those 44 numerically. This is an
additional diagnostic and does not rewrite the original observations.

Identical family names do not identify identical sampling laws across sources.
The small clustered generator uses two random centers per spectrum with noise
between approximately 1e-7 and 0.5. The adversarial clustered generator uses
specified real and imaginary centers, and the dense clustered generator uses
three centers. Near-real noise scales and cancellation constructions also
differ. We keep source, family, mixer, dimension and permutation design separate.

2. Conditioning changes the apparent four-dimensional difficulty

We reconstructed all complete permutation products for 7,348 noncontrol small
records with n=3,...,9. The n=2 cases were omitted from two-dimensional shape
analysis because their permutation hull is a segment. The derived features are
in small_shape_features.jsonl under build/moc_extensive_20261031/.

For the primary shape analysis, the hull must have numerical affine rank two,
the ratio of minor to major RMS covariance axes must exceed 1e-4, and the minor
RMS axis must exceed 1e-7 after scaling by the product/determinant common scale.
The first cutoff bounds the whitening transformation's condition number by
10,000. The second excludes tiny hulls whose apparently reasonable aspect ratio
can conceal catastrophic relative roundoff. These guards retain 6,034 records
overall and 3,442 of the 4,167 noncontrol n=4 records. They are numerical guards,
not certified arithmetic error bounds.

Whitening subtracts the uniform permutation centroid and normalizes its full
two-dimensional covariance. In exact arithmetic, an invertible affine change
of the product plane changes the whitened coordinates only by an orthogonal
map. The resulting Euclidean hull clearance is therefore affine invariant.
The boundary lane independently checks a second invariant: for each edge,
divide its support deficit by the full support width in the same direction,
then take the smallest ratio.

For n=4 Haar draws, the primary median whitened clearances are:

  family                    retained       median
  Gaussian                   626           0.92810
  uniform disk               626           0.90264
  anisotropic                264           0.81727
  clustered                  444           0.46826
  near pair cancellation     625           0.75647

Before conditioning, the Gaussian and anisotropic median margins differed by
roughly 23,000. After conditioning, their difference is modest. It would be
wrong to call the small raw anisotropic margins comparably strong evidence of
intrinsic boundary proximity. Clustered spectra remain closer to the boundary
under either invariant metric, even after excluding nearly scalar tiny hulls.

The threshold whitened margin <0.05 is used below as a descriptive boundary
proximity label. It is not a violation threshold. Among eligible n=4 Haar draws,
this label occurs in 16/444 clustered cases, 3/625 cancellation cases, 1/264
anisotropic cases, and none of the 626 Gaussian or 626 disk cases. The zero
counts do not imply a population probability of zero.

3. The near-permutation tail has a geometric explanation

A near-permutation mixer in this campaign approaches the identity matrix, so
its determinant approaches the identity matching product z_id. That product
need not be a vertex of the permutation hull. It can lie strictly inside.
Treating all small-mixing cases as equally close to the boundary discards this
distinction.

Among the 857 eligible n=4 near-permutation cases:

  z_id on the numerical hull boundary: 258/277 have whitened margin <0.05;
  z_id in the numerical hull interior: 105/580 have whitened margin <0.05.

Their median whitened margins are approximately 5.43e-6 and 0.33618,
respectively. Boundary membership here uses a whitened margin cutoff of 1e-7;
the underlying continuous natural-product clearance is retained in the data.

There is a direct deterministic explanation. If m(x) is the minimum inward
distance to a hull edge, then

  |m(d)-m(z_id)| <= ||d-z_id||.

This is the Lipschitz property of the minimum of affine functions with unit
normal vectors. The largest numerical residual in this inequality over the
derived records was 5.54e-17. An interior matching product supplies initial
clearance that small perturbations need not consume.

For small mixing, determinant displacement is quadratic in the mixing
amplitude. Its first variation at a diagonal commuting pair vanishes because
the perturbation is off-diagonal. The recorded nonmonomial score is itself of
quadratic order. Regressing log whitened displacement on log(nonmonomial
score/n), restricting the score/n below 0.01 and displacement above 1e-9, gives
n=4 family slopes between 0.966 and 1.009. These are descriptive fitted slopes,
consistent with the expansion, not estimates of a universal bound constant.

The practical consequence is to rank small-mixing inputs by the face geometry
of their limiting matching product. A small mixing parameter alone gives a
large collection of ordinary interior cases.

4. Held-out prediction, confounding and transfer across dimensions

We fixed a deterministic 60/40 split using a hash of record_id. Four specified
ridge-logistic models predict the label whitened margin <0.05 using mixing,
family, and the identity product's clearance. The models used 2,055 eligible
n=4 training cases and 1,387 held-out cases. Because earlier descriptive
summaries had already inspected the corpus, this is a retrospective model
holdout, not a fresh prospective experiment.

The distinction between pooled and conditional evaluation is substantial.
The mixing-only model has pooled held-out ROC AUC 0.918, but its AUC within the
336 held-out near-permutation cases is only 0.635. Much of the pooled result
comes from separating Haar from near-permutation draws.

Adding identity-product clearance raises the within-near-permutation AUC to
0.913. At the fixed probability threshold 0.5 it has 90.0% precision and 67.8%
recall in that stratum. Adding family as well gives AUC 0.926 and precision
92.8%, with recall 70.5%. The simpler two-feature model captures most of the
gain. These are predictions of geometric closeness, not MOC truth or failure.

A paired bootstrap within spectral families, keeping their held-out counts
fixed and holding the fitted models fixed, gives an AUC improvement of 0.278
for mixing-plus-clearance over mixing alone, with percentile interval
[0.232,0.323]. This calculation assumes the independently seeded draws behave
as independent samples from the stated generators. It excludes training-set
uncertainty and says nothing about failure probabilities for unrestricted MOC.

The n=4 model was then applied without refitting to other dimensions:

  near-permutation test dimension      mixing AUC   mixing+clearance AUC
  n=3, 145 eligible cases                0.705              0.976
  n=5, 260 eligible cases                0.543              0.830
  n=6, 151 eligible cases                0.615              0.815
  n=7,  63 eligible cases                0.595              0.761

Higher-dimensional strata are too small to support a precise performance
claim: n=8 has only 23 eligible near-permutation cases and n=9 has five.
Haar boundary labels are also rare. The n=4 holdout contains only nine such
positive Haar cases, and the pooled classifier does not identify them at its
0.5 probability threshold. It should not be advertised as a universal
counterexample-search oracle.

5. Dimension growth depends on the spectral family

For eligible Haar draws, Gaussian median whitened margin increases from
0.452 at n=3 to 0.928, 1.378, 2.176 and 3.511 at n=4,5,6,7. The corresponding
clustered medians are 0.294, 0.468, 0.554, 0.434 and 0.435. Pooling these
families would produce a misleading statement about dimension making every
kind of input easier.

The Gaussian counts at n=3,...,7 are 126,626,188,101,45; the clustered counts
are 87,444,135,91,44. At n=8 and n=9 the Gaussian Haar counts fall to 15 and
five. All shape comparisons are conditional on passing the stated numerical
guards. Anisotropic cases fail those guards more often, so their retained
distribution is not the original full epsilon distribution.

The complete-hull small-n result and the sampled-hull large-n result measure
different things. Covariance-normalized clearance can grow as extreme
permutation products move outward relative to the bulk. A negative radial
ratio by itself remains only a necessary condition for hull membership.

6. Large-n replays separate mixer effects from permutation selection

The dense lane replayed 1,298 original Haar records with n>=32 under two
permutation designs: the original mixture of random and deterministic choices,
and the first 128 uniformly generated permutations alone. All 1,298 targets
pass the angular-disk criterion and a three-point containment certificate in
both designs. The pure random list therefore suffices for these observed
instances; deterministic high-product choices are not needed for their
containment, although they substantially change the reported radial maximum.

The low-rank reflector lane replayed the original 5,994 records using only
their first 128 random permutations. An angular certificate succeeds in 5,880
cases. Replacing the actual determinant modulus by a row-Hadamard upper bound
still certifies 2,759 cases numerically, so these instances admit a sufficient
test that does not evaluate the determinant itself. This succeeds very
unevenly across spectral families; it is not a uniform MOC theorem.

For Gaussian reflector records, median log(|det|/|z_id|) is about -10.17,
whereas median log(|det|/max selected |z_pi|) is about -231.25. The median
increase of the heuristic maximum over the maximum of the 128 random products
is about 188.93 log units. Thus an apparently exponential radial gap can come
largely from how matching products were selected. It cannot be interpreted as
the determinant's contraction relative to a typical matching product.

The reflector rank also matters. Rank 2,4,8 cycles with dimension and family
in the original schedule; a pooled rank comparison is confounded. Separate
rank/family analyses and the fixed-r asymptotic argument identify a
polynomial-scale ratio to the natural matching product, rather than the same
mechanism as dense Haar mixing. The detailed calculations are in
moc_extensive_reflectors.txt and reflector_rate_fits.json.

The dense rate models use dimensions 128..255 for training, 256..511 for
selection, refit on 128..511, and test on 512..1000. This avoids a split by
n modulo five, which would leak or remove entire scheduled families. Their
results and residuals are in dense_rate_models.json; they remain descriptions
of the specified generators, not universal asymptotic laws.

7. Sparse certificates work often, but proposed universal shortcuts fail

For points z_j with mean mu and covariance Sigma, canonical affine weights

  w_j = [1 + (z_j-mu)^T Sigma^{-1}(d-mu)] / m

sum to one and reconstruct d exactly when Sigma is invertible. If all weights
are nonnegative, they are a genuine sufficient certificate. In the stricter
shape-qualified n=4 data, numerical nonnegativity occurs in 1,532/2,585 Haar
cases and 112/857 near-permutation cases. Every one of these successes has
minimum weight numerator greater than 1e-8. The associated ellipsoid test is
more conservative: 934/2,585 and 60/857. The construction is useful for
interior points and misses many valid boundary-near instances.

Other sparse rules select permutation products near a large entrywise matching
of |U_ij|^2 or from a greedy Birkhoff decomposition. Their pass/fail tables and
exact failure witnesses are in the sparse-certificate lane. Their failures are
failures of selected inner hulls, not counterexamples to MOC. In particular,
an exact radius-two selection obstruction prevents promotion of a high
empirical success rate to a universal selection theorem.

Among 4,167 noncontrol n=4 cases, the one-transposition neighborhood succeeds
numerically in 3,869 and the greedy Birkhoff set in 3,947. After high-precision
resolution of one roundoff case, the radius-two neighborhood succeeds in
4,166/4,167. Its union with the Birkhoff set succeeds in all 4,167 observed
cases, but fails some numerical n=5 and n=7 cases. The remaining n=4 radius-two
failure has an exact rational Cayley-unitary version with a unique base
assignment and 24 distinct nonzero products. A separating line excludes all
18 local products. A positive full-hull triangle uses permutation distances
2,3,3 from the base assignment. Thus a relative four-cycle is necessary for
that example, even though local product selection almost always worked here.

The root's local-block test uses only three permutations: the identity and two
successive local swaps in one four-index block. Among the original records
with n>=256 it succeeds for all 153 dense Gaussian cases and all 745 reflector
Gaussian cases. It succeeds only in 30/153 dense near-real and 51/745
reflector near-real cases, and in none of 745 correlated reflector-cancellation
cases. Its useful Gaussian behavior therefore does not extend to arbitrary
spectra. The replay examines 15,412 original n>=4 small/dense/reflector records;
it creates no new primary samples.

A separate exact Fourier-matrix construction refutes the proposed universal
purity contraction d in mu+sqrt(kappa)(P-mu). Its determinant still has an exact
positive three-permutation representation. Second moments constrain aggregate
size; they do not supply the corresponding pointwise affine contraction.

8. The actual boundary cases and the missing nonordinary region

The independent boundary lane ranks cases by edge-width-relative clearance and
reconstructs the most suspicious ones at 80-digit precision. A clustered case
whose floating-point invariant clearance was negative becomes positive at
higher precision. Other very small positive margins remain positive. Selected
nearby rational spectra and exactly unitary Cayley mixers have exact positive
triangle certificates. These checks validate representative difficult inputs;
they do not certify every floating-point record.

An exact mixed boundary example has nonmonomial score 0.9216 and zero
clearance, with all 24 products distinct and nonzero and neither spectrum
concyclic. Consequently there can be no strictly positive universal clearance
bound based on mixing alone under those generic-looking hypotheses. The
example lies on the hull boundary and respects MOC.

The edge combinatorics also defeat a tempting simplification. Hull edges can
join permutations differing by two disjoint transpositions, not just a single
nontrivial cycle. The boundary lane observes 1,820 such edges in its discovery
split and 463 in its holdout. Single-cycle adjacency is not a valid general
description of these planar product hulls.

Most importantly, the ordinary-cone audit examines all hull edges, rather than
only the edge closest to the observed determinant. With its stated aspect
ratio guard, it finds zero nonordinary edges among 31,214 edges from 4,095
spectral pairs. The smallest dual value is +3.19749e-8. The known exact
outside-cone control has dual value about -0.202013, demonstrating that the
test distinguishes the missing region. These are numerical cone tests on the
corpus, not an exact proof for each spectrum.

The closest-edge contact counts also mostly describe ordinary two-contact
geometry. Of 3,442 primary cases, 3,296 have two tolerance contacts and only one
has six or more. The unresolved archived MOC4 mechanism concerns nonordinary
support costs with more intricate contact geometry. The generated sample
design largely avoided it.

9. What the optimization and exact obstructions jointly imply

The 3,189 support-optimization starts found no numerical exterior. Their
largest normalized support excess is approximately 1.17e-15. Forty-five saved
nonmonomial, low-gradient clustered outputs were subsequently polished by
pair rotations; all became nearly monomial. Three still reached suboptimal
permutations. Thus polishing improves numerical search but does not establish
global convergence.

The exact Walsh family makes the limitation stronger. For every r>1, let
a=(1,-1,-ri,ri), b=(-r,r,-i,i) and d=r(r^2-1). At the specified dense Walsh
mixer, each coordinate SU(2) fiber has support d cos^2(t), the full nontrivial
Hessian has eigenvalues -4d six times and zero six times, but a coupled path has
support d(1+16cs^3), where c^2+3s^2=1. It has cubic ascent. Pairwise maximality
and a negative semidefinite Hessian together still do not certify a local
maximum. The family is compatible with MOC and is an exact obstruction to a
proof strategy, not a violation of the conjecture.

10. Proved results beyond the floating-point corpus

The data motivated a separately proved Gaussian/Haar theorem. Let all a_i and
b_i be independent standard complex Gaussians, and let U be independent Haar.
For d=det(D_a+U D_b U*) and the full permutation hull P, the audited argument
proves, for n>=4,

  Pr(d outside P) <= (1-p0)^floor(n/4) + exp(-n/400)
                     + (8/3)(n+2)(n+3) exp(-n/50),
  p0=exp(-5)/200^16.

The bound may be truncated at one. It implies an exponential upper bound on
failure as n grows. The proof combines a three-product triangle from one good
four-index spectral block, a lower-tail estimate for the identity product,
and the exact Haar determinant second moment. It is not inferred from the
observed zero-failure count. Its explicit constants are vacuous through
n=1000. The independent audit passed; no novelty claim or Lean formalization
is claimed for this theorem. The proof and audit are respectively

  docs/research/moc_extensive_theory.txt
  docs/research/moc_gaussian_triangle_independent_audit_20261031.txt.

There is also a new exact n=1000 instance. Rational spectra and two explicitly
specified noncommuting Householder reflections give a complex noncommuting
pair. Neither spectrum is concyclic. An integer-only verifier proves
|det(A+UBU*)|<2^-1000 using Frobenius, Hadamard and rank bounds, without
evaluating the determinant. Three exact permutation products enclose a disk
of radius greater than 2^86. These two bounds prove containment for that
specified input. This is a new rationalized input derived from one source
seed, not an exact certification of the original floating-point rank-eight
record and not an extra empirical observation. Its proof and full data are

  docs/research/moc_exact_n1000_certificate.txt
  build/moc_extensive_20261031/exact_n1000_certificate.json
  build/moc_extensive_20261031/verify_exact_n1000_certificate.py
  build/moc_extensive_20261031/exact_n1000_verification.json.

Finally, code/lean/Moc0710DPath.lean has been accepted by Lean 4.19.0. The main
theorem transports strict feasibility from an admissible point along the
explicit canonical c=0 path to d=1, proves positivity and the polynomial
constraint along the path, and is accompanied by continuity and endpoint
theorems. Its audited public declarations contain no sorry or custom axiom.
This closes a specific path-feasibility gap. The reduction from arbitrary
complex normal matrices to that canonical family, remaining global topology,
and determinant-to-hull implications are outside this theorem's scope. The
accepted compiler status and precise hypotheses are recorded in

  docs/research/moc0710_dpath_lean_status.txt.

11. Conclusions that the data support

The next numerical attack should start from verified nonordinary actual
spectra and retain the cone test as a coverage diagnostic. Without that
constraint, the present generators mainly produce support inequalities already
handled by ordinary-minor positivity. Sampling more dimensions does not repair
this four-dimensional coverage gap.

For boundary searches, use affine-conditioned or edge-width-relative clearance,
absolute hull scale, the face containing the limiting matching product, and
precision checks. Tiny raw margins, small pair sums, and a small optimizer
gradient each have common benign explanations. The held-out analysis gives
concrete evidence that the limiting product's face geometry improves search
triage across several dimensions.

For generic random large matrices, the productive mechanism is sparse angular
coverage plus a determinant-size bound. It produces actual numerical
certificates and supports a separately proved Gaussian/Haar high-probability
result. The explicit rigorous constants of that result are not informative
at n<=1000; empirical local-block success frequencies must not replace those
constants in a rigorous probability claim. Neither that random theorem nor
the campaign resolves unrestricted MOC4.
