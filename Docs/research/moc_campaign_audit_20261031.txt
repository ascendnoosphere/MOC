Full five-parameter (0,7,10,18,22) closure

I independently read docs/research/eight0710_full_closure.txt and its verifier.
The root uniqueness and global continuation arguments hold on the stated
domain d>0, a!=b, a,b,c,e>=0, P=0, Q<0.

For ab<1, the point ab-1 is strictly negative and A(ab-1)<=0. If the upward
quadratic A has a positive root, its other root is strictly negative. This
remains valid when the test value equals zero, including a=0 or b=0: the
negative test point itself is then the negative root. Hence S>0 and the
positive root is unique and simple. For ab>=1, S=(ab-1)(d-1)^2+d(p-4)>0
except the stated a=b=1 exception, where no positive root exists. The
exception is already outside the unequal branch.

The c-to-zero path keeps the numerator of Q affine in c and negative at
both endpoints. For d<=1 the zero endpoint is automatically negative.
For d>1 its slope is positive, so the zero endpoint is less than the
starting endpoint. No component can be lost in this step.

At c=0 and d>1, Q<0 means w>p(d-1). Since A has one negative and one
positive root, this is equivalent to A(p(d-1))<0, and therefore to K(d)<0.
The derivative K'(d)>0 for d>=1 shows that decreasing d to 1 retains this
inequality. A negative value at the positive comparison point guarantees
a positive root at every intermediate d; the root lemma supplies S>0.
For d<1, the derivative of d+1/d-2 is negative; multiplying by ab-1<0
makes S/d increase towards d=1 when ab<1. The ab>=1 case follows directly
from the positive expression for S. Thus every path retains the same
continuous simple root and ends at p>4.

The two endpoint domains become intersections of halfspaces in
(log(a+1),log(b+1)), with strict ordering and strict sum>log(4). Each is
convex and includes the stated anchor. The nonvanishing L and R derivative
certificates therefore justify sign continuation on the full branch.

For formalization, this last step has a simpler piecewise-affine path.
When a<b and p>4, first increase b to B=max(b,4) with a fixed. The product
p increases and the ordering holds. Then decrease a to zero with b=B;
p remains at least B+1>=5 and the ordering holds. Finally decrease b from
B to 4 with a=0; again p>=5. The symmetric three segments connect a>b
to (4,0). Thus no logarithm or abstract connected-component theorem is
needed. On S>0 an explicit positive root is

  w=(pT+sqrt(p^2 T^2+4qpS))/(2q).

Its continuity follows from ordinary arithmetic and square-root continuity;
sqrt(p^2 T^2+4qpS)>abs(pT) proves positivity. Each explicit path segment,
combined with a continuous nonzero pencil coefficient and the intermediate
value theorem, transports the anchor sign. The entire scalar continuation
and sign result can be packaged without axioms. Applying it to actual
spectral support still requires the separate established pencil-sign theorem
and the boundary/height theorems cited in the original closure.

Haar second moment

Assume all a_i and b_j are independent, centered complex variables with
E|a_i|^2=E|b_j|^2=1. For fixed U, Cauchy--Binet expands the determinant as

  d_U=sum_(S,T), |S|=|T| |det U_(S,T)|^2
       product_(i notin S) a_i product_(j in T) b_j.

Different monomials are orthogonal in L^2: an index in their symmetric
difference appears on only one conjugated side, and independence and
centering kill the cross expectation. Each diagonal expectation is 1.
Therefore E|d_U|^2=sum_(S,T) |det U_(S,T)|^4. Circular symmetry and fourth
moments of the spectral variables are unnecessary because every spectral
variable is used at most once in each monomial.

For Haar U independent of the spectra, take an n-by-k standard complex
Gaussian matrix G=QR with positive diagonal R. The Stiefel Q and R are
independent. Taking the first k rows gives

  |det G_top|^4=|det Q_top|^4 det(G*G)^2.

The numerator expectation is k!(k+1)!. The denominator expectation is
n!(n+1)!/[(n-k)!(n-k+1)!], from independent complex Bartlett gamma variables.
This gives the stated fourth minor moment. Multiplication by binomial(n,k)^2
reduces the kth contribution to (k+1)(n-k+1)/(n+1). Summation from k=0 to n
gives (n+2)(n+3)/6. Independently, each fixed permutation product has second
moment 2^n because its n pair sums are independent and have variance 2.

This theorem describes ensemble RMS scales. It proves neither pointwise
contraction nor membership in a permutation hull. The assumptions should
be retained whenever the formula is used to interpret data.

Numerical campaign checks

The auxiliary fixed-reflector asymptotic proposition in
docs/research/moc_fixed_reflector_asymptotic.md also passes independent
review. With r fixed, all four spectral functions b, 1/(a+b), b/(a+b),
and b^2/(a+b) are absolutely integrable for the stated disk and Gaussian
laws. Weighted laws of large numbers therefore apply entrywise to the
Gaussian-normalized reflector columns. Rotation gives means 0,0,0 for
b, 1/(a+b), and b^2/(a+b), and exchangeability gives mean 1/2 for b/(a+b).
Together with T tending to -2I, these limits give I+MS tending to zero.
The exact determinant ratio tends to zero in probability, and almost surely
under the stated nested coupling. The claim supplies no convergence rate
and no permutation-hull containment theorem.

code/research/moc_campaign_audit.py independently forms explicit products of
Householder matrices, compares the compact determinant with dense slogdet,
reconstructs broad-scale small-n cases by direct products after matrix
rescaling, replays dense seeds, checks synthetic hull signs, differentiates
the support function through matrix exponentials, and compares the 80-digit
Givens reconstruction against double precision. The machine-readable
results are build/moc_campaign_20261031/audit.json.

The compact identity is algebraically correct even for nonreal b. Put
W=[V,BV] and C=[V*;V*B]. Then UBU*=B+W M C, so the determinant lemma uses
S=C D^-1 W. Both off-diagonal blocks of S contain b, and the bottom-right
block contains b^2; conjugating b would be wrong.

An inside result for a sampled permutation hull supports numerical
membership in the full hull. An outside result for that sampled hull is
inconclusive. A log-window subset of sampled products is also an inner
hull and obeys the same logic, provided all its points and the target use
one common scale. Float tolerances are not interval certificates.

The large sweep samples products of 2, 4, or 8 Householder reflectors;
rank(U-I)<=8. Dense entries do not give full unitary coverage. The separate
dense Haar sweep supplies unrestricted-unitary samples only at dimensions
actually recorded. Neither dataset covers all spectra or all unitaries.

Independent audit of the exact pair-maximum obstruction

The new obstruction in docs/research/moc_pair_maximum_moonshot.txt passes
independent exact verification. I replayed its original verifier in /tmp,
then wrote code/research/moc_pair_maximum_audit.py using a separate Hessian
calculation. The results are recorded in
build/moc_campaign_20261031/pair_maximum_independent_audit.json.

For the full parameter family, direct polynomial determinants for all six
complex pair blocks [[c,x+iy],[-x+iy,c]] reduce to r(r^2-1)c^2 modulo
c^2+x^2+y^2-1. Thus every such pair fiber is globally maximized at the
Walsh point when r>1. Every SU(2) block factors as a left diagonal phase
times a block of this form. Left diagonal phases commute with diagonal A,
so determinant support is unchanged. This verifies the entire SU(2) fiber,
including phases, without relying on selected derivative directions.

I computed the full 16-coordinate Hessian from the Leibniz determinant
expansion through second order along exp(tK), then used polarization.
This calculation does not use the original inverse/log-determinant formula.
Its 12-by-12 off-diagonal block agrees exactly with the recorded matrix.
All four diagonal gauge rows and columns are exactly zero. The full matrix
has eigenvalues -96 with multiplicity six and zero with multiplicity ten.

The twelve raw off-diagonal coordinates cover the tangent modulo four left
diagonal gauge directions. They are not a minimal double-quotient chart:
three projected right phase-stabilizer directions remain redundant. This
redundancy is harmless for the semidefiniteness claim. The displayed -96
eigenvalues refer to these raw coordinate generators, each of Frobenius
norm sqrt(2); orthonormalizing changes the numerical eigenvalues, not signs.
The note states its coordinate convention correctly.

The audit independently checks every permutation product against all four
oriented edges of the claimed hull and checks that each claimed vertex
actually occurs. The determinant -43-24i satisfies every edge inequality
strictly, and the positive rational triangle weights reproduce it exactly.
The spectra have nonzero generalized-circle determinants.

Finally, K^2=-3I, the reduced polynomial identity
F((cI+sK)U)=24+384cs^3, and the endpoint determinant -48i all pass exactly.
Substituting c=cos(sqrt(3)t) and s=sin(sqrt(3)t)/sqrt(3) gives derivatives
0,0,2304 at orders one,two,three. For sufficiently small positive t both
c and s are positive, hence this is a strict ascent arbitrarily close to
the Walsh point. The same term is negative for small negative t.

The valid conclusion is a precise obstruction: coordinate-pair global
maximality and a negative semidefinite full Hessian do not imply local
maximality, even for this unrestricted complex 4x4 spectral input. The
example satisfies MOC strictly and cannot contradict it. No higher-order
sufficiency claim follows from the example.
