Combined spinor/unitary minimax: acceleration duality and the mixed Schur bound
31 October 2026

This note strengthens the necessary conditions in
docs/research/moc_compact_spinor_minimax_20261031.txt. It does not exclude a
negative compact minimum, so it is not a proof of unrestricted MOC.

1. Setup and the correct nonsmooth second-order statement

Let x=(w,U) be a hypothetical negative global minimum of

 h(w,U)=max_pi f_pi(w,U),       f_pi=Re(p_pi(w)-d(w,U)),

on eight unit spinors and U(4). Put h(x)=m<0 and let A be the active set.
The same argument below applies to every dimension with 2n spinors.

All derivatives here are real derivatives on the product manifold. Let
g_pi be the first derivative covector of f_pi at x, and write

 Lambda={lambda_pi>=0: sum_A lambda_pi=1,
                         sum_A lambda_pi g_pi=0}.

This set is nonempty by the first-order maximum-function KKT condition.
Equivalently, separation of 0 from conv{g_pi} would give a common strict
descent direction. Lambda includes all sphere-phase stationarity equations,
not only the complex-orthogonal CP1 equations.

Call v critical here if g_pi(v)=0 for EVERY active pi. Let q_pi(v) denote
the second derivative along the product geodesic with initial velocity v.
For any real tangent acceleration a, a smooth curve can be chosen with
these initial velocity and covariant acceleration. Taylor expansion gives

 f_pi(x(t))=m+(t^2/2)(q_pi(v)+g_pi(a))+O(t^3),    pi in A.

The inactive functions stay inactive for small t. Local minimality therefore
implies max_A(q_pi(v)+g_pi(a))>=0 for every a. Finite linear-program duality
now gives the exact necessary condition

   max_(lambda in Lambda) sum_A lambda_pi q_pi(v) >=0.           (1)

Indeed the left side equals inf_a max_A(q_pi(v)+g_pi(a)); the dual equations
are exactly sum lambda=1 and sum lambda g=0. Both the finiteness and dual
feasibility follow from Lambda nonempty. This derivation is a finite LP
argument, not an appeal to a smooth Hessian of the maximum.

An arbitrary FIXED lambda in Lambda need not give a nonnegative quadratic
form, even on this critical space. If Lambda is a singleton, (1) does give
that stronger condition. Without uniqueness or a suitable additional
constraint qualification, the maximizing lambda in (1) can depend on v.

2. A concrete warning against a fixed-multiplier Hessian

In R2 take three unit line directions at angles 0,pi/3,2pi/3 and put

 q_j(x)=(e_j dot x)^2-(2/3)||x||^2.

Every line direction is within pi/6 of one of these three lines, so

 max_j q_j(x)>=(3/4-2/3)||x||^2=||x||^2/12.

Thus max_j q_j has a strict minimum at 0, and all active first derivatives
are zero there. But every convex combination of the three quadratic matrices
has trace 1-4/3=-1/3 and hence cannot be positive semidefinite. In particular,
there need not be ANY single multiplier vector whose Hessian works for all
critical directions. Direction-dependent maximization in (1) is essential.

3. Exploit that all unitary terms are shared by the active contacts

Take a complex-orthogonal spinor tangent z=(z_k), w_k^*z_k=0. Impose the
additional real critical equations

   Re D_z(p_pi-d)=0                 for every active pi.       (2)

Let K be any traceless skew-Hermitian unitary tangent. At the global minimum
U maximizes Re d for these fixed spinors, since the p_pi do not depend on U.
Consequently Re D_K d=0 and

   H(K,L)=-Re D_KD_L d

is a positive semidefinite real symmetric form (with the orbit-geodesic
interpretation for its diagonal). Every v=(z,K) satisfies the active critical
equations, regardless of K.

Write

 b_z(K)=-Re D_zD_K d,

 Q_lambda(z)=Re D_z^2(sum lambda_pi p_pi-d)
                             -m sum_k ||z_k||^2.              (3)

The sphere correction in (3) is exact: every p_pi and d is linear in each
individual spinor, and each active gap has value m. Along the spinor sphere
geodesic the normal acceleration of spinor k is -||z_k||^2 w_k.

All active second derivatives therefore have the common unitary and mixed
terms, and (1) becomes

 max_(lambda in Lambda) Q_lambda(z)+2 b_z(K)+H(K,K)>=0
                                                   for every K. (4)

This yields two rigorous additional constraints:

   b_z annihilates ker H;                                      (5)

   b_z^T H^dagger b_z <= max_(lambda in Lambda) Q_lambda(z).    (6)

For (5), if H(K0,K0)=0 and b_z(K0) were nonzero, substitute arbitrary real
multiples of K0 in (4). The affine term has one sign of unbounded descent.
For (6), after (5) the minimum of H(K,K)+2b_z(K) is exactly
-b_z^T H^dagger b_z. This follows by diagonalizing the positive semidefinite
real form H; dagger is its inverse on its range and zero on its kernel.

Both statements are invariant under a change of the real coordinates on the
unitary tangent. Stabilizer directions have zero b_z automatically. Physical
null directions impose nontrivial mixed derivative identities.

The quantifier is important: (5)-(6) hold for each z satisfying (2), and the
right side of (6) can choose a different KKT multiplier for each z. Replacing
it by Q_lambda for an arbitrarily chosen multiplier is not justified.

One-sided extension. The same conclusions hold on the larger critical cone
g_pi(v)<=0 for all active pi. First-order stationarity implies the maximum
of these derivatives is zero. Only A0(v)={pi:g_pi(v)=0} remains active for
t>0. Every lambda in Lambda is automatically supported on A0(v), because
sum lambda_pi g_pi(v)=0 is a convex combination of nonpositive numbers.
Consequently the acceleration LP on A0(v) has the SAME dual Lambda, and the
proof of (1),(5),(6) is unchanged. For this extension a feasible spinor z
need only satisfy Re D_z(p_pi-d)<=0 for all active pi. The opposite direction
-z may not belong to this cone; no two-sided Hessian inference should be made
from a one-sided critical direction.

Full sphere tangents. Complex orthogonality w_k^*z_k=0 was only a convenient
restriction above. The derivation of (3)-(8) works for every real sphere
tangent Re(w_k^*z_k)=0, including phase components. Its geodesic still has
normal acceleration -||z_k||^2w_k. Allowing these phase components can enlarge
the contact-critical cone; the KKT set Lambda already includes them.

4. Polynomial computation of the mixed block, including singular matrices

Write u_i=w_i, v_j=w_(n+j), C_ij=[u_i,v_j], M=C circ U. For fixed K put

 N=C circ (KU),       R=C circ (K^2 U),
 M_z=C_z circ U,      N_z=C_z circ (KU).

Since K is traceless, det(exp(tK)U)=det U. Determinant multilinearity gives

 D_K d=(det U)^(-1) Ddet_M[N],
 D_K^2 d=(det U)^(-1)
                 (D^2det_M[N,N]+Ddet_M[R]),

 D_zD_K d=(det U)^(-1)
                 (D^2det_M[M_z,N]+Ddet_M[N_z]).                (7)

These expressions are cofactor polynomials and require no inverse of M.
Explicitly Ddet_M[N] is the sum of the determinants obtained by replacing
one row of M with the corresponding row of N. The bilinear second derivative
D^2det_M[N,L] sums over ordered distinct row replacements. Thus (7), H and
b_z are computable exactly at singular and nonsingular inputs alike.

The purely spectral second derivative must include the second derivative of
the brackets: (C_zz)_ij=2[z_i,z_(n+j)]. In particular

 D_z^2 d=(det U)^(-1)
       (D^2det_M[M_z,M_z]+Ddet_M[C_zz circ U]).                (8)

The sphere correction is then added separately as in (3). Omitting either
term in (8) when both halves of a bracket vary gives an incorrect pencil.

5. What this resolves and what remains

The combined condition retains information unavailable to a fixed-spectra
support maximization: the spectral perturbations must be compatible with
all active matching contacts, and they constrain the physical kernel of the
unitary Hessian through (5), as well as its inverse through (6).

For a pairwise-strict support maximum whose unitary Hessian is positive
definite after gauge removal, (6) becomes an ordinary Schur-complement bound.
For a degenerate maximum, (5) is the first test; it generalizes the principle
that an actual flat maximum stays stationary under support-preserving
transport. No sign identity is proved here that contradicts (6) when m<0.
The balance equation sum w_k w_k^*=nI and the tensor eigen-equations must
still be used together with (2),(5),(6) to obtain a universal contradiction.

6. An exact mixed cancellation for row-pair transfers

Let K be supported on row pair i,j. Define the unconstrained complex-linear
spinor replacement Z by Z u_i=u_j and all other spinor variations zero.
Then

                 D_ZD_K d=0.                                  (9)

To verify (9) directly in (7), write the two nonzero rows of KU as
alpha U_j and -conjugate(alpha) U_i. The only row changed in M_Z is
[u_j,v_l]U_(i,l). In D^2det[M_Z,N], the only possible other changed row is
row j of N, which is proportional to this same row; the determinant is zero.
In Ddet[N_Z], the replaced row i is proportional to original row j of M;
this determinant is zero too. This proves the identity at singular M as well.
The reverse transfer and the corresponding column-pair transfers also hold.

Since d is linear in u_i, replacing u_i by itself gives D_Kd. For
c=u_i^*u_j and the complex-orthogonal tangent supported at i,

 z_i=beta(u_j-c u_i),

we consequently have the exact identities

 D_zD_Kd=-beta c D_Kd,       ||z_i||^2=|beta|^2(1-|c|^2).     (10)

At a support maximum D_Kd is purely imaginary. If the whole single-label
complex tangent plane belongs to the active critical space (2), then its
pure spectral second derivative vanishes by separate linearity. Restricting
(4) to real multiples of this K and optimizing the phase of beta gives

 |c|^2 |D_Kd|^2 <= (-m)(1-|c|^2) H(K,K).                    (11)

For H(K,K)=0 this is read as c D_Kd=0, in accordance with (5). For
H(K,K)>0 it follows by minimizing the scalar quadratic in K; no matrix
inverse is needed. A unique active contact implies all first derivatives of
that contact vanish at the minimum, so the stated single-label critical-plane
hypothesis holds automatically. With multiple active contacts it must be
checked explicitly: arbitrary beta need not preserve the contact equations.

Equation (11) quantitatively bounds the transverse complex determinant
derivative by the negative minimum m and the actual unitary curvature. It
does not yet contradict balance or the other KKT equations.

7. A stronger phase/unitary bound when the active contact is unique

If there is a unique active matching, its gap f_pi is smooth near the
minimum and every real sphere tangent is critical. Take z_i=i u_i and all
other spinor components zero. Separate linearity gives

 D_zD_Kd=iD_Kd,      D_z^2(p_pi-d)=0,      ||z||=1.

At the minimum Re D_Kd=0, so |D_Kd|=|Im D_Kd|. Applying the scalar Schur
bound to this phase direction and any unitary direction gives

             |D_Kd|^2 <= (-m) H(K,K).                        (12)

This stronger estimate needs no row-overlap factor and applies to any K,
not only a row-pair generator. It is simply the nonnegative determinant of
the phase/unitary 2x2 Hessian block with diagonal entries -m and H(K,K).

For an orthonormal real unitary tangent basis K_a, summing (12) gives

 sum_a |D_(K_a)d|^2 <= (-m) tr H.                            (13)

Both sides vanish on stabilizer directions, so they can equivalently be
computed after removing gauge directions. This is a quantitative relation
between the complex determinant gradient energy and support curvature.

With multiple active contacts the phase derivative is
-Im(p_pi-d), which can differ among the contacts. The phase direction may
therefore fail the critical-cone inequalities; (12) cannot be asserted in
that case without an explicit additional contact-preserving perturbation.
