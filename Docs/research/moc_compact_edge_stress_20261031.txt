Explicit edge fields at a hypothetical negative compact minimum
31 October 2026

The compact normalization and its analytic assumptions are those in
moc_compact_spinor_minimax_20261031.txt. This calculation translates the
first derivatives into an n-by-n matrix with a forced singular subspace.
It does not exclude a negative minimum.

Notation.
S=[u1 ... un], T=[v1 ... vn] are2-by-n unit-column matrices;
J=[[0,1],[-1,0]], C=S^T J T, M=C Hadamard U, d=detM/detU.
At a negative compact minimum m, an active probability law lambda gives
P=sum lambda_pi p_pi-d=m and spinor derivatives m times conjugate spinors.

Assume first Cij!=0 and M invertible, as holds on a generic chart. Define

 qij=sum_(pi(i)=j) lambda_pi p_pi,
 Lij=qij/Cij,
 Rij=d (M^-1)ji Uij,
 Delta=L-R.

The formulas extend polynomially without either division: L is the formal
partial derivative of sum lambda_pi product_i C_i,pi(i) with respect toCij;
R is the formal derivative of det(C Hadamard U)/detU. In particular every
identity below remains meaningful at zero brackets or singularM when these
cofactor definitions are used.

1. Explicit constrained edge flow

Let h=Re d+m be the common real part of the active p_pi and put
 Bij=sum_(pi(i)=j) lambda_pi.
Then B is doubly stochastic and

 Re qij=h Bij,
 sum_j qij=sum_i qij=d+m,
 sum_j Rij Cij=sum_i Rij Cij=d.                         (1)

Thus q is not an arbitrary complex edge flow: its real part is a scalar
multiple of a genuine nonnegative doubly-stochastic matrix, with a common
support matching law. This constraint is essential and is not discarded.

2. Matrix criticality and two forced singular values

Differentiating in row and column spinors gives

             J T Delta^T=m barS,
            -J S Delta=m barT.

Equivalently,

             S Delta=m J barT,
             Delta T^T=m S* J.                         (2)

Taking the adjoint of the first identity and using the second gives

             Delta Delta* S*=m^2 S*,
             Delta* Delta T^T=m^2 T^T.                (3)

For rankS=rankT=2, two singular values ofDelta are therefore exactly -m.
Their left singular subspace iscol(S*) and their right subspacecol(T^T).
This is an explicit matrix restriction directly computable from U, spinors
and the contact law. It is stronger than merely recording a scalar balance.

3. Balanced polar decomposition

Moment balance gives A=SS*, B0=TT*=nI-A. The2-by-2 identity
J B0 J*=A^T implies the nonzero singular values of C are the two eigenvalues
ofA. In particular ||C||_*=trA=n. Equivalently, using(2),

        barC=(Delta/m)(T^T barT).                      (4)

The restriction ofDelta/m fromcol(T^T) tocol(S*) is an isometry, as follows
either from(3) or directly frombalance. Let W be the polar partial isometry
of barC. Then

                    Delta=m W+N,

where N maps the orthogonal complement ofcol(T^T) to the orthogonal
complement ofcol(S*). For n=4 this remaining block has size2-by-2.
The conjugation here matters: W=polar(barC), notpolar(C)^T.

The normal block is not controlled by balance alone. It belongs to the
conormal freedom of the rank-two bracket-matrix variety. Bounding it requires
the actual determinant/active-contact structure or a correct second-order
argument, not a generic claim about tensor critical points.

4. Two genuine contraction bounds, insufficient by themselves

Every matching derivative matrix D_pi has exactly one nonzero per row and
column, of modulus product_(k!=i)|C_k,pi(k)|<=1. Thus ||L||op<=1 by convexity.

The Schur multiplier X -> U Hadamard X has operator norm<=1: Uij is the
inner product of the unit vector e_i and the jth unit column ofU, giving an
isometric left/right factorization of that multiplier. Since M is a
contraction, ||adj(M)||op<=1. Hence ||R||op<=1 as well.

These bounds only give -m<=2; they do not contradict(3). No stronger bound
onN has been proved here. Re q=hB and common matching support in(1), plus
unitary stationarity, must enter any decisive argument using these fields.

5. Saturated determinant cannot be a counterexample

For completeness the contraction embedding gives a strict interior condition
on a hypothetical negative minimum: |d|<1. Indeed |d|=1 makes M unitary.
For isometriesA0,B0 with M=A0*(U tensorI2)B0 this implies
 (U tensorI2)B0=A0M.
Its(i,j) block says Uij Jvj=barui Mij. Whenever Uij!=0, therefore
 Jvj=Cij barui and |Cij|=1.

On each connected component of the nonzero-entry bipartite graph ofU,
all row spinors have one projective direction and all column spinors its
orthogonal direction. The Cij on supported entries factor into row/column
unit phases. Every supported perfect matching consequently has the same
product p, and detM=pdetU. At least one such matching exists because detU!=0.
Thus d=p is itself one of the comparison products. Saturation gives no
violation in any dimension.

The remaining target is unchanged: exclude m<0 with |d|<1, the constrained
contact flow(1), stress(2)--(4), and full unitary/spectral higher variations.
