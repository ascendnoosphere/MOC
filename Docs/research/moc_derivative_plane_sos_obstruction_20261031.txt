Independent derivative-plane moments do not suffice for a score-gap SOS
31 October 2026

The new real-orthogonal signed-variance theorem implies a positive Hermitian
moment matrix on each separate function space span_C{1,z,r_i}, wherez is the
matching product andr_i is one ai or bj derivative. It is tempting to express
an actual support gap as a sum of squares from these eight spaces, with
nonnegative ordinary squared-minor terms. The following exact actual edge
rules out that completion, even allowing arbitrary nonnegative Hermitian
Gram matrices independently on all eight planes.

Full independent-plane SOS obstruction.

For one derivative r let

 psi_r(pi)=(z21-z20)(r_pi-r20)-(r21-r20)(z_pi-z20),
 s_r(pi)=|psi_r(pi)|^2.

Every function inspan_C{1,z,r} vanishing atbothcontacts is a complex multiple
ofpsi_r: the two contact evaluation rows are independent becausez20!=z21.
Consequently any nonnegative Hermitian Gram expression on this plane that
vanishes atbothcontacts is a nonnegative real multiple ofs_r. Indeed the
contact evaluation vectors must belong to the kernel of its PSDGram, whose
remaining support has dimension atmost1.

Every nonnegative summand in a proposed SOS for g must vanish atbothcontacts,
since g does. Thus an independent-plane completion would necessarily have
the form

              g=A w+sum_(eight r) c_r s_r,
              w>=0, c_r>=0,

whereA consists of ordinary minor indicators (only those vanishing atboth
contacts could actually occur, but allowing all of them strengthens the
impossibility argument).

This is not an optimizer failure or numerical infeasibility report. It is
a rational cone separator and an exact rank obstruction for the entire
independent-plane Gram family at a genuine two-contact spectral edge.
It does not refute the signed-variance/derivative-plane PSDtheorems, and the
edge is already covered by the repository's weighted-minor inequality.

The next variance-based global argument must couple derivative planes or
use additional positive function spaces. Merely summing independently
