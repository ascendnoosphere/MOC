Connectedness lemma for the positive-height residual-face branch

Put A=a+1 and

  D=d A^2-A[c(d^2+1)+(d+1)^2]+c^2 d,
  b=A(c+1)(d+1)(A-cd)/D,
  Q=b(d-1)-cd-1.

Assume c>=0, d>=0, cd<1, A>=1, D>0, and Q<0. These
are the dangerous branch after solving P=0 for b; in particular the
numerator of b is positive and b>0. Since D=-A(c+1)<0 at d=0,
necessarily d>0. Also

  D(1)=-d^2-d-1-c(d^2+1)+c^2 d < 0,

because c^2 d<c (with the conclusion also clear for c=0).
The quadratic D in A consequently has two real roots, with 1 strictly
between them. Write A_+(c,d) for the larger root. For A>=1, D>0 is
equivalent to A>A_+(c,d); this root is continuous on the domain.

For fixed c,d and A>A_+, direct differentiation gives

  db/dA = -(c+1)(d+1)[c(A-cd)^2+A^2(d+1)^2]/D^2 < 0,

and b tends to (c+1)(d+1)/d as A tends to infinity. When d<=1,
Q<0 holds for every A>A_+. When d>1, Q is strictly decreasing
in A, tends to +infinity as A descends to A_+, and tends to

  -(c-d^2+d+1)/d

as A tends to infinity. Thus the fiber with Q<0 is nonempty precisely
when c>d^2-d-1, and then it is an open ray A>A_Q(c,d), with a
unique finite root Q(A_Q)=0. One may equivalently define the fiber
threshold as A_+ for d<=1 and A_Q for d>1. This threshold is
continuous: continuity for d>1 follows from the strict derivative;
as d tends to 1 from above, for every fixed A>A_+(c,1), Q is
negative at nearby parameters, while A_Q is always above A_+.

Consequently the base of the dangerous domain is

  0<d<delta,  max(0,d^2-d-1)<c<1/d,

plus the boundary points c=0 where they satisfy the original strict
inequalities. Here delta is the unique positive zero of
d^3-d^2-d-1. Indeed the lower c bound is strictly below 1/d
exactly for d<delta, and the displayed lower-bound condition is
automatic when d<=1. The base with c>0 is path connected: interpolate
d through (0,delta), and take c halfway between its continuous upper
and lower bounds. The full domain with c>0 is the epigraph of the
continuous threshold and is therefore path connected. Alternatively,
over any compact base path one can raise A to a common sufficiently
large value, travel along the path, then lower A. Every admissible
point with c=0 is a limit of admissible points with c>0, because the
conditions D>0, Q<0, cd<1, and A>=1 remain valid under sufficiently
small positive perturbation of c (in fact A>A_+>1).

Conditional inertia propagation

Let H_x be the real symmetric Hessian at x and let H_e be the Hessian
at e. The spectral argument needs the following separate exact facts:

  (i) every 13 by 13 minor of H_x vanishes throughout this domain,
      so corank(H_x)>=2;
  (ii) the coefficient of z^2 in det(H_x+z H_e) is nonzero.

These facts must be established by polynomial identities; this note
does not establish either one. Condition (ii) alone gives only
corank(H_x)<=2 and does not rule out corank one. Together, (i) and
(ii) give corank exactly two. In an orthonormal decomposition into
ker H_x and its orthogonal complement, the z^2 coefficient equals
the determinant of the invertible 12 by 12 Hessian block times the
determinant of H_e restricted to ker H_x. Hence (ii) also proves that
the restricted form is nonsingular. Since the kernel bundle and the
restricted quadratic form vary continuously on the connected domain,
their inertia is constant. Positive definiteness at one exact point
then implies it throughout the interior. The same argument extends to
c=0 using nonvanishing of the same identities there and continuity.

One exact checkable reference is a=20, c=1/2, d=1, b=5166/1345.
For the basis produced by SymPy's nullspace method from the original
14-coordinate Hessian, the restriction of Q=det B has

  upper-left entry = 2053835401869962825470275 /
                     11142654539008626568621442,
  determinant      = 326426415890044646250 /
                     795903895643473326330103,

both strictly positive. The reference calculation must be included in
an exact independent verifier along with (i), (ii), and the claimed
Hessian-pencil identity before this becomes a proof of exclusion.
