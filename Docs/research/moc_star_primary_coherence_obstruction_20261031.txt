STAR primary coherences are constrained by the entire matching function
31 October 2026

Status. Exact necessary inequalities for every FULL matching-SOS Gram,
and an exact obstruction to the Hilbert--Schmidt projector primary map
on the actual nonordinary STAR control. No obstruction to full C0 and
no MOC counterexample is asserted.

1. Three full-feature identities

Use the ten STAR coordinates (A,B,C,D,E,F,U,R,S,T). For a vector-valued
matching function f_pi in an arbitrary real Hilbert space, put

  A=f_8, B=f_15, C=f_4.

Direct equality of the ten feature coefficient vectors gives

  B-A = f_23-f_7+f_13-f_22,
  C-A = f_18-f_7+f_16-f_22,
  B-C = f_23-f_18+f_13-f_16.                 (1)

These identities use the full ten-dimensional space. They do not
restrict the remaining twelve primary coherences, the cross block, or
the auxiliary Gram.

Suppose ||f_pi||^2=g_pi and ||A||^2=a, ||B||^2=b, ||C||^2=c. For any
positive weights lambda_k with sum 1/lambda_k=1, weighted Cauchy--Schwarz
applied to one identity yields

  2<D_i,D_j> >= w_i+w_j-sum_k lambda_k g_k,    (2)

where D=(A,B,C), w=(a,b,c), and the four k are the listed indices.
The right side is an affine expression in the cost. The inequality
itself is certified by the four-by-four PSD matrix

  diag(lambda)-epsilon epsilon^T,

with epsilon the four signs in (1). Thus it is a genuine necessary
moment inequality, not an estimate based on a particular SOS solution.
Optimizing these four scalar weights gives the familiar upper bound
(sum sqrt(g_k))^2 for ||D_i-D_j||^2. Rational weights suffice below.

2. Exact all-phase obstruction for the oblique-projector HS kernel

Let F_i be the exact two-by-two oblique projectors of the actual STAR
control, i in {0,1,3}, and H_ij=tr(F_i^*F_j). The complementary projectors
Q_i=I-F_i have the identical HS Gram, because X -> tr(X)I-X is unitary
on Mat_2 with its Hilbert--Schmidt inner product.

Consider ANY paired primary construction using the normalized HS kernel,
arbitrary node phases, and arbitrary pairwise O(2) orientation choices.
For opposite-leaf pairing (A,E)->3,(B,D)->1,(C,F)->0, it necessarily has

  |<D_i,D_j>| <= sqrt(w_i w_j) rho_ij,

where rho_ij^2=|H_ij|^2/(H_ii H_jj) with the opposite-leaf indices. This
bound survives all the stated phases and reflections.

The exact control and rational Cauchy weights in the artifact give

  pair A,B: required rho^2 >= .7272520528 > HS .7013111369,
  pair A,C: required rho^2 >= .7385851119 > HS .6458405762,
  pair B,C: required rho^2 >= .8881517378 > HS .6147980961.     (3)

Every comparison in (3) is between exact positive rationals. Square
roots used to choose the Cauchy weights are replaced by rational upper
bounds with denominator 10^7; the checker verifies the resulting
four-by-four PSD matrices by all principal minors. It also checks the
twenty-four gap data are nonnegative and all three feature identities.
Consequently no choice of phases or reflections repairs this HS map.

This strengthens the earlier gauge-inequivalence calculation: the HS
kernel is not merely different from the canonical doubled descriptor
kernel, but is incompatible with any full matching Gram on this actual
control in the stated paired construction. The original C0 cone has
an exact positive Gram for the same control, so the obstruction concerns
only the selected primary map.

3. What this says about source-positive kernels

Positivity of a three-node source kernel alone cannot determine a valid
primary frame. The remaining fourteen squared norms force substantial
coherence among A,B,C. Here they are almost the same vector, because
the four-vector corrections in (1) have small norms. The source kernel
must meet these cross constraints before any auxiliary completion is
possible. The full source-dependent cross-block problem is still open.
