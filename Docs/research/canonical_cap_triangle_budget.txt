Canonical f34 cap and the triangle-capacity candidate

This is a sufficient-certificate calculation and a tested candidate,
not a proof of the global nonnegative-support inequality. In particular,
the proposed universal inequality below is not established and could
be stronger than MOC4.

Use the lexicographic permutation order and write g_i for the support
gap at permutation i. The 14 rays f34 and r_j for the 13 j below are
a basis for the 14-dimensional permutation-product space. Every
ordinary cap coefficient is exactly one of the following gaps:

  ordinary ray  j :  0  7 10 13 17 18 20 22 23 27 28 30 31
  coefficient    : g1 g17 g6 g22 g12 g18 g20 g8 g14 g15 g9 g23 g7

The mixed coefficient is

  lambda = g0-g1-g6-g14-g23.

The 13 listed gap indices are precisely the zeros of f34, and the
corresponding ordinary-ray evaluation matrix is the identity. For
any nonnegative gap in the permutation-product space, all ordinary
cap coefficients are therefore nonnegative automatically; that gap
belongs to this cap exactly when lambda>=0. The exact inverse and all
24 reconstruction identities are independently checked by
code/research/verify_f34_triangle_coordinates.py.

The three ordinary coefficients occurring in the known weighted-minor
pair certificates are

  p=g18,  q=g14,  r=g9.

Each pair (18,23), (18,28), or (23,28) can compensate a mixed weight
sqrt(uv) using ordinary budgets u,v allocated to that pair. Optimize
over nonnegative allocations to all three pairs, respecting each ray's
total budget. Put S=p+q+r and m=max(p,q,r). The exact maximum mixed
weight obtainable by these combined pair certificates is

  C(p,q,r) = S/2              if m<=S-m,
             sqrt(m*(S-m))    if m>=S-m.

For the first case, assign edge weights (p+q-r)/2, (p+r-q)/2,
(q+r-p)/2 and allocate the same amount to both endpoints of each
edge. For the second case, the largest-budget vertex is the center
of a star; distributing its budget proportionally to the other two
budgets gives sqrt(m*(S-m)). Zero-budget cases follow by continuity.
Optimality follows from the dual inequality sqrt(uv)<=y_i u+y_j v
whenever y_i y_j>=1/4. In the balanced case use y_i=1/2. In the
unbalanced case with p=m use y_p=(1/2)sqrt((q+r)/p) and
y_q=y_r=(1/2)sqrt(p/(q+r)); these choices meet the respective
primal values. Hence C is the exact capacity of this certificate
family, not merely a lower bound for it.

Candidate sufficient claim: every actual gap in the cap with lambda>0
satisfies C(p,q,r)>=lambda. If true, the three weighted pair
certificates would cover every such cap gap; together with the existing
cone and symmetry reductions, this would imply the desired global
nonnegative-support conclusion. No proof of the candidate is claimed.

The actual gap in actual_gap_outside_ordinary_cone.txt gives
lambda=42246975256, p=65769370688, q=216282432, r=58010468072.
Here p>q+r and

  C^2-lambda^2 = p(q+r)-lambda^2
                = 2044729819574190561216 > 0.

The formerly proposed stronger claim that every actual gap belongs to
the ordinary-minor cone is false: this same exact gap is outside that
cone, while its weighted certificate proves its support direction.
A differential-evolution search of 126552 spectral evaluations found
best C/lambda-1=0.364273, which is evidence only; the stored numerical
data are build/six_symmetric/triangle_budget_search.npz.
