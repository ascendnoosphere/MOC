Extensive reflector analysis of the frozen MOC campaign
2026-10-31

The 5,994 reflector records support three distinct conclusions. Their Gaussian
contraction fits a logarithmically corrected fixed-rank rate. Their large
negative gaps against the largest sampled product are mostly caused by matching
heuristics, rather than by the fixed-rank determinant correction. A simple
angular certificate, combined with a Hadamard upper bound, verifies a sufficient
containment test in 2,759 of these original cases without evaluating their
determinants. The underlying criterion is valid in every dimension; the
reported passes are floating-point diagnostics, not exact interval certificates.

Source and replay boundary

The input is the immutable 20,127-record file
build/moc_campaign_20261031/all_moc_data.jsonl. Its SHA-256 before and after

This lane uses all 5,994 records with source large_reflector: six spectral
families at every integer n=2,...,1000. For n>=8, reflector rank r is 2,4,8,

from the r=2,4,8 rate comparisons. The matrices are generally dense in their

uniformly sampled permutations from the saved seeds. It adds no independent
spectral instances. The original remaining 47 products include deterministic
orders, nearby transpositions and a greedy magnitude-improving matching. Only
their already recorded maximum is used in the comparisons below. The first 128
products alone supply the new angular tests.

1. Separate the natural product, a random matching, and the selected maximum

Let z_id=prod_i(a_i+b_i), and define

  L_nat = log|det(A+UBU*)| - log|z_id|.

This is the logarithm of the small determinant kernel used by the reflector
algorithm. It is different from the radial gap against the maximum of all 175
selected products. The latter also includes a substantial matching advantage.

For five families, the natural matching is close to the median random matching
on this per-dimension scale. In the cancellation family, b_i=-a_i+0.03 c_i makes
the natural matching exceptionally small by design. Its baseline deficit is
about 3.845 n. A large positive L_nat there does not mean the determinant is
large relative to the full permutation hull.

The magnitude heuristic adds a linear-in-n advantage in every family. Thus a
gap of several hundred in log modulus against the selected maximum should not
be attributed entirely to random-unitary mixing. The fixed-rank correction is
usually much smaller. This decomposition is visible directly in each replay
row, rather than inferred from a pooled correlation.

2. A precise fixed-rank limit separates the six spectral families

Here is a useful extension of the earlier fixed-reflector observation. Suppose
coordinate pairs (a_i,b_i) are iid; dependence within each pair is allowed. Let
r be fixed and let the reflector vectors be independent uniform complex unit
vectors. Assume the expectations below are absolutely integrable, and write

  p=E[b/(a+b)],       q=E[1/(a+b)],
  h=E[ab/(a+b)],      m=E[b].

Assume a+b is nonzero almost surely. Then

  det(A+UBU*) / prod_i(a_i+b_i)
       --> [ (1-2p)^2 + 4qh ]^r                 in probability.       (1)

The same convergence is almost sure under the nested Gaussian-vector coupling
used in the earlier note.

Proof. Write U=I+VTV*, where V contains r normalized independent Gaussian
vectors. The compact reflector recurrence gives T --> -2I. With D=A+B, define
Q=V*D^-1 V, P=V*D^-1 B V, J=V*D^-1 B^2 V, G=V*BV. Weighted laws of large
numbers give

  Q --> qI, P --> pI, J --> tI, G --> mI,
  t=E[b^2/(a+b)]=m-h.

The exact determinant kernel is I+MS, where

  M=[TGT*, T; T*,0],        S=[Q,P;P,J].

Its scalar 2 by 2 limiting block has determinant

  (1-2p)^2+4q(m-t)=(1-2p)^2+4qh.

There are r identical blocks, proving (1).

This formula explains genuine differences between the data families:

- For independent identically distributed a,b, exchangeability gives p=1/2.
  If their joint law is invariant under (a,b)->(-a,-b), then q=h=0. Gaussian,
  disk, near-real and logwide inputs satisfy these symmetries, so their limit
  is zero. The symmetries do not give a uniform finite-n convergence rate.
- In the pair-cancellation family, b=-a+epsilon c with independent centered
  circular Gaussian a,c. Direct expectation gives p=1 and q=h=0. Its limit is
  one, so L_nat eventually tends to zero. The positive finite-n correction is
  a long crossover, not evidence for a nonzero positive asymptotic exponent.
- The clustered family is iid across a and b but is not centrally symmetric.
  Its limit is the nonzero number (4qh)^r.

The clustered prediction can be evaluated accurately without fitting data.
Its centers are c=(-1-0.7i,0.8-0.2i,0.1+1.1i), with independent circular Gaussian
noise of standard deviation sigma=0.035. Conditional on two centers, put
mu=c_j+c_k and nu=c_j-c_k. Then

  E[1/(a+b) | j,k] = (1-exp(-|mu|^2/(2 sigma^2)))/mu,
  E[ab/(a+b) | j,k] = mu/4 - (nu^2/4) E[1/(a+b) | j,k].

For the first identity, averaging 1/(mu+rho exp(i theta)) over theta gives
1/mu for rho<|mu| and zero for rho>|mu|; integrate the Gaussian radial density.
For the second, a+b and a-b are independent proper complex Gaussians given
the centers, and ab/(a+b)=(a+b)/4-(a-b)^2/[4(a+b)].

Average the formulas over the nine center pairs. The smallest |mu|^2 is 0.85,
so the Gaussian corrections are below exp(-346.9). To the displayed precision,

  4qh = -0.013607512381172367 - 0.022332580263230557i,
  log|4qh| = -3.643843185447626.

The observed late-dimensional median L_nat/r=-3.25856 is moving toward this
finite limit. Fitting a zero-kernel power law to this family would be the wrong
asymptotic model.

3. Why the Gaussian rate needs a logarithmic correction

For Gaussian or disk spectra, fixed-rank kernel entries obey the upper rate

  I+MS = O_p(sqrt(log n/n)) entrywise,

and consequently

  |det(A+UBU*) / prod_i(a_i+b_i)|
       = O_p((log n/n)^r).                                    (2)

Here r is fixed. This is a proved upper rate, not a two-sided distributional
limit for the log determinant.

An elementary truncation proof explains the logarithm. For Gaussian spectra
write d=a+b and e=a-b. They are independent proper complex Gaussians, and

  1/d,       b/d=1/2-e/(2d),
  b^2/d=d/4-e/2+e^2/(4d).

Each weighted compression entry is an empirical average involving these
variables and products of independent Gaussian reflector coordinates. The
reciprocal terms have tails bounded by C/t^2. For example, when a numerator N
is independent of d, P(|N/d|>t)<=E|N|^2/(2t^2). The Gaussian-coordinate weights
have the needed finite moments. For disk spectra, bounded numerators and a
bounded planar density for a+b give the same tail bound.

A centered iid variable with this tail bound, truncated at sqrt(n log n), has
truncated second moment O(log n). The probability that any of n observations
exceeds the threshold is O(1/log n), and the truncation bias of the sample mean
is O(1/sqrt(n log n)). Chebyshev's inequality therefore gives sample-mean error
O_p(sqrt(log n/n)). Normalizing the Gaussian reflector columns changes the
matrices only at the smaller O_p(n^-1/2) scale. Their Gram matrix tends to I,
and T+2I=O_p(n^-1/2). Substitution in the exact kernel proves its entrywise rate.
The determinant has size 2r, so its polynomial formula gives (2).

The reciprocal variables generally have infinite second moments. An ordinary
finite-variance central limit theorem would therefore miss the logarithm.
A nonsingular limiting Gaussian kernel would suggest the stronger typical
shape

  L_nat = -r log n + r log log n + O_p(1),                       (3)

but establishing that joint limit and its nonsingularity is beyond the upper-
rate proof above. The dataset can test the shape; it cannot prove (3).

For n=100,...,1000, regress L_nat/r separately for each family and rank. The
following slopes use either log n or log n-log log n as the predictor. The
latter would have slope -1 under (3).

Gaussian data fit the log-corrected shape well in this dimension range. Disk

Near-real slopes per rank are approximately -0.047, 0.041 and 0.032. A fixed
imaginary scale of 0.001 makes these spectra nearly one-dimensional on the
sampled range. Constants in the planar reciprocal-tail estimates become large.
The asymptotic zero-kernel statement alone does not predict their finite-n
behavior. The logwide family also converges slowly on these dimensions.

The earlier 108-case probe at n=100,1000,10000 is an auxiliary illustration,
not part of the 20,127 records or this replay. Its Gaussian rank-4 median
L_nat values -6.305,-13.836,-21.980 support the same trend, but they do not add
108 containment observations.

Neither (1) nor (2) proves containment in a fixed sampled inner hull. The
natural product itself fluctuates relative to other matchings. A ratio that
shrinks relative to one vertex does not supply angular coverage or a lower
inradius for the other sampled vertices.

4. Conditioning and selection effects

The maximum compact-kernel condition number is 106751.9. Family medians are
28.4,31.4,97.3,11.2,24.3,134.4 in the table's family order. The implementation's
1e12 cutoff rejects none of these records. Its near-zero-baseline regeneration
rule also regenerated no spectra. Thus these particular acceptance rules did
not censor this dataset, although both matter for reproducing the method.

The compact kernel can be ill-conditioned when its determinant is small even
though the original determinant calculation is sound. The reference diagonal
D=A+B can also contain small entries and amplify the determinant ratio. The
rank correction, kernel condition number and minimum pair sum are different
quantities; the report preserves each. For example, within Gaussian records,
Spearman correlation between log kernel condition and L_nat is -0.586, whereas
correlation with the minimum reference-diagonal magnitude is only -0.155.
These are descriptive, unadjusted associations across the dimension/rank
schedule, not causal or error-probability estimates.

Original global scaling classified 5,260 hull calculations as degenerate.
Selecting bounded-log-radius subsets recovered 5,216 numerical inclusions.
That is a computational selection effect: a very large selected product erased
smaller products at the chosen floating-point tolerance. A successful subset
remains a valid inner-hull witness, but its rescaled margin cannot be compared
directly with a margin normalized by the full maximum. The subset search is a
post-selected certificate search, not an independent sample of hull geometry.

5. An all-dimension angular/Hadamard sufficient criterion

Let z_1,...,z_k be any actual permutation products. Suppose their nonzero
arguments, in circular order, have maximum gap Delta<pi and all moduli are at
least R. Then

  disk(0,R cos(Delta/2)) subset conv{z_1,...,z_k}.                (4)

Proof. In every direction, some selected argument lies within Delta/2. The
support function in that direction is at least R cos(Delta/2). Comparing
support functions proves disk containment. This argument allows unequal
vertex radii; assuming a particular ordered chord is a hull edge is unnecessary.

For arbitrary dimension, spectra and unitary U, put

  H_row = prod_i sqrt(sum_j |a_i+b_j|^2 |U_ij|^2).

Hadamard's determinant inequality applied to C=diag(a)U+U diag(b) gives

  |det(diag(a)+U diag(b)U*)| = |det C| <= H_row.                (5)

Consequently H_row <= R cos(Delta/2) is a sufficient MOC containment criterion
in every dimension. It uses spectral data, unitary entries and selected
permutation products; it does not require computing the target determinant.
The analogous column-norm bound can improve it, but this replay uses rows only.
The criterion does not claim that the inequality always holds.

For rational complex input one can turn such an argument into exact algebra:
choose a polygon of permutation products containing the origin, compute the
squared distances from the origin to its supporting edge lines, and compare
them with H_row^2, a product of rational row-norm squares. This avoids relying
on floating-point angles for an exact certificate. No such interval/rational
conversion is asserted for these random floating-point records.

The replay optimizes (4) over every radial threshold among the first 128
uniform products. For each threshold it retains all products above that radius,
computes the maximum angular gap, and keeps the largest disk bound. A sorted
circular list and heap update the maximum gap in O(m log m), m=128. Duplicate
angles retain their true circular wrap gap. Using zero for the wrap gap of
coincident points would create false origin-coverage certificates; explicit
degenerate tests and the brute-force comparison guard against this error.

Seventeen originally degenerate rows pass the angular determinant test
(12 near-real, five logwide). Conversely, 87 originally successful hull rows
fail this origin-centered disk test: a containing hull need not contain a
sufficiently large disk centered at zero. These results are complementary,
not replacements for the original classifications.

The Hadamard sufficient test passes at least one original instance at 995 of
the 999 dimensions; the only dimensions without a pass are 2,3,4 and 22. This
is finite coverage of instances, not a theorem about every matrix at those
orders. At n>=100, every one of the 901 pair-cancellation cases passes the
Hadamard test. Their small reference pairing makes them easy for this criterion
even though their natural-product ratios are large and positive.

Among successful angular tests with n>=100, the selected threshold retains a
median of five products in every family; the 90th percentile is eight or nine.
Thus much of the observed containment has a short angular witness. This does
not make those products independent angular observations: all come from the
same pair of spectra, and the threshold is selected after seeing them.

For n>=8, Hadamard passes decrease with reflector rank: 1141/1986 for r=2,
955/1986 for r=4 and 657/1986 for r=8. Actual determinant disk tests pass
1947,1959 and1962 of those respective groups. More mixing makes the determinant
smaller in the observed symmetric families while making the row bound less
sharp. A certificate's failure can therefore mean its norm bound is loose,
rather than that the determinant is near the true hull boundary.

Exact closing experiment, kept separate from the frozen data

The large dimensional sufficient criterion was converted into a rigorous
certificate for a new rationalized n=1000 pair-cancellation instance using two
noncommuting exact Householder reflections. The exact bound

  ||A+UBU*||F^2/1000 <=226788766/1000000000<1/4

gives |det|<2^-1000. Three exact permutation products have a triangle inradius
strictly greater than 2^86, checked with Gaussian-integer cross products. The
target determinant was never evaluated. Exact checks also establish that A
and UBU* do not commute, the two-reflection product is not a single Hermitian
Householder reflection, and neither spectrum lies on a generalized circle.
This proves one 1000-dimensional instance and illustrates a data-to-proof
conversion with substantial slack; it does not change the 20,127 sample count.
See docs/research/moc_exact_n1000_certificate.txt and the standalone verifier
build/moc_extensive_20261031/verify_exact_n1000_certificate.py.
