Second-order contact rank at a regular actual nonic minimum
31 October 2026

Result. Under the precise regularity assumptions below, a negative
minimum of a physical linear functional cannot have contact-evaluation
rank at most4. More generally, write k for that rank and r for the
rank of contact evaluation on the five-dimensional affine Gauss kernel.
At a stationary minimum with nonzero nonic multiplier one necessarily
has

                             k-r >= 4.                     (1)

For any nonempty contact set r>=1, so (1) forces k>=5. This is a local
necessary condition, not a proof of MOC. Singular pencils, failure of
face regularity, and a boundary of the actual source image remain
outside the theorem.

1. Setup and hypotheses

Let V be the real14-dimensional matching space. Let M:V->Sym9(R) be
the universal inverse quadratic pencil and set

                 N(g)=2^32 det M(g), M(e)=0,

where e is the constant matching function. Let ell be the uniform
mean of the24 matching values. Fix an actual nonnegative cost g with
ell(g)=1. Let C:V->R^s evaluate all its active zero contacts, and put
k=rank C. Thus Cg=0 and Ce=(1,...,1).

The needed assumptions are:

 (a) A=M(g) has rank8 and inertia(4+,4-,1zero).
 (b) For a nonzero h in ker A, the linear map
                       B(d)=M(d)h
     has rank9.
 (c) DN(g) is nonzero on ker C. This is the constraint qualification
     for the nonic inside the contact face. It also gives constraint
     qualification after adding ell(g)=1, since DN(g)[g]=0,
     Cg=0, and ell(g)=1.
 (d) Near g, the admissible actual costs contain a relative open
     neighborhood in the real manifold
               N=0, Cg=0, ell(g)=1.
     In particular no future/realization boundary is active there.

For actual costs the inertia bound proved in
moc_actual_inverse_pencil_rank_four_20261031.txt gives (a) whenever
rank A=8. It does not by itself establish (b), (c), or (d).
At a negative minimum, condition(c) follows automatically when k<=5,
as established in Section8 below; it need not be an extra assumption
for the conclusion excluding k<=4.

Set K=ker B. Assumption(b) gives dim K=5. Both g and e belong to K:
M(g)h=0 and M(e)=0. They are independent because Cg=0 and Ce!=0.
Define r=rank(C restricted to K). Therefore

                            1 <= r <= 4.                  (2)

2. Exact determinant Hessian

Choose h of unit length and decompose R9=h-perp direct-sum Rh. In
this orthonormal basis,

                      A=[A8 0;0 0],

with A8 invertible of inertia(4,4). Put kappa=2^32 det A8>0.
The determinant derivative is

                    DN(g)[d]=kappa h^T B(d).                (3)

For a direction d tangent to the nonic, h^T B(d)=0. Expanding the
bordered determinant by its Schur complement gives

 D²N(g)[d,d]=-2 kappa B(d)^T A_plus B(d),                    (4)

where A_plus is the inverse of A on h-perp and is zero on Rh.
There is no trace term in (4): the bottom-right linear coefficient is
zero in a tangent direction. The form A_plus on h-perp has inertia
(4,4). Formula(4) is a direct derivative of a linear symmetric pencil;
it does not assume that arbitrary nonic points are actual.

3. Contact restrictions and the dimension count

The tangent space of the normalized contact manifold is

 T={d: Cd=0, ell(d)=0, DN(g)[d]=0}.

By(c), dim T=12-k. Its intersection with K has dimension4-r: first
dim(K intersect ker C)=5-r, then normalization removes exactly one
dimension because g is in that intersection and ell(g)=1. Thus

                 dim B(T)=8-k+r.

By(3), B(T) lies in h-perp, of dimension8. Its codimension there is
exactly

                              q=k-r.                      (5)

Restricting a form of inertia(4,4) to a subspace of codimension q
leaves at least4-q positive and4-q negative directions. Indeed the
subspace meets each of a positive4-space and a negative4-space in
dimension at least4-q. In particular if q<=3 the restriction is
indefinite. Formula(4) therefore makes D²N restricted to T indefinite.

4. The multiplier and the minimum condition

Let L be the physical linear objective, and put m=L(g)<0. At a local
minimum satisfying(a)--(d), first-order stationarity gives

                L-m ell=lambda DN(g)+C^T beta.              (6)

The second-order necessary condition on T is

                       -lambda D²N(g)|T >= 0.              (7)

If lambda!=0, indefiniteness from q<=3 contradicts(7), proving(1).
The argument applies with the physical mixer fixed at its minimizing
value: joint minimization over mixers still supplies a local minimum
with respect to g alone.

The nonzero multiplier is automatic if there is a nonzero ordinary
matching indicator p with Cp=0. Such an indicator has ell(p)>0 and
L(p)>=0 by the physical common-minor identity. If lambda=0, evaluating
(6) on p would give

                   0=L(p)-m ell(p)>0,

a contradiction.

Whenever k<=5 such an ordinary indicator ALWAYS exists. Choose k
contact evaluations spanning the row space of C. Fix any two-set I.
Those k permutations use at most k of its six possible images J.
An unused J gives the nonzero ordinary indicator p(pi)=1_{pi(I)=J},
which vanishes on the chosen spanning contacts, hence on all contacts.
Thus in particular the k<=4 exclusion has no multiplier loophole.

Consequences for regular minima with a nonzero multiplier are:
 k=5 requires r=1;
 k=6 requires r<=2;
 k=7 requires r<=3.
For k=5, all contact evaluations have IDENTICAL restrictions to K,
since those restrictions span a line and all evaluate e as1. The
Gauss leaf need not vanish on the contacts: e is in K and prevents
that incorrect conclusion.

5. A concrete sufficient test for actual-image interior

Assumption(d) should be verified, not inferred from inertia alone.
A sufficient source test is available. Let Z be the smooth complex
critical cone of M8, of complex dimension6, and realize

                         g=t e-Re z, z in Z.

If

       dim_C span(e,T_z Z,T_conjugate(z) Z)=13,              (8)

then the real source map(t,z)->t e-Re z has differential rank13.
To see this, (8) says the two conjugate6-spaces have zero intersection
and e is outside their sum. The real-part map on T_z Z is then
injective as a map of real12-spaces, and e supplies a thirteenth
direction. The real submersion theorem makes its image open in the
smooth real nonic. This establishes(d), including after contact and
mean restrictions. In the audited critical cubic model T_z Z can be
computed as ker Hess(F)(z) at a smooth source point.

If (8) fails, or the source point is singular, this argument gives no
actual-image neighborhood. Such a boundary must be treated separately.

6. What remains for a global proof

The theorem reduces regular interior negative minima to special
contact configurations of rank at least5 with (1). It does not show
that those configurations are SOS or impossible. It also does not
exclude rank<=7 pencils, failure of the nonic constraint qualification
on the face, or a critical/future boundary of the realization map.
No all-N9 or all-real-normal positivity assertion is used.

7. Extension when rank Dpsi drops

The same argument gives a useful version without assumption(b). Put
t=rank B and assume DN(g)!=0, so B(V) meets a direction not orthogonal
to h. Then dim K=14-t and, with r=rank(C restricted to K),

                   dim B(T)=t-1-k+r.

Its codimension in the full neutral8-space h-perp is

                         q=9-t+k-r.

Consequently a nonzero-multiplier local minimum requires

                         k-r >= t-5.                      (9)

This is only a dimension bound; it does not identify the actual
inertia on B(T) if q>=4. It nevertheless extends the exclusion into
some lower-rank strata. For example rank B=8 is excluded when
k-r<=2, and rank B=7 is excluded when k-r<=1.

Rank B=9 must not be inferred just from rank M(g)=8. The exact actual
H-contact example a=(100,200,i,2i), b=(300,400,3i,4i), eta=i has
rank M(g)=8, rank B=7, k=4, r=3, and face regularity. It still falls
under the exclusion(9), since k-r=1<t-5=2, but illustrates why the
rank hypothesis needs its own check. No source-image interior claim
for that particular example is being made here.

8. Full inequality KKT and the generic multiplier argument

There is a useful way to justify first-order KKT before imposing face
constraint qualification. Assume only that the real nonic is smooth
and the actual-image neighborhood condition holds. The equalities
N=0 and ell=1 have independent gradients, since DN(g)[g]=0 while
ell(g)=1. The direction

                              d=e-g

satisfies DN(g)[d]=0, ell(d)=0, and Cd=(1,...,1). Thus the full problem
with inequalities g_pi>=0 satisfies the Mangasarian--Fromovitz
constraint qualification. Full KKT gives

              L=m ell+lambda DN(g)+sum_C mu_pi eval_pi,
                              mu_pi>=0.                   (10)

When k<=5 the ordinary indicator p constructed in Section4 now yields

                    lambda DN(g)[p]=L(p)-m ell(p)>0.

This proves BOTH lambda!=0 and face constraint qualification(c).
Consequently, for k<=4, the regular-rank9 theorem needs no separate
face-CQ hypothesis beyond smoothness and the actual-image neighborhood.

For arbitrary k, the parent's generic-objective argument also removes
the zero-multiplier possibility. If lambda=0 in(10), then L(u)>=m for
EVERY pointwise nonnegative matching function u with ell(u)=1; hence
g globally minimizes L on that polytope. For a generic L, that minimum
is a unique normalized extreme ray. A negative minimum excludes every
ordinary ray by physical positivity. Each of the48 mixed census rays
has det M nonzero: direct exact replay gives N9=+1 or-1 on them. Thus
the unique mixed minimizer cannot satisfy N(g)=0. This contradicts
lambda=0.

The required generic objective is available without assuming a generic
single mixer. Permutation mixers give all24 evaluation functionals,
whose affine span on V14 is the full13-dimensional hyperplane L(e)=1.
Their convex hull therefore has nonempty relative interior. Starting
from any physical negative separator, a sufficiently small mixture
with an interior point of this hull preserves its negative value on
the given actual cost. A further arbitrarily small generic perturbation
inside that convex hull remains a convex combination of physical
functionals. Positivity for every physical functional is equivalent
to positivity for their convex combinations, and the needed common-
minor nonnegativity is preserved by these combinations.

For k>5 this argument removes lambda=0, but DOES NOT by itself supply
face constraint qualification. The second-order theorem still needs
that condition, or a separate analysis of a singular contact section.

9. A direct rank formulation for computation

Let W_C=ker C. Under face constraint qualification,

                    rank(B restricted to W_C)=t+r-k.

The nonic tangent removes exactly the h-component from this image;
mean normalization removes only the radial kernel direction g. Thus
the subspace on which the determinant Hessian is tested has dimension

                    rank(B restricted to W_C)-1.

Any subspace of dimension at least5 in a nonsingular neutral8-space
has both positive and negative vectors. Therefore the necessary
second-order condition is equivalently

                  rank(B restricted to W_C)<=5.            (11)

For a fixed contact set this is an explicit determinantal condition:
choose a basis f_j of W_C and form the9-by-d matrix with columns
M(f_j)h. Every6-by6 minor must vanish at a regular interior negative
minimum. This avoids computing k and r separately.

If k>=8, dim W_C<=6 and g is already a nonzero kernel vector, so(11)
is automatic. These small-dimensional high-contact faces are therefore
not excluded just by the index count. For k<=7, the rank condition may
still give a nontrivial algebraic restriction on possible minima.
