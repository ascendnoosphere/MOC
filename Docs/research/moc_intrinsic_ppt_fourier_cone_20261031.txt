Intrinsic Fourier form of the auxiliary PPT cone; ordinary-ray absorption
31 October 2026

Status and scope

The reduced fourteen-dimensional PSD/four-dimensional PPT cone has an
intrinsic description without auxiliary spectra. Its generators are
squares of functions whose [22] Fourier matrix has rank at most one.
All34 distinct ordinary nonconstant Boolean indicator rays already have
rank-one witnesses in this cone. This removes the ordinary-ray extension
of the closed cone. It does not prove universal actual-support membership
or unrestricted MOC4.

Exact verifier and artifact

  code/research/moc_ppt_ordinary_rankone_witnesses.py
  data/certificates/moc_ppt_ordinary_rankone_witnesses.json

Running the script is read-only; --write regenerates the artifact.
The verification uses Gaussian-rational matrix arithmetic, with no
optimizer or floating point. All34 indicators plus the constant are
checked, including evaluation, exceptional rank one, and a rank-one
factor of each partial-transposed exceptional corner. All70 labeled
Boolean events of all subset sizes are explicitly identified with one
of these35 functions.

1. Physical coordinates

Use beta(w)=(0,1,i,w), F(w)=F0+wF1, and J=[F0 F1]. Its24 rows are
indexed by lexicographic permutations and its32 columns by parameter
degree followed by the sixteen subset masks. Let j_pi=J[pi,:]^T.

The independently established geometry is rankJ=14 and
 W=range(J^T), E=C^2 tensor U subset W,
where U is the two-dimensional size-two Boolean coefficient space with
zero four-vertex incidence sums. The physical cost convention is
 g_pi=j_pi^* T j_pi.

The script uses exactly the same rational basis as
moc_exact_ppt_jet_certificate.py:
 Wmatrix=J^T[:,pivots],
 pivots=(0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,12,13,16,18),
 psi=J conjugate(Wmatrix), Q=Wmatrix^* E.
Here Wmatrix denotes a matrix, whereas W above denotes its range.
For a coefficient column c in C^14 put v=Wmatrix c and
 h_pi=v^*j_pi=(psi conjugate(c))_pi.
Then T=vv^* gives cost |h_pi|^2. In these coordinates T=Wmatrix C Wmatrix^*
with C=cc^*. The exceptional vector is u=E^*v=Q^*c.

The real U basis used here is
 e1(01)=e1(23)=1, e1(02)=e1(13)=-1, others0;
 e2(01)=e2(23)=e2(02)=e2(13)=1, e2(03)=e2(12)=-2.
It is not normalized, which is harmless: its Gram correction is local
on the second tensor factor and preserves product rank and PPT.

2. Exact Fourier determinant bridge

Let q0=01|23,q1=02|13,q2=03|12. Use the [22] representation rho22 on
q0-q2,q1-q2, and define
 B(h)=sum_pi h_pi rho22(pi^{-1}).
The complete function space range(J) is
 V14=C directsum Mat3([31]) directsum Mat2([22]).

For row-major vectorization of B and of the two-by-two reshaping U of u,
the verifier proves the linear identity
 conjugate(u)=A vec(B(h)),
where A is the following exact four-by-four matrix:

 [(-1+i)/12, (1+i)/12, (1-i)/12, (-1-i)/12]
 [(-1+i)/4,  (1+i)/4,  (-1+i)/4, (1+i)/4  ]
 [(-1-i)/12, (1+i)/12, (1+i)/12, (-1-i)/12]
 [(-1-i)/4,  (1+i)/4,  (-1-i)/4, (1+i)/4  ].

This is verified as an equality of four-by-fourteen matrices, not just
on the ordinary examples. A symbolic four-variable expansion then gives

 det reshape(conjugate(u)) = ((-1+i)/12) det B(h).             (1)

In particular u is a product vector in C^2 tensor C^2 exactly when
rank B(h)<=1. This also verifies that the kernel of the exceptional
projection is precisely the ten-dimensional [4]+[31] coefficient space.

3. Intrinsic finite-square theorem

Let Caux be the closed cost image of
 {T supported on W:T>=0, (E^*TE)^PT>=0}.
Then, exactly,

 Caux={ sum_k |h_k|^2 : h_k in V14, rank B(h_k)<=1 }.         (2)

There is a finite sum in (2), not an unspecified limiting expression.
One direction is immediate from (1): each rank-one witness vv^* has a
product exceptional component, hence a PSD partial-transposed corner.

For the other direction, use an orthogonal split W=Eperp directsum E
and write a feasible T as [[A,C],[C^*,R]]. The two-qubit PPT theorem makes
R a finite sum of product projectors r_k r_k^*. Positivity of T gives
 C=C R^+ R, A-C R^+ C^*>=0.
Thus T is the sum of projectors on
 (C R^+ r_k,r_k)
and projectors supported on Eperp. Every such vector has a product
exceptional component, including zero. Equation(1) proves (2).
Caratheodory gives at most16 product terms for R, and the residual
has rank at most10, so at most26 squares suffice in this argument.

The established closed-all-auxiliary-square theorem identifies this same
cone with the closure of fixed-auxiliary multiaffine polynomial squares.
Equation(2) removes the exceptional-parameter limiting issue by allowing
the intrinsic rank-one-[22] functions directly.

The only non-elementary positivity input here is the genuine two-qubit
PPT=separable theorem. Primary source: Horodecki--Horodecki--Horodecki,
arXiv:quant-ph/9605038v2, Theorem3, pages7--8:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/quant-ph/9605038
It is used only for a two-by-two tensor product, not for fourteen- or
sixteen-dimensional bipartite systems. The rest of this section is the
explicit Schur lift also proved in moc_qft_auxiliary_cone_closure_20261031.txt.

4. Every ordinary ray is already in Caux

For |I|=|K| define r_{I,K}(pi)=1_{pi(I)=K}. At degenerate auxiliary values
beta_j=1_{j in K}, the multiaffine polynomial
 H_I(x)=product_{i in I} x_i product_{i notin I}(1-x_i)
has H_I(beta_pi)=r_{I,K}(pi), and its square equals the same indicator.
Closure and auxiliary Mobius normalization therefore already imply
ordinary-ray inclusion. The following gives exact finite reduced witnesses.

The indicator is a matrix coefficient of the permutation representation
on k-subsets. The k=1,3 representation is [4]+[31], so B(r)=0. For k=2
it is [4]+[31]+[22], with each summand occurring once, so B(r) has rank
at most one. The constant k=0,4 cases also have B=0. Thus (1)--(2) give
a rank-one reduced witness for every indicator.

The script solves psi conjugate(c)=r canonically, computes u=Q^*c and
checks its two-by-two determinant. It then factors u=alpha tensor v and
checks the full exact four-by-four identity
 (u u^*)^PT=(conjugate(alpha) tensor v)
            (conjugate(alpha) tensor v)^*.
Thus the PT assertion is proved by an explicit square, including cases
with zero corner; no approximate eigenvalue is used.

There are34 distinct nonconstant rays:16 singleton indicators and18
distinct two-subset indicators, after identifying simultaneous subset
complements. With the constant this covers all70 labeled (I,K) pairs.
Consequently
 Caux + cone(ordinary rays) = Caux.                         (3)
Ordinary variables can improve conditioning, but do not enlarge the
closed certificate cone.

For the exact existing single-support certificate, retain records with
r[1]=0; this gives the same27 rays in the same order as setup(). Given
its exact C and weights w_j, the ordinary-free coefficient is
 Ctotal=C+sum_j w_j c_j c_j^*.
Every added term is PSD, kills the contact vector k=psi[1,:]^T, and has
PSD exceptional PT. Therefore the existing strict13-dimensional contact
face and strict four-dimensional PT positivity are preserved.

5. Precise conic dual and the remaining full-target statement

For real q=(q_pi), (2) gives

 q in Caux^* iff sum_pi q_pi |h_pi|^2>=0
                 for every h in V14 with rank B(h)<=1.     (4)

This removes all auxiliary sampling and all separate Boolean marginal
conditions. In Fourier coordinates split h=(x,y), with x in C^10 and
y the four entries of the two-by-two [22] matrix. Write its Hermitian
q-moment form as [[A,C],[C^*,D]]. Condition(4) is equivalent to
 A>=0, rangeC subset rangeA,
 and D-C^* A^+ C is nonnegative on all product vectors in C^2 tensor C^2.
The singular range condition follows by varying the unconstrained x;
the remaining assertion is the exact Schur minimum, not an ansatz.

The missing universal theorem is that every actual supported spectral
gap g_pi=max_sigma Re(eta z_sigma)-Re(eta z_pi) has nonnegative pairing
with every q satisfying (4). Closedness of Caux makes this statement
equivalent to universal membership, including boundary and coincident
spectra. The Fourier determinant identity and ordinary-ray absorption
do not establish this spectral inequality.

6. A global positive-map consequence

Let P_pi permute the four tensor factors of H=(C^2)^{tensor4} and define
 Phi_q(X)=sum_pi q_pi P_pi X P_pi^*.
Then an equivalent version of (4) is

             q in Caux^* iff Phi_q is a positive map.       (5)

Proof. The weight-k subspaces of H are precisely the k-subset
permutation representations. Summing k=0,...,4 gives
 H = C^5 tensor[4] directsum C^3 tensor[31] directsum C tensor[22].
Consequently the functions h_pi=<x,P_pi y> have arbitrary scalar and
three-by-three standard Fourier blocks, while their two-by-two [22]
block has rank at most one. Conversely any such Fourier profile factors
in these multiplicity spaces, so every h in (4) is such a matrix
coefficient. Positivity of Phi_q is equivalent to
 <x,Phi_q(|y><y|)x>=sum_pi q_pi |<x,P_pi y>|^2>=0
for all x,y. This proves (5).

Combining (5) with the established all-auxiliary-square closure theorem
also proves that, for these signed permutation-conjugation maps,
positivity on all pure four-qubit PRODUCT inputs already implies
positivity on ALL sixteen-dimensional inputs. This is a special property
of the displayed multiplicities; it is not a general positive-map theorem.

In particular the independently proved real-O4 fixed-b kernel theorem
implies a new universal operator consequence: for any real orthogonal O4,
q_pi=sgn(pi) product_i O[i,pi(i)]/det(O), the map Phi_q is positive,
unital, and trace preserving. This includes detO=-1 and singular entries.
It does not assert complete positivity, and makes no corresponding claim
for arbitrary complex-unitary determinant amplitudes.

Finally a strict separator for an actual g can always be normalized by
sumq=1. Equation(4) implies sumq>=0 using h=1. If sumq=0, positivity of
Phi_q and trace Phi_q(|y><y|)=0 force Phi_q=0. For a permutation-fixed
nonzero symmetric vector s,
 Phi_q(|y><s|)=(sum_pi q_pi P_pi)|y><s|,
so the mean permutation operator is also zero. Its matrix coefficients
span V14, and therefore q annihilates every actual gap. Such q cannot
strictly separate. The nontrivial obstruction candidate is thus exactly
a normalized positive signed-permutation map violating one actual
spectral support inequality. No such example or exclusion theorem is
proved in this note.
