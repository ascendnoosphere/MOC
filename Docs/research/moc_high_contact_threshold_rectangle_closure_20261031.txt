Uniform threshold-rectangle matching-SOS closure: budgets [7,13,18,27,31]
31 October 2026. Ray and permutation indices are zero based.

THEOREM. Let t,a,b,c,d,e be nonnegative. The matching cost
  g=t*f34+a*r7+b*r13+c*r18+d*r27+e*r31
is a sum of squares of matching functions whenever det M(g)=0.
This includes every zero-budget boundary. This is one restricted family
theorem; it does not settle MOC or the source/contact-rank reduction.

The verifier uses exact SymPy arithmetic only. It checks the full
factorization, all polynomial identities below, both independent core
Gram/square identities on all24 permutations, and all24 cap stabilizers
on the entire14-dimensional matching-function space. Runtime about12s.

Normalize t=1; t=0 is an ordinary Boolean-square sum, and t>0 follows by
scaling. The exact nonic has one factor with nonnegative coefficients
and positive constant, and one mixed factor R. Thus det M(g)=0 implies
R=0. Put
  p=bc-1, q=ab-1, S=(a+1)(b+1),
  D1=p*d-b, D2=q*e-S.
The established small-core theorems say:
  D1>=0 and b*c*d>0 => f34+b*r13+c*r18+d*r27 is matching-SOS;
  D2>=0 => f34+a*r7+b*r13+e*r31 is matching-SOS.
Adding the remaining ordinary budgets preserves matching-SOS.

R is bilinear in d,e. Its exact decomposition is
  R=S^2(c+1)+S*U*d+S*(c-q)*e+C*d*e,
where
  U=(c+1)(a+c)-p,
  C=(a+c)(-q)+(-p)+ac+ab^2+b^2+b+c^2.
R at b=0 has nonnegative coefficients and constant1. Any root has b>0.
Consequently, if D1>=0, then p*d>=b>0, so b,c,d>0 and the D1 core is
valid. If D2>=0 its core is valid directly. It remains to rule out a
root when BOTH D1<0 and D2<0.

1. If p<=0 and q<=0, U>=0, c-q>=0 and C>=0. The displayed bilinear
   decomposition gives R>=S^2(c+1)>0 for all d,e>=0.

2. If p<=0<q, then 0<=e<S/q. Interpolate affinely in e between0 and S/q.
   At e=0, U>=0 gives R(d,0)>0. At the other endpoint,
     q*R(0,S/q)=abc*S^2>=0,
     q*(d-slope of R at e=S/q)=b*S*W>=0,
   where
     W=a^2c+ac^2+ab+b+1+a*(1-bc)>=0.
   Thus R(d,S/q)>=0. The first interpolation weight is strictly
   positive because D2<0, so R(d,e)>0.

3. If q<=0<p, then 0<=d<b/p. Interpolate affinely in d. At d=0,
   R(0,e)=S^2(c+1)+e*S*(c-q)>0. At d=b/p the constant term is positive:
     p*R(b/p,0)
       =S*[p*(S(c+1)-b)+b*(c+1)*(a+c)]>0,
   because S(c+1)-b has nonnegative coefficients and constant1.
   The e-slope at this endpoint is also nonnegative. To verify it,
   write N=p*(e-slope of R at d=b/p), substitute
     a=r/[b(r+v)], c=(u+1)/b,
   and clear the positive denominator b^2(r+v)^2. The result is
     b^2*((r+v)u-br/2)^2 + H,
   where
     H=b^5(r+v)^2+b^4(7r^2/4+3rv+v^2)
       +b^2u(r^2+5rv+3v^2)+b^2v(2r+v)
       +bu^2(r+v)(3r+2v)+bu(5r^2+9rv+3v^2)
       +b(r+v)(2r+v)+u(u+1)r(r+v).
   Every term is nonnegative for b,u,r,v>=0. In the present case choose
   u=p>0, r=ab>=0, v=1-ab>=0; then r+v=1 and b>0. Hence the entire
   endpoint R(b/p,e) is positive for e>=0, and so is R(d,e).

4. If p>0 and q>0, the assumptions D1,D2<0 put (d,e) inside the
   rectangle [0,b/p] x [0,S/q]. A bilinear polynomial is the convex
   combination of its four corner values. The first three corners are
   positive by
     R(0,0)=S^2(c+1)>0,
     p*R(b/p,0)=S*[p*(S(c+1)-b)+b*(c+1)*(a+c)]>0,
     q*R(0,S/q)=abc*S^2>0.
   For the fourth, put u=p>0,v=q>0, so a=(v+1)/b,c=(u+1)/b. Then
     p*q*R(b/p,S/q)=(b+1)(b+v+1)*G/b^3,
   where
     G=b^2(v+1)(u-b/2)^2
       +b^5+b^4(3v+7)/4+b^2u(v+1)
       +bu^2(v+1)(v+3)+bu(v+1)(2v+5)
       +b(v+1)(v+2)+u(u+1)(v+1)^2.
   This is strictly positive since b>0. All four corner values are
   positive, so R(d,e)>0 throughout the rectangle.

All four cases contradict R=0. Therefore every nonnegative nonic root
meets a valid D1 or D2 SOS core. No numerical source reconstruction,
inertia assumption, local genericity, or positivity sampling is used.
