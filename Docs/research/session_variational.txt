Singular stationary points: a cross-ratio obstruction

Let A=diag(a_1,...,a_n), with distinct a_i, and B be any matrix. Set C=A+B and suppose rank C=n-1. Let eta be nonzero. Suppose that Re(eta det(A+exp(tK) B exp(-tK))) has derivative zero at t=0 for every skew-Hermitian K. Normality of B is not needed for this lemma.

Write adj C=u v*, with nonzero u,v; this factorization exists since adj C has rank 1. The stationary equation is H=[eta adj C,A]=H*, because differentiation gives Re tr(K [B,eta adj C]) and [B,adj C]=[C-A,adj C]=-[A,adj C]. Thus, for i != j,

  (a_j-a_i) eta u_i conjugate(v_j)
   = -(conjugate(a_j-a_i)) conjugate(eta u_j) v_i.       (1)

The supports of u and v are equal, call this S. Indeed, if i is in supp u and j is in supp v with i != j, (1) forces i in supp v and j in supp u. If no such pair exists, both supports equal the same singleton. When |S| >= 2, (1) also implies |eta u_i/v_i|=|eta u_j/v_j| for all i,j in S; in particular the two null vectors have proportional coordinatewise magnitudes. Here C u=0 and C* v=0, since C adj C=adj C C=0.

For any four distinct i,j,k,l in S, define d_pq=a_q-a_p and r_p=eta u_p/v_p. Equation (1) becomes

  d_pq r_p = -conjugate(d_pq) conjugate(r_q).              (2)

Multiply the equations for (i,j) and (k,l), then divide by those for (i,k) and (j,l). Since the r's have equal absolute values, the non-spectral factors cancel. We obtain

  (d_ij d_kl)/(d_ik d_jl) is real.                        (3)

Equivalently, a_i,a_j,a_k,a_l lie on one generalized circle (a circle or straight line). This last equivalence is the elementary real-cross-ratio criterion; it follows by translating a_i to zero and applying a complex fractional linear map sending a_j,a_k to 1,infinity.

Consequently, if n=4 and the four distinct a_i are not on one generalized circle, then |S| <= 3. There is an index m for which both the mth row and mth column of adj C vanish; in particular det C with row and column m removed is zero. The same statement applies to any nonconcyclic quadruple in arbitrary dimension: no singular stationary point has both null vectors nonzero on every index of that quadruple.

Scope and gap: this is a necessary condition at a rank-(n-1) stationary point, including a maximizer whose determinant happens to vanish. It neither gives a common eigenvector of A and B nor proves MOC4. Rank <= n-2 makes adj C=0 and is not covered. The conclusion is not valid without the nonconcyclic assumption: choose e.g. distinct real a_i and Hermitian C of rank n-1 with a null vector having all coordinates nonzero; then B=C-A is Hermitian, the determinant is real, and eta=i makes the support identically zero.

Nonsingular maxima: pair curvature, coupled curvature, and a local-global obstruction

Let F(B)=Re(eta det(A+B)), and assume C=A+B invertible. For skew-Hermitian K, put E=[K,B], G=[K,[K,B]], z=eta det C, and R=C^{-1}. Direct differentiation yields

 F'(0)=Re(z tr(RE)),
 F''(0)=Re(z {tr(RG)+tr(RE)^2-tr((RE)^2)}).                (4)

The second formula includes the genuinely coupled variation; treating its terms separately as nonpositive is invalid. The first condition is equivalent to [zR,A] being Hermitian, as in the singular adjugate formula above.

At ANY stationary point, define d_ij=F(B)-F(P_ij B P_ij), where P_ij swaps coordinates i,j. Let K_ij(w)=w E_ij-conjugate(w) E_ji. The support restricted to this U(2) rotation is an affine function of its Bloch-sphere coordinate: moving the rotation from B to A leaves a determinant linear in the rotated 2x2 A block (the quadratic 2x2 determinant is fixed). Stationarity kills both transverse linear terms. Consequently, exactly for all real t and |w|=1,

 F(exp(tK_ij(w)) B exp(-tK_ij(w)))=F(B)-d_ij sin^2(t).   (5)

This also holds without invertibility. At a local maximum all d_ij>=0, and if d_ij=0 the full pair fiber is support-flat. The existing flat_resonance_closure.md treats the nonzero-height flat branch with its stated hypotheses; equation (5) identifies the pairwise-strict branch that remains.

For two distinct coordinate pairs e,f, the t^2 coefficient of (4) for K=K_e(x)+K_f(y) has the form

 Q(x,y)=-d_e |x|^2-d_f |y|^2+2 Re(alpha xy+beta x conjugate(y)),

where alpha,beta are determined by polarization of (4) at the point. A local maximum forces the exact necessary inequality

 |alpha|+|beta| <= sqrt(d_e d_f).                          (6)

To see this, independent choices of arg x and arg y align both complex terms, so the maximum cross contribution is 2(|alpha|+|beta|)|x||y|. Conversely the displayed scalar inequality makes Q<=0 for all x,y. The collection of (6) provides 15 explicit four-real-dimensional tests for the complete second variation, but it is not equivalent to negativity of the full 12-real-dimensional form. It does not rule out irreducible points by itself.

A strict local maximum can be far below the global support, even with nonconcyclic spectra and positive height. Take eta=1, and

 a=(2+5i, 2-3i, -2+i, 5+4i),
 b=(1+i, -2+2i, 3+3i, 1-5i),  U=I.

The determinant is 129+108i. The six transposition gaps d_12,d_13,d_14,d_23,d_24,d_34 are respectively 472,96,42,1104,1218,126. As B is diagonal, the full second variation is exactly

 Q(K)=-sum_{i<j} d_ij |K_ij|^2,

with all cross terms zero: invariance under diagonal conjugation forbids cross terms between distinct matrix entries, and (5) supplies each coefficient. Thus the maximum is strict on the unitary B-orbit, where the diagonal part of K is its stabilizer. Nevertheless permutation (2,1,4,3) gives determinant 1099-126i and hence a larger support value 1099. The cross ratios ((a2-a1)(a4-a3))/((a3-a1)(a4-a2)) and the analogous b ratio are (41+i)/29 and (13+69i)/58, respectively, so neither spectrum lies on a generalized circle. This exact example is not a counterexample to MOC: both points are permutation points. It shows that a proof based only on strict local maximality cannot infer global maximality, even in the nonconcyclic nonsingular positive-height regime.

Numerical exploratory check (not a proof): four random U(4) starts for each of 100 independent random complex pairs, and separately 100 nearly real pairs with support direction -i, found no irreducible local maximum satisfying a gradient-norm threshold 1e-4 and the generous nonpermutation filter max_j |U_ij|^2 <0.97 for every row. These runs cannot establish reducibility; convergence, optimization tolerances and the finite sample all limit the test. The proposed irreducible-strict-maximizer theorem remains open here.
