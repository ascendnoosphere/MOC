Independent audit: Kovacec 2026 Conjecture 7 example
2026-10-30

Verdict: the exact example is a counterexample to Conjecture 7 as stated in
A. Kovacec, DMUC Preprint 26-11, 7 April 2026, pp. 4--5:
 https://www.mat.uc.pt/preprints/ps/p2611.pdf
The example is not a counterexample to Marcus--de Oliveira: the determinant
is strictly inside a triangle of matching products and hence inside their
ordinary convex hull. The exact checker and its certificate give the explicit
spectra, skew-Hermitian Cayley parameter, determinant and graph geometry.

I independently read Definitions 6 and Conjecture 7 in the primary paper and
ran code/research/verify_kovacec_polygon_counterexample.py from the repository
root. It passed every exact rational/integer assertion. The input spectra are
Gaussian integers scaled by 1000; fourth-degree determinant coordinates are
therefore scaled by 1000^4. The matrix K is exactly skew-Hermitian; the
Cayley construction and the displayed matrix product establish exact
unitarity, so A=diag(alpha) and B=U diag(beta) U* are normal with the
specified spectra. The determinant of A+B is independently evaluated with
exact Gaussian-integer and rational arithmetic.

The 24 matching products are distinct. Their transposition graph has 72 edges;
the exact intersection and half-edge traversal constructs 317 planar vertices
and 343 face cycles. There are no collinear supporting-edge pairs, there is
exactly one negatively oriented cycle, and its 14 vertices are distinct, so
it is a simple outer Jordan polygon. All 14 oriented-edge determinants place
k=(65,3), in unscaled determinant units, strictly on the interior side. Hence
k is in the strict kernel of this polygon.

The paper's exterior is defined by arbitrarily long unobstructed straight
segments, not merely by the usual unbounded component of the graph complement.
The kernel check bridges that distinction for this example: each point
outside the polygon sees infinity on the ray directed away from k, because a
ray from a strict kernel point meets the polygon in one interval and the
entire graph lies in the closed polygon. A point inside the polygon cannot see
arbitrarily far without crossing its Jordan boundary. Hence the paper's
exterior is exactly the unbounded exterior of this polygon, and its boundary
is the verified Jordan curve.

The exact ray from the determinant in direction (-19,6) misses all 72
transposition segments: after consistent determinant orientation, 35
intersection parameters lie strictly behind the ray origin, 14 lie strictly
past one segment endpoint, and 23 lie strictly past the other. All denominators
are nonzero. These strict inequalities persist in a neighborhood of the
determinant, so it belongs to the open exterior and is outside the closure of
the bounded Jordan component, as required to refute Conjecture 7.
Independently, strict orientation tests put the same determinant inside the
triangle of matching-product indices 0,1,5 (zero-based lexicographic order).
