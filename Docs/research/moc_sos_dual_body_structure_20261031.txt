1. The normalized dual body is a compact23-dimensional spectrahedron

Let V be any24-by14 real matrix with orthonormal columns spanning the
matching-function space V14=[4]+[31]+[22]. For real y define

 H(y)=V^T diag(y) V,
 D={y:sum y=1,H(y)>=0}.

This is precisely the real matching-SOS dual. Its mean functional on
matching costs is the profile under study. The uniform vector y0=1/24
is strictly feasible, with H(y0)=I14/24. Thus D has affine dimension23.

Put P=VV^T. The group-invariant projection has the exact character formula

 P_pi,sigma=[1+3chi31(pi^-1 sigma)+2chi22(pi^-1 sigma)]/24.

On cycle types id,(12),(12)(34),(123),(1234), its values are respectively

                 7/12,1/6,1/12,-1/24,-1/12.

In particular every normalized moment has trace7/12. Let Q=P entrywise
squared. The checker proves all five exact central eigenblocks of Q:

 irrep       [4]   [31]  [22]  [211] sign
 eigenvalue  7/12  3/8   17/48 7/24  1/6
 multiplicity 1    9      4     9     1.

For all y,

 ||H(y)||_F^2=y^TQy >= ||y||_2^2/6.                  (1)

Consequently the moment map is injective, and every feasible normalized
y satisfies

 ||y||_2 <= sqrt(6) traceH = 7sqrt(6)/12.             (2)

Thus D is compact. The profile projection of D is also compact. No
unbounded hidden signed weights can invalidate separation or attainment.
One exact recovery formula is

 y=Q^-1 a,  a_pi=v_pi^T H(y) v_pi,

where v_pi^T is row pi of V.

2. Extreme-rank structure

Let H(y) have rank r, and let N span its kernel. A normalized y is an
extreme point of D exactly when the only real perturbation z satisfying

                  sumz=0, H(z)N=0                  (3)

is z=0. This is the full face condition, including cross blocks. The
weaker null-block condition N^T H(z)N=0 is a tangent condition and does
not characterize the face.

Dimension counting gives r(r+1)/2<=82 for any extreme point: the24-
dimensional linear moment space intersects the r(r+1)/2-dimensional
PSD face inside Sym14, dimension105; an extreme ray must have
intersection dimension at most1. Therefore every extreme point has
rank at most12. Linear objectives attain an extreme point, so rank13
or14 interior points are useful strict numerical approximations, not
the final exposed optimum unless the objective is constant.

Rank-one normalized moments are classical permutation evaluations.
Indeed Boolean indicators r have L(r^2)=L(r), while a rank-one moment
makes every variance zero; hence L(r) is zero or one. Singleton
partitions select one permutation. Their zero-support relations force
all pair-subset indicators to agree with that permutation. These
indicators span V14, so H is its evaluation rank-one matrix. Injectivity
in(1) then gives y=delta_pi. Nonclassical extreme duals therefore have
rank at least2. No claim classifying ranks2 through12 is made.

3. Exact barrier support oracle and its error bound

Use y=y0+Nx, with columns of N an orthonormal basis of sumz=0, and let
A_i=H(N[:,i]). The logarithmic barrier for maximizing s dot y is

       -s dot y-mu logdet H(y).

Its Hessian is mu tr(H^-1 A_i H^-1 A_j), strictly positive definite
because the moment map is injective. Every positive mu therefore has
a unique central point. Write

 t_pi=v_pi^T H^-1 v_pi,
 lambda=mean(s+mu t),
 r=s+mu t-lambda 1.

For every feasible y', positivity gives

 s dot y' = lambda-mu tr(H^-1 H(y'))+r dot y'
          <=lambda+R||r||_2, R=7sqrt(6)/12.           (4)

At an exact stationary central point r=0. Pairing its stationarity
equation with y gives the exact support upper bound

        max_D s dot y' <= s dot y+14mu.              (5)

For a nonzero residual, the precise version is
s dot y+14mu-r dot y+R||r||, or the weaker norm-only bound
s dot y+14mu+2R||r||. These are analytical error controls; floating-point
matrix positivity and residuals still need reliable numerical or exact
bounds before promoting a numerical oracle result to an exact theorem.
If the objective was rescaled, the support bound must be rescaled too.

4. Eliminating both a3 and b3 produces rank-one support normals

Work on the dense normalized spectral chart
 a=(0,1,infinity,x), b=(b0,b1,b2,y).
The row at infinity contributes its homogeneous leading coefficient.
For p=(i,j,k,l),

 z_p=beta_i(1+beta_j)(x+beta_l)=[x,1] M_p [y,1]^T.

The checker constructs all24 complex two-by-two coefficient matrices
symbolically and verifies their determinant pattern. There are four
cases, each with six permutations:

 l=3: M_p=k_p [[0,1],[1,0]], k_p=beta_i(1+beta_j);
 k=3: M_p=k_p [[0,1],[0,beta_l]];
 i=3: M_p=(1+beta_j) [[1,0],[beta_l,0]];
 j=3: M_p=beta_i [[1,1],[beta_l,beta_l]].

Thus18 matrices have rank at most one, while the remaining six lie
on one common rank-two line. This algebraic pattern is more special
than a general set of24 points in complex Mat2.

For a dual y define M_y=sum_p y_p M_p. Its full actual spectral test
on this fiber is

 Re tr(C^T M_y) <= max_p Re tr(C^T M_p)
                  for every complex rank-one C.          (6)

Indeed C=eta [x,1]^T[y,1] on a dense chart; all complex rank-one matrices
are obtained by allowing homogeneous coordinates and scaling. Hence
the fixed-fiber problem is a convex set defined by rank-one complex
support normals. Replacing(6) by all complex matrix normals is stronger
and is not justified. Likewise applying two-qubit PPT=separability to
a mixed-state relaxation need not produce a single violating product
normal: the objective contains a minimum over24 supports, which does
not commute automatically with product-state convexification.

The unresolved constructive step is to use the displayed18-plus-six
cofactor structure to prove(6) for every normalized matching-moment PSD
y. The sharp cap34 separation shows that ordinary physical-profile
identification cannot substitute for this spectral step.
