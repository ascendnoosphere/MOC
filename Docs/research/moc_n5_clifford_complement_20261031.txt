Complementation for the dimension-five polar Clifford candidate
31 October 2026

This proves the universal complementary-pair step of the proposed n=5 Gram
identity. The remaining low-cardinality Gram identities are separate proof
obligations. No unrestricted MOC conclusion is asserted.

Let B be an invertible complex symmetric normal 5-by-5 matrix, C=bar(B),
and d=det(B). Let H identify the five grade-four Clifford coordinates
with ordinary coordinates by H[S,j]=(-1)^j for S={0,...,4}\{j}.
Define M_B=H B C^{-1} H^T.

Because B,C are symmetric and commute, M_B is unitary and

 M_(B^{-1})=H B^{-1}C H^T=M_B^{-1}=M_B^*.

For any row subset I define the untwisted even Clifford coefficient row

 v_B,I=sum_(|J|=|I|)det B[I,J] reverse(e_I)e_J.

In coordinate S=I symmetric_difference J its sign is

 sigma(I,J)=(-1)^(|I|(|I|-1)/2+# {(i,j) in I times J:i>j}).

For all equal-cardinality pairs I,J of subsets of {0,...,n-1},

 sigma(I,J)(-1)^(sum I+sum J)=sigma(I^c,J^c).           (1)

Indeed put k=|I|=|J| and N(I,J)=# {(i,j) in I times J:i>j}. Then

 N(I^c,J^c)=n(n-1)/2-sum(I)-(n-1)k+sum(J)+N(I,J).

The total exponent in the ratio of the two sides of (1) is therefore

 n(n-1)-k(2n-k-1),

which is even, since it equals n(n-1)-2nk+k(k+1).
The exact verifier also exhausts all 252 pairs for n=5. Jacobi's
complementary-minor identity and symmetry of B^{-1} give

                  v_B,I=d v_(B^{-1}),I^c              (2)

in every even Clifford coordinate, without an extra grade sign.

The candidate row w_B,I leaves grades 0 and 2 unchanged. In grade 4 it
uses bar(v_B,I,4) when |I|=2 and bar(v_B,I,4) M_B when |I|=3; all other
row cardinalities have zero grade-four part.

For grades 0 and 2, (2) gives

 w_B,I,low=d w_(B^{-1}),I^c,low.

For grade 4 and |I|=2 the complement has cardinality 3, so

 w_(B^{-1}),I^c,4=bar(v_(B^{-1}),I^c,4) M_B^{-1},
 w_B,I,4=bar(d) w_(B^{-1}),I^c,4 M_B.

For |I|=3 its complement has cardinality 2 and the same last formula
follows directly. Thus all 32 rows obey one common column transformation

 w_B,I=w_(B^{-1}),I^c D_B,
 D_B=diag(d I_11,bar(d) M_B),
 D_B D_B^*=|d|^2 I_16.                               (3)

Equivalently w_B,I=d w_(B^{-1}),I^c U_B with the common unitary
U_B=diag(I_11,(bar(d)/d)M_B).

It follows immediately that the candidate Gram entries satisfy

 <w_B,I,w_B,K>=|d|^2 <w_(B^{-1}),I^c,w_(B^{-1}),K^c>. (4)

The actual row-flag determinant has exactly the same complement rule.
Its row types are I,C,B,BC according as a row is in neither subset,
the first only, the second only, or both. Right multiplication by
(BC)^{-1} changes these types to (BC)^{-1},B^{-1},C^{-1},I, respectively,
which are the complementary row types for B^{-1},C^{-1}. The determinant
multiplier is det(BC)=|d|^2.

Consequently proving the Gram identity for |I|+|K|<=5 suffices for all
1024 ordered pairs when B is invertible. This includes empty-subset
cases and permutation/Hermitian transports of the low-cardinality cases.
Any eventual universal PSD/rank theorem then extends to singular B by
continuity of the original polynomial kernel; one need not assign a
unique polar phase to a zero eigenvalue.
