## Mathematical audit

### Actual spectra and support-coordinate mapping: correct

**Locations:** `organized/research/rational_sinkhorn_certificates.py:110-120`; `organized/research/SINKHORN_SOS_CERTIFICATE_ROUTE.md:138-158`.

For each permutation, the product has the form
\[
z_\pi(z)=f_\pi z+b_{\pi(4)}f_\pi,\qquad
f_\pi=\prod_{j=1}^{3}(a_j+b_{\pi(j)}).
\]
Consequently
\[
\Re(\eta z_\pi(z))
=\Re\!\left((\eta z)f_\pi+\eta b_{\pi(4)}f_\pi\right),
\]
exactly matching `cost` and `pc`.

The imaginary-coordinate signs are correct: coefficients are \(-\Im f_\pi\) and \(-\Im(b_{\pi(4)}f_\pi)\), because the support convention is \(\Re(\eta F)\), not \(\Re(\overline\eta F)\).

The proof actually certifies a linear extension to all \(\theta\in\mathbb R^4\). Some such coordinates do not represent finite \((z,\eta)\), notably \(\eta=0,\eta z\ne0\), but certifying a larger parameter space is harmless. Every required \((z,\eta)\) is included.

### Complete 68-facet fan: sufficient and correctly enumerated

**Locations:** `organized/research/rational_sinkhorn_certificates.py:123-141`; `organized/research/SINKHORN_SOS_CERTIFICATE_ROUTE.md:149-158`.

The exhaustive enumeration is mathematically complete:

1. Exact affine rank is four.
2. Every facet of a full-dimensional four-polytope contains four affinely independent vertices.
3. The alternating \(3\times3\) determinants produce a normal to their affine span.
4. Both orientations are tested against every vertex.
5. Primitive normalization and set deduplication identify repeated facets.

Thus the 68 normals are not merely sampled directions.

Every vertex normal cone is generated by its incident facet normals. Within that cone, the same vertex realizes the support maximum, so both the height and gap are linear in the direction. Nonnegative combinations of the incident ray certificates establish every direction in the cone. The cones cover \(\mathbb R^4\), including boundaries and tied maxima.

**The 68 certificates therefore suffice for all support directions and all \(z\).**

### Cauchy–Binet mapping: actual linked spectral costs, not independent-minor costs

**Locations:** `organized/research/rational_sinkhorn_certificates.py:100-118`; `organized/research/SINKHORN_SOS_CERTIFICATE_ROUTE.md:128-136`.

The model uses the correct expansion
\[
\det(A+WBW^*)=
\sum_{|I|=|J|}
\left(\prod_{i\notin I}a_i\right)
\left(\prod_{j\in J}b_j\right)
|\det W[I,J]|^2.
\]

The implementation correctly tests whether the variable spectral entry \(a_4=z\) belongs to the complementary row set. Its coefficient is then assigned to \(\eta z\); otherwise it is assigned to \(\eta\).

No step replaces these product-linked costs by arbitrary independently chosen assignment/minor slacks. The minors also remain those of one common \(W(T)\), not independently variable quantities.

### Wedge-two signed conjugation: correct

**Locations:** `organized/research/rational_sinkhorn_certificates.py:95-106`; `organized/research/SINKHORN_SOS_CERTIFICATE_ROUTE.md:95-126`.

In pair order \((12,13,23)\),
\[
\wedge^2V=\det(V)\,J\overline VJ.
\]
Since \(V=N_+/\delta_-\) and
\(\det V=\overline{\delta_-}/\delta_-\),
\[
\wedge^2V=\frac{J\overline{N_+}J}{\delta_-}.
\]

The conjugation, minus sign in \(J\), denominator, and block ordering are consistent with the code’s combination ordering. Using an adjoint instead of entrywise conjugation here would be wrong; the implementation does not make that mistake.

### Exact PSD and coefficient cancellation: valid for the supplied integers

**Locations:** `organized/research/rational_sinkhorn_certificates.py:19-32`, `organized/research/rational_sinkhorn_certificates.py:145-152`.

For symmetric integer \(L\), the unpivoted Bareiss pivots are the leading principal determinants. All are strictly positive, so Sylvester’s criterion gives \(L\succ0\). Each division is checked for exact divisibility in the executed run.

The actual Gram is
\[
X=ZLZ^T/\mathrm{den}\succeq0.
\]
Neither full rank of \(Z\) nor positive definiteness of the ambient \(20\times20\) Gram is necessary.

Crucially, verification substitutes the actual adjugate/determinant polynomials and checks every resulting monomial coefficient. It does **not** incorrectly demand equality of canonical Gram matrices, which may differ through polynomial relations among the coordinates.

Representative certificates contain dimensions 16, 13, and 14 respectively, with denominator 10,000 and positive leading minors: `organized/research/certificates/sinkhorn_spectral_family/ray_000.json:661-680`, `organized/research/certificates/sinkhorn_spectral_family/ray_012.json:508-524`, `organized/research/certificates/sinkhorn_spectral_family/ray_031.json:557-574`.

### Zero-free normalization and full-\(U(4)\) extension: sound

**Locations:** `organized/research/SINKHORN_GLOBAL_REDUCTION.md:19-43`, `organized/research/SINKHORN_GLOBAL_REDUCTION.md:75-93`.

For Hermitian \(T\),
\[
\det(I+T^2)=\prod_j(1+\lambda_j^2)>0.
\]
Thus no denominator sign ambiguity or excluded real-Hermitian locus occurs. No division by \(A+B\), a permutation product, or a spectral gap is used; repeated eigenvalues and singular sums cause no exception.

The external Sinkhorn theorem supplies diagonal unitaries \(L,R\) for **every** \(U\). Its stated conclusion is confirmed in the cached original, `organized/research/external/1408.5728v3.txt:943-1001`. Diagonal phase invariance then gives exactly the same determinant range on \(W=H\operatorname{diag}(1,V)H^T\).

Finally, Cayley matrices are dense in \(U(3)\). Although \(T\) may diverge near a matrix with eigenvalue \(-1\), one extends the **original continuous determinant inequality**, after dividing by the positive normalization—not the sextic at “infinite \(T\).” This closes the omitted Cayley locus.
