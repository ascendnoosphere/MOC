RMT and heat-flow visual atlas: what survives viewing the figures
31 October 2026

Four figures were generated from saved data and exact formulas. No fresh
random spectra or unitary samples were generated. Every figure was opened
with view_image. The first inspection found overlapping bar labels in figure
1, dense time ticks in figure 2, and an overlapping subtitle in figure 4;
these were corrected and the final files were inspected again.

The source inventory has 20,127 records. The heat panels use every one of the
5,006 small-complete n=4 replay rows: 4,167 noncontrols and 839 controls or
special-locus cases. Other producer records are not silently counted as heat
replays. The concentration panels use all 1,298 saved dense n>=32 replays
with the first 128 uniform permutations, plus the indicated saved local-block
test rows. Exact, numerical, and inconclusive quantities are labelled.

1. Failed heat monotonicity is a narrow phase-cancellation effect

The first figure retains the broad cloud, the small failing tail, controls,
and the effects of conditioning. The exact heat mean relative to its centroid
is H(t)=exp(-4t)X+exp(-6t)Y. Put A=|X|, B=|Y|, and let theta be their angle.
An initial outward motion occurs exactly when

  4A^2+6B^2+10AB cos(theta)<0.

If A and B are nonzero, this is possible only when

  1 < A/B < 3/2,
  theta > arccos(-2 sqrt(6)/5) = 168.463040967... degrees.

The curved red boundary is the exact equality
cos(theta)=-(2(A/B)^2+3)/(5(A/B)); its shaded region is the strict outward
region. The two upper panels show the same observations at different scales.
There are 4,849 rows with usable mode phases; 157 have a mode below the
stated numerical cutoff and are omitted only from that phase diagram. All
5,006 remain in the signed-weight figure. All 64 noncontrol initial-expansion
rows pass the phase cutoff and lie in the exact permitted region. There are
zero violations of its two necessary bounds.

This supplies an explanation, rather than just a visual association: almost
opposite slow and fast modes cancel initially, and their unequal decay rates
can release that cancellation. The exact dense rational example in figure 2
has X=-Y and begins precisely at the centroid; its heat mean then leaves.

Most rows do not exhibit this failure. Among 4,154 noncontrols passing the
basic nontrivial-hull guard, 64 initially move outward and 59 exceed their
initial radius by one percent at a sampled time. The corresponding counts
after the established affine covariance guard are 24 and 20 out of 3,442.
The largest growth factor falls from 11.342 to 2.596 after whitening.
The stronger conditioning removes much of the apparent effect but does not
remove the counterexamples. The different denominators are printed on the
bars; they are not estimates from identical retained populations.

The cumulative plot deliberately shows the mass at zero and the small tail,
with controls separate. Growth is sampled at 201 times in [0,1]; no claim is
made that these samples locate every true maximum. There is a numerical
floor around zero, and tiny growth there should not be read as a theorem.
The exact algebraic example carries the failure of universal monotonicity.

2. Two exact heat thresholds and a positive replacement law

The second figure plots the two proven finite-time certificates. The stronger
cone theorem begins at t*=0.107560451768694..., where r*=exp(-2t*) satisfies
2r*^3+3r*^2=3. Its restrictive capacity is (3-3r^2-2r^3)/2. The elementary
explicit weight formula becomes uniformly nonnegative at log(3)/4, about
0.274653, because its lower bound is (1-3r^2)/24.

The cone threshold is sharp for the stated inclusion of the 82-ray cost
cone into the ordinary-minor cone. It is not asserted sharp for positivity
on all actual unitary profiles. In both plots a negative certificate margin
means that particular sufficient argument fails, not that MOC fails.

The lower-left panel displays the exact departure
|H(t)|/|X|=exp(-4t)-exp(-6t) when X=-Y. It has maximum 4/27 at
t=log(3/2)/2. This is a failed contraction with fully exact input, without
a floating or anisotropic-hull explanation.

The lower-right panel shows why a particular negative weight is an
incomplete diagnostic. At r=4/5 and the dense rational obstruction U,
the canonical heat law has minimum -547/5043000. Another exact law on 14
permutations has only nonnegative weights and reproduces all 70 heat-evolved
compound marginals. The negative bar is too small to be prominent on the
full weight scale, so its exact value is annotated. The positive alternative
is supplied by the stronger cone result, not by deleting or clipping the
negative entry. The heat-time-zero gap remains open for unrestricted MOC.

3. Canonical negativity is strongly related to mixing and is not MOC failure

The third figure contains all 5,006 saved mixers, with no shape exclusions.
Each has a canonical weight below -1e-10. Their total negative mass ranges
from approximately 0.01804 to 5/6, with median 0.12816. Haar and near-identity
mixers form visibly different distributions. The negative mass decreases
as the nonmonomial score increases: the within-mixer Spearman correlations
are -0.89961 for 3,756 Haar rows and -0.9999992 for 1,248 near-permutation
rows. These are descriptive correlations, not evidence against MOC.

There is a local algebraic explanation for the almost straight near-identity
trace. At U=I the negative canonical entries are exactly the eight three-
cycles and six four-cycles. In the sign chamber retaining those labels,
direct permutation summation gives

  negative mass = -5/6 - S1_id/12 + S2_id/3.

Along U=exp(epsilon K), for fixed skew-Hermitian K, let
A=sum_{i<j}|K_ij|^2. Then S1_id=4-2A epsilon^2+O(epsilon^4),
S2_id=6-4A epsilon^2+O(epsilon^4), while the nonmonomial score divided
by 4 is A epsilon^2+O(epsilon^4). Thus the displayed tangent is

  negative mass = 5/6 - (7/6)(nonmonomial score/4) + higher terms.

For the 662 saved rows with score/4 between 1e-7 and .01, the ratio
(5/6-negative_mass)/(score/4) has median 1.166675, against the predicted
limit 7/6=1.166667. The maximum absolute tangent error is 5.52e-5 in that
window. This is a local expansion and replay check, not a global inequality.

The lower panels prevent a misleading reading of the correlation. Identity
has canonical negative mass 5/6 but an obvious positive point-mass law;
Walsh has canonical negative mass 1/6 and an independently proved positive
common law. The rational U has smaller canonical negative mass, about .14738,
but no spectrum-independent common law: its four-term mixed cost is
1/1681+1/1681+0-49/1681=-47/1681, although that cost is nonnegative at every
permutation. This last fact is an exact marginal obstruction. It is not an
MOC counterexample, since MOC permits weights depending on the spectra.

4. The all-dimensional theorem and the finite-dimensional data differ sharply

The fourth figure keeps the rigorous but weak bound visible. Its certified
good-block probability is p0=exp(-5)/200^16, about 1.02813e-39. At n=1000,
the sum of its three failure-bound terms is about 1.0876089; clipping at one
gives no nontrivial probability guarantee. The clipped bound is exactly one
for every integer dimension 4 through 1000. The dominant good-block term
reaches roughly one half only at n around 2.70e39. The last panel is explicitly
a theoretical dimension scale; there are no observations there.

The numerical dense data are much farther inside small selected hulls than
these constants can explain. Every one of the 1,298 first-128-permutation
replays has a positive optimized disk slack. The weakest disk radius is
5.156 times the determinant modulus, at the n=391 near-real record. Those
are floating sufficient certificates for the sampled matrices, not interval
proofs and not population failure probabilities.

The apparent straight lines in the logarithmic disk slack are quantitatively
different by family. Descriptive regressions on n>=128 give:

  family          rows    slope per dimension      R^2
  Gaussian         181         .19915              .99525
  clusters         179         .19514              .99570
  cancellation     181         .07199              .94028
  log radius       180         .08239              .91618
  near-real        180         .00935              .10869

Thus the Gaussian/cluster trend is visually strong in this selected sample,
while a linear trend explains little of the near-real variation. Neither
fit is asserted as an asymptotic theorem or a universal radial estimate.
The cluster law is also not the Gaussian law appearing in the rigorous bound.

The cheaper, theorem-inspired local four-index triangle test has substantial
inconclusive regions even when the more flexible 128-product disk succeeds.
For dense n>=256, its successes are 153/153 Gaussian, 153/153 clusters,
124/153 cancellation, 143/153 log radius, and only 30/153 near-real. The gray
parts of the bars are retained and labelled inconclusive. They are failures
of this certificate, not determinant values outside the MOC hull.

The figures therefore support a precise research distinction: generic large
matrices can be easy to certify numerically, explicit ensemble estimates can
still be useless at the sampled dimensions, and delicate worst-case n=4
support geometry is not settled by either observation. No full unrestricted
proof is claimed by this visual analysis.
