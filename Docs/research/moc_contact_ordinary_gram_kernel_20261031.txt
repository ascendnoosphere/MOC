1. A13-dimensional ordinary basis

Fix the contact permutation pi1=(0,1,3,2), lexicographic index1. Of the
34 ordinary0/1 rays, exactly27 vanish at that contact. They span the
13-dimensional space V_contact={f in V14:f(pi1)=0}. An explicit basis is

 B=[r7,r10,r13,r17,r18,r20,r22,r23,r27,r28,r30,r31,r1].

Write R for its24-by13 evaluation matrix. Its rank is13. The canonical
mixed ray has the particularly useful expression

 f34=r10+r22+r28+r31-r1.                              (1)

For a supported gap in the positive f34 cap with this contact, v0=0:
the canonical ordinary coefficient v0 is exactly g(pi1). Thus

 g=f34+sum_{j in cap ordinary minus0} v_j r_j,

has a diagonal Gram in basis B with exactly one negative diagonal:
coefficient-1 on r1; coefficient1+v_j on j=10,22,28,31; coefficientv_j
on the other eight basis rays. Affine Gram freedom must repair this
negative entry. An affine PSD choice uniform on all gaps is already
excluded by the independent no-affine-Gram obstruction.

2. Every Gram-kernel direction is a Boolean/orthogonality relation

Let x_j in Q13 be the coordinates of each of the27 contactzero ordinary
rays. For a symmetric13-by13 matrix S, the cost is

 g(pi)=R_pi S R_pi^T.

The quadratic evaluation map has rank23, so its kernel has dimension
91-23=68. This full kernel is generated by the following elementary
relations:

 (a) Q_j=x_j x_j^T-diag(x_j), for the27 ordinary rays;
 (b) Q_jk=(x_j x_k^T+x_k x_j^T)/2 whenever r_j r_k=0
     pointwise on all24 permutations.

Exactly14 of the(a) generators are nonzero and independent. There are
162 disjoint-support pairs in(b). Together they have rank68, verified
over the rationals. Every generator evaluates to zero at every point.
Therefore they span ALL possible corrections to the diagonal Gram,
not merely a restricted ansatz.

Stronger exact simplification: the162 orthogonality generators(b) ALONE
have rank68. All Boolean generators(a) are redundant combinations.
Thus the whole affine Gram freedom is a pure disjointness-graph space.
For example, writing Q_ab=sym(x_a x_b^T), the sparse star correction
in Section3 equals

 -Q_(1,13)+Q_(1,21)-Q_(1,30)+Q_(3,7)-Q_(7,26)-Q_(7,32).

Every one of these six pairs is disjoint. The displayed identity is
an equality of symmetric13-by13 matrices, not just of evaluated costs.

Permutation relabeling acts transitively on contacts and preserves the
34 ordinary rays. This construction and kernel presentation consequently
hold for every contact, after relabeling the basis. No spectrum or
orthogonal mixer was specialized.

This gives a fully explicit nonlinear completion problem in ordinary
indicator coordinates. It still requires a PSD choice of the free
Boolean/orthogonality coefficients; no chordality conclusion is assumed.
In fact neither natural full graph is chordal: the disjoint-support
graph has induced4-cycle (1,2,6,23), and the intersection graph has
induced4-cycle (1,4,2,7). A useful chordal ansatz must therefore select
or introduce further Gram freedom; the entire graph is not chordal.

3. A sparse one-parameter Schur certificate

One especially sparse exact quadratic identity is

 r1^2=r1(r10+r22+r28+r31)-r7 r20.                     (2)

Add tau times the zero polynomial obtained from(2) to the diagonal
Gram in Section1. The resulting matrix splits into a five-vertex star,
one independent two-vertex edge, and six nonnegative diagonal entries:

 star center diagonal: tau-1 on r1;
 four leaf diagonals: alpha_j=1+v_j, j=10,22,28,31;
 center-leaf entries: -tau/2;
 separate edge: entries tau/2 between r7,r20,
                diagonals v7,v20.

Thus it is PSD exactly when

 tau-1 >= (tau^2/4) sum_{j=10,22,28,31}1/(1+v_j),
 tau^2 <= 4 v7 v20,

with tau>=1 and all ordinary coefficients nonnegative. Let
k=sum_j1/(1+v_j). The first inequality is feasible iff k<=1; its smallest
allowed tau is2/(1+sqrt(1-k)). Hence this exact certificate exists iff

 k<=1 and sqrt(v7 v20)>=1/(1+sqrt(1-k)).              (3)

This is a nonlinear rational/Schur construction. It is a sufficient
family, not a claimed consequence of N9=0. Other ordinary completions
and combinations of the complete68-dimensional kernel remain available.

5. Rational weighted-projector section has no generic edge-domain gap

On a two-contact face let R be the22-by12 full-rank matching evaluation

verifier above, for contact pairs(0,1),(0,7),(0,3),(0,9).

For ANY positive remaining gaps g_i, set

 M=R^T diag(g_i^-2)R,
 H=R M^-1 R^T,
 B=H Hadamard H,
 mu=B^-1 g,
 C=M^-1 R^T diag(mu)R M^-1.                            (4)

The matrix M is PD. The matrix B is PD because it is the Gram matrix
of the independent outerproducts after invertible congruence. Thus(4)
is a globally defined RATIONAL matrix function on each generic edge
contact face. Its diagonal matching evaluations equal g exactly:

 diag(R C R^T)=B mu=g.

This is the p=-1 weighted-projector construction being developed by the
parent. Its only remaining certificate obligation is C>=0; domain,
inverses, and interpolation do not require a spectral hypothesis.
The same argument gives the one-contact case from the rank23 result
in Section2. This does not assert C>=0 for arbitrary nonnegative gaps.
