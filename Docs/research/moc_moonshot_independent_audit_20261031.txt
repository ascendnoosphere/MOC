Assertion 2. Exact polynomial identity on the full Hermitian chart: PASS.

Let T be a general Hermitian 3x3 matrix with nine independent real entries,
K=I-iT, delta=det(K), and N=(I+iT)adj(K). The independent verifier constructs

 B=H diag(delta,N) H^T,

where H is the real Walsh matrix divided by two. Thus B/delta is the
balanced Cayley unitary H diag(1,(I+iT)(I-iT)^(-1)) H^T.

For a singleton minor the numerator is an entry of B. For a two-by-two
minor the verifier forms the actual determinant of the corresponding
submatrix of B and divides it exactly by delta as a polynomial over Q(i).
All these divisions have zero remainder. It combines the thirteen needed
absolute numerator squares with their signed rational coefficients.

Independently form q=(coordinates(T),coordinates(adj(T)),det(T)). The
resulting actual-minor polynomial equals q^T X q exactly. The common
polynomial has degree six and 325 nonzero monomials. Equality as a polynomial
checks every coefficient, including absent monomials; this agrees with the
producer's zero residual across its larger 838-position ambient basis.
The computation retains all three imaginary off-diagonal coordinates.
It verifies the individual inequality, without reflection averaging.

Assertion 4. Extension to every complex U(4): PASS.

The primary source was checked directly:
 Martin Idel and Michael M. Wolf, Sinkhorn normal form for unitary matrices,
 arXiv:1408.5728v3, Section 2, Theorem 2.
 https://arxiv.org/html/1408.5728v3

Its quantifier is every U in U(n). It supplies diagonal unitaries on the
left and right that give row and column sums one, without genericity or
nonzero-entry hypotheses. This is an existence theorem. Convergence of a
numerical scaling algorithm is neither assumed nor needed.

Every squared absolute minor is unchanged by left and right diagonal
unitaries: its determinant acquires a product of unit-modulus row and
column factors. The same invariance holds for their signed sum L.
For the resulting balanced W and e=(1,1,1,1)^T/2, We=e. Unitarity gives
W*e=e, hence H^TWH=diag(1,V) for some V in U(3).

On the Hermitian Cayley chart,

 |det(I-iT)|^2 L(W)=q(T)^T X q(T)>=0.

The denominator is det(I+T^2)=product_k(1+t_k^2)>0 for real eigenvalues t_k.
This requires no inverse of a spectral sum, no nonzero minor, and no
invertibility of T. Only I-iT is inverted; it is always invertible.

If V has eigenvalue -1, keep an orthonormal eigenbasis and perturb its
eigenphases to avoid -1. The resulting unitaries converge to V and admit
Hermitian Cayley parameters. Extend the original continuous function L(W),
after dividing by the positive denominator, to the limit. Divergence of
some Cayley coordinates causes no problem. Phase invariance then proves
L(U)>=0 for every complex U(4). No reduction to real orthogonal matrices
is used.

When w(f_j)>=0 the assertion is immediate. The remaining tests reduce

Assertion A. Full determinant identity and parameter independence: PASS.

I recompiled det(Hg-hH1) over Q(a,b,p,q,r,h), using a five-dimensional final
Schur block instead of the producer's four-dimensional one. The result
has 2776 terms and degree three in h. Substitution and exact denominator
clearing give, coefficientwise in all five remaining variables,

 Q^3 det(Hg-(F/Q)H1)=-4 Q N^2,

Here A,B,C0 are exactly the producer's displayed polynomials. The fraction-

Assertion B. Insufficient-capacity cases are exhaustive: PASS.

If all budgets are at most one, either the balanced formula or the
dominant-budget formula applies, and the proof's cube estimates are valid
on the whole cube. If some budget exceeds one, capacity below one is
equivalent to that budget times the sum of the others being less than one.
Two budgets cannot exceed one in the insufficient region. Thus the cube
and the three named stars cover every possible counterexample.

In the cube, A,B<=0 and
 -C0/(r+1)=(1-r)(1-pq)+r(1-p)(1-q).
This vanishes only when at least two budgets equal one, already giving
capacity at least one. Hence C0<0 and D=abA+(a+b)B+C0<0 for a,b>=0.
In a p-star, delta=1-p(q+r)>0 and q,r<1. The three polynomial rewrites in
the producer's note are exact. In the B rewrite,
 pr(1-r)+q-2<0 follows from pr<1 and q<1; the remaining term is strictly
negative even when r=0. Thus A,B,C0<0. The q-star follows by symmetry.
These cases have r<=1, so F<=-1. Q=0 therefore cannot occur for an actual
gap, and nonzero D contradicts its singular Hessian through the identity.

Assertion C. Exceptional Q=0 in the r-star: PASS.

Write u=p+q, v=pq. Here r>1, ru<1 and v<1/4. If Q=0 then
a+b=Q0/(r-1), Q0=v+ru+1 in [1,9/4). The elementary bound
ab<=(a+b)^2/4 gives

 F<=Q0(Q0-4)/(4(r-1))-vr-1<0.

This contradicts F-hQ=0 regardless of the real support height h. Thus
the argument does not silently divide by zero.

Assertion D. Connectedness of the whole admissible D=0 branch: PASS.

On the r-star, A>0, B<0, C0<0. Define t=-B/A and k=t^2-C0/A>t^2.
Every nonnegative solution of D=0 has a>t, b>t and is uniquely

 a=t+s, b=t+k/s, s>0.

Conversely these values solve D=0 and are positive. The base is the
connected set r>1, P=rp>=0, R=rq>=0, P+R<1. Its product with s>0 maps
continuously onto the complete branch, including p=0 or q=0.
There is no other nonnegative component at aA+B=0: there the original
equation would require aB+C0=0, impossible since both terms are negative.

The proof also keeps Q positive on the whole branch. Indeed
a+b=2t+s+k/s>=2t+2sqrt(k)>4t. The strict bounds
 -4B>3r^2+5r-1,
 A0 Q0<(9/4)(1+2r), A0=1+2r-v-ru,
give
 -4B-A0 Q0>3(r-1)^2+(13/2)(r-1)+1/4>0.
Therefore 4t(r-1)>Q0 and Q>0 everywhere on the branch. These estimates
remain valid at p=q=0.
