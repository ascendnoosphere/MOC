01_moment_chamber_census.png

This accounts for all 4,167 noncontrol n=4 small-complete records in the raw
20,127-row file. The raw count is not the chamber-classified count: 183 fail
the hull-shape guard, and another 1,365 fail the Hessian-rank guard. The 2,619
retained cases contain 2,164 definite, 371 indefinite, and 84 near-zero moment
kernels. The figure also includes all 161 targeted perturbations through their
88 mixed and 73 ordinary chamber distributions.

The eligibility bars reveal a substantial selection effect. All 834 Gaussian
and 834 disk cases pass; only 330/833 clustered, 185/833 cancellation, and
436/833 anisotropic cases pass. The omitted rows are explicitly displayed and
are not classified as harmless. All 84 near-zero chamber values are anisotropic.
Their smallest retained nonzero Hessian eigenvalue has median 3.04e-7 and
maximum 4.85e-6, close to the 1e-7 rank cutoff. The apparent near-zero band
therefore needs conditioning checks before any geometric interpretation.

An additional weak pattern is now measured rather than omitted. The Spearman
correlation of the nonmonomial score, 4-sum_ij |U_ij|^4, with the normalized
moment-kernel determinant is 0.0037225 across retained records. Within-family
correlations are -0.0128 anisotropic, 0.0208 clustered, 0.0432 disk, -0.0288
Gaussian, and 0.0808 cancellation. This is almost no monotone relationship in
this selected dataset. It does not establish independence or rule out nonlinear
relationships. The chart's support direction is the hull edge nearest the
recorded determinant, so it also depends on that recorded unitary.

02_exact_indefinite_mixed.png

The old 88 mixed examples form a narrow positive cluster. The small exact
Gaussian-integer example lies well within the omitted negative chamber: its
numerically displayed orthonormal-basis descriptor is -0.4132147, while its
negative sign is established by exact rational arithmetic. Its weighted
capacity/mixed coefficient ratio is 8.268821. Thus it refutes the proposed
restriction to definite moment kernels while remaining covered by a valid
MOC support certificate. The picture makes the original sampling bias clear;
it does not show a MOC violation.

The second panel plots the two eigenvalues of its exact affine moment matrix.
The PSD interval has scaled endpoints 0.1067957563 and 1. Both rank-one
endpoints are PSD; at nonpositive parameter the determinant is negative.
Consequently there is no negative-semidefinite moment and no split-real
critical decomposition. The curves are numerical evaluations of exact rational
formulas, with the endpoint/sign claims taken from the exact certificate.

03_orbit51_geometry.png

The first panel plots the actual capacity formula at r=2. The second plots
the exact positive branch 5ab-7(a+b)-3=0 at p=q=0,r=2. Its anchor is
(a,b)=(2,17/3). The third compares computed eigenvalue locations for the
forbidden anchor, a neutral secant candidate, and an actual mixed example.
All three have exact rank/signature certificates, but the middle point is
labelled only as a neutral secant candidate: its finite spectral realization
is not asserted.

The branch's inertia is (5,7,2), whereas actual rank-12 spectral real parts
require (6,6,2). The established connectedness/constant-rank proof excludes
the entire branch, not just the plotted points. The figure illustrates the
already audited five-parameter closure; it supplies no new numerical proof
and no unrestricted MOC conclusion. No schematic surface was invented.

04_flat_local_obstruction.png

This deliberately preserves an uninformative result. The exact equality seed
has 11 active contacts; nine KKT multipliers are positive and the multipliers
at labels 12 and 20 vanish. Its restricted Lagrangian Hessian is the exact zero
8-by-8 matrix. Every zero is written into the image; no color scale or rounding
makes small numbers look like zero. There is no strict quadratic curvature
certificate at this seed. Higher-order behavior remains undetermined by this
calculation. A flat Hessian does not prove that the objective is constant.

Scope and reproducibility

The figures combine empirical descriptors with separately labelled exact
certificates. Normalized det(W) uses an orthonormal kernel basis; the exact
rational kernel basis in the proof certificate has a different determinant
magnitude, so the certificate's raw magnitude is not plotted on that axis.
Only its coordinate-invariant sign is imported as exact. The current atlas
adds one near-null empirical correlation and makes selection effects visible;
it does not establish a new general spectral theorem.
