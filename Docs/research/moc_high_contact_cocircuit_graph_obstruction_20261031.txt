For the15 five-budget families left at an earlier closure stage, take
the complete six-dimensional contact-null matching-feature space,
with evaluation matrix F=[f34,r_j1,...,r_j5]. The verifier checks that
these six features are independent and span the contact-null space.
Let K be the full kernel of quadratic evaluation on these features:
  K={Q=Q^T : F_i Q F_i^T=0 for every permutation i}.
Its exact dimension is5,6,or8. The artifact lists a complete rational
basis, each corresponding quadratic's exact factorization and rank,
and every evaluated feature vector.

QUESTION. Does some invertible real or complex change of these six
feature coordinates make the ENTIRE space K precisely the span of k
free off-diagonal products y_i*y_j? This is the ordinary free-edge
positive-semidefinite matrix-completion representation. The following
lemma reduces this question to a finite exact combinatorial test.

COCIRCUIT REPLACEMENT LEMMA.
For any full-column-rank finite evaluation matrix F over Q, if an
invertible linear feature basis has its entire quadratic kernel spanned
by a set E of off-diagonal products, there is such a basis whose every
coordinate form is a rational evaluation cocircuit normal: the normal
to a hyperplane spanned by n-1 independent evaluation rows.

Proof. Work over R or C. Fix one coordinate form ell_j. Let W_j be the
span of evaluation rows at which any neighboring coordinate form
ell_l, jl in E, is nonzero. Each required product ell_j*ell_l vanishes
on every row, so ell_j lies in W_j^perp. Choose an evaluation-row basis
of W_j and extend it to a full evaluation-row basis. The dual normals
to the added basis rows span W_j^perp; each vanishes on n-1 independent
evaluation rows and hence is an evaluation cocircuit normal.

The original ell_j is outside the span of the other n-1 coordinate
forms. Therefore some one of these dual cocircuit normals is outside
that span too. Replace ell_j by it. This preserves basis independence
and every required incident product relation; other edge relations are
unchanged. Distinct products of independent coordinate forms are
independent quadratics. Since |E|=dim K, the preserved products still
span the entire kernel, and no new edge relation is possible. Repeat
for all coordinate forms, preserving all previously selected cocircuit
coordinates. The resulting rational cocircuit basis has exactly E.
This argument works over C as well as R. End proof.

FINITE VERIFICATION.
The verifier enumerates every five-element subset of the distinct
projective evaluation rows. Whenever their rank is5 it records the
primitive rational normal. This includes every cocircuit normal.
Additional split-factor forms and coordinate units may be included;
their inclusion cannot invalidate a negative test. Two candidate forms
have a kernel edge exactly when their evaluation supports are disjoint.

For each possible number q<=6 of nonisolated graph vertices, it
enumerates every q-element subset of incident candidate forms with
exactly k induced edges. Such forms must be independent. Every such
candidate receives an exact rational rank test. Independent candidates
are extended to a six-form basis and the entire transformed kernel is
checked to be exactly all free off-diagonal entries. Isolated vertices
cause no omission: an independent q-form edge basis may be extended
arbitrarily; kernel dimension excludes extra edges. The artifact stores
all candidate forms and edges, counts of all exhausted subset sizes,
and every edge-count-qualified subset's exact rank.

RESULT.
Exactly one of the15 families has a free-edge representation:
  remaining budgets [13,17,18,20,28], dim K=6.
In the original feature order z=(f34,r13,r17,r18,r20,r28), one basis is
  y=(z5,z4,z3,z2,z1-z2-z3,z0-2z1+z2-z4).
Its six free edges are
  (0,4),(0,5),(1,2),(1,3),(1,4),(2,3).
The specified graph is nonchordal; the artifact lists four chordless
four-cycles and its full symbolic specified Gram matrix.

For the other14 families the finite test rejects every possible basis.
By the replacement lemma this rules out an arbitrary real or complex
invertible linear basis representing the ENTIRE quadratic kernel as
free edges. These are exact structural obstructions, not failed random
searches. Most have no edge-count-qualified subset at all; the two
other rejected cases have an explicitly listed dependent candidate.

SCOPE. This does not rule out PSD Grams, gauge sections with only part
of the kernel free, split products in redundant coordinates, additional
features, nonlinear Gram formulas, or other SOS constructions. Several
of these14 families already have independent uniform SOS proofs. In
particular a block-cap affine Gram section may have a useful completion
graph even when the entire six-feature gauge kernel has no graph basis.
