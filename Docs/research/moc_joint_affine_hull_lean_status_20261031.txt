Scope

Let I be any nonempty finite index type, u,v:I->C be arbitrary complex
cloud coordinates, and d,e be arbitrary complex candidate means. The
convex hull is taken over REAL scalars in C times C, hence in four real
dimensions. No spectral normalization or matrix identity is used.

For a real function a on I, finiteMax(a) is the exact finite supremum
Finset.univ.sup'. It is a maximum, not a numerical approximation and
not an extended-real supremum.

The file proves all of the following equivalent:

1. (d,e) belongs to conv_R{(u_i,v_i):i in I}.

2. For every complex mu,eta,
 Re(mu*d+eta*e)<=max_i Re(mu*u_i+eta*v_i).

3. For every complex x,eta,
 Re(eta*(d*x+e))<=max_i Re(eta*(u_i*x+v_i)),
and for every complex mu,
 Re(mu*d)<=max_i Re(mu*u_i).

4. There exist REAL weights w_i>=0 with sum_i w_i=1 and
 sum_i w_i*u_i=d, sum_i w_i*v_i=e.
The formal sums use the equivalent real scalar action on C.
The weights are on the ORIGINAL finite index set, including when two
cloud points coincide; no injectivity assumption is required.

It also proves a stronger equivalence:

5. The planar inequality in3 for all FINITE complex x already suffices;
the separate infinity/slope inequality follows automatically.

Proof ingredients checked in Lean

- finite_convex_hull_support_iff proves the real functional separation
  characterization for a finite hull in any real normed space. It uses
  mathlib's proved strict separation theorem for closed convex sets and
  proved compactness/closedness of a finite convex hull.

- continuous_functional_as_complex_pair explicitly represents every
  continuous REAL linear functional f on C^2 as Re(mu*z+eta*w), with
  mu=f(1,0)-i*f(i,0), eta=f(0,1)-i*f(0,i).
  Thus the duality does not accidentally test only complex-linear maps.

- joint_support_iff_planar_and_slope handles eta!=0 by x=mu/eta and
  eta=0 by the slope inequality. Complex distributivity is checked by
  ring normalization.

- finite_convex_hull_weights_iff obtains a finitely supported affine
  combination from hull membership and extends its weights by zero
  to the original finite index type. The converse uses the proved
  finite convex-combination membership theorem.

- joint_support_iff_common_weights specializes that result to the two
  complex components.

- slope_support_of_planar removes the separate infinity condition by
  an entirely finite algebraic argument. For fixed mu let
  A=Re(mu*d), B=Re(mu*e), M=max_i Re(mu*u_i), N=max_i Re(mu*v_i).
  The planar inequality at x=0 gives B<=N. If A>M, choose the positive
  real parameter t=(N-B+1)/(A-M). The planar inequality at x=t gives
  t*A+B<=t*M+N, while t*(A-M)=N-B+1, a contradiction.
  There is no unformalized limiting argument at infinity.

- common_weights_uniform_affine_identity proves that the SAME weights
  give sum_i w_i*(u_i*x+v_i)=d*x+e simultaneously for EVERY complex x.

What remains outside this module

For the proposed normalized MOC4 application, one intends to use
 u_pi=b_pi(0)*(1+b_pi(1)), v_pi=u_pi*b_pi(3),
and the actual determinant's corresponding slope/intercept means.
The common Mobius normalization to a=(0,1,infinity,x), the determinant
coefficient identification, the transfer between real orthogonal and
arbitrary complex unitary mixers, and degenerate spectral limits are
not formalized here.

Most importantly, the existence of the common nonnegative weights for
ALL actual b and mixers is NOT proved. The theorem proves the exact
equivalence between that joint-weight statement and the family of all
one-parameter planar support inequalities. It provides a verified
global reduction, not a completed unrestricted MOC proof.
