Closed auxiliary-square cone and its two-qubit presentation
31 October2026

Scope. This note characterizes an established universal sufficient cone.
It does not prove that every unrestricted MOC4 support cost belongs to it.
The needed universal positivity input is the independently proved
four-dimensional twisted-Clifford Gram identity. The finite-dimensional
closure result below additionally uses the genuine2x2 PPT separability
theorem, not a higher-dimensional PPT separability assertion.

Notation and convention

For beta(w)=(0,1,i,w), let F(w) have24 rows indexed by permutations and16
columns indexed by subsets I, with F(w)_{pi,I}=product beta(w)_{pi(I)}.
Write F(w)=F0+wF1, J=[F0 F1], j_pi=J_{pi,:}^T.
For consistency with the earlier exact jet verifier use
 W=range(conjugate(J)^T), g_pi=j_pi^T T conjugate(j_pi).
All statements can be globally conjugated to the physical convention
 W=range(J^T), g_pi=j_pi^*Tj_pi.

Let U be the real two-dimensional space of size-two subset coefficients
whose four vertex-incidence sums vanish. Let E=C^2 tensor U, dimension4.
The reduced operator cone is
 R={T supported on W:T>=0, PT_parameter(E^*TE)>=0}.
An orthonormal real basis of U is understood in the displayed compression.
The feature vectors (v01,v23)=(1,1),(v02,v13)=(-1,-1), others0,
and (v01,v23,v02,v13)=(1,1,1,1),(v03,v12)=(-2,-2), divided by2 andsqrt12,
provide one such basis. DimW=14 and E subset W.

Let S be the cone of finite sums of compressed product projectors
 P_W |alpha tensor f><alpha tensor f| P_W,
where alpha in C^2 and f in C^16. A rank-one term with alpha0!=0 is
precisely an auxiliary-w polynomial square, up to scale and the stated
global-conjugation convention. Alpha0=0 is the limit w=infinity.

Theorem1. R is the closure of S.

First, every compressed product has a product E component because
 P_E P_W(alpha tensor f)=alpha tensor P_U f.
Its E corner is a separable two-qubit rank-one operator. Hence S subset R,
and R is closed as the intersection of two inverse images of PSD cones.

For the converse, take T in R and split W=Eperp directsum E:
 T=[[A,C],[C^*,R0]].
The4x4 matrix R0 is PSD and PPT. By Horodecki--Horodecki--Horodecki,
Theorem3, it is a finite sum R0=sum r_k r_k^* with each r_k=alpha_k tensor v_k.
This theorem applies after normalization by trace; the zero matrix needs
no terms. A finite sum follows from compactness and finite-dimensional
Caratheodory, even with a definition of separability using limits.

Positivity of T gives C=C R0^+ R0 and A-C R0^+ C^*>=0. Thus
 T=sum psi_k psi_k^* + [[A-C R0^+C^*,0],[0,0]],
 psi_k=(C R0^+r_k,r_k).
Decompose the residual PSD operator into pure states. Every pure state
in this expression has a product E component, possibly zero.

It remains to approximate a vector psi in W with product E component by
compressions of product vectors. Fix alpha=(1,w) and consider
 A_alpha:f -> P_W(alpha tensor f).
Its image is contained in the12-dimensional space
 L_alpha={psi in W:P_E psi lies in alpha tensor U}.
The rank of A_alpha equals rank(F0+wF1). A specific12x12 minor is
 (-2+2i) w^3 (w-1)^3 (w-i)^5.
Its row indices are0,1,2,3,4,6,7,8,9,10,12,18 and column indices
0,1,2,3,5,6,7,8,9,11,12,13, with zero-based indexing. Thus imageA_alpha
equals L_alpha whenever w is distinct from0,1,i. For alpha=(0,1), the
rank is also12. These statements are checked by exact elimination in
moc_qft_ppt_reduced_cone.py; the displayed determinant itself is a
polynomial identity, proving generic rank rather than sampling it.

Consequently, if the product E component of psi uses a nonexceptional
alpha, psi has an exact product extension alpha tensor f. If its alpha
is exceptional, approximate alpha by nonexceptional alpha_n and replace
only the E component of psi by alpha_n tensor v. These vectors converge
to psi and each has an exact product extension. If its E component is
zero, any one fixed nonexceptional alpha works exactly. This proves
psi psi^* belongs to closureS, and hence T belongs to closureS.

Theorem2. The permutation-cost image C of R is closed. It is the common
closure of the all-auxiliary-square cost cone and the32-dimensional PPT
cost cone. Adding the finitely generated ordinary-ray cone preserves
closedness whenever each ordinary column has positive sum and entries
are nonnegative.

For T>=0 supported on W, all costs are nonnegative and
 sum_pi g_pi=tr(T B), B=sum_pi conjugate(j_pi) j_pi^T.
The frame B is positive definite on W, since the24 vectors span W.
Thus bounded costs bound trT and hence T in finite dimension. Any
convergent cost sequence in C has a convergent subsequence of witnessing
T's. Its limit lies in the closed cone R and witnesses the limiting
cost. This proves closedness. Theorem1 and continuity give the claimed
common closure, since separable products lie in the full PPT cone and
compression of every full PPT matrix lies in R.

With ordinary rays, the sum of all costs bounds both trT and every
nonnegative ordinary-ray coefficient, because every column has strictly
positive sum. The same subsequence argument applies. This bound also
prevents hidden unbounded variables in this reduced formulation.

Theorem3. The closed cone C is invariant under row labels, column labels,
and permutation inversion.

Row labels act unitarily on subset features and preserve U, so they
preserve the reduced cone. Column labels can be handled by Theorem1:
they permute the four auxiliary values. A common Mobius transform maps
any generic ordered triple to0,1,i; after multiplication by the product
of all four denominators, independent of pi, the full squarefree feature
span is unchanged by an invertible tensor-power linear map. Degenerate
or pole cases follow by limits and the closedness just proved.

Inversion has an exact independent finite-matrix verification in
moc_qft_ppt_inversion_symmetry.py. There is an invertible involution D
on W with D conjugate(j_pi)=conjugate(j_inverse(pi)); it preserves E and
its orthogonal complement. In the physical globally conjugated
convention its E matrix in the unnormalized basis above is
 D22=(A tensor B) SWAP,
 A=[[0,i],[(1-i)/2,(-1-i)/2]],
 B=[[1,1+i],[-i,0]].
Both local matrices are invertible. Local invertible congruences and
SWAP preserve the two-qubit PPT cone, so T->D^* T D preserves R and
inverts the cost index. The exact verifier checks the full24 frame
vectors, both invariant subspaces, and the tensor factorization, not
only the4x4 corner identity. This also follows from the intrinsic
S4 matrix-coefficient decomposition1 directsum Mat3([31]) directsum
Mat2([22]); inversion transposes each matrix factor.

Consequences and limits

Different auxiliary samples, column labelings, and inverse orientations
can improve numerical conditioning but do not enlarge the closed
reduced cone. Finite sampled cones can differ. A full32PPT solution is
convenient evidence for a reduced certificate, but the reduced14PSD+
4PPT certificate is already an exact universal positivity mechanism.
No finite32PPT extension is needed to justify it.

The unrestricted MOC4 proof still needs universal membership of the
actual spectral support costs in this cone or another argument.
Theorems1--3 characterize the certificate cone; they do not establish
that missing spectral statement.

Ordinary-ray corollary (degree2_symbolic observation).
Every ordinary indicator cost1_{pi(I)=K}, |I|=|K|, already belongs to C.
Choose the degenerate auxiliary spectrum beta_j=1_{j in K} and the
squarefree polynomial h(x)=product_{i in I}x_i product_{i outside I}(1-x_i).
Then h(beta_pi)=1_{pi(I)=K}, and its absolute square is the same indicator.
Arbitrary auxiliary spectra, including these Boolean degeneracies, lie
in C by the Mobius-normalization and closedness argument above.
Consequently adding any cone generated by these ordinary rays does not
enlarge C. The separate-ray closedness argument in Theorem2 is valid
without using this extra observation, but is unnecessary for these rays.
