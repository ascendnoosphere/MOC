Dimension-five Clifford transport and the containment base block
31 October 2026

This note supplements moc_n5_clifford_complement_20261031.txt. It supplies
the signed-permutation transport needed for the finite orbit reduction,
and a structured commutator certificate for I=(0,1), K=(0,1,2).

1. Equivariance under signed permutations

Let P be a signed permutation matrix in O(5), with underlying permutation
p. Let R=direct_sum_(k even) wedge^k(P), written as a row-coordinate
matrix: a Clifford row x transforms to xR under e_i -> sum_j P[j,i] e_j.
The matrix R is real orthogonal and block diagonal in grades 0,2,4.

For B'=P B P^T and I'=p(I), the untwisted row obeys

                       v_B',I'=v_B,I R.                 (1)

To see the signs directly, write T(e_I)=s_P(I)e_p(I), where s_P(I)
includes the basis signs and the sorting sign. The minor with sorted
rows p(I),p(J) acquires the factor s_P(I)s_P(J). These are precisely
the signs of T(reverse(e_I)e_J), proving (1). There is no residual
subset-dependent row phase.

Let R4 be the grade-four block and let H be the signed Hodge matrix
H[S,j]=(-1)^j for S={0,...,4}\{j}. The orientation formula is

                  R4 H=det(P) H P^T.                    (2)

Therefore for C=bar(B), M_B=H B C^{-1} H^T,

 M_B'=R4^{-1} M_B R4,
 R4 M_B'=M_B R4.                                       (3)

Because R is real, grade-four conjugation commutes with transport.
Equation (3) makes the corrected weight-three row transform by R4 too.
Thus every corrected row has the same transport law as (1):

                       w_B',p(I)=w_B,I R.               (4)

This proves the invariance of its Gram entries and justifies reducing
ordered subset pairs by their two cardinalities and intersection size.
The real signed-permutation subgroup of O(5) suffices; no assertion of
an equally simple row-index law for arbitrary orthogonal P is needed.

One can also prove (4) directly for the polynomial weight-three row.
For |I|=3 and I^c=(a,b), put

 r_C,I[i]=(-1)^(sum(I)+position_I(i)) det C[I\{i},I^c]

for i in I and zero otherwise. It obeys

 v_C,I,4 H=r_C,I C,
 r_(PCP^T),p(I)=det(P) r_C,I P^T.                       (5)

For invertible C, (5) follows from the first equality and (1),(2) by
cancelling C. Both sides are polynomial minors, so it holds for all C.
Consequently the corrected Hodge row r_C,I B transforms as
det(P)(r_C,I B)P^T, proving (4) in the polynomial formulation even for
independent, noncommuting symmetric B,C.

2. A structured certificate for the contained pair (01,012)

Write J=(0,1,2) and Q=BC-CB. For independent symmetric matrices B,C,
let Gram denote the candidate bilinear Gram expression obtained by
conjugating the first row through interchange of B and C.

The grade-four term of Gram is exactly

                    det C[01,34] det B[012,234].        (6)

Here is why the polar correction reduces to a single product. Put
m_ab=det B[01,ab] and consider the Hodge row

                 a=(0,0,-m_34,m_24,-m_23).

It annihilates B rows 0 and 1 by the elementary cross-product identity.
The corrected grade-four row for K has Hodge form r_C,K B. Since r_C,K
is supported on J, only its component at 2 contributes to a B r_C,K^T.
That component is -det C[01,34], while (Ba)_2=-det B[012,234]. Their
product is (6).

Expression (6) is also the top-grade term of the untwisted Clifford
pairing between v_C,01 and v_B,012. The lower grades were unchanged.
For the untwisted pairing, equal symmetric differences require the
column sets to be L and L union {2}, where L is a two-subset not
containing 2. Its Clifford sign is (-1)^(# {l in L:l>2}). Thus the
pairing is

 sum_(L subset {0,1,3,4}, |L|=2)
   (-1)^(# {l in L:l>2}) det C[01,L] det B[012,L union {2}].

Cauchy--Binet followed by expansion along the last row identifies this
sum as

              Gram=det(rows (CB)[0,J], (CB)[1,J], B[2,J]).  (7)

The actual row-flag determinant uses BC in its first two rows. Replacing
those rows one at a time gives the following compact exact certificate:

 G_(01,012)-Gram
   =det(rows Q[0,J],(BC)[1,J],B[2,J])
    +det(rows (CB)[0,J],Q[1,J],B[2,J]).                  (8)

Each determinant is linear in Q with coefficients of bidegree B^2 C.
The residual therefore vanishes whenever B and C commute. This proves
the entire containment orbit without an ideal-membership calculation.

In fact the certificate uses only three commutators. Set A=BC, so
CB=A^T and Q=A-A^T. Expanding (8), cancelling its B_22 coefficient,
and collecting yields

 G_(01,012)-Gram
   =Q_01(B_02 A_12-B_12 A_20)
    +Q_02(B_12 A_10-B_02 A_11)
    +Q_12(B_02 A_10-B_12 A_00).                        (9)
