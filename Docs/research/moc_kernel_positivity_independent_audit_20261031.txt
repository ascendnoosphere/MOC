Independent audit of the dimension-four fixed-spectrum kernel
31 October 2026

Scope

This audit concerns the fixed-b 16 by 16 signed product-feature kernel for
real orthogonal O. It does not establish the Marcus--de Oliveira support
inequalities. The universal positive-kernel proof is in
moc_fixed_b_clifford_kernel_20261031.txt, with its own exact nine-case
verifier. The independent calculations below support and check that proof;
finite examples and the numerical search are not offered as substitutes.

Proof review

I checked the nine-case reduction, the complementary-minor step, and the
orientation of the row determinant. No gap was found.

For B=O diag(b) O^T and C=conjugate(B), symmetry and BC=CB hold. Expanding
the selected row flags gives the signed permutation coefficient matrix:
the row types are I, C, B, BC according to membership in the two subsets.
Right multiplication by O^T cancels det O, including det O=-1.

For subset pairs with combined cardinality at most four, permutation of
indices and Hermitian exchange reduce the nonempty cases to nine types.
The top-degree conjugation commutes with index permutations because their
action on the pseudoscalar is the real sign of the permutation. Thus the
proposed Gram matrix has the needed permutation equivariance. Empty cases
retain only the scalar Clifford coordinate and are immediate.

The nine formal symmetric-matrix calculations have six identically zero
errors and three errors in the commutator ideal. These are genuine
polynomial identities before assuming commutation. They establish the
base cases under BC=CB.

For invertible B, Jacobi's complementary-minor identity transposes the
complementary minor; symmetry of B is what removes that transposition.
The checked Clifford sign identity then makes the pre-twist factor det B
independent of the row subset. After the twist the factors are det B in
degrees zero and two and conjugate(det B) in degree four. Every column
therefore contributes the same multiplier |det B|^2 to the Gram matrix.
The row determinant has exactly this complementary transformation after
right multiplication by (BC)^(-1). Complementation reduces all remaining
cases to combined cardinality below four. Polynomial continuity covers
singular B and repeated spectra. This proves rank at most eight and PSD
for every real orthogonal O and every complex b in dimension four.

Independent compact sum-of-squares check

The audit program does not import the Clifford signs, the Gram-factor
routine, or an orthogonal diagonalizer. Instead, start with an integer
real symmetric S and take T to be a real polynomial in S. Then
B=S+iT is complex symmetric normal. Let A=diag(a0,a1,a2,a3), C=A+B, and

 N=A conjugate(A)+A conjugate(B)+conjugate(A) B+B conjugate(B).

The eight compact amplitudes are

 det C;
 (ai-aj) adj(C)_ij, one for each i<j;
 Pf([A,conjugate(B)]).

Their Hermitian coefficient Gram matrix equals the row-flag determinant
kernel. Consequently the universal Clifford theorem gives the explicit
scalar identity

 det N = |det C|^2
         + sum_{i<j}|(ai-aj) adj(C)_ij|^2
         + |Pf([A,conjugate(B)])|^2.                    (1)

The program expands the eight amplitudes in all sixteen complementary
squarefree a-monomials, independently computes every row-flag determinant,
and checks all 256 coefficients. Results:

 * generic integer symmetric normal B: 256/256 exact, Gram rank eight;
 * singular integer symmetric normal B: 256/256 exact, Gram rank seven;
 * nonnormal negative control: 87 coefficients fail, as expected.

The negative control demonstrates that the normality hypothesis is doing
real work. The examples audit formula orientation and the singular case;
universality rests on the reviewed nine-case/complement argument.

One equality consequence

Suppose the ai are pairwise distinct and C=A+B is invertible. Equality in
the scalar variance bound det N >= |det C|^2 holds if and only if B is
diagonal. Indeed, (1) forces all off-diagonal entries of adj(C) to vanish;
invertibility makes C^(-1), and hence C and B, diagonal. The converse is
immediate. No conclusion about singular C follows from that argument.

Numerical falsification search

A separate optimization used six real Cayley coordinates for dense SO(4)
and the generic GL2-normalized spectrum (0,1,i,w). The eight real variables
were optimized for 600 differential-evolution iterations, totaling 48,080
objective evaluations. The objective was the smallest generalized
eigenvalue of the first eight principal features against the corresponding
absolute-weight Gram matrix. This avoids the eight structural zero modes
that flatten optimization of the full minimum eigenvalue.

No robust negative was found. The best objective was 2.5386652328929227e-10.
At that point the full normalized kernel had eight eigenvalues within
4e-17 of zero and eight positive eigenvalues from 0.00540336 to 0.40397643.
The small principal objective does not indicate a nearly negative full
kernel; its feature restriction is approaching alignment with the exact
nullspace. These numerical observations have been superseded by the
universal factorization, and no additional random search is needed for
this kernel theorem.
