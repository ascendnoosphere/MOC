Variational and exact 4x4 geometry atlas
31 October 2026

Four PNGs contain 17 panels, including deliberately flat and redundant plots.
They use the proved cross-spectral family

  a=(1,-1,-ir,ir), b=(-r,r,-i,i), r>=1,

and three previously verified rational complex Cayley examples at r=2,3,5.
This lane visualizes exact formulas and explicit examples; it does not claim
to summarize all 20,127 empirical records. The broader atlas has separate data
lanes. None of the frozen campaign files was modified.

The scripts enumerate all 24 permutation labels in every displayed hull.
There is no permutation subsampling. The three Möbius examples involve all
72 products before and after transformation. Local numerical checks use 50
positive step sizes; the kernel direction diagnostic uses 6,000 seeded unit
vectors in three real coordinates. These are plotting/diagnostic inputs, not
new unrestricted random tests of MOC.

1. Exact hull transition: 01_exact_hull_transition.png

The first three panels use identical axis scales after division by (r+1)^4.
The r=1.2 hull is visibly thin, while the r=3 and r=5 hulls are wider. All
three belong to the same proved class. The two right edges are outside the
ordinary-minor cone and use the weighted-minor inequality; the two left edges
have ordinary-minor certificates. Thinness alone therefore cannot measure
whether these spectra defeat the available proof.

The right vertex is X=max(4r^2,2r(r-1)^2). Its transition at r=2+sqrt(3)
changes which permutation values determine the boundary. At r=1.2 and r=3,
a right edge has six active permutation labels. It has eight at r=5 and ten
at the transition. In every case there are only TWO distinct contact points
on that edge. The 24 products occupy nine distinct locations in the three
ordinary examples and only six at the transition. The contact-count bar chart
is included to expose this degeneracy rather than to suggest a new hard
high-contact example.

Quantitative follow-up confirms a precise visible pattern. The full hull
height divided by its full width is

  H(r)=4r(r^2-1)/[(r+1)^4+X].

It has its exact maximum sqrt(3)/5=0.3464101615 at r=2+sqrt(3). This is a
geometric fact about this family, not a numerical fitted optimum. With
w=r+1/r, the derivative numerator on the first branch has the opposite sign
of w^3-16w-16. For 2<=w<=4, this is w(w^2-16)-16<0, so H increases. On the
second branch it has the opposite sign of w^3-8w-24. For w>=4, this is at
least 4*8-24>0, so H decreases. The kink and the permutation collisions
therefore occur at the same exact parameter.

2. Cubic escape and precision: 02_cubic_escape_and_roundoff.png

The exact coupled path has normalized support

  F(U(t))/d0=1+16 c s^3,
  c=cos(sqrt(3)t), s=sin(sqrt(3)t)/sqrt(3), d0=r(r^2-1).

A single coordinate-pair move instead has support cos(t)^2, and each of the
three real coordinate null directions is exactly flat. The comparison shows
why testing pair directions or one Hessian-null coordinate at a time misses
the ascent. The coupled path reaches a signed permutation, with normalized
support 2, at t=pi/(3 sqrt(3)). This is an exact path for every r>1, not a
trajectory fitted to an optimizer.

The log plot deliberately compares the exact change with a direct double-
precision 4x4 determinant computation at r=3. Only 31 of the 50 tested steps
have relative error at most ten percent. The smallest resolved step is about
3.73e-6 on this grid; below that, cancellation often dominates the true cubic
change. Two exactly zero floating-point differences are marked with downward
triangles at a stated plotting floor rather than silently treated as tiny
positive measurements. The fitted log slopes of the exact curves below .01
are 2.999994 and 1.999999, consistent with their already proved cubic and
quadratic orders. Those slopes are not independent evidence for the laws.

The shaded roundoff region is illustrative, not a certified error bound.
Its practical implication is specific: a tiny finite-difference support gain
at this saddle cannot be interpreted without step-size and precision checks.

3. Hessian and null slices: 03_hessian_kernel_and_null_slice.png

The full 12-coordinate Hessian, not just its six coordinate-pair curvatures,
has six negative eigenvalues and six zero eigenvalues. Three zero directions
are right-phase stabilizers. Three remain after removing phase freedom.
On those effective kernel coordinates the cubic term is exactly

  D^3 F/(96d0)=x1*x2*x3.

The x3=1 slice changes sign in four quadrants. The x3=0 panel is intentionally
blank: the cubic vanishes throughout that two-dimensional slice. This zero
panel is not a failed rendering and does not prove the full support is flat
on every such combined path. It shows that a whole coordinate slice can miss
the third-order mechanism. The individual coordinate-null flows are separately
known to be exactly flat.

Among 6,000 seeded random unit vectors in the three effective coordinates,
2,982 cubic values were positive and 3,018 negative; 5,913 had absolute value
above .001. The largest observed absolute normalized value was .19244437,
near the exact sphere maximum 1/(3 sqrt(3))=.19245009. These sample counts are
descriptive. The exact product formula, rather than the sign balance, proves
that a direction with three nonzero coordinates admits one sign of ascent.

During image inspection, the zero-mode explanation overlapped a bar. It was
moved into clear space. The null-slice caption was narrowed to the cubic term,
so it no longer suggests an unproved all-orders flatness statement.

4. Möbius covariance and a null overlay:
   04_mobius_covariance_and_null_overlay.png

The top pair shows an original hull and the hull after the nonaffine spectral
map f(z)=((1+i)z+2)/(z+7+2i), with a'=f(a), b'=-f(-b). The transformed
spectra produce matching products K*z and determinant K*d for one common
nonzero complex K. This is proved by exact rational identities in the source
certificate. The resulting hull is only rotated and scaled.

The lower-left panel divides the transformed values by K. They overlay the
original points for all three examples. This null overlay matters: generating
many Möbius transforms can make a gallery look more varied without adding new
planar hull geometry or independent evidence about unrestricted inputs.

The lower-right panel plots direct floating-point residuals for all 24 labels
in each example. Maximum relative residuals are 2.53e-16, 2.33e-16 and 1.59e-16
for r=2,3,5. The exact algebraic residual is zero. No trend is inferred from
spikes indexed by the arbitrary lexicographic permutation order. Lines joining
those spikes were removed during inspection to avoid suggesting continuity.
