An exact isospectral obstruction to a common polynomial weight

This concerns an auxiliary strengthening, not a counterexample to the
original Marcus-de Oliveira conjecture. The proposed strengthening
would require one probability vector w_pi, independent of t, such that

 det(t Id + diag(a)+U diag(b) U*)
    = sum_pi w_pi prod_i(t+a_i+b_{pi(i)})

as a polynomial in t. Drury's Hermitian version is stated in Theorem
0.3 of K. Rodtes, https://arxiv.org/html/2006.14846. Huang's survey
reports that the corresponding normal-matrix statement is false for
n>=4 and attributes it to Drury and Cload, "On the determinantal
conjecture of Marcus and de Oliveira", Linear Algebra Appl. 177
(1992), 105-109, https://doi.org/10.1016/0024-3795(92)90319-6.
The primary paper was identified, but its full counterexample was not
available to this session. The exact example below was found independently
and is not claimed to reproduce that paper's particular matrices.

Take identical complex spectra

 a=b=(-3+6i, -7+3i, 4+6i, 4+3i)

and the rational orthogonal matrix

 U=(1/42)[[-2,24,-28,20], [20,12,-14,-32],
           [-8,-30,-28,-4], [36,-12,0,18]].

Write a polynomial as t^4+c3 t^3+c2 t^2+c1 t+c0. The target
determinant and all 24 matching polynomials share c3 and leading
coefficient. On the six real coordinates

 (Re c2, Re c1, Re c0, Im c2, Im c1, Im c0),

the integer functional

 (-326197,-82678,667,-1000000,21037,4777)

strictly separates the target from all 24 matching polynomials; the
smallest target-minus-matching value is exactly 9247841/441>0. Thus
no one probability vector works for the full polynomial. The exact
unitarity, polynomial coefficients, and all 24 strict inequalities are
checked in code/research/verify_polynomial_normal_counterexample.py.

The example also demonstrates why adaptive weights matter. At t=0 and
t=1 the very same five positive weights work: with zero-based lexicographic
permutation indices (1,2,6,11,23), the weights are

 100218024/1036746557, 27980631911/1632875827275,
 2868667728/181430647475, 3718742120/9330719013,
 10005895156/21206179575.

They sum to one, and both scalar identities hold exactly. Nevertheless,
the same weights cannot be required to work at t=0,1,2: the nonleading
difference has degree at most two, so agreement at those three points
would give a polynomial identity. Equivalently, evaluation at t=0,1,2
has an explicit separating functional with real-part coefficients
(-76829,321682,-243519) and imaginary-part coefficients
(-1053557,2084148,-1021037); its minimum strict gap is 18495682/441.
The same verifier checks both the positive two-point weights and the
three-point separator. This leaves the original pointwise question
untouched: for each t, an admissible probability vector may depend on t.

An elementary positive family is U block diagonal with blocks of size at
most two, up to permutation matrices, for arbitrary complex spectra.
For a 2 by 2 block, its determinant polynomial is the convex combination
of the two matching polynomials with weights |U11|^2 and |U12|^2.
Multiplying independent block identities gives common weights as products
of these block weights. code/research/verify_common_weight_block_family.py
checks a non-Hermitian complex 4 by 4 example exactly. This special family
does not extend to arbitrary unitaries by the counterexample above.
