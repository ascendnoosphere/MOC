2. Exact rational Gram construction

Let B be a 24 by14 integer basis for the matching-feature space generated
by 1[pi(I)=J], |I|=|J| in{1,2}. The verifier constructs these columns and
retains the first14 independent columns in exact rref order. Let E be the
14 by13 rational nullspace basis of row9 of B. Define F=BE, so F has rank13
and its row9 is zero. Put d=g/max(g).

The certificate is a rational symmetric 13 by13 matrix G satisfying

 d_pi=F_pi G F_pi^T                           for every pi in S4.  (1)

Its construction begins with the positive numerical ellipsoid section at
power-1/2. If R consists of the23 noncontact rows of F and D is the diagonal
matrix of their normalized positive costs, write

 D^(-1/2) R=Q U,
 P=Q Q^T,
 lambda=(P Hadamard-squared)^(-1) 1,
 Z=Q^T diag(lambda) Q,
 G_initial=U^(-1) Z U^(-T).

The floating Z has minimum eigenvalue approximately0.613635596 and maximum
4.826344106. Its diagonal residual is approximately1.1e-14. These numbers
are construction diagnostics only.

To obtain the final rational G, round every upper-triangular entry of
G_initial to12 decimal places. Build the exact linear map from the91
symmetric entries of a 13 by13 matrix to its24 values in(1). Its rank is23;
the contact equation is identically zero. Choose its first23 pivot columns
and solve the exact residual equations in those columns. This changes no
entry by more than approximately1.94e-12. The resulting G satisfies all24
identities(1) exactly.

The exact verifier then computes

 G=L diag(p0,...,p12) L^T,

with rational unit lower-triangular L, checks the factorization entry by
entry, and verifies every rational pivot is strictly greater than1/10000.
Their approximate values are

Thus G is positive definite by exact rational inequalities. In the original

reproduces d. Multiplication by the positive rational max(g) reproduces g.
Equivalently, the columns of FL give an explicit sum-of-squares identity

All matrices and exact pivots are retained, so the identity is reviewable
