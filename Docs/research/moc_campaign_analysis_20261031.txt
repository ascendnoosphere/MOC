The clearest small-matrix pattern is the effect of affine conditioning.
In these n=4 Haar draws, Gaussian and disk spectra leave substantial raw inward clearance. Anisotropic and clustered spectra produce much smaller raw margins. Whitening the covariance of all 24 permutation products largely removes the anisotropic contrast. Invertible affine maps preserve hull membership, so this is a direct geometric control rather than a different conjecture.
The Gaussian/anisotropic median raw-margin ratio is about 23,000; the corresponding whitened-margin ratio is about 1.15. Much of the apparent near-real difficulty is hull flattening. Clustered spectra retain smaller whitened margins and therefore remain the more useful boundary-biased family in this experiment.
Whitened margins are descriptive diagnostics only; their calculation can amplify roundoff. The code requires covariance axis ratio > 1e-10 and keeps the original robust classification.

This subset inclusion is sufficient for full-hull membership of that instance; subset exclusion would be inconclusive. An exact obstruction found after the sampling campaign strengthens this limitation. For a=(1,-1,-3i,3i), b=(-3,3,-i,i), eta=i, and the dense Walsh matrix U, every coordinate SU(2) fiber is globally maximized at U. The full nontrivial Hessian has eigenvalues -96 six times and zero six times. Nevertheless an explicit coupled direction K gives F((cI+sK)U)=24+384cs^3 when c^2+3s^2=1, so F has cubic ascent and U is not a local maximum.
The determinant is strictly inside its permutation hull. This is an obstruction to the sufficiency of pairwise maximality plus a negative semidefinite Hessian, not a MOC counterexample. It is a separate exact construction and does not change the campaign's sample counts.

Consequences for the next attack:
Use affine-conditioned hull clearance to select difficult n=4 instances. Raw thin-hull margins and tiny pair sums were weak proxies for intrinsic proximity to violation. Clustered spectra remain tighter after conditioning. For nearly flat objectives, normalize by directional support width and require pair-rotation improvement checks before treating a small gradient as stationary.
A broad Gaussian sweep at large n becomes easier because generic mixing contracts the determinant while selected matching products retain a large radius. The exact second-moment identities in moc_campaign_theory_20261031.txt explain this sampling effect: for independent centered variance-one entries, Haar mixing gives E|det|^2=(n+2)(n+3)/6, versus E|z_pi|^2=2^n for one fixed matching product.
The same theory document gives E|det|^2=2^n(28n-4)/((n+1)(n+2)(n+3)) for one uniformly oriented complex Householder reflection. The comparison establishes that mixer distribution matters. The data's products of 2,4,8 reflections are a different family, so that one-reflection formula is not a fit to those records.
The useful follow-up is an adversarial search that keeps the determinant near a genuine supporting edge while varying spectral thickness and mixing. More independent generic samples would add coverage but would not resolve the remaining unrestricted four-dimensional claim.
