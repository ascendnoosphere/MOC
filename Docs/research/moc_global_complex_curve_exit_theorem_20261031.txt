GLOBAL COMPLEX-CURVE EXIT AND A CONVEX-CONE CONSEQUENCE
31 October 2026.

Provenance.  The curve-exit idea is present in Theorem 3.6 of Dongzhe
Zheng's October 2026 preprint, DOI 10.13140/RG.2.2.36790.25923.  The
argument below records our independent audit in an abstract form and
adds the separating-cone consequence.  It does not verify the preprint's
89 missing supplementary certificates or assert the full MOC theorem.

1. An abstract theorem.

Let X be an irreducible affine complex cone of dimension d in C^m.
Let p in C^m and let f_1,...,f_N be complex-linear forms that span the
dual space and satisfy f_i(p)=1.  Fix a reference index r and set
g_i=f_i-f_r.  Suppose that every affine line x+C p contained in X has
its real gap vector (Re g_i(x)) in a real subspace E.

Let ell be a real-linear functional on gap vectors, nonnegative on all
nonnegative vectors in E.  If some x in X satisfies

    Re g_i(x)>=0 for every i,       ell(Re g(x))<0,

then another such x has at least d zero real gaps.  No smoothness,
unramified parametrization, real-normal genericity, or Hessian-rank
condition is required.

Proof.
The sum of the real gaps is positive at a negative point; otherwise
every gap is zero.  Positive scaling normalizes that sum to one.
Suppose its exact contact set has k<=d-1 indices, including r.  Fix the
k-1 complex contact gaps and also fix the complex sum of all gaps.
Every irreducible component through x of the resulting algebraic set
has dimension at least d-k>=1.

If all gaps are constant on such a component, the component lies in
the affine line x+C p because the common kernel of all g_i is C p.
Its positive dimension and closedness make it the whole line.  The
assumption about E then contradicts ell<0.

Otherwise take an irreducible affine curve through x on which at least
one gap is nonconstant.  Normalize it and complete it to a connected
compact Riemann surface Sigma.  Only finitely many points S were added.
The noncontact gaps extend as meromorphic functions H_j.  Put h_j=Re H_j
and L=ell(h).  The chosen complex-sum constraint gives sum_j h_j=1.

Assume no point on the curve has L<0 and a new contact.  On the component
Omega of {all h_j>0, L<0} containing x, define v=-L, and define v=0
elsewhere on Sigma\S.  A finite boundary point of Omega must have L=0:
if L were negative, one of the h_j would vanish there, giving the
excluded new contact.  Harmonic pasting therefore makes v continuous
and subharmonic.  On Omega the h_j lie in the unit simplex, so v is
bounded by a constant depending only on ell.  Hence v has a removable
subharmonic singularity at every point of S.

A subharmonic function on compact connected Sigma is constant.  Its
positive value at x forces Omega=Sigma\S.  All h_j are consequently
positive and at most one everywhere off S.  The real part of a genuine
meromorphic pole is unbounded above and below on every punctured disc,
so none of the H_j has a pole.  Each H_j is then holomorphic on compact
Sigma and constant, contradicting the curve's choice.  There is a new
negative point with an additional contact.  Iteration terminates with
at least d contacts.

2. Application to the complete M8 source, not merely a real nonic.

Use the 14-dimensional matching source space, with f_i the 24
permutation-evaluation forms and p the constant profile.  The actual
complex source cone X=M8 has dimension six.  Its quadratic ideal is the
gradient ideal of the verified skew cubic F.  If x+C p lies in X, then

    grad F(x+t p)=grad F(x)+t H_F(p)x,

because grad F(p)=0.  Thus H_F(p)x=0.  The exact rank-four matrix
H_F(p) has kernel equal to the ten-dimensional entry-function space
E=[4]+[31].  This is the exceptional line space in the abstract theorem.

At a real orthogonal mixer O, its action on a nonnegative entry gap is
nonnegative by the doubly stochastic matrix (O_ij^2) and Birkhoff's
theorem.  Therefore a genuine negative MOC support value at any actual
source, including singular sources and rank-deficient real parts,
forces a negative actual source with at least six contacts.  This is
why the theorem avoids the previously unresolved five-contact Morse
and ramification issues.  Replacing X by the real nonic loses the
complex algebraic curve and is not an admissible substitution.

3. A global cone statement, independent of a particular mixer.

Let A be all nonnegative real gap vectors from complex sources, A6 the
ones with at least six contacts, and E+ the nonnegative entry vectors.
The abstract theorem, applied to every separating real functional,
implies

    A subset closure(cone(A6 union E+)).

Indeed, separation from the closed cone on the right would supply a
functional nonnegative on A6 and E+ but negative on A, contradicting
the theorem.  This converts the maximum principle into a global
positive convex-generation reduction rather than a generic local
stationarity assertion.

If every member of A6 has a full matching-SOS Gram, the whole of A does.
The matching-SOS cone is closed: for any PSD Gram G, the sum of its 24
evaluations is tr(G F^T F), which bounds tr G because F has full column
rank.  A convergent sequence of cost vectors therefore has bounded PSD
Grams and a convergent Gram subsequence.  Entry nonnegative costs are
ordinary, hence already matching-SOS.  This implication retains the
actual complex-source condition throughout.
