## 1. Finite complex spectra realize the entire matching-product cone

Let \(X\subset\mathbb C^{14}\) be the affine cone of the order-four matching-product variety. The relevant primary input is the invariant-theoretic description of eight points on a projective line: degree-one matching invariants generate the coordinate ring; its invariant cubic has the matching variety as its singular locus; and the cubic's Hessian divisor is twice its secant divisor. These statements, including the divisor multiplicity in Proposition 3.19, were checked in Howard–Millson–Snowden–Vakil, [*The geometry of eight points in projective space*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1103.5255).

The common-chart realization argument is sound. Every nonzero cone point has a representative by eight nonzero spinors. Choose a single second-coordinate linear form which is nonzero on all eight spinors; a finite set of forbidden projective directions cannot exhaust the available forms. Complete it to an \(SL_2(\mathbb C)\) change of basis. In the new basis, write

\[
p_i=\lambda_i(a_i,1),\qquad
p_{4+j}=\mu_j(-b_j,1),
\qquad \lambda_i\mu_j\ne0.
\]

Then

\[
[p_i,p_{4+j}]=\lambda_i\mu_j(a_i+b_j),
\]

and every bipartite matching product has the same nonzero prefactor

\[
\kappa=\prod_i\lambda_i\prod_j\mu_j.
\]

Absorb \(\kappa\) into the complex support multiplier. This realizes the point with finite complex spectra. The zero cone point is trivial. Rank-at-most-two cross-bracket matrices give the converse. Consequently the claim is an exact finite realization, rather than a dense-chart-only assertion.

There is one necessary real-form distinction: a real point of the invariant cone need not have a representation by real spinors. Accordingly, the split class below means a sum of two real invariant-cone points. The exact examples which are claimed to use real spectra do provide those spectra explicitly.

## 2. Independent reconstruction of the cubic and singlet direction

The checker reconstructs all 105 perfect-matching coordinates from the 70 squarefree bracket monomials. The selected 14 bipartite coordinates have exact rank 14. It then forms

\[
F=\frac16\sum_M\operatorname{sgn}(M)\,m_M^3
\]

directly, obtaining the same 49 nonzero cubic terms as the categorical certificate. Each of the 14 quadratic derivatives vanishes identically after substitution of eight arbitrary spinors. This is coefficient comparison in the spinor polynomial ring.

For the constant vector \(\mathbf1\), the reconstruction gives

\[
F(\mathbf1)=0,\qquad \nabla F(\mathbf1)=0,
\qquad Q=D_{\mathbf1}F=\det B,
\]

where \(B\) is the natural two-by-two singlet block. Its derivative in the constant direction is zero, so

\[
F(x+t\mathbf1)=F(x)+tQ(x).
\tag{1}
\]

The four singlet forms, the change-of-basis matrix, and its determinant \(-1\) are independently checked. This provides a reliable normalization for all subsequent Hessian determinants and signs.

## 3. The projected secant equation really has degree nine

In the checked coordinates \(x=T(t,a,B)\), with nine coordinates \(a\) and four entries of \(B\),

\[
F(T(t,a,B))=tQ(B)+C(a,B),\qquad Q(B)=\det B,
\tag{2}
\]

where \(C\) is the height-zero cubic. The qualification “height zero” matters: it is not an arbitrary representative \(F(t,a,B)\).

Let

\[
\det H_F(T(t,a,B))=h_0+h_1t+h_2t^2+h_3t^3.
\]

The degree is at most three because the height row of the Hessian must use an entry from the singlet block. The exact block determinant gives

\[
h_3=-2Q\det K,
\]

with \(K\) the nine-by-nine Hessian block in the \(a\) variables. Define

\[
N=Q^2h_0-CQh_1+C^2h_2+2C^3\det K.
\tag{3}
\]

It is a homogeneous degree-eighteen polynomial and, on \(Q\ne0\),

\[
N=Q^2\det H_F\bigl(T(-C/Q,a,B)\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

Projection from the constant point is an isomorphism between the cubic's open set \(Q\ne0\) and the corresponding open set in the thirteen-dimensional quotient. The primary Hessian-divisor theorem therefore says that on this open set, the divisor of \(N\) is twice one irreducible projected-secant divisor. Any extra polynomial factor could only be supported on \(Q=0\). Since the determinant of a generic two-by-two matrix is irreducible, the only such possible factor is \(Q\).

The exact witness with \(Q=0\) and

\[
N=-47,775,744=-6912^2\ne0
\]

rules out that factor. Hence

\[
\boxed{N=-4P_9^2}
\tag{5}
\]

after the stated real normalization, with \(P_9\) irreducible and homogeneous of degree nine. This also proves that the projected secant has principal ideal generated by \(P_9\).

The Zariski density claim for actual real-part spectral costs is sound: after writing spinors as \(u+iv\), complexification makes \(u+iv\) and \(u-iv\) independent spinor collections. Their sum is the general secant parametrization. Thus no lower-degree polynomial equation can hold on all projected actual costs. This is a statement about polynomial identities, not a statement that every real zero of \(P_9\) is an actual spectral cost.

## 4. Real forms: the sign in the quadratic equation is decisive

For a real cubic,

\[
\nabla F(c+id)=\nabla F(c)-\nabla F(d)+iH_F(c)d.
\tag{6}
\]

Together with the finite realization and the singular-locus theorem, this proves that a real vector \(c\) is the real part of one complex matching-product cone point exactly when

\[
\exists d\in\mathbb R^{14}:
\quad H_F(c)d=0,\qquad \nabla F(d)=\nabla F(c).
\tag{7}
\]

A sum of two real cone points is governed instead by

\[
H_F(c)d=0,\qquad \nabla F(d)=-\nabla F(c),
\tag{8}
\]

with endpoints \((c+d)/2\) and \((c-d)/2\). Arbitrary real points of the complex secant variety need not be of the split type (8).

For a class modulo constants, one must first retain the constant lift. If \(Q\ne0\), equation (1) supplies its unique cubic lift; if \(Q=0\) and \(F\ne0\), no lift exists; if both vanish, the height remains an existential variable. The categorical note makes these cases explicit.

The three real-form certificates were rebuilt from their finite spectral data. Each Hessian has rank 12, hence a two-dimensional kernel. The gradient-alignment equation on that kernel has exactly the two listed projective directions; a nonzero mixed coefficient ensures completeness. Their gradient scales are:

| Exact example | Two alignment scales | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Overlap | \(1,-1\) | Both classes |
| Conjugate only | \(1,239\) | (7) holds, (8) fails |
| Split only | \(-1,-337982862721\) | (8) holds, (7) fails |

Rescaling a real kernel vector multiplies its gradient by a nonnegative square, so these signs establish the claimed distinctions.

## 5. Common-facet normals have an exact spectral exclusion buffer

For the chosen nontrivial common facet \(f\), the checker obtains

\[
F(f)=Q(f)=-1,
\qquad \det H_F(f-\mathbf1)=-4.
\tag{9}
\]

The only cubic lift is therefore outside the secant variety. Row and column relabelings fix the constant direction and transport the conclusion to all 48 facets in that orbit. They cannot be actual spectral costs, even after nonzero real scaling or additive constants.

The exclusion is quantitatively stable in the specified thirteen-coordinate slice. Let \(c_0=f-\mathbf1\), and perturb its free coordinates by at most \(\epsilon\). The independently reconstructed coefficient estimates give

\[
\left\|-H_F(c_0)^{-1}
\bigl(Q(c)H_F(c)-F(c)H_F(\mathbf1)\bigr)-I\right\|_\infty
\le\frac{175}{2}\epsilon+\frac{879}{2}\epsilon^2
+\frac{5301}{2}\epsilon^3.
\tag{10}
\]

At \(\epsilon=1/100\), this is

\[
\frac{1843201}{2000000}<1.
\]

The simultaneous bound \(|Q(c)-Q(c_0)|\le543/10000<1\) keeps the unique lift defined. The Neumann criterion therefore proves that the entire closed box is outside the projected secant locus. This exact buffer applies to this normalization and coordinate slice; it is not an unspecified norm ball in every representation.


## 7. The higher Pfaffian and Lorentz statements have correct limits

The all-order polynomial

\[
P_{n,p}=\sum_M\operatorname{sgn}(M)m_M^p,
\qquad p\text{ odd},
\]

is a polarized Pfaffian of an alternating tensor Gram matrix. Its tensor dimension is \(2^p\), proving that the polynomial is identically zero if \(2n>2^p\). Complementary binary tensor basis vectors prove nonvanishing if \(2n\le2^p\). On a single actual spinor configuration, the relevant symmetric tensor space has dimension \(p+1\), giving a genuine product-cone relation when

\[
p+1<2n\le2^p.
\]

The corresponding derivative-rank argument gives the stated jet criterion

\[
(p-k+1)2^k<2n,
\]

and the two-configuration polarization gives a secant equation for \(p=2k-1\) when

\[
k(k+1)<2n\le2^{2k-1}.
\tag{12}
\]

All ranks used here are bounds on explicit alternating forms, so the arguments include singular and zero configurations. A concrete limitation is that **no value of \(k\) satisfies (12) for \(n=5\) or \(n=6\)**. For \(k=2\) the upper bound is eight; for \(k=3\) the strict lower bound is twelve; larger \(k\) only increase that lower bound. This family supplies no nonzero secant relation in those two dimensions.

The Lorentz identity is also correct:

\[
|[p_i,p_j]|^2=\frac12q(p_i)^T\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)q(p_j),
\]

with \(q(p)\) a future null vector. Thus this entrywise nonnegative Gram matrix has rank at most four. For \(W\succeq0\) with nonnegative entries, the bound

\[
0\le\langle W,G\rangle\le\frac12t^TWt
\]

follows directly from \(G=(tt^T-SS^T)/2\). These identities retain a coupled low-rank condition. Dropping it and keeping only the nonnegative coefficients in the cubic Gauss formula cannot prove a supporting sign: the signed matching generators span the whole space and have a strictly positive dependence, so their positive hull is the whole space. The categorical note states this limit correctly.


## 9. The reflection formula and the arbitrary-base correction

For \(R=I-2P\), \(\operatorname{rank}P=2\), the expansion of the determinant uses at most four modified labels because the Hadamard compression has rank at most four. Its reflection gap is

\[
g=4\sum_{i<j}\delta_{ij}|P_{ij}|^2
-16\sum_{|S|=4}Q_S.
\tag{15}
\]

For \(S=\{i,j,k,l\}\), define the three paired Plücker products \(w_1,w_2,w_3\), with \(w_1-w_2+w_3=0\). The exact four-label term can be written

\[
Q_S=\frac12\left[
(\alpha-\beta+\gamma)|w_1|^2
+(\beta-\alpha+\gamma)|w_2|^2
+(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)|w_3|^2\right],
\tag{16}
\]

where \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are the specified transposition and double-transposition deficit combinations. The averaging under interchange of the two spectra is necessary; reflection invariance supplies it. Conjugating the second spectrum would be wrong. The note includes the averaging and the lower-label cancellation, so (15)–(16) are accepted for complex spectra.

The prescribed-height Schur reduction also retains the correct base dependence. Let \(c=h-f(I)>0\), and let \(\mathcal A_0\) use deficits measured from \(f(I)\). The exact pencil has

\[
B_\mu=cW_4+4\mathcal A_0-\mu D_4,
\]

and the condition

\[
B_\mu\succeq0,\quad \ell\in\operatorname{range}B_\mu,
\quad g_h-\frac{\mu^2}{4c}\ge\ell^TB_\mu^+\ell.
\tag{17}
\]

If one rewrites it using deficits measured from \(h\), the extra term is

\[
c\left[2\sum_i(y_iTy_i^*)^2-\operatorname{tr}(T^2)\right].
\tag{18}
\]

It cannot be discarded. At \(c=0\), positivity forces \(\mu=0\), recovering the clean maximizing-base criterion \(g\ge\ell^T\mathcal A^+\ell/4\). An arbitrary reordering of the spectrum may change the unitary's relative rank, so one cannot force a maximizing base while retaining an arbitrary rank-two assumption.


## 11. A positive order-four theorem

The complementary-minor identity (25) of `review_derangement_stability.md` proves that every actual unitary profile lies in the affine hull of the permutation profiles. It justifies transferring an equality checked on all permutation vertices to all actual squared-minor profiles.

For each of the 26 mixed facets active at the order-four identity, the continuous certificate expresses its sum with its transposed facet as a nonnegative integer combination of off-diagonal squared minors. The independent checker generates all 48 facets, selects exactly those 26, and verifies every identity on all 24 permutations. Completeness of the facet catalog therefore puts

\[
\frac12\bigl(R(U)+R(U^T)\bigr)
\]

in the tangent cone of the common-minor polytope at the identity.

Two genuine positive consequences follow.

* Every Hermitian order-four unitary obeys every common-minor support inequality whose original identity pairing is maximizing.
* Every inversion-even common-minor cost whose original identity pairing is maximizing obeys its support inequality on every complex order-four unitary.

The first follows because \(U^T=\bar U\) for a Hermitian unitary, so the squared-minor profile is already symmetric. The second follows by evaluating an inversion-even cost on the symmetric profile. These statements are about the tangent cone, and do not assert that every symmetric profile lies in the whole common-minor polytope.

For isospectral costs the inversion symmetry is automatic, but the original identity need not maximize a given complex support direction. That hypothesis cannot be omitted.

## 12. Derangement Hessian rigidity and its closure extension

The derangement-zero cost space has dimension five and the table and inequalities reproduced in `review_derangement_stability.md`. On it,

\[
Q=(d-e)^2,
\qquad F=(a-b)[bc+e(c-d)]+bd(c-e).
\]

The exact Hessian factorization is

\[
\boxed{\det H_F(g+s\mathbf1)=4s^3Q(g)F(g)^3.}
\tag{22}
\]

The independent proof reconstructs three common kernel vectors of the five-variable Hessian pencil, forcing a factor \(s^3\), and the symbolic nine-by-nine identity \(\det K=-2F^3\). Section 3's degree bound then proves (22) for all complex \(s\), not merely for a grid of values.

The positive chamber factorizations give \(F\ge|d-e|^3\). If \(d\ne e\), the unique cubic lift has Hessian determinant \(-4F^6/Q^2\ne0\), so cannot be a secant. If \(d=e\), the deficit is a sum of four nonnegative diagonal assignment weights, and \(F=e_3(w)\). The cubic condition forces at most two weights to be nonzero.

For actual costs, this proof needs only the elementary necessary secant equations \(F=0\) and \(\det H_F=0\). Those follow directly from the cubic gradient identities at two cone points. No unproved sufficiency assertion for secant equations is used.

The reviewer adds a stronger closure statement. Substitute \(s=-F/Q\) into (22) and use (5). On \(Q\ne0\),

\[
N=-4F^6.
\]

Both sides are polynomials on the five-dimensional space, and \(Q\) is not identically zero there. Hence the identity extends across \(Q=0\), and the integral-domain property gives

\[
\boxed{P_9|_{\mathcal W}=\pm F^3.}
\tag{23}
\]

The six-cone classification thus holds for the full Zariski-closed projected secant, beyond the finite-lift hypothesis. This is the input that makes the quantitative theorem possible.

## 13. The two-weight spectral converse is finite and holds on every unitary

For real \(x,y\), put

\[
p=(1-ix,1+y,1,1),\qquad \kappa=i-1,
\]

and use the rank-two matrix with rows \(p,p+\kappa\mathbf1,\mathbf1,\mathbf1\). Expansion in its first two rows gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\det(C\circ U)}{\det U}
={}&x|U_{11}|^2+y|U_{22}|^2\\
&+i\left[1+y|U_{12}|^2-x|U_{21}|^2
-xy|\det U[12,12]|^2\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{24}
\]

The real part is the desired assignment deficit for every complex unitary. The checker verifies every polynomial coefficient in \(x,y\) for all 24 permutations; the complementary-minor identity proves the full-unitary identity.

The finite normal-spectral gauge uses \(\tau=2+i\):

\[
a'=\left(\frac1\tau,\frac1{\tau+\kappa},0,0\right),
\qquad b'_j=\frac1{p_j-\tau},
\]

\[
\Gamma=\tau(\tau+\kappa)\prod_j(p_j-\tau).
\]

The row and column factors satisfy \(C_{ij}=r_is_j(a'_i+b'_j)\). None of these denominators vanishes for real \(x,y\): the first has real part \(-1\), while the remaining \(p_j-\tau\) have imaginary part \(-1\). Thus every two-weight deficit has an actual finite complex spectral representative. Arbitrary additive constants are still handled only as cost classes, not as a claim that every chosen height shift preserves finite spectral representation.

The full54 certificate for every nonnegative four-weight assignment deficit is also valid. The anchored correction \(Y=w_4Pe_4e_4^*P\) is a quadratic completely positive map; the two compressed slacks are the nonnegative rank-one matrices with weights \(w_1,w_3\); and the scalar trace budget leaves \(w_2p_2\ge0\). Thus the solved spectral face has an explicit certificate within the proposed cone. This does not prove that the cone certifies all actual costs.

## 14. Isoclinic support has an exact scalar criterion and an exact common-law region

For \(J^2=-3I\), \(J\) the displayed signed real skew matrix, let

\[
U(r)=\frac{rI+J}{\sqrt{r^2+3}}.
\]

The signed determinant coefficients give, for a nonnegative permutation deficit,

\[
(h-f(U(r)))(r^2+3)^2
=Ar^4+Tr^2-Kr+D,
\tag{25}
\]

where \(A\) is the identity deficit, \(T\) the sum of six transposition deficits, \(D\) the sum of nine derangement deficits, and \(K\) the oriented three-cycle sum. The linear relations give

\[
T\ge2A,\quad |K|\le2T-4A,
\qquad D=T-3A+2E,
\]

with \(E\ge0\) the double-transposition sum.

For \(A,D>0\), define

\[
z=\frac{\sqrt{T^2+12AD}-T}{6A},\qquad H=T+2Az.
\]

Since \(D=3Az^2+Tz\), completing squares gives

\[
Ar^4+Tr^2-Kr+D
=A(r^2-z)^2+H\left(r-\frac K{2H}\right)^2
+zH-\frac{K^2}{4H}.
\tag{26}
\]

The sharp global criterion is therefore

\[
K^2\le4z(T+2Az)^2.
\tag{27}
\]

Necessity follows by minimizing \((Ar^4+Tr^2+D)/|r|\); the unique positive critical point has \(r^2=z\). The quadratic and zero-constant degeneracies stated in the dual note are correct. Formula (26) is an exact sum-of-squares certificate, not just a local second-derivative test.

The complete common-law region on this family is also established exactly. The reviewer restricts all 48 mixed facets and confirms that 46 have strictly positive numerators. The two remaining numerators are

\[
2r^4+6r^2-4r,
\qquad 2r^4+6r^2+4r.
\]

The basic facets are squared minors and already nonnegative. By the complete facet catalog,

\[
\boxed{R(U(r))\text{ has a common permutation law}
\iff r=0\text{ or }|r|\ge\rho,}
\tag{28}
\]

where \(\rho\) is the unique positive root of \(\rho^3+3\rho-2=0\). This agrees with the independently derived six-class \(A_4\) quotient and its six facets.

On the spectral boundary \(D=0\), all nine derangements maximize. The rigidity theorem then gives \(T=3A\) and \(K=0\), proving the bound on the whole family and, in fact, on all unitaries. A remaining actual-spectral violation on this family would have to occur in the interior regime \(A>0\), \(0<D<T\), \(0<|r|<\rho\), and violate (27). The universal actual-spectral inequality (27) is not proved.

## 15. New stability and the exact zero-gap reflection contact

The complete proofs are in `review_derangement_stability.md`. Their statements are:

* If \(0\le g_\pi\le1\), \(P_9(g)=0\), and \(\epsilon\) is the largest of the nine derangement deficits, then the distance to the six cones of at most two nonnegative assignment weights is at most \(C_*\epsilon^{1/9}\). The constant is explicitly specified by a fixed right inverse and a polynomial gradient bound; it is independent of \(g\).
* If \(M=\max g_\pi\) and \(\delta\) is the largest derangement deficit, then \(g(U)\ge-24C_*M^{8/9}\delta^{1/9}\) for every complex unitary. The signed determinant expansion supplies the uniform norm estimate.
* At the explicit real reflection \(R_0\) in that note, every identity-active facet satisfies the exact relative-derangement inequality \(\phi_\pi\le(81/4)\phi(R_0)\). The independent orbit enumeration finds exactly six zero-slack generators, of rank five, with exactly the required nine common-zero vertices.
* Consequently an actual identity-maximal spectral deficit with \(g(R_0)=0\) is globally a sum of at most two nonnegative entry squares in the zero positions of \(R_0\). Its entire first derivative at that reflection vanishes. The same argument gives the displayed Hölder bound in terms of a small positive reflection gap.

The new quantitative theorem uses (23) and the positive chamber structure. It does not assume a general error-bound theorem, smoothness of the secant, or a transversality statement at singular points. Its elementary proof includes the face boundary and vanishing weights.
