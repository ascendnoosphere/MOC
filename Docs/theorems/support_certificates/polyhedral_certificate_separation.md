## 1. The finite certificate cones

Write

\[
c_\pi=\operatorname{Re}\!\left(\eta\prod_{i=1}^4(a_i+b_{\pi(i)})\right),
\qquad h=\max_\pi c_\pi,\qquad g_\pi=h-c_\pi.
\]

The common squared-minor cost space has dimension 14, including constants.
Its permutation evaluation map is injective. Consequently a linear identity
between costs verified on all 24 permutations is also an identity of common
squared-minor functions on U(4).

The individual one-minor and two-minor nonnegativity inequalities have 34
distinct permutation evaluation vectors: 16 singleton rays and 18 two-minor
rays, after complement identities. The newly proved hard inequality

\[
2s_1+4s_2+s_+\ge2
\]

has 48 distinct rays under independent row and column relabeling. Its
primitive permutation values are 5 on the identity, 2 on transpositions,
1 on double transpositions, 0 on four-cycles, and 0 or 1 on the two oriented
three-cycle classes. Let C82 denote the cone generated by these 82 rays.

The additional proved border inequality

\[
1-Y-Z\ge0
\]

has another 48 distinct relabelings. Its primitive permutation values are
2 on the identity and transpositions, 0 on double transpositions, 1 on
four-cycles, and 0 or 4 on the oriented three-cycle classes. Adjoining those
rays gives C130. Odd relabeling includes the opposite orientation in each
orbit.

A decomposition of g as a nonnegative linear combination of these rays
would prove its support inequality. The question investigated here is
whether every actual spectral g admits such a decomposition.


Take

\[
a=\left(0,\frac12,1,\frac{15-3i}{26}\right),
\qquad
b=\left(\frac{-2+i}{3},\frac{-11+3i}{26},
\frac{-14-6i}{29},-\frac23\right),
\qquad
\eta=\frac{819-351i}{128}.
\]

Exact evaluation of the 24 products gives

\[
h=\frac3{64}.
\]
