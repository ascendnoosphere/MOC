# Order-four Marcus–de Oliveira cases A–CA

For ordered complex spectra `a,b` and a relative eigenbasis `W`, put
\[
 d=\det(D_a+WD_bW^*),\qquad z_\pi=\prod_i(a_i+b_{\pi(i)}).
\]
MOC asks whether \(d\) lies in the real convex hull of the permutation products. A probability distribution on permutations with expectation \(d\) certifies the inclusion. A strict violation in one real support direction would refute it.

**The block and monomial cases are proved. The unrestricted order-four inequality remains open in this work.** Several other requests amount to that entire inequality. For example, the opposite shifts \((A+tI,B-tI)\) preserve the sum and every vertex while imposing equal traces. Likewise every normal pair admits a relative eigenbasis with distinct eigenvalues. Neither hypothesis yields the missing bound.

## Status of the individual requests

Here \(K=\mathrm{Sym}^3(SU(2))\) in the binary-cubic weight basis. We take \(L_3\) and \(G_4\) to partition the **four distinct, nonconcyclic** spectral lists according as some triple is collinear or no triple is collinear. Statements that use a restriction on only one list say so explicitly. A generalized circle includes a line. “Regular” means simple eigenvalues of the relative unitary. Repeated eigenvalues of either normal matrix allow additional choices of eigenbasis. The proofs distinguish a prescribed basis from all bases of that pair.

| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A, B, C, G | Proved. In order four an invariant coordinate plane gives a \(2+2\) decomposition. A \(3+1\) decomposition also suffices. A monomial mixer realizes a single comparison product in every dimension. [Blocks](Blocks.md) |
| D | Equal traces alone are equivalent to full MOC in the same dimension. Even simultaneous equality of \(e_1\) and \(e_2\) gives an equivalent test by fractional linear covariance and closure. [Spectra](Spectra.md) |
| E | The assertion over every qualifying conjugacy class is equivalent to full order-four MOC. Central and rank-one phase-update classes have proofs in every dimension. [Conjugacy classes](Symplectic.md) |
| F | In order four the universal request already includes a single fixed ellipse, parabola or hyperbola whose test yields the unrestricted conjecture. In order five a single fixed parabola or hyperbola still suffices. [Conics](Conics.pdf) |
| H, L, T, AD, AL, AM | Proved on two open families for all spectra. The full fixed symplectic group, including its whole square-minus-identity locus, remains unresolved. A four-by-four positive semidefinite matrix test expresses the remaining support bound for the group. [Symplectic cases](Symplectic.md) |
| I, J, M, N, R, S | Proved for arbitrary spectra. The determinant factors exactly. The products of block weights cover both named spectral types. [Blocks](Blocks.md) |
| K, O | O is equivalent to full order-four MOC by density and regular rephasing. K already suffices if the collinear-triple restriction concerns one list. An explicit open family of partners and mixers works for a whole spectral pencil. [Regular cases](Regular.md) |
| P, Q, BC, BR | For simple spectra, left and right monomial gauges characterize precisely the spin-representable eigenbases. Diagonals alone characterize rephasing for fixed orderings. A finite membership test and the centralizer correction at repeated eigenvalues are given. [Gauge proof](Symplectic.md) |
| U, V, AP, AQ | Every collinear triple can be made real by a similarity, so AQ is empty. For one specified list a Möbius transformation puts any nonconcyclic simple quadruple in the collinear-triple chart. A nonreal cross-ratio parametrizes the fourth point but still leaves the support bound. [Normalizations](Spectra.md) |
| W, X, AR | For every prescribed simple spectrum in these three families an open set of partners works for all mixers, including both spectra in \(G_4\) at cross-ratio \(i\). Their full universal assertions remain unresolved. [Cross-ratio certificate](Spectra.md) |
| Y, Z, AA, AS | The non-unit-modulus nonreal values, nonreal algebraic values, transcendental values and complement of the orbit of \(i\) are each dense. Each universal assertion therefore suffices for full order four. [Density](Spectra.md) |
| AB, AC, AN, AO | For a bisector of a chosen pair, AB is an available projective normalization. Equidistance from all three distinct collinear points is impossible. Under the circumcircle-of-the-other-three interpretation, AN also gives representatives of generic pairs. Avoiding all pair bisectors or circumcenter equalities is dense in the respective charts. A full inequality on those families is still required. [Spectral geometry](Spectra.md) |
| AE, AF, AG, BT | A regular unitary has a maximal torus as centralizer. Every singular value of every unitary is one, so AF is empty and AG covers all regular mixers. [Regularity](Regular.md) |
| AH, AI, AJ, AK, BE, BF, BZ | Block restrictions preserve normality and admit the product law including all multiplicities and determinant phases. Every complex \(1\times1\) matrix is scalar, so AI is empty. [Block proof](Blocks.md) |
| AT, AU, AV, AW, AX | The spin and coordinate-block families with their allowed gauges are closed proper subsets. Approximating arbitrary regular mixers from those subsets cannot prove inclusion. The remaining direct inequality is reduced to 24 explicit strict polynomial inequalities whose simultaneous infeasibility would prove MOC. Boundary checks alone supply no such proof. [Perturbation and algebraic tests](Regular.md) |
| AY, AZ, CA | The proposed reduction to O and the implication from O to arbitrary normal pairs are proved. CA also suffices for full order four. A strict violation would persist off the spin orbit on a dense generic stratum. The universal assertions O and CA remain open. [Reduction](Regular.md) |
| BA, BB, BD | The spectral split is exhaustive on its stated simple nonconcyclic domain. It omits repeated and generalized-circle spectra if used for all pairs. The proposed unitary union is not disjoint, since spin elements can be regular. The named block and symplectic subgroups omit the connected maximal subgroup \(SO(4)\). The unspecified set of “other” subgroups would require a definition to characterize its union. [Spectra](Spectra.md) · [groups](Regular.md) |
| BG, BH | Simultaneously changing coordinates of a pair preserves the determinant. Conjugating the mixer alone can change it. An explicit regular perturbation loses supporting equality even for two generic spectra. [Symplectic conjugation](Symplectic.md) · [equality example](Regular.md) |
| BI, BJ, BX | Each ordered parameter has a six-element relabeling list, with possible coincidences. The orbit of \(i\) and its complement partition the domain. The other proposed tests overlap, for example at \(i\). Collisions can make a cross-ratio undefined. [Parameter domains](Spectra.md) |
| BK, BL, BM, BQ, BV, BW | Weak support inequalities pass to finite limits. Generic spectra approximate line, circle and collision configurations. Regular mixers approximate block and spin elements. The complement of \(K\) in \(Sp(2)\) approaches \(K\). These are directional implications from a dense family to its closure. [Spectra](Spectra.md) · [regular mixers](Regular.md) · [symplectic limits](Symplectic.md) |
| BN, BO, BP | A specified actual tuple has the normal realization \(D_a,WD_bW^*\). Explicit supporting equalities occur in O, K and line-or-circle cases. Realizability of a formal candidate requires the defining equations of that candidate. [Examples](Regular.md) |
| BS, BU, BY | A repeated eigenvalue leaves at most three distinct points in that order-four spectrum and hence invokes the generalized-circle theorem. A single zero can always be introduced by opposite shifts. A zero on both sides invokes the order-four rank-sum theorem. Symplectic multiplicities occur in quaternionic pairs and admit the stated regular approximations. [Degeneracies](Regular.md) · [symplectic eigenangles](Symplectic.md) |

The unresolved assertion can also be posed as real convex feasibility. After projective normalization of three eigenvalues, write the determinant and the 24 vertices as affine functions of the fourth eigenvalue. For **every** partner and relative rotation, the coefficient pair of the determinant must belong to the convex hull of the 24 coefficient pairs in four real dimensions. The pencil certificate above solves this feasibility problem on an open family. The universal quantifier is the missing step.
