# Simultaneous laws for centrally symmetric spectral pairs

Consider the sum convention. Let the first spectrum be \((u,-u,v,-v)\) and the second \((p,-p,q,-q)\). Fix the relative unitary \(W\). One sufficient argument would find a single probability law on the 24 permutations that works at once for every \(u,v,p,q\). Coefficients of the two homogeneous determinant polynomials can be compared directly. This checkpoint records the projection and the results of testing this stronger requirement.

For each permutation form the coefficient vector of
\[
 (a_1+b_{\pi(1)})(a_2+b_{\pi(2)})(a_3+b_{\pi(3)})(a_4+b_{\pi(4)}).
\]
The corresponding coefficient vector for the normal pair is computed from
\[
 \det(D_a+WD_bW^*)=\sum_{|I|=|J|}\left|\det W[I,J]\right|^2a_{I^c} b_J.
\]
A simultaneous law exists exactly when the latter vector lies in the real convex hull of the former. The following ranks come from rational row reduction of the monomial coefficients, including the constant probability constraint. Repeated vertices are counted once.

| Constraint on the second spectrum | Distinct coefficient vectors | Affine dimension |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Independent \(p,q\) | 16 | 9 |
| \(p=tu,\ q=tv\), free \(t\) | 13 | 6 |
| \(p=u,\ q=v\) | 7 | 3 |

For independent pairs, a numerical run with NumPy seed 6542 and 500 real orthogonal matrices generated by QR of independent standard normal \(4\times4\) matrices found a coefficient vector outside the joint hull. The largest computed outward facet displacement was about 0.059 in the coordinates chosen by pivoting the monomials in lexicographic order. This is evidence against requiring the same weights for all paired spectra. Each individual pair is allowed its own weights in MOC.

For proportional pairs, numerical facet enumeration gave 23 facets. Nineteen facet costs admitted nonnegative decompositions into singleton or two-set permutation indicators. The analogous linear programs for the other four did not find such decompositions. In 500 samples the maximum signed displacement from this hull was below zero. These observations leave the proportional case unresolved.

For identical pairs, numerical enumeration gave seven facets and all seven costs admitted nonnegative decompositions into singleton and two-set indicators. This suggests a single law for all \(u,v\) at each fixed \(W\). The facet enumeration and decompositions here were performed with floating-point convex hull and LP routines; an exact replay would be required to certify this strengthening. Ordinary pointwise MOC in this last row already follows from Section 3 of the [report](../MOC_progress.pdf). Indeed, opposite shifts by a shared value of \(A\) and \(-B\) reduce both ranks to at most three, giving rank sum at most six in dimension four.

The computations used the 13 squared-minor coordinates listed in the [boundary certificate](../../data/cone82.json). To reproduce the map to spectral coefficients, augment its permutation profile matrix by a column of ones and solve for each spectral coefficient column on the 24 permutations. These linear expressions extend to unitary profiles by the signed permutation expansion in Section 5 of the report. The rank figures concern exact coefficient interpolation. The floating-point hull observations are exploratory.
