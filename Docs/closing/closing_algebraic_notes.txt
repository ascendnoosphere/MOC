COMPLEX-CURVE PROPAGATION FOR ACTUAL SPECTRAL MOC SUPPORT COUNTEREXAMPLES
2026-10-17. Constructive research notes. This is not a proof of unrestricted MOC.

Main new result of this round (Sections7-8). For any fixed unitary U and
n>=4, a strict MOC violation, if one exists, can be moved to spectra with
at least 2n-2 active permutation labels, keeping the same U. Any prescribed
dense Zariski-open condition on the actual spectral invariant vector may
be preserved. The exact positive support gap can be kept unchanged.
No global minimum or bounded spectral parameters are assumed. The simplest
proof uses meromorphic functions on a compactified algebraic curve and
connectedness after deletion of finitely many exceptional points.

The argument was first established for the four-site single-cycle
capacity and its stronger complementary-pair bound (Sections1-4). That
version likewise reduces a violation to at least six active labels.
The terminal high-contact configurations still need an independent
positivity theorem; these notes do not settle them.

The harmonic observation was developed by the variational agent; the
bounded-subharmonic compactification/gluing argument and the elementary
quartet obstruction to invisible curves were developed here and audited
with that agent. The capacity upgrade is due to the variational agent.

1. DEFINITIONS AND EXACT SIGN CONVENTIONS.

Let a,b be four complex spectra. For pi in S4 put
  z_pi = product_(i=0..3) (a_i+b_pi(i)).
Use P={(0,1),(2,3)}, Q={(0,2),(1,3)}. For a bit string
s=(s0,s1,s2,s3), let p_s perform the first two optional P-swaps and q_s
the last two optional Q-swaps, and put pi_s=q_s o p_s. Let
chi_s=(-1)^(s0+s1+s2+s3). The exact alternating identity is
  sum_s chi_s z_pi_s = 2 Gamma,
  Gamma=(a1-a0)(a3-a2)(b0-b2)(b1-b3).
Thus the normalized top Walsh coefficient is Gamma/8.

For fixed eta in C, put h=max_pi Re(eta z_pi) and
  delta_pi=h-Re(eta z_pi)>=0.
The target is T=2|Re(eta Gamma)|=|sum_s chi_s delta_pi_s|.
If T=0 there is nothing to prove. In the rest of the argument T>0,
so Gamma is nonzero. The exact all-bias single-cycle support condition is
  C(delta) >= T,
  C(delta)=inf_(x in R4) sum_s delta_pi_s exp(<2s-1,x>).
This equivalence, including coherent phase maximization, is proved in
residual_variational_notes.txt; it does not assert the condition is true.

The stronger complementary-pair quantity is
  B(delta)=2 sum_(s=0..7) sqrt(delta_pi_s delta_pi_(15-s)).
Pairwise AM-GM gives C>=B. Both C and B are nonnegative, continuous,
concave functions of the nonnegative deficit vector.

For C, concavity follows because it is the infimum of positive linear
forms. Upper semicontinuity follows from the same formula. If delta^(j)
converges to delta>=0, then for each epsilon>0, eventually
delta^(j)>=(1-epsilon)delta componentwise. Monotonicity and homogeneity
give liminf C(delta^(j))>=C(delta). Hence C is continuous, including
points with zero deficits and capacities whose infimum is not attained.

2. THE OMITTED QUARTET AND THE COMMON-TRANSLATION OBSTRUCTION.

Use these four permutations in zero-based notation:
  u00=(0,2,1,3), u01=(0,2,3,1),
  u10=(2,0,1,3), u11=(2,0,3,1).
Writing Cij=a_i+b_j, define
  x0=C00 C12, x1=C02 C10,
  y0=C21 C33, y1=C23 C31.
Then z_uij=xi yj. Therefore
  z_u00 z_u11-z_u01 z_u10=0,                         (Q1)
  z_u00-z_u01-z_u10+z_u11=Gamma.                    (Q2)
The second equality is the product of two 2-by-2 additive determinants.

If all 24 coordinates are shifted by a common complex c, the left side
of (Q1) becomes c Gamma. Consequently, on the actual spectral invariant
variety with Gamma nonzero, there is no nonconstant curve on which all
permutation differences z_pi-z_pi0 are constant. This is elementary;
no Hessian or nonic elimination is needed for this particular fact.

3. AN ABSTRACT ALGEBRAIC-CURVE MAXIMUM PRINCIPLE.

Let X be an irreducible affine algebraic curve over C, let z_1,...,z_N
be regular coordinate functions on X, and choose a reference label 0.
Fix eta. Some differences z_j-z_0 are constant with zero real part after
multiplication by eta. Let S be those labels, including 0. Put
  d_j=Re(eta(z_0-z_j)).
Suppose p in X has d_j(p)>0 for j not in S and d_j=0 for j in S.
Let Psi be a nonnegative continuous concave function on R^N_+ and let
T>0 be constant on X. On the feasible locus define
  u=T-Psi(d).

Claim. If some d_j is nonconstant and u(p)>0, then some FINITE point q
in X has d_j(q)>=0 for every j, has a NEW zero d_j(q)=0 for j not in S,
and satisfies u(q)>0.

Proof. Normalize X and compactify the normalization to a compact connected
Riemann surface Sigma. The missing set E is finite. Every z_j becomes a
meromorphic function on Sigma, holomorphic on Sigma\E. Pick a point over p.
Let Omega be its connected component in the locus d_j>0 for j not in S.
Deficits in S vanish identically. On Omega all deficits are harmonic.

Concavity and the mean-value property imply that Psi(d) is superharmonic:
  Psi(d(center))=Psi(mean d)>=mean Psi(d).
Thus u is subharmonic on Omega, and u<=T everywhere on Omega. This includes
Psi=C: alternatively, inf_x mean L_x(d)>=mean inf_x L_x(d) proves it
directly. No differentiability of Psi is needed.

Suppose the conclusion is false. At every boundary point of Omega in
Sigma\E, continuity gives nonnegative deficits and at least one new zero;
by supposition u<=0 there. Define v=max(u,0) on Omega and v=0 outside Omega.
The usual subharmonic pasting lemma applies across every such boundary:
the inside limsup is at most the outside value 0. Hence v is subharmonic
on Sigma\E. It is globally bounded above by T. Bounded-above isolated
singularities of a subharmonic function are removable, so v extends
subharmonically to all of Sigma. It is therefore constant.

A nonconstant d_j is the real part of a nonconstant meromorphic function
on Sigma. Such a function takes values with negative real part on an open
set disjoint from E. This set is strictly infeasible, and v=0 there.
Thus v is identically zero, contrary to u(p)>0. This proves the claim.

Details that matter. Normalization handles singular points and their
separate branches. At finite boundaries where complementary deficits
vanish, continuity of Psi suffices; no derivative of a square root is
used. Points at infinity need no prescribed boundary limit: the uniform
upper bound u<=T is precisely what permits their removal after pasting.

Quantitative version. For any 0<r<u(p), applying the same argument to
max(u-r,0) shows that some finite new-contact boundary has u>r. Exact
preservation of u(p) is not needed and is not claimed. In a finite
iteration one may, for example, retain at least half the positive gap
at each step.

4. WHY A CURVE EXISTS BEFORE SIX CONTACTS.

Work in the affine cone V of four-by-four complex spectral product
vectors (z_pi) in their 14-dimensional linear invariant span. This is
the usual eight-spinor invariant cone. Its complex dimension is six:
eight points on P1 modulo PGL2 have dimension five, and overall scale
adds one. Generically the invariants recover all cross-ratios. One can
see this without a coordinate formula: products of arbitrary perfect
matching brackets are linear combinations of bipartite products by
repeated Plucker straightening; ratios of two such matching products
recover cross-ratios. The affine cone is irreducible and pure dimensional.

The standard invariant-cone model includes projective spectral points
and collision limits. For a nonzero realizable invariant point, a common
Mobius chart can make all 8 spinors finite; the resulting common nonzero
product scale is absorbed by rescaling spectra or the support multiplier.
When using this lemma as part of a final proof, one must retain this
standard identification of V with compactified actual spectral products,
not replace V by the full 14-dimensional linear cost space.

Suppose a violating point has exactly k active labels. Choose an active
reference pi0. Impose the following complex affine equations on V:
  z_pi-z_pi0 = its value at the point, for the k-1 other active labels;
  Gamma = its value at the point.
There are at most k equations. Every local component of their intersection
through the point has dimension at least 6-k. If k<=5, an irreducible
algebraic curve through the point exists in this fiber.

On that curve, the originally active real differences remain zero, eta
is fixed, and T=2|Re(eta Gamma)| stays fixed. If every deficit were
constant, holomorphicity would force every complex difference to be
constant. Section 2 would then force the whole z-vector to be constant,
contradicting the choice of a nonconstant curve in V. Thus the abstract
lemma applies and supplies an additional finite active label with a
positive violation. The support level at a feasible point is exactly
h=Re(eta z_pi0); no separate assumption that the old reference remains
maximal is needed, since all deficits are required nonnegative.

Repeat. Each step adds at least one active label, never deletes an old
one, and preserves a positive violation. After at most 6-k steps, one
has a violating actual compactified product vector with at least six
active permutation labels. This works for Psi=C and for Psi=B.

Precise conclusion: to prove the all-bias four-site matching-cycle
statement, it is sufficient to prove C>=T on all actual spectral
configurations with at least six active labels. Likewise, proving B>=T
on those configurations proves the stronger B bound everywhere.
It is NOT asserted here that all six-contact configurations satisfy
either inequality. Their classification/positivity is the remaining step.


7. UPGRADE TO ARBITRARY FIXED UNITARIES (JOINT TEAM DERIVATION).

The variational agent and parent found a stronger normalization after
the preceding proof was written. It removes the restriction to matching
cycles. The same proof then gives the following genuine necessary form
of any unrestricted MOC counterexample, for n>=4:

  If MOC fails for a fixed unitary U, there are spectra giving a strict
  failure for the SAME U and a support direction with at least 2n-2
  active permutation LABELS. The spectra may be required to lie in any
  prescribed dense Zariski-open part of the actual invariant model.

This is still a reduction, not the exclusion of those counterexamples.
Here is the complete proof and its hypotheses.

Write d_U(z)=sum_pi q_pi z_pi with real coefficients q_pi and sum q_pi=1.
Such coefficients exist explicitly:
  q_pi=Re( sgn(pi) product_i U_i,pi(i) / det U ).
Indeed the complex Leibniz coefficients give the exact determinant, whose
polynomial in the a,b variables has real coefficients |minor(U)|^2;
discarding the imaginary parts of the Leibniz coefficients leaves the
same polynomial. The sum of the coefficients is one by det U's own
Leibniz expansion.

Fix an active reference pi0 and put
  delta_pi=Re(eta(z_pi0-z_pi)),
  S=sum_pi(z_pi0-z_pi),
  M=Re(eta S)=sum_pi delta_pi.
A strict counterexample has M>0: if M=0 all nonnegative deficits vanish
and sum q=1 forces zero support gap. Freeze the COMPLEX value S and the
k-1 complex active differences. On every feasible point of the resulting
fiber, M is a fixed positive real number and
  u=Re(eta d_U)-Re(eta z_pi0)=-sum_pi q_pi delta_pi
is harmonic along holomorphic curves and bounded above, for example by
  u <= M max_pi(-q_pi).
The deficits lie in the compact real simplex delta>=0, sum delta=M;
their imaginary counterparts need not be bounded and need not be.

The actual 2n-spinor invariant cone has complex dimension D=2n-2, by the
same cross-ratio argument as in Section4. Freezing S and k-1 active
differences imposes at most k complex equations. If k<=D-1, there is a
nonconstant algebraic curve in the fiber through the point.

Choose a disjoint-swap quartet with nonzero mixed difference R at the
starting point. Such a choice can be made after arbitrarily small generic
perturbation of a strict counterexample: for n>=4, a fixed disjoint-swap
mixed difference is a nonzero polynomial in the actual spectra. For a
quartet differing by independent disjoint row swaps,
  z00 z11=z01 z10,
and R=z00-z01-z10+z11. Thus a common translation c changes its determinant
relation by cR, exactly as before.

Along each chosen irreducible curve R is not identically zero. Delete
its finitely many zero points and include them in the exceptional set E.
The pasting/removability proof in Section3 is unchanged: u remains
uniformly bounded above, so the extra isolated holes cause no problem.
Hence any strict violation reaches a finite new-contact point where R
is still nonzero. All old contacts persist, U and eta remain fixed, and
S remains fixed. A curve with all deficits constant would have every
complex difference constant; since R is nonzero it would have constant
z, impossible for a nonconstant curve in the invariant cone.

Iterate until k>=D=2n-2. At every step a positive support violation is
preserved. This proves the assertion.

Dense-open strengthening. Let W be a fixed dense Zariski-open subset
of the invariant cone, and start with a generic strict counterexample
in W. Every curve through a point of W meets the closed complement in
only finitely many points, unless contained in it, which it is not.
Delete these points along with the zeros of R and infinity. The same
bounded-above removable-singularity proof applies. Consequently every
step can remain in W. In particular, choose W in the dense actual
finite-spectrum image furnished by the dominant invariant parametrization;
this avoids relying on realization of every boundary point of the full
algebraic closure. One may also exclude algebraic collisions or vanishing
brackets when these define a proper closed subset.

This dense-open argument answers a technical realization issue in the
earlier cycle version as well. It does not exclude real support ties:
those are equations on real parts, not a proper complex algebraic
exceptional subset that can automatically be deleted.

The analytic gluing argument has been independently audited with the
variational agent; the full upgrade has been sent to the parent and
literature agent for their independent checks.
The terminal high-contact positivity problem remains unsolved here.

8. SIMPLER ARBITRARY-U PROOF: PRESERVE THE EXACT COMPLEX VIOLATION.

The parent and literature agent identified a simpler normalization after
Section7. It strengthens that theorem by preserving the EXACT complex
quantity d_U-z_pi0, so in particular the positive support gap is unchanged.
The capacity versions above still need the subharmonic argument; this
arbitrary-U version does not.

For fixed U, the Leibniz expression makes d_U a complex linear function
of the product coordinates z. At a strict counterexample choose an active
reference pi0 and put P_U=d_U-z_pi0. Fix P_U as one complex affine equation,
and fix the k-1 complex differences of the other active labels from pi0.
There are at most k equations on the D=2n-2 dimensional invariant cone.
For k<D choose a nonconstant irreducible algebraic curve through the
point in this fiber, normalize and compactify it, and delete all points
at infinity together with zeros of a fixed nonzero quartet mixed
difference R and the complement of the chosen dense actual chart W.
Only finitely many points are deleted. The resulting punctured compact
Riemann surface Y is connected.

Let Omega be the component containing the original point of the locus
where every previously inactive deficit is strictly positive. If Omega
has a boundary in Y, continuity gives an actual finite feasible point
with at least one new active label. All original contacts persist, and
the support gap remains exactly Re(eta P_U)>0. This is the desired point.

Suppose there is no such boundary. Then Omega is both open and closed
in connected Y, hence equals Y. Every deficit is now nonnegative on Y
and is the real part of a meromorphic function on the compactification.
A nonconstant meromorphic function takes values with negative real part
on an open set; a finite deleted set cannot remove that open set. Hence
every deficit is constant. Holomorphicity then makes every complex
permutation difference constant. Since R is nonzero, the quartet
translation identity forces every z-coordinate constant. This contradicts
the choice of a nonconstant curve in the invariant cone.

Thus a new active label is obtained with exactly the old positive gap.
Iterating proves the arbitrary-U theorem through k>=2n-2. It preserves
U, eta, P_U, every previous complex active difference, and the prescribed
dense open chart. No assumptions about minima, growth, or attainment
occur. The real signed permutation expansion of Section7 is valid but
unnecessary for this shorter proof.

The independent algebraic agent audit agrees with this simplified
argument. A proof that every terminal high-contact configuration obeys
MOC is still required before the unrestricted conjecture is resolved.

9. ELEMENTARY DIMENSION AND ACTUAL-CHART JUSTIFICATION.

The literature agent supplied the following convenient independent
dimension calculation, avoiding any reliance on the eight-point cubic.
Let C be a generic n-by-n rank-two complex matrix and let
z_pi(C)=product_i C_i,pi(i). The rank-two matrix variety has dimension
4n-4. Suppose generic full-support matrices C and C' have identical
nonzero matching products. For any distinct i,k and distinct j,l, extend
the two assignments i->j,k->l and i->l,k->j by the SAME matching on the
remaining rows and columns. Taking the ratio of their equal matching
products shows
  Cij Ckl/(Cil Ckj) = C'ij C'kl/(C'il C'kj).
Thus the entrywise ratio Mij=C'ij/Cij has every 2-by-2 determinant zero,
so Mij=r_i c_j. Equality of the complete matching products imposes
  product_i r_i product_j c_j=1.
Conversely these row and column scalings preserve every matching product.
The scalings have 2n parameters, minus the one-dimensional redundant
transformation r->t r,c->t^(-1)c, minus the product-one equation. Generic
fibers therefore have dimension 2n-2. The image cone has dimension
  (4n-4)-(2n-2)=2n-2,
and is irreducible because the rank-two source is irreducible.

A generic rank-two matrix can be written
  Cij=u_i v_j+x_i y_j
with every u_i and y_j nonzero. Then
  Cij=u_i y_j(a_i+b_j),
  a_i=x_i/u_i, b_j=v_j/y_j.
Its matching products equal the actual additive spectral products times
the common nonzero scalar lambda=product_i u_i product_j y_j. Taking an
n-th root t of lambda and replacing a,b by ta,tb realizes those products
EXACTLY by finite spectra, not just projectively. Hence the finite
spectral-product parametrization is dominant onto the image cone.
By constructibility of the image of a polynomial map, that image contains
a nonempty dense Zariski-open subset W. The preservation-of-W argument
in Sections7-8 therefore ensures genuine finite spectral realizations at
every step, without an assertion about all nongeneric closure points.
