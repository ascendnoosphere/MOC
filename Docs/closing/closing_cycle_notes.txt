HOLOMORPHIC CONTACT LOCALIZATION FOR THE CYCLE CAPACITY
2026-10-17. Constructive follow-up research; unrestricted MOC is not proved.

1. The scalar quantity to control.

For n=2r and the two perfect matching layers P,Q forming a single cycle,
use the notation of residual_variational_notes.txt. There are n gate bits
s, their permutation outcomes pi_s, and chi_s=(-1)^|s|. Fix complex
spectral products z_pi=product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)) and a support multiplier eta.
Let h=max_pi Re(eta z_pi), delta_pi=h-Re(eta z_pi), and let delta_s mean
the corresponding outcome gaps. The exact all-bias, all-phase condition is

 C(delta) >= T,
 C(delta)=inf_(x in R^n) sum_s delta_s exp(<2s-1,x>),
 T=|sum_s chi_s delta_s|=2|Re(eta Pspec)|,
 Pspec=product_(i,j in P)(a_j-a_i)
       product_(k,l in Q)(b_k-b_l).

The earlier complementary estimate B(delta)=2 sum_pairs sqrt(delta_s
delta_bar_s) is sufficient because C>=B. The localization theorem below
works for C itself and hence does not assume the stronger B inequality.

2. Capacity continuity and superharmonicity (proved).

C is nonnegative, monotone coordinatewise, positively homogeneous, and
finite, with C(delta)<=sum_s delta_s. It is upper semicontinuous as an
infimum of continuous linear functions. If delta^(j)->delta in the closed
nonnegative orthant, then for every epsilon>0, eventually

delta^(j) >= (1-epsilon) delta

coordinatewise: this is immediate on zero coordinates, and there are only
finitely many positive coordinates. Thus C(delta^(j))>=(1-epsilon)C(delta).
It follows that C is also lower semicontinuous, hence continuous everywhere
on the closed orthant, including configurations with zero gaps.

Suppose every delta_s(z) is nonnegative harmonic on a complex curve.
For a closed disk centered at z0, every fixed-x linear expression
L_x(delta)=sum_s delta_s exp(<2s-1,x>) is harmonic. Therefore

 C(delta(z0)) = inf_x average_boundary L_x(delta)
             >= average_boundary inf_x L_x(delta)
              = average_boundary C(delta).

Continuity supplies the remaining condition in the mean-value definition:
C(delta(z)) is superharmonic. No attainment of the infimum over x is
needed. The elementary complementary bound B is also superharmonic;
for positive harmonic u,v,

 Laplacian sqrt(uv) = -(sqrt(uv)/4)*|gradient log u-gradient log v|^2.

If a nonnegative harmonic gap vanishes at an interior point, it vanishes
identically locally, so zero paired terms cause no exception.

3. The algebraic space and the contact-preserving curves.

Let X_n be the affine cone of rank-two permutation-product vectors
(z_pi)_(pi in S_n), equivalently the invariant cone of 2n labelled
spinors. It has complex dimension 2n-2. Every nonzero point of X_n has a
realization by finite complex spectra up to a common nonzero complex
factor. The factor can be absorbed in the support multiplier, or in a
common nth-root scaling of all eigenvalues. This finite-chart fact and
the dimension calculation are described in N067 of the supplied volume.
The coordinate functions z_pi are regular on X_n.

Suppose k permutation labels are active at a support point; choose one
of them, pi0. Freeze the following COMPLEX linear quantities:

 Pspec, and z_pi-z_pi0 for every other active label pi.       (H1)

There are k equations. Thus when k<=2n-3, the fiber through the point
has positive complex dimension (local dimension at least 2n-2-k).
Choose an irreducible affine algebraic curve in this fiber through the
point, and take its normalization. This also handles singular fibers.
On this curve the active real ties remain fixed. In a neighborhood of the
base point the inactive gaps remain positive. On the connected feasible
component, every gap is the nonnegative real part of a holomorphic
function, hence harmonic. The loop value Pspec is constant, so T is
constant. Thus u=T-C(delta) is continuous and subharmonic, with u<=T.

Crucial normalization warning: one may normalize Pspec to 1 by complex
homogeneity, but eta changes correspondingly. One may not simultaneously
set eta=1 without changing the support question.

4. A finite-boundary propagation lemma (proved).

Let Gamma be a nonconstant irreducible affine algebraic curve through an
actual violating point (u>0), satisfying (H1). Let Omega be the connected
component where all previously inactive permutation gaps are positive.
At a finite boundary point of Omega, a new permutation label becomes
active. Suppose, for contradiction, that u<=0 at every such boundary point.
Define v on the normalized affine curve by

 v=max(u,0) on Omega, and v=0 off Omega.

The standard subharmonic pasting lemma applies because the limsup from
Omega at each finite boundary is <=0. Thus v is subharmonic. Its upper
bound T gives removable subharmonic singularities at the finitely many
points of a smooth projective compactification. A subharmonic function
on a compact connected Riemann surface is constant.

If some gap is negative somewhere on Gamma, its negative open set lies
off Omega, where v=0. Thus v must be identically zero, contradicting the
positive base value. There remains only the possibility that all gaps
are nonnegative throughout Gamma. Each such gap is the real part of a
meromorphic function on the compactification. A genuine pole makes its
real part take both signs in every punctured neighborhood, so there are
no poles. The real parts, and hence all holomorphic coordinate differences
z_pi-z_pi0, are constant.

Consequently the product vector could move only by a common translation
z_pi=c_pi+t. The next paragraph excludes this when Pspec is nonzero.
Therefore a finite boundary point with a new active label and u>0 must
exist. There is no hidden assumption of an attained global minimum, and
no uncontrolled limit at infinity: bounded subharmonic removability is
what disposes of the finitely many punctures.

5. Why a common translation is impossible (proved).

For n=4, choose the omitted contingency sector mapping each P-pair to
one Q-pair. Its four products form an outer-product rectangle:

 z00 z11=z01 z10,
 R=z00-z01-z10+z11=+/-Pspec.

Adding t to all four entries changes the left side minus the right side
by tR. Thus if R is nonzero, a curve in the spectral variety cannot move
by nonconstant common translation.

For general even n>=4, choose any two P-pairs and the corresponding two
Q-pairs in an omitted block assignment. In every remaining pair choose
one of its two local assignment products to be nonzero. This is possible
because the two choices differ by a nonzero paired a-gap times paired
b-gap, as Pspec!=0. The quartet obtained by varying only the two selected
pair assignments again has a rank-one product identity and nonzero mixed
difference

 R=(q1^0-q1^1)(q2^0-q2^1)*product_(other pairs) q_l !=0.

All four products acquire the same common translation on Gamma; the
same tR calculation excludes a nonconstant translation. This argument
uses only the exact product identities, not the skew-cubic Hessian.

6. Consequence for the true cycle inequality (proved).

If an actual spectral violation C(delta)<T exists in any even dimension
n>=4, then T>0 and Pspec!=0. Whenever fewer than 2n-2 permutation labels
are active, Sections 3-5 move it to another actual violating point with
strictly more active labels. Repeating finitely many times proves:

  Any failure of the all-bias, all-phase single-cycle support inequality
  can be realized at a spectral configuration where at least 2n-2
  permutation labels lie on the exposed supporting line.

For n=4 the threshold is six labels. Labels may correspond to coincident
complex vertex values; the theorem does not assert six distinct points.
The same theorem holds for failure of the stronger complementary bound.
This is an exact global localization theorem. It does NOT establish that
these high-contact configurations are safe; that classification is the
remaining step for this route.

7. Extension to arbitrary relative unitaries (independently audited).

For fixed U, the determinant is an affine real linear combination of the
permutation products:

 d_U=sum_pi c_pi(U) z_pi,  c_pi(U) real, sum_pi c_pi(U)=1.

One proof expands det(D_a U+U D_b) and averages with the analogous formula
for conjugate(U). Equality of the two determinant symbols follows from
their squared-minor expansion. The coefficients need not be nonnegative.

At a strict MOC violation put delta_pi=h-Re(eta z_pi) and
u=Re(eta d_U)-h=-sum_pi c_pi delta_pi>0. Hence
M=sum_pi delta_pi>0. Instead of fixing Pspec, freeze the complex quantity

 S=sum_pi(z_pi0-z_pi),

as well as the k-1 active complex differences. Then all gaps remain
nonnegative harmonic on the feasible curve component, their sum is the
constant M=Re(eta S), and u is harmonic with an upper bound such as
M*sum_pi|c_pi|. The same compactification/pasting proof applies.

A strict counterexample may first be perturbed to have all relevant
spectral gaps and sums nonzero. Choose one nonzero multiplicative-quartet
mixed difference R. Along the curve it is regular and not identically
zero. Its finitely many zeros on the compactification can be removed
as additional exceptional points; the bounded subharmonic pasted
function extends across them exactly as across infinity punctures.
Thus the propagated violation may retain R!=0. The common-translation
exception is excluded by that quartet. Iteration would give the same
2n-2 active-label threshold for a completely unrestricted MOC
counterexample, while retaining the original relative unitary U.

The algebraic and literature agents independently audited this general
extension and found the argument valid. Their companion notes give
further details, including preservation of any fixed dense Zariski-open
set by deleting its finitely many exceptional intersections on each
curve. It is not a proof that no such counterexample exists.
