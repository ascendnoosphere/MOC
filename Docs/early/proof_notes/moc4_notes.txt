ORDER-FOUR MOC ATTACK: EXACT TRANSPOSITION-INVARIANT CONE THEOREM

Status: rigorous restricted theorem and exact finite certificate. This is not a proof of unrestricted MOC, and no priority claim is made. The repeated-eigenvalue MOC case itself is known. The result concerns the entire common-minor cone with a transposition symmetry and gives an explicit certificate and sharp isoclinic budget.

THEOREM
Let g be a real function in the order-four common squared-minor function space. Assume g(pi)>=0 for every permutation pi, and g(pi o (12))=g(pi). Then g(U)>=0 for every U in U(4). More precisely, g is a nonnegative linear combination of ordinary singleton and two-minor indicators. Its isoclinic coefficients satisfy the sharp inequality K^2 <= 4 T D, with conventions of N129, for every choice of base permutation and every conjugate isoclinic orientation. The same assertions hold for left-transposition invariance by inversion.

A row/column permutation transports any transposition to (12). Thus all single-transposition invariances are covered. In particular, either spectrum having any repeated eigenvalue implies the hypothesis; but the actual-cost symmetry can also arise without repetitions. E.g. a=(i,-i,2,3), any real b, eta real has Re(eta z_pi)=Re(eta z_{pi o (12)}) because the products are conjugate.

PROOF OF THE EXACT CONE
The invariance means g is an array g_jk, j != k, where j=pi(3), k=pi(4). Exactly two permutations correspond to each ordered pair.

The common-minor linear identities are precisely the condition
  g_jk - g_kj = d_j - d_k
for some real numbers d_1,...,d_4, unique up to a constant.

Necessity can be checked directly on each transposition-averaged minor indicator. Size-one indicators from row 3 or 4 give functions u_j or v_k; those from rows 1/2 give half the complement of membership among j,k. Row-pair {3,4} gives an arbitrary symmetric unordered-pair indicator; row-pair {1,2} is its complementary version. A mixed row-pair {1,3} or {2,3}, averaged over swapping 1,2, is (1/2)1_{j in J,k not in J}, for a two-element J. Its antisymmetry is (1/2)(1_J(j)-1_J(k)). The pairs involving row 4 give the reversed expression. Complementary-minor identities cover sizes 3 and 4. Hence every averaged minor has the displayed potential-difference property.

Sufficiency: an arbitrary symmetric off-diagonal array is a combination of the six pair indicators, and d_j is a linear combination of the four singleton indicators for row 3. Thus every array satisfying the condition is in the common-minor invariant space. Dimension is 6+3=9.

Set m_jk=min(g_jk,g_kj)>=0 (symmetric). Then exactly
  g_jk = m_jk + max(d_j-d_k,0).
Order the d's, d_(1)<=...<=d_(4), and let S_r be the vertices with the largest 4-r values, r=1,2,3. Then
  max(d_j-d_k,0)
  = sum_{r=1}^3 (d_(r+1)-d_(r)) 1_{j in S_r,k not in S_r}.
Ties cause zero coefficients and are harmless.

Therefore the cone is generated by 6 symmetric-edge arrays E_ab and 14 directed-cut arrays C_S (nonempty proper S subset [4]):
  E_ab(j,k)=1_{{j,k}={a,b}},
  C_S(j,k)=1_{j in S,k not in S}.
These 20 arrays are rays; extremality is not needed for the certificate.

Each generator has an explicit nonnegative extension to every unitary U. Let rho_{I,J}=|det U[I,J]|^2.
  E_ab = rho_{34,{a,b}}.
  C_{ {a} } = rho_{3,a}.
  C_{[4] minus {a}} = rho_{4,a}.
  C_{ {a,b} } = rho_{13,{a,b}} + rho_{23,{a,b}}.
The last identity holds on permutations: if pi(3) is in {a,b} and pi(4) is outside, exactly one of pi(1),pi(2) supplies the remaining member of {a,b}; otherwise neither pair minor can contribute. The common-minor permutation evaluation map is injective (dimension 14, e.g. N139), so these permutation identities are identities of common-minor functions. This proves nonnegativity and the explicit certificate.

QUANTITATIVE EXTENSION TO NONSYMMETRIC COSTS

Theorem. Fix tau=(12). Suppose g0 is a transposition-invariant common-minor function with
  g0(id)=g0(tau)=0,
  m=min_{pi not in {id,tau}}g0(pi)>0.
Let g be ANY common-minor function satisfying g(id)=0 and g(pi)>=0 for every permutation. If
  ||g-g0||_infinity <= m/84,
where the norm is the maximum absolute difference on the 24 permutations, then g is in the cone generated by individual singleton and two-minor indicators. In particular g(U)>=0 for every U in U(4). No actual-spectral assumption and no transposition symmetry of g is required.

Exact proof. There are 34 distinct nonconstant singleton/two-minor indicators, after complement duplicates. Exactly 23 vanish both at id and tau. Let H be their sum. Permutation of the first two rows permutes these 23 functions, so H is tau-invariant. Exact enumeration gives max_pi H(pi)=6. Thus g0-(m/6)H is permutation-nonnegative and tau-invariant, and the preceding cone theorem places it in the minor cone.

The following 12 active indicators form a basis for the 12-dimensional subspace of common-minor functions vanishing at id and tau:
 rho_{1,3}, rho_{1,4}, rho_{2,3}, rho_{2,4},
 rho_{3,1}, rho_{3,2}, rho_{3,4}, rho_{4,1},
 rho_{12,13}, rho_{12,14}, rho_{13,12}, rho_{13,14}.
Adjoin v=rho_{1,2}; this is a basis of the 13-dimensional subspace vanishing at id. Since every active indicator vanishes at tau and v(tau)=1, the coefficient of v in delta=g-g0 is delta(tau)=g(tau)>=0.

Evaluate this 13-element basis on the permutations
 1243,1324,1342,1423,2134,2143,2314,2341,2413,3124,3142,3412,4123.
The resulting 13x13 0/1 matrix is invertible. Its inverse has largest absolute row sum 14 among the first 12 rows (the active coefficients). Consequently delta=sum_i beta_i f_i+g(tau)v, with |beta_i|<=14||delta||_infinity<=m/6.
Every f_i occurs with coefficient m/6 in (m/6)H. Hence
 g = [g0-(m/6)H] + [(m/6)H+sum_i beta_i f_i] + g(tau)v
is a sum of elements of the ordinary minor cone. This proves the theorem.

This is a finite exact argument with rational arithmetic. moc4_exact_verify.py constructs every indicator, checks the active count, H bound and invariance, computes the inverse over Fraction, and verifies all 20 isoclinic-generator determinants. The constant 84 is convenient and verified; no sharpness is claimed.

A directly testable sufficient condition for a generic support gap g: for any row transposition tau with g(id)=0, set
 g0(pi)=[g(pi)+g(pi o tau)-g(tau)]/2.
Then g0 is invariant and vanishes at id,tau. Compute
 m=min_{pi not in {id,tau}}g0(pi),
 r=max_pi |g(pi)-g0(pi)|.
If m>0 and 84r<=m, full unitary support holds. This criterion proves a nontrivial wedge of nonsymmetric costs around the generic symmetric locus, but it is not satisfied by every actual spectral cost and cannot close the general conjecture by itself.

EXPLICIT COMPLEX SIMPLE-SPECTRUM SUPPORT FAMILY
For every real epsilon with |epsilon|<=1/200, put
 a=(epsilon+i, -epsilon+i, 1, 2+i),
 b=(-1+i, -2-2i, 2-i, 1+2i).
Then for every U in U(4),
 Re det(D_a+U D_b U*) <= 66+6|epsilon|-12epsilon^2.
The right side is exactly the largest real part of the 24 permutation products. For nonzero epsilon both spectra are simple and noncollinear. This is one support direction, not full complex convex-hull membership for these spectra.

Proof. At epsilon=0 the maximum is 66, attained only by id and (12), and the smallest other gap is m=10. For epsilon>=0, the identity product has real part 66+6epsilon-12epsilon^2; the (12) product has real part 66-6epsilon-12epsilon^2. Write each permutation product's real part as c_pi(0)+l_pi epsilon+q_pi epsilon^2. Exact Gaussian rational multiplication gives
 max_pi |6-l_pi|=22,
 max_pi |-12-q_pi|=16.
Consequently the candidate gaps from identity differ from g0 by at most 22epsilon+16epsilon^2. At epsilon<=1/200 this is at most 69/625. All gaps outside {id,(12)} remain positive because their initial gap is at least 10, and the (12) gap is 12epsilon>=0. Therefore identity really is a maximizer and the candidate gaps are actual support gaps. Finally 84(69/625)=5796/625<10, so the quantitative nonsymmetric theorem proves the stated bound for every unitary. Negative epsilon just interchanges the first two rows, leaving the maximized support and universal unitary claim unchanged. The script moc4_actual_example.py checks all coefficients and inequalities exactly over Fraction.

FULL MOC ON AN ENTIRE SIMPLE COMPLEX SPECTRAL FAMILY

Theorem. For every real epsilon with |epsilon|<=1/20, let
 a(epsilon)=(epsilon+i,-epsilon+i,1,2+i),
 b=(-1+i,-2-2i,2-i,1+2i).
Then for EVERY U in U(4),
 det(D_a+U D_b U*) belongs to conv{product_i(a_i+b_{pi(i)}): pi in S4}.
This is full complex convex-hull membership, including every supporting direction and every unitary. For every nonzero epsilon in this interval, both spectra have four distinct eigenvalues and neither spectrum is collinear.

The proof is a finite exact polynomial certificate, not a numerical test. The certificate is
 moc4_full_family_certificate.json
and its stand-alone verification-only program is
 moc4_full_family_verify.py.
The latter uses only Python's standard library, integers and fractions; it invokes no optimizer and performs no sampling. The companion discovery script used a linear program only to find candidate bases before rational polynomial reconstruction. It is not part of the proof.

Proof for 0<epsilon<=1/20. Write
 z_pi(epsilon)=product_i(a_i(epsilon)+b_{pi(i)}).
These are complex polynomials of degree at most two. The permutation polygon has the following nine vertices, in counterclockwise order, specified by their one-based permutation labels:
 3412, 4312, 3241, 4231, 1234, 2134, 2143, 4123, 4132.
For each consecutive pair (l,r), including the closing pair, define the oriented edge-gap functional
 G_lr(w;epsilon)=Im(conj(z_r(epsilon)-z_l(epsilon))*(w-z_l(epsilon))).
Thus the polygon lies in G_lr>=0 on the left side of each directed edge.

The saved certificate gives 12 terms for each of the nine edges, with the exact identity
 G_lr(d(U);epsilon)
 = sum_{term t} lambda_{lr,t}(epsilon) |det U[I_t,J_t]|^2,
where |I_t|=|J_t| is 1 or 2 and every lambda is a real rational polynomial of degree at most four. There are 108 such explicitly stored coefficient polynomials in total. Identity is checked coefficient by coefficient for all 24 permutations, which proves it for all U by the injectivity of the common-minor permutation evaluation map (the dimension-fourteen identity established in the supplied volume). There are no unrecorded optimization variables.

Every polynomial lambda(e)=sum_{k=0}^4 a_k e^k is checked nonnegative on [0,E], E=1/20, by expressing it in the degree-four Bernstein basis:
 lambda(E t)=sum_{j=0}^4 B_j binom(4,j)t^j(1-t)^(4-j),
 B_j=sum_{k=0}^j a_k E^k binom(j,k)/binom(4,k).
All five B_j are nonnegative rational numbers. Therefore every facet identity is a sum of nonnegative squared minors for the ENTIRE real interval, establishing G_lr(d(U);epsilon)>=0.

The verifier also checks all 36 squared distances among the nine listed vertices, and all nine consecutive edge cross products. Their Bernstein coefficients are nonnegative, at least one is positive, and their values at E are positive. Thus the nine vertices are distinct and the polygon turns are strictly positive for every 0<epsilon<=E. Together with each edge supporting all 24 permutation points (already implied by the nonnegative identity), this proves the listed edges describe precisely the permutation convex hull. Hence their nine inequalities imply the claimed convex-hull membership for every U.

At epsilon=0, continuity of determinant and of a finite-vertex convex hull proves the result: take epsilon_j>0 tending to zero; each d_epsilon_j(U) lies in the corresponding finite convex hull, and subsequential compactness of its convex weights gives the limit inclusion. Equivalently the recovered repeated-eigenvalue theorem applies. For epsilon<0, interchange the first two a-labels, mapping epsilon to -epsilon. Permuting a-labels preserves the universal unitary assertion and the permutation polygon. This covers the closed interval [-1/20,1/20].
