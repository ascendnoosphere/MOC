INDEPENDENT REVIEW OF THE SMALL-SPECTRUM THEOREM
Reviewer track: literature / independent audit. 17 October 2026.
Reviewed file: variational_notes.txt, sections 1--11.

VERDICT
The stated sufficiently-small-complex-t theorem is proved by the supplied
argument. I found no mathematical gap in its assignment-face classification,
normal Hessian argument, or uniformity over all support phases. This is an
independent informal mathematical review, not formal proof-assistant checking.
It does not establish unrestricted MOC or a quantitative admissible radius.

EXACT SCOPE
For fixed n>=2, nonzero a_i, and beta_j, assume all n! sums
    s_pi = sum_i a_i^{-1} beta_{pi(i)}
are distinct and no three are collinear in C=R^2. Then some epsilon>0
works simultaneously for every unitary U and every complex |t|<epsilon:
    det(D_a+t U D_beta U*) is in conv{prod_i(a_i+t beta_{pi(i)})}.
Both quantifier uniformities are justified below. The genericity hypothesis
is genuinely satisfiable and open dense, but the proof supplies no control
of epsilon near nongeneric spectral shapes.

1. NORMALIZATION AND ASSIGNMENT REDUCTION
The polynomial expansion of G_t in section 1 is correct: factoring D_a
reduces the kth coefficient to principal minors of U D_beta U*, followed by
Cauchy--Binet. Thus its t=0 value is the assignment functional on |U_ij|^2.
For t!=0 the normalization is an invertible complex affine map and preserves
real convex combinations. The original t=0 conclusion is immediate.

2. MAXIMIZER CLASSIFICATION
The no-three-collinear hypothesis permits at most two optimal assignments
in each support direction. If two optimal permutations differed by several
disjoint cycles, every partial-cycle replacement would also be optimal:
its nonnegative deficit contributes to a sum of deficits equal to zero.
This contradicts exactly two optimizers. Thus the relative permutation is
a single cycle.

When that cycle has length>=3, a strict mixture of the two permutation
matrices cannot be unistochastic: consecutive cycle rows have precisely
one common allowed nonzero column and cannot be orthogonal. Consequently
the unitary equality set consists of two phase tori, not a hidden larger
critical family. For a transposition the equality set is precisely one
U(2) block times scalar phase blocks. The singleton-optimizer case is one
phase torus.

3. AN ELEMENTARY VERSION OF THE COERCIVE LEAKAGE BOUND
The assignment dual construction in section 3 is valid. It can also be
avoided: let delta be the smallest deficit of a nonoptimal permutation,
let E be the union of optimal permutation supports, and write the doubly
stochastic matrix |U|^2 as a convex combination sum lambda_pi P_pi.
Every optimal permutation uses no edge outside E, and any other uses at
most n such edges. Therefore
    h-F_0(U) >= delta sum_nonoptimal lambda_pi
              >= (delta/n) sum_{(i,j) outside E}|U_ij|^2.
If there are no nonoptimal permutations, this is the n=2 all-U(2) solved
case. No bound on a chosen Birkhoff decomposition is otherwise required.

4. NORMAL HESSIAN IS STRICT IN EVERY NECESSARY CASE
At U0 in the equality manifold S, leakage along exp(sK)U0 begins with
    s^2 sum_outside_E |(K U0)_ij|^2.
For a phase torus its kernel is diagonal skew-Hermitian K. For a U(2)
block family it is exactly block-diagonal K. For a cycle of length>=3,
after monomial normalization each additional allowed off-diagonal edge
has a forbidden transpose; skew-Hermiticity forces both entries to zero.
Thus again only diagonal K lies in the kernel. These are precisely the
tangent spaces to S in the respective cases.
Compactness of S and its unit normal bundle makes the lower Hessian bound
uniform in U0. The two tori in the long-cycle case are disjoint compact
components, so disjoint normal neighborhoods may be chosen.

5. THE CRUCIAL PERTURBATION ERROR IS QUADRATIC
The proof correctly uses more than uniform C0 approximation. On each
component of S, A+t U0 B U0* is block diagonal for the same fixed partition.
An off-block skew-Hermitian generator gives an off-block first variation.
The determinant derivative is therefore zero. The adjugate argument works
also at singular block matrices by polynomial continuity. It follows that
every G_t, and every support functional of G_t, has zero first derivative
in every normal direction at S, for all t and all support phases.

Consequently E_t=G_t-G_0 has a normal Taylor remainder bounded uniformly by
C|t| ||K||^2. A phase change similarly contributes at most
C'|eta'-eta| ||K||^2 to the comparison between exp(K)U0 and U0. Polynomial
dependence on t gives these C2 estimates uniformly for complex t in a
closed small disk. The strict negative base normal Hessian dominates both
errors after shrinking that disk and the phase neighborhood.

6. LOCAL-TO-GLOBAL AND PHASE UNIFORMITY
Normal exponential coordinates cover a sufficiently small tube around the
compact S. Inside the tube every perturbed objective is at most its value
at its base point in S. At a phase torus that value is a permutation value.
On the U(2) block the determinant is exactly a convex combination of its
two permutation endpoints, with nonnegative weights summing to one; the
exterior singleton factors cause no problem even when complex.

The compact complement of a smaller open tube has a positive base support
gap. One can compare its perturbed values to a fixed point of S; uniform
continuity preserves a positive gap. Thus every unitary is covered.
Finally each base support phase has an open phase neighborhood and a
positive t-radius. A finite subcover of the support circle and the minimum
of those radii give the simultaneous all-phase, all-U, complex-disk claim.
There is no interchange of an uncontrolled pointwise limit with a supremum.

7. GENERICITY AND EXACT EXAMPLE
For each permutation pair the difference of assignment sums is a nonzero
complex polynomial in x_i=a_i^{-1}, beta_j. If any triple orientation were
identically zero, the corresponding holomorphic rational ratio would take
only real values on a complex open set, hence would be a real constant.
Comparing bilinear coefficients would then put three distinct permutation
vertices on one real affine line, impossible for vertices of a convex
polytope. Each forbidden condition is therefore a proper real algebraic
set; their finite union has empty interior. This establishes genericity.

Executed python variational_example_check.py. It reported 24 distinct
assignment sums, 2024 nonzero triple orientations, and minimum absolute
orientation 315, agreeing with the manuscript. This checks the listed
n=4 example, not the analytic epsilon.

MINOR EDITORIAL QUALIFICATIONS
The phrase 'noncollinear instances in every dimension' should say n>=3:
any two-point spectrum is collinear, although the theorem itself is valid
for n=2. The open-set claim can be justified by letting a,beta vary in a
sufficiently small neighborhood during the same finite-patch argument;
all the Hessian, gap, and polynomial estimates then remain uniform.

LIMITATION
Nothing here permits replacing 'sufficiently small |t|' by arbitrary t,
and simultaneous scaling of A and B does not change their relative scale.
The result proves a substantial restricted class in every dimension. No
novelty claim or comparison with all existing small-perturbation literature
was performed in this audit.

APPENDIX-C FOLLOW-UP AUDIT: EXPLICIT RADIUS 10^(-12)
The nine-edge refinement in Appendix C is valid, conditional on the
quantitative distance/Hessian lemma in Appendix A (under separate review).
I checked the algebraic bounds and the precise polygon-topology issue
below, and executed variational_improved_radius_check.py successfully.

Subtracting the k=4 term from G_t is legitimate: its coefficient is
product(x_i) product(beta_j) for every U, so it is a common translation.
The remaining vertex displacement is bounded by
    R=1366923 r+679895624 r^2.
The coefficient bounds are complex L1 bounds, hence safely dominate the
Euclidean absolute values for every complex |t|<=r. The global Hessian
constants B1,B2,B3 remain applicable to the nonconstant terms; no B4 is
needed because that term has identically zero derivatives in U.

For each directed original edge u->v, and every other labeled point j,
the oriented area changes by at most 4DR+4R^2. This follows by expanding
the determinant of the two difference vectors, whose perturbations have
norm at most 2R. The L1 diameter D=2502 is a valid Euclidean upper bound.
Thus the strict first inequality in Appendix C implies that every other
perturbed point lies strictly to the left of each of the nine directed
perturbed edge segments.

WHY CHECKING ONLY THE NINE ORIGINAL EDGES IS SUFFICIENT
Each such segment is consequently an actual exposed edge of the convex
hull of all 24 perturbed points: its supporting line contains exactly its
two endpoint labels. Those endpoints are distinct, and the endpoints are
vertices of the hull. Consecutive designated edges are not collinear,
because the next endpoint lies strictly to the left of the previous
designated edge. The nine designated edges therefore form a closed cycle
in the boundary graph of a nondegenerate convex polygon, with two distinct
designated edges incident at every designated vertex. A polygon boundary
graph is connected and every vertex has degree two. Hence this closed
cycle is the whole boundary graph: no extra edge or vertex can occur.
Equivalently, the intersection of the nine left half-planes is exactly
the perturbed hull. Full preservation of all triple orientations is not
necessary for this conclusion.

For each original edge the minimum nonendpoint support gap is at least
    area_e / L_e,
since its true Euclidean length is at most the L1 length L_e. Dividing by
n=4 gives the stated gamma_e. Its true length is at least l_e, so the
change of the outward unit normal is at most 4R/l_e. Therefore the stated
second inequality is precisely a conservative version of Appendix A's
all-unitary certificate, using C4<245. This confirms that all nine edge
half-planes contain every actual G_t(U), and consequently confirms the
claimed full convex-hull membership on |t|<=10^(-12).
