1. CLOSED FAMILY: THREE DISTINCT VALUES WITH ONE SINGLETON

Independently checked the algebraic agent's theorem and proved a direct determinant presentation.

Theorem. Let A,B be normal n by n matrices. Suppose B has at most three spectral values b0,b1,b2, with b2 of multiplicity one and the others of arbitrary multiplicities r,s (r+s+1=n). Then det(A+B) lies in the permutation-product convex hull. For a fixed relative unitary U and fixed index groups, one probability distribution over permutations works simultaneously for all complex eigenvalues a1,...,an and all b0,b1,b2.

Proof. Diagonalize A and write
  B=b0 I+delta P+kappa vv*,
where P is an orthogonal rank-s projection, v is a unit vector orthogonal to its range, delta=b1-b0, and kappa=b2-b0. Put Q=P+vv*. If delta!=0, let tau=kappa/delta. The determinant is affine in a rank-one perturbation, so
  det(A+B)=(1-tau)det(A+b0 I+delta P)
                  +tau det(A+b0 I+delta Q).
This is an algebraic identity; tau may be nonreal.

For a rank-s projection P, define p(X)=det P[X,X] for |X|=s. Cauchy-Binet gives p(X)>=0 and sum_X p(X)=1. The projection generating polynomial identity gives, simultaneously for arbitrary complex a,b0,b1,
  det(diag(a)+b0 I+(b1-b0)P)
     =sum_|X|=s p(X) product_i in X(a_i+b1) product_i outside X(a_i+b0).
One proof expands the left determinant in principal minors and uses the DPP marginal identity
  det P[I,I]=sum_{X superset I, |X|=s} det P[X,X],
itself a Cauchy-Binet identity for an orthonormal frame. The corresponding rank-(s+1) probability q(Y)=det Q[Y,Y] has the same identity.

There exists a coupling mu(X,Y) with nonnegative entries, row marginal p, column marginal q, supported on X subset Y. Here is a self-contained proof of the only nontrivial transport fact. Choose an orthonormal basis u1,...,us of range(P), put x=u1 wedge ... wedge us and y=x wedge v. Their squared coordinate moduli are p and q. For a family F of s-subsets, let A_F be the orthogonal coordinate projection of x onto F, and let N(F) contain all (s+1)-subsets containing some member of F. A_F wedge v is supported on N(F), has norm at most ||A_F||, and, by the adjoint contraction identity and v perpendicular range(P),
  |<A_F wedge v,y>|=||A_F||^2.
Cauchy-Schwarz, projecting y onto N(F), gives
  p(F)=||A_F||^2 <= ||A_F|| sqrt(q(N(F))).
Hence p(F)<=q(N(F)), including the zero case. These are exactly the weighted Hall conditions. The finite capacitated max-flow/min-cut theorem supplies mu.

For every coupled X subset Y, let c be the single member of Y\X. The corresponding two vertex polynomials satisfy
 (1-tau)[product_X(a_i+b1) product_outsideX(a_i+b0)]
 +tau[product_Y(a_i+b1) product_outsideY(a_i+b0)]
 =product_X(a_i+b1) (a_c+b2) product_outsideY(a_i+b0).
Thus pairing the two projection expansions with the SAME nonnegative mu produces exactly the desired permutation-product convex combination. The weights depend only on P,v, hence U and the index groups, not on any spectral value. The case delta=0 follows directly from rank-one MOC or by continuity; coincidences and singular sums cause no issue because all identities are polynomial.

Scope: this proves a whole all-dimensional family, not unrestricted MOC. At n=4 it reproduces repeated-eigenvalue cases; n=5 multiplicities (2,2,1) are the first case not covered merely by the n-2 multiplicity condition. No global priority claim is made. Multiple exceptional eigenvalues require a compatible coupling on a branching family of subset inclusions; pairwise nested couplings alone do not establish that.

3. FIRST-JET NECESSARY CONDITION: PROVED IMPLICATION, NOT A SUFFICIENT THEOREM

For real a,b, C=diag(a)+Udiag(b)U*, D=det C is real. If unrestricted MOC holds, then
 (D, partial_a D, partial_b D)
 belongs to the convex hull of
 (z_pi, partial_a z_pi, partial_b z_pi).
Proof: choose arbitrary real c,h,k; perturb a to a+i epsilon h and b to b+i epsilon k, and choose support parameter eta=-i+epsilon c. Both determinant and each permutation product are holomorphic in spectral entries, with real Taylor coefficients at real data. The coefficient of epsilon in the support inequality is
 cD+h.partial_a D+k.partial_b D
 <= max_pi(c z_pi+h.partial_a z_pi+k.partial_b z_pi).
This holds for every real functional if MOC holds, giving the joint convex hull. Conversely, a strictly separating first-jet functional would produce an actual MOC counterexample for all sufficiently small positive epsilon, by Taylor expansion with a strict margin. That converse concerns failure only; first-jet feasibility alone does not prove MOC.

For nonsingular C, gradients are diag(adj C) and diag(U*adj C U); at singular C use polynomial cofactors directly. Vertex gradients are products with one factor omitted, avoiding division at zeros.

Executed numerical LP probes:300 random n4,150 random n5,50 random n6 instances, real mixed-sign spectra and complex Haar unitaries. Every first-jet LP was feasible. These runs are evidence only. Script gradient_hull_probe.py records the experiment; no theorem of universal feasibility was proved.
