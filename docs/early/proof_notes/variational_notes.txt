VARIATIONAL TRACK: A COMPLETE SMALL-SPECTRUM POSITIVE THEOREM
17 October 2026. Derived in this session; no priority claim.

This note does NOT prove unrestricted MOC. It proves MOC on an explicit,
nonempty open class in every fixed dimension. Its new input relative to the
volume's stability method is a complete assignment-face classification at a
scalar-spectrum limit. All assertions below concern actual determinant data.

THEOREM.
Let n>=2, a_i in C\{0}, beta_j in C, and x_i=1/a_i. Suppose the n! numbers

             s_pi = sum_i x_i beta_{pi(i)}

are pairwise distinct and no three are collinear in the real plane C. Then
there is epsilon>0 such that for every complex t with |t|<epsilon and every
unitary U,

 det(diag(a)+t U diag(beta) U*)
       belongs to conv{ product_i(a_i+t beta_{pi(i)}): pi in S_n }.

The hypothesis is satisfied on a nonempty open dense subset of (a,beta) in
(C\{0})^n x C^n. Thus for n>=3 this gives genuinely complex, noncollinear
instances. Appendices A--C give explicit radius certificates, including the
certified radius 10^(-12) for the exact four-dimensional example below.

1. THE NORMALIZED LIMIT IS AN ASSIGNMENT FUNCTIONAL.
Write d=product_i a_i and

 G_t(U) = [det(diag(a)+t U diag(beta)U*)/d - 1]/t   (t nonzero).

The exterior-minor expansion extends G_t polynomially to t=0:

 G_t(U)=sum_{k=1}^n t^{k-1}
        sum_{|I|=|J|=k} x_I beta_J |det U[I,J]|^2,

so G_0(U)=sum_ij x_i beta_j |U_ij|^2. For t nonzero, the affine complex map
w -> (w/d-1)/t preserves convex-hull membership. It therefore suffices to
prove G_t(U) is in conv{G_t(P_pi)}.

Fix a unit complex support phase eta. At t=0 the real objective is
F_{eta,0}(U)=sum_ij c_ij |U_ij|^2 with c_ij=Re(eta x_i beta_j).
The matrix (|U_ij|^2) is doubly stochastic, so its maximum equals
h_{eta,0}=max_pi Re(eta s_pi). The hypothesis implies precisely one or two
optimal permutations, since a line supports at most two of the s_pi.

2. THE TWO OPTIMAL PERMUTATIONS DIFFER BY ONE CYCLE.
After relabeling, suppose the optimal permutations are id and rho. If rho
has two or more disjoint nontrivial cycles, apply each cycle separately to
id. Each such partial permutation has nonnegative deficit from h, and
the sum of their deficits is the deficit of rho, which is zero. Therefore
each partial permutation is also optimal. This contradicts having exactly
two optimal permutations. Hence rho is a single cycle, of length k>=2.

3. STRICT ASSIGNMENT DUAL SLACKS.
Let E be the union of the edges of the one or two optimal permutations.
There exist row and column potentials r_i,q_j such that

 c_ij <= r_i+q_j,
 r_i+q_j-c_ij=0 on E,
 r_i+q_j-c_ij>=gamma>0 outside E,
 sum_i r_i+sum_j q_j=h.

Here is an elementary strictness argument using ordinary finite LP duality.
Every nonoptimal permutation has deficit at least delta>0. Choose
0<gamma<delta/n, and replace c_ij by c_ij+gamma outside E. The same optimal
permutations still maximize and have the same value h. A dual solution for
the modified assignment problem has nonnegative modified slacks. Its
original slacks are at least gamma outside E. Equality on every edge in E
follows by summing original slacks along the optimal permutation containing
that edge. (If there are no nonoptimal permutations, n=2 and the full orbit
is already a two-dimensional solved block.)

Consequently the exact support deficit is

 h-F_{eta,0}(U)=sum_ij d_ij |U_ij|^2
                    >=gamma L_E(U),
 L_E(U)=sum_{(i,j) not in E}|U_ij|^2.

4. COMPLETE CLASSIFICATION OF THE UNITARY EQUALITY SET.
By strict slacks, equality holds precisely when U is supported on E.
If there is one optimal permutation, these are its monomial phase torus.
If there are two and their cycle has length 2, these are a full U(2) block
on that cycle together with scalar phase blocks at the other indices.
If the cycle has length >=3, only the two monomial phase tori occur.

To verify the last assertion, every doubly stochastic matrix supported on
E is in the assignment face, namely the segment between the two optimal
permutation matrices. If it is a strict mixture, every row along the cycle
has both its allowed entries nonzero. Consecutive cycle rows have exactly
one allowed column in common, so their unitary inner product cannot be
zero. Thus the strict mixture is not unistochastic. This proves the claim.

Denote this compact equality manifold (possibly disconnected) by S. Each
connected component is either a monomial phase torus or a U(2) block with
scalar phase blocks. In particular every component is a solved block
family for the actual determinant in every dimension.

5. STRICT NEGATIVE NORMAL HESSIAN AT THE LIMIT.
For U0 in S and a skew-Hermitian tangent generator K,

 L_E(e^{sK}U0)=s^2 sum_{(i,j) not in E}|(KU0)_ij|^2+O(s^3).

The displayed quadratic vanishes exactly on the tangent space of S:

* One permutation: after phases and relabeling U0=I, only diagonal K can
  have no forbidden entries.
* Transposition: E is exactly a 2x2 diagonal block plus singleton blocks.
  Since U0 is block unitary, KU0 is supported on E iff K is block diagonal,
  which is precisely tangency to S.
* Longer cycle: at either monomial U0, an additional allowed off-diagonal
  edge lacks its transpose in E. Skew-Hermiticity forces that entry to
  vanish as soon as all forbidden entries vanish. Thus only diagonal K
  remains, again exactly tangent to the phase torus.

On the normal complement, compactness of the unit normal bundle gives a
uniform positive lower bound for this quadratic. The exact slack formula
then makes the Hessian of F_{eta,0} uniformly negative definite in all
normal directions. This argument deals explicitly with the long-cycle
case; it does not assume all linear assignment maximizers are isolated.

6. NORMAL FIRST DERIVATIVES VANISH IDENTICALLY FOR ALL PERTURBATIONS.
At every U0 in S the matrix diag(a)+t U0 diag(beta)U0* is block diagonal
with the corresponding block partition. A tangent direction normal to the
block family has off-block K, so the first variation
t[K,U0 diag(beta)U0*] is off-block. The adjugate of a block-diagonal matrix
is block diagonal, including singular cases by polynomial continuation.
The derivative of the determinant, tr(adj(C) delta C), is therefore zero.

For monomial phase tori the same argument uses singleton blocks. It follows
that the normal derivative of G_t and of every real support F_{eta',t}
vanishes identically on S, for every t and eta'. The assertion at t=0
follows either directly or by polynomial continuation.

7. QUADRATIC ERROR DOMINATION, NOT JUST CONTINUITY.
Use normal exponential coordinates U=e^K U0 near S. Section 5 gives,
after taking a sufficiently small tubular neighborhood,

 F_{eta,0}(e^K U0)-F_{eta,0}(U0) <= -c ||K||^2

uniformly on S, with c>0. Define E_t=G_t-G_0. Its polynomial expansion has
a factor t, and its first normal derivative on S is zero by Section 6.
Taylor's theorem with an integral remainder and compactness therefore give

 |E_t(e^K U0)-E_t(U0)| <= C |t| ||K||^2.

Likewise the change in support phase contributes at most
C' |eta'-eta| ||K||^2. These are quadratic bounds, crucially stronger than
a plain O(t) bound near the equality locus. Since the leakage quadratic is
uniformly positive on normals, locally ||K||^2 <= C'' L_E(e^K U0); hence
the perturbation error is also bounded by C'''|t| times forbidden mass.

For |t| and |eta'-eta| sufficiently small, it follows that

 F_{eta',t}(e^K U0) <= F_{eta',t}(U0).

On a monomial family U0 is already a permutation value. On a U(2) block,
the determinant is a convex combination of its two block permutation
values, with weights |(U0)_11|^2 and |(U0)_12|^2; the exterior singleton
factors multiply both endpoints identically. Therefore in either case

 F_{eta',t}(U0) <= max_pi F_{eta',t}(P_pi).

The complement of the chosen tube is compact and has a strictly positive
base support gap. Uniform continuity preserves that gap for the same kind
of sufficiently small perturbations. This proves the desired inequality
for every U, for all eta' in a neighborhood of eta, and all sufficiently
small complex t.

8. UNIFORMITY OVER EVERY SUPPORT PHASE.
The unit support circle is compact. Finitely many phase neighborhoods from
Section 7 cover it. Take the minimum of their positive t-radii. Every phase
then satisfies the support inequality for every |t| below that minimum.
The support characterization of planar convex hulls gives the theorem.
The t=0 original determinant is the single value d, so it is also covered.

9. GENERICITY OF THE HYPOTHESIS.
For distinct permutations pi,rho the polynomial
s_pi-s_rho = sum_ij (P_pi-P_rho)_ij x_i beta_j is not identically zero.
For three distinct permutations pi,rho,tau, suppose the real polynomial
Im[(s_rho-s_pi) conjugate(s_tau-s_pi)] vanished identically. On a connected
complex open set where s_tau-s_pi is nonzero, the holomorphic rational
function (s_rho-s_pi)/(s_tau-s_pi) would be real-valued, hence constant,
with a real constant c. Polynomial continuation would imply
P_rho-P_pi=c(P_tau-P_pi). But no three distinct vertices of the Birkhoff
polytope are collinear: one would lie on the segment between the others
and fail to be an extreme point. This is a contradiction.

Thus each forbidden equality/collinearity is a proper real-algebraic zero
set. A finite union of them has empty interior; its complement is open
dense and nonempty. Intersecting with x_i!=0 preserves this conclusion,
and inversion x_i=1/a_i is a homeomorphism on that open domain.

10. EXACT NONEMPTY FOUR-DIMENSIONAL EXAMPLE.
Take
x=(16+27i, 14-7i, 16+20i, -19-27i), a_i=1/x_i,
beta=(-13+8i, -22+11i, 6+25i, -22+6i).
In lexicographic permutation order the 24 assignment sums are
(-479+1085i, -566-896i, -157+301i, 78+126i, -701-886i, -379+920i,
 -599+785i, -686-1196i, -178+238i, -399-255i, -722-949i, -856+539i,
 -697+981i, -462+806i, -598+1218i, -819+725i, -498+1053i, -954+735i,
 -651-1196i, -329+610i, -552-959i, -686+529i, 92+63i, -364-255i).
They are distinct. For every one of the 2024 triples, the integer
Im[conjugate(s_j-s_i)(s_k-s_i)] is nonzero; its minimum absolute value is
315. The companion script variational_example_check.py verifies this using
Python integer arithmetic only.
The four-point concyclicity determinant with rows
(|z_i|^2,Re z_i,Im z_i,1) is 173208 for x and 36750 for beta.
Both are nonzero. Complex inversion preserves generalized circles, so a=1/x
is also nonconcyclic; nonzero scaling preserves this property for t beta.
Thus the example is outside the classes where both spectra lie on circles
or lines, for every t!=0.

11. WHAT THIS DOES NOT DO.
The theorem supplies actual convex-hull membership in a full neighborhood
of a scalar-spectrum limit along every generic fixed spectral shape. It
does not continue that neighborhood to t=1, and no such continuation is
claimed. Beyond the controlled neighborhood a non-block equality locus or
loss of normal coercivity may occur. The unrestricted conjecture is not
proved by this note.

APPENDIX A. A GLOBAL QUANTITATIVE PERTURBATION CERTIFICATE.

The following strengthens the local tube argument. Equip U(n) with its
bi-invariant Frobenius Riemannian metric. Keep a base phase eta0 and its
one/two optimal assignment permutations, allowed edges E, solved equality
manifold S, and forbidden mass L=L_E(U). If there are nonoptimal
permutations, put

 delta=min_{pi nonoptimal} [h_{eta0,0}-Re(eta0 s_pi)]>0,
 gamma=delta/n,
 C_n=(pi^2/2)(1+12n).

Then the explicit fixed-phase certificate is

 B_1 |eta-eta0| + sum_{k=2}^n B_k |t|^{k-1} <= 2 gamma/C_n,       (A1)
 B_k=4k^2 e_k(|x_1|,...,|x_n|) e_k(|beta_1|,...,|beta_n|),

where |eta|=|eta0|=1. Under (A1), the entire actual support inequality for
G_t is valid in direction eta. This is a proved sufficient condition with
explicit constants, not an equivalent unsolved condition.

A.1. The leakage bound needs no LP duality.
By a Birkhoff decomposition, p_ij=|U_ij|^2=sum_pi lambda_pi (P_pi)_ij.
The base objective gap is at least delta times the total weight of the
nonoptimal permutations. Every optimal permutation has no forbidden edge,
and every other permutation has at most n forbidden edges. Therefore

 h_{eta0,0}-F_{eta0,0}(U) >= (delta/n) L_E(U)=gamma L_E(U).

A.2. A global distance estimate.
For all U,

                     dist(U,S)^2 <= C_n L_E(U).                (A2)

For a single monomial family, or a U(2)-plus-singletons family, mask U to
its allowed diagonal blocks, obtaining W. Each block is a contraction.
Choose a unitary polar factor in each block, giving V in S. If sigma_l
are all block singular values, then

 ||U-V||_F^2 = L + sum_l(1-sigma_l)^2
             <= L + sum_l(1-sigma_l^2)=2L.

The geodesic distance between any two unitaries is at most pi/2 times
their Frobenius chord distance, since theta^2 <= (pi^2/4)|e^{i theta}-1|^2
for |theta|<=pi. This proves dist(U,S)^2<=pi^2 L/2 in the block cases.

For a single cycle of length k>=3, relabel so allowed cycle entries are
U_ii and U_i,i+1 (cyclic indices). Put d_i=|U_ii|^2, c_i=|U_i,i+1|^2,
and let r_i and q_i be the forbidden mass in cycle row i and column i.
Row/column normalization gives

 d_i+c_i=1-r_i, d_i+c_{i-1}=1-q_i,
 |c_i-c_j|<=sum_l(r_l+q_l)<=2L.

For consecutive cycle rows the masked row vectors W_i,W_{i+1} share exactly
one column. Orthogonality of the full rows gives

 |W_i W_{i+1}*| <= sqrt(r_i)+sqrt(r_{i+1}),
 c_i d_{i+1} <=2(r_i+r_{i+1})<=2L.

Writing c=c_1, we have d_2>=1-c-3L, so c(1-c)<=5L. Consequently
min(c,1-c)<=10L. If c<=10L, every c_i<=12L; if 1-c<=10L, every d_i<=12L.
Choose the corresponding identity or cycle permutation. Its total
off-permutation mass is at most (1+12k)L. Choosing phases to match the
selected entries gives a monomial V with

 ||U-V||_F^2=2 sum_i(1-|U_i,pi(i)|)
            <=2 sum_i(1-|U_i,pi(i)|^2)
            <=2(1+12n)L.

The same chord/geodesic inequality proves (A2). The bound holds globally,
including large L; its looseness is harmless for a positive certificate.

A.3. Explicit second derivative bound.
For f_IJ(U)=|det U[I,J]|^2 with |I|=|J|=k, exterior powers show that along
U(s)=e^{sK}U, K skew-Hermitian,

 |d^2/ds^2 f_IJ(U(s))| <=4k^2 ||K||_F^2.

Indeed the induced exterior generator has operator norm at most
k||K||_op<=k||K||_F, and differentiating the squared matrix coefficient
twice gives 2|z'|^2+2Re(z'' conjugate(z)), with |z|<=1. Summing the
absolute spectral coefficients gives the B_k above.

Let H=F_{eta,t}-F_{eta0,0}. It follows that every geodesic directional
second derivative of H is bounded in absolute value by

 M ||K||_F^2,
 M=B_1|eta-eta0|+sum_{k=2}^n B_k|t|^{k-1}.

Choose V in S closest to U in the Riemannian distance, and a minimizing
geodesic from V to U. Its initial velocity is normal to S (first variation
of length; S is compact and each component is a smooth block subgroup
coset). Section 6 shows the first derivative of H along that normal is
zero. Taylor's integral remainder over the full geodesic therefore gives

 H(U)-H(V) <= (M/2)dist(U,S)^2 <=(M C_n/2)L.

Since F_{eta0,0}(V)=h_{eta0,0}, Section A.1 now gives

 F_{eta,t}(U)-F_{eta,t}(V) <=(-gamma+M C_n/2)L <=0

under (A1). Every V in S satisfies actual MOC by its at-most-two-dimensional
blocks, so the support inequality follows. If n=2 and both permutations
are optimal, S=U(2) and MOC already holds without this certificate.

A.4. Consequence.
At each phase eta0, (A1) provides an explicit positive disk in t and an
explicit phase neighborhood. A finite subcover still yields the complete
small-spectrum theorem. Calculating a convenient finite cover and its
radius is a numerical certification task. Appendix B avoids that task by
checking only the finitely many edge normals of the perturbed polygon and
provides a numerical certified radius for the exact example.

APPENDIX B. FINITE EDGE CERTIFICATES AND AN EXPLICIT RADIUS.
The edge-normal method in this appendix was suggested by the root agent
and checked jointly with this track. It removes the phase-cover computation.

B.1. Definitions and sufficient radius conditions.
Assume n>=3 and the theorem's generic hypothesis. Let

 alpha=min_{distinct pi,rho,tau}
       |Im(conjugate(s_rho-s_pi)(s_tau-s_pi))| >0,
 D >= max_{pi,rho}|s_pi-s_rho|,
 l <= min_{pi!=rho}|s_pi-s_rho|,  l>0.

Let A_k=e_k(|x|)e_k(|beta|), B_k=4k^2 A_k,
S=sum_{k=2}^n A_k, T=sum_{k=2}^n B_k,
Q=4 B_1 S/l+T, and C>=C_n.
Larger nonnegative coefficient bounds A_k are equally valid.
If 0<r<=1 satisfies

                 4 D S r+4 S^2 r^2 < alpha/2,                (B1)
                 C r Q < alpha/(n D),                       (B2)

then MOC holds for the entire complex disk |t|<=r.
These are explicit sufficient bounds in the original spectral data.

B.2. Proof of preservation of the supporting edges.
At each permutation,
v_pi(t)=G_t(P_pi)=s_pi+error_pi(t), |error_pi(t)|<=R:=rS,
because |t|<=r<=1 and the determinant expansion is finite.
For any three points, the change in their oriented area determinant is at
most 4DR+4R^2: each of the two difference vectors changes by at most 2R
and each original difference vector has length at most D. Condition (B1)
therefore preserves every triple orientation sign. An edge is supporting
exactly when all other labeled points lie strictly on the same side of its
line, so the original and perturbed polygons have precisely the same
supporting edges and vertex labels.

For an original supporting edge between s_pi and s_rho, let eta0 be its
outward unit support direction. Its nonendpoint vertex gaps are the
absolute oriented triangle areas divided by the edge length, so

       delta_edge >= alpha/D, gamma_edge>=alpha/(nD).

Let eta(t) be the corresponding perturbed edge's outward unit direction.
The edge vector changes by at most 2R, and its original length is at least
l. For nonzero vectors, the difference between normalized directions is
at most twice the vector perturbation divided by original length. Hence

                    |eta(t)-eta0| <=4R/l.

The quantity M in (A1) is consequently at most

 B_1(4R/l)+sum_{k=2}^n B_k r^{k-1} <= r Q.

Condition (B2) is stronger than M<=2gamma_edge/C_n, so Appendix A proves
the support inequality for every perturbed polygon edge. A convex polygon
is the intersection of its edge half-planes. This proves full MOC for
|t|<=r without estimating an uncountable support family individually.

B.3. Certified numerical radius for Section 10's example.
Use integer upper bounds on absolute values:

 X=(43,21,36,46), Y=(21,33,31,28).

Then the respective products e_k(X)e_k(Y), k=1,2,3,4, are

 16498, 37059829, 15799252770, 899499740832.

The corresponding B_k are

 65992, 592957264, 568773099720, 57567983413248.

Therefore

 S=915336053431,
 T=58137349470232,
 Q=4 B_1 S+T=241677564701544440.

All s_pi are Gaussian integers, distinct, so l=1 is valid. From the listed
values max_pi(|Re s_pi|+|Im s_pi|)=1882, hence D=3764 is valid.
The exact orientation audit gives alpha=315. Since pi^2<10,
C_4=(pi^2/2)(1+48)<245, so take C=245.

Set r=10^(-22). Exact rational arithmetic verifies

 4 D S r+4 S^2 r^2 < 315/2,
 245 r Q < 315/(4*3764).

For scale, the two left sides are respectively less than 0.000001379 and
0.005922; the right sides are 157.5 and greater than 0.02092. Thus the
strict inequalities have substantial arithmetic slack.

CONCRETE COMPLETED CONCLUSION:
For the spectra a_i=1/x_i and beta from Section 10, for every unitary U in
U(4) and every complex t with |t|<=10^(-22), the original determinant lies
in the convex hull of all 24 spectral permutation products.

This is an explicit certified all-unitary/all-support result for a
genuinely complex four-dimensional family, not a sample of unitary tests.
The radius is deliberately conservative and is not asserted optimal.

APPENDIX C. IMPROVED CERTIFIED RADIUS 10^(-12) FOR THE SAME EXAMPLE.
This refinement uses the same proved inequalities and a nine-row exact
edge certificate. Its verification is variational_improved_radius_check.py.

In dimension four the full-size term of G_t is
t^3 x_1x_2x_3x_4 beta_1beta_2beta_3beta_4, constant in U. Subtracting this
translation changes neither convex-hull membership nor any derivative.
For the remaining normalized vertices, direct integer expansion gives
the coefficient bounds

 max_pi |[t]G_t(P_pi)| <=1366923,
 max_pi |[t^2]G_t(P_pi)| <=679895624,

where the bounds are actually the maximum complex L1 norms, hence valid
upper bounds on absolute values. Thus use

 R(r)=1366923 r+679895624 r^2

instead of the coarser rS. The first-order hull vertices in counterclockwise
order have zero-based lexicographic permutation indices

                 17,10,7,18,22,3,5,0,14.

The maximum L1 distance between any two first-order points is D=2502.
For each of the nine directed edges (u,v), let

 d=(Re(s_v-s_u),Im(s_v-s_u)),
 l_e=max(|d_1|,|d_2|), L_e=|d_1|+|d_2|,
 a_e=min_{j not u,v} det(d,s_j-s_u)>0,
 gamma_e=a_e/(4L_e).

The minimum of all a_e is 3320. For r=10^(-12), exact rational arithmetic
checks, separately at every edge,

 4D R(r)+4R(r)^2 < a_e,
 (245/2)[4 B_1 R(r)/l_e + B_2 r + B_3 r^2] < gamma_e.

Here B_1=65992, B_2=592957264, B_3=568773099720 as before.
The first inequalities keep every original edge strictly supporting and
therefore preserve the closed boundary cycle. The second inequalities are
Appendix A's global all-unitary support criterion at every corresponding
perturbed edge normal. Therefore the full convex-hull inclusion holds for
every complex |t|<=10^(-12), improving Appendix B's simpler radius.

For example the most restrictive original edge 17->10 has
gamma_e=415/958>0.43319, while the left side of the Hessian inequality is
less than 0.09889. All nine inequalities have strict exact margins.
This is a mathematical certificate using finite integer/rational checks;
it does not rely on optimization or sampling unitaries.
