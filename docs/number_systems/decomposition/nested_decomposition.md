# Nested common blocks and a rigorous extension to small coupling

This note distinguishes exact common reducing decompositions from independent
diagonalizations. It proves closure, gives quantitative perturbation bounds,
and supplies an exact four-dimensional example with an open neighborhood.
These are sufficient conditions for MOC, not a proof for arbitrary normal
pairs. No literature priority claim is made.

## 1. Exact nested closure, with explicit weights

Suppose normal A and B have a common orthogonal reducing decomposition

    C^n = H_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum H_r,
    A = A_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum A_r,
    B = B_1 direct-sum ... direct-sum B_r.

Let n_l=dim H_l. Suppose each leaf pair has a proved convex representation

    det(A_l+B_l) = sum_(pi in S_(n_l)) p_(l,pi) z_(l,pi),
    p_(l,pi)>=0,    sum_pi p_(l,pi)=1,
    z_(l,pi)=product_(j=1)^(n_l) (a_(l,j)+b_(l,pi(j))).

For a tuple of leaf permutations (pi_1,...,pi_r), let pi be the global
permutation preserving each leaf and acting there as pi_l. Set

    p_pi = product_l p_(l,pi_l),

and set p_pi=0 for every other global permutation. These weights are
nonnegative, sum to one, and satisfy

    det(A+B)
       = product_l det(A_l+B_l)
       = sum_(pi_1,...,pi_r) (product_l p_(l,pi_l))
                            (product_l z_(l,pi_l)).

Every product in the last sum is a genuine global MOC vertex. This proves
MOC for (A,B). Repeating the construction along any finite decomposition
tree gives the same formula: the weight at a final permutation is the
product of its leaf weights. No compatibility theorem beyond actual common
reducing subspaces is being assumed.

Permitted leaves include any previously proved class, for example:

- dimension at most three;
- one matrix having two distinct eigenvalues.

The leaf matrices remain normal because the subspaces are reducing.

Different leaves may independently undergo

    (A_l,B_l) -> (lambda_l A_l+t_l I,
                 lambda_l B_l-t_l I).

The leaf determinant and every leaf vertex are multiplied by lambda_l^n_l;
the same convex weights work. The case lambda_l=0 is trivial. Complex
conjugation also preserves a proved leaf identity. Degenerate eigenvalues
cause no difficulty: permutations label eigenvalue occurrences, not only
distinct values.

## 2. How to find legitimate common blocks

A projection P is a common reducing projection exactly when

    P=P*=P^2,      PA=AP,      PB=BP.

For finite normal matrices, A* is a polynomial in A on its finite spectrum;
therefore commutation with A already implies commutation with A*. The same
holds for B. Thus the joint commutant

    C={X: XA=AX and XB=BX}

is a finite-dimensional star algebra. The pair is irreducible if and only
if C consists only of scalar matrices. A nonscalar Hermitian X in C gives
a nontrivial reducing decomposition through its spectral projections.

The intrinsic decomposition comes from the finite-dimensional star algebra
generated by A and B. Up to unitary coordinates it has the form

    direct-sum_alpha (I_(m_alpha) tensor M_(d_alpha)(C)),

acting on direct-sum_alpha (C^(m_alpha) tensor C^(d_alpha)). Its minimal
central projections determine canonical isotypic pieces. Within a piece
having m_alpha>1, choosing a basis of the multiplicity space gives repeated
irreducible blocks; that choice is not canonical.

When A is diagonal with simple eigenvalues, every projection commuting with
A is diagonal in this basis. Form an undirected graph on {1,...,n}, placing
an edge i--j when B_ij or B_ji is nonzero. Its connected components are
exactly the finest common reducing coordinate blocks. For repeated
eigenvalues of A, this graph alone is insufficient: a reducing projection
may be nondiagonal within an eigenspace, so use the commutant.

If A=D_a and B=U D_b U*, a sufficient block certificate is

    U_(I_l,J_m)=0 for l!=m,       |I_l|=|J_l|.

After separate reindexing of rows and columns, U is block diagonal. Connected
components of the bipartite support graph of a unitary automatically have
equal row and column counts, and give such a decomposition. Independent
diagonalization of A and B supplies no such zero pattern: the relative
eigenbasis matrix U is precisely the remaining coupling.

For simple spectra and Haar-distributed U, all its entries are nonzero
almost surely. The pair therefore has no nontrivial common reducing blocks
almost surely. Exact block closure by itself consequently covers a measure
zero class in this model. Section 4 extends it to genuine open classes.

## 3. Tensor constructions that really reduce to leaves

Let C,D be commuting normal m by m matrices. Simultaneously diagonalize
them, with joint eigenvalue pairs (c_j,d_j).

For normal X,Y,

    C tensor X,     D tensor Y

have common blocks (c_j X,d_j Y). Consequently MOC follows whenever each
of these scaled leaf pairs is covered by a proved theorem. In particular,
this holds when X,Y have dimension at most three. It also holds for
leaves of any dimension if one of X,Y has at most two distinct eigenvalues.
One must not infer validity for all independently scaled leaves merely
from validity of a single pair (X,Y); that would be an additional
unsupported closure statement.

For the Kronecker-sum construction

    A=C tensor I + I tensor X,
    B=D tensor I + I tensor Y,

the common blocks are (c_j I+X,d_j I+Y). Thus dimension-at-most-three
leaves again give MOC in arbitrary total dimensions 2m and 3m. More generally
one needs validity for the shifted leaf pairs. If C,D do not commute, this
argument supplies no common blocks.

## 4. Strict interior is stable under small coupling

Let (A_0,B_0) be a normal reference pair already proved by nested blocks,
and let K_0 be its full permutation hull. Suppose

    closed disk(det(A_0+B_0), r) is contained in K_0,
    r>0.

Write M_0=||A_0||_op+||B_0||_op. Let A,B be normal and put

    eta=||A-A_0||_F+||B-B_0||_F.

Then the following sufficient condition proves MOC for (A,B):

    2 n (M_0+eta)^(n-1) eta <= r.               (1)

No common block structure is required of the perturbed pair.

### Determinant bound

If ||X||_op,||Y||_op<=R, multilinearity along the segment joining X and Y
and Hadamard's determinant inequality give

    |det X-det Y| <= n R^(n-1)||X-Y||_op.        (2)

Each of the n differentiated-column terms is bounded by R^(n-1)||X-Y||.
In the present setting the determinant changes by at most

    L=n(M_0+eta)^(n-1)eta.

### Matching the spectra without assuming Hermitian matrices

For normal matrices A,A_0 with orthonormal eigenbases, their overlap matrix
P_ij=|<u_i,v_j>|^2 is doubly stochastic, and

    ||A-A_0||_F^2 = sum_(i,j) P_ij |a_i-a^0_j|^2.

Birkhoff's theorem therefore supplies an eigenvalue matching with

    sum_i |a_i-a^0_pi(i)|^2 <= ||A-A_0||_F^2.

In particular the maximum matched distance is no larger than the Frobenius
distance. Apply this separately to A and B and relabel. Each permutation
vertex then changes by at most the same L, by telescoping its n factors.
Hence every supporting function of the new hull K is at least that of K_0
minus L. The new determinant changes by at most L. Condition (1) leaves
all supporting inequalities valid and proves inclusion.

This also proves that every reference pair having a strictly interior
determinant has an open neighborhood, within normal pairs, satisfying MOC.
The argument does not give an open neighborhood at a boundary determinant.

### Fixed spectra and a nearby relative eigenbasis

If A=D_a, B=D_b are fixed and U_0 is a reference relative eigenbasis with
strict interior margin r, then MOC holds whenever

    2n (||A||+||B||)^(n-1) ||B|| ||U-U_0||_op <= r.  (3)

Indeed, ||UBU*-U_0BU_0*||<=2||B||||U-U_0||. The vertex hull is unchanged,
so only the determinant error is needed. Any scalar can first be subtracted
from B in this difference bound, if that improves the norm.

More generally, allow labelled spectra a',b' and a new unitary U. Put

    eta=max_i|a'_i-a_i|+max_j|b'_j-b_j|,
    delta=||U-U_0||_op,
    R=||D_a||+||D_b||+eta.

A sufficient condition is

    n R^(n-1) [2eta+2||D_b||delta] <= r.         (4)

One copy of eta controls vertex motion; the other copy plus the unitary
term controls determinant motion. All spectral distances here are complex
moduli, not separate real and imaginary coordinate bounds.

### Obtaining a strict margin from the nested weights

If all displayed leaf weights are positive, their product weights are
positive on every displayed product vertex. Thus the determinant is in the
relative interior of their convex hull. If three of those product vertices
are noncollinear, it lies in the planar interior, giving r>0. In particular,
two nontrivial 2 by 2 leaves with generic complex spectra provide such
reference pairs. This turns exact nested closure into open families with
arbitrary sufficiently small off-block coupling.

## 5. Exact worked four-dimensional example

Take

    a=(0,1,2+i,3+i),
    b=(1,2,-1-i,-2),
    H=(1/sqrt(2))*[[1,1],[1,-1]],
    U_0=H direct-sum H,
    A_0=diag(a),    B_0=U_0 diag(b) U_0*.

The first block has permutation determinants 3 and 4, and its actual
determinant is 7/2. After an opposite scalar shift by 2+i, the second block
has spectra (0,1) and (1,i); its permutation determinants are 1+i and 2i,
and its actual determinant is (1+3i)/2. Therefore

    d_0=det(A_0+B_0)=(7+21i)/4
       =(1/4)[(3+3i)+6i+(4+4i)+8i].

The four displayed products are legitimate global permutation vertices.
Writing z=x+iy, their convex hull Q is exactly

    x>=0,       y>=x,       6<=x+y<=8.

At d_0=(7/4,21/4), its distances to the four sides are

    7/4,       7/(2sqrt(2)),       1/sqrt(2),       1/sqrt(2).

Thus a disk of radius r=1/sqrt(2) centered at d_0 lies in Q and hence in the
full MOC hull. Both spectra are simple; neither is collinear or concyclic;
none of the sixteen sums a_i+b_j vanishes. The proof uses the actual
two-block structure, with two-dimensional leaves.

Since ||A_0||=sqrt(10), ||B_0||=2, the following is an explicit open-class
certificate:

    A,B normal,
    ||A-A_0||_F <= 1/5000,
    ||B-B_0||_F <= 1/5000
                 imply MOC for (A,B).           (5)

Indeed eta<=1/2500, and M_0+eta<21/4. The bound in (1) is at most

    8*(21/4)^3*(1/2500)=9261/20000<7/10<1/sqrt(2).

Alternatively, a labelled-spectra/eigenbasis version is

    max_i|a'_i-a_i| <= 1/10000,
    max_j|b'_j-b_j| <= 1/10000,
    ||U-U_0||_op <= 1/10000
                 imply MOC for (diag(a'), U diag(b') U*).  (6)

The total bound in (4) is again at most 9261/20000. These neighborhoods
include generic fully coupled normal pairs, not just exact block matrices.

There is also a uniform version over a family of block unitaries. For the
first block write its determinant as 3+p, and for the second as
(1-q)(1+i)+q(2i), with p,q in [1/4,3/4]. Their product has margin at least
sqrt(2)/4 in Q. The same reasoning proves inclusion for every such block
reference, spectral-coordinate perturbations at most 1/20000, and relative
unitary perturbation at most 1/20000. Its total error bound is
9261/40000<1/4<sqrt(2)/4.

Appending scalar leaves with nonzero sums multiplies d_0, Q, and the margin
by their product. The same construction and perturbation theorem therefore
give open normal-pair classes in every dimension n>=4.

## 6. Real and complex symmetry: what it proves

If a complex symmetric matrix A=A^T is normal, write A=X+iY with X,Y real
symmetric. Direct expansion shows

    AA*=A*A  iff  XY=YX.

Commuting real symmetric X,Y have a common real orthogonal eigenbasis.
Consequently every complex symmetric normal matrix is real-orthogonally
diagonalizable. Two such matrices can therefore be compared using a real
orthogonal relative eigenbasis. Their four real symmetric parts need not
all commute, so they need not have common reducing blocks. Nor is a complex
symmetric normal matrix necessarily Hermitian; for example diag(1,i) is not.
This symmetry classification alone does not prove unrestricted MOC for the
real-orthogonal relative eigenbasis case.

There is a useful stronger symmetry condition. Let J,K be antiunitary
conjugations, meaning J^2=K^2=I, and assume both make A and B symmetric:

    JAJ=A*,   JBJ=B*,   KAK=A*,   KBK=B*.

Then the linear unitary S=JK commutes with A and B. If S is nonscalar, its
spectral subspaces give genuine common reducing blocks. If J,K commute,
S is a self-adjoint involution and its +1 and -1 spaces are the blocks.
A commuting family of such conjugations gives commuting linear
involutions relative to one fixed conjugation, and joint sign spaces give
a nested decomposition. MOC follows when the resulting leaves are covered
by a proved class. One conjugation alone need not yield any such blocks.
