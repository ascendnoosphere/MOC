# Determinantal ranges across number systems

Research notebook and exact certificates — 16 October 2026.

The original complex Marcus–de Oliveira conjecture is **not proved or
disproved by this notebook**. The results below resolve specified analogues,
identify restrictions equivalent to the original problem, and give a
quantitative open class of complex normal pairs satisfying the desired
inclusion. No claim of publication priority is made.

In the ring and adele rows, similarity means U B U^(-1). It is a different
group from the unitary group. Finite-field affine hulls and ring ideals
do not impose nonnegative real weights.

The coefficient-descent theorem in
`rings/COEFFICIENT_DESCENT_AND_CHARACTERISTIC_TWO.md` unifies the affine
constructions without assuming an integral trace projection. Its positive
real counterpart fails even arbitrarily close to the identity: see
`../theorems/profile_geometry/algebra_local_marginal_obstruction.md` for an
explicit rational SO(4) path and its quartic separating gap. This is an
obstruction to simultaneous spectrum-independent weights, not to MOC itself.


## The exact order-two mixing formula

For diagonal A and B over a quadratic extension L/K with its involution,
write z_0 and z_1 for the two permutation products. If U is unitary,

\[
\det(A+UBU^*)=(1-s)z_0+s z_1,\qquad s=N(U_{12}).
\]

Every s with s=N(b), 1−s=N(d) is attained, using

\[
U=\begin{pmatrix}\bar d&b\\-\bar b&d\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The same identity has different coefficient sets:

- C/R: the interval [0,1].
- F_(q²)/F_q: the entire field F_q.
- Unramified quadratic local extension, integral entries: the s in O_K
  for which v(s) and v(1−s) are even nonnegative integers or infinity.

In the last case the additive Haar measure is (q−1)/(q+1). This is additive
measure on the norm-parameter set, not the probability law obtained by
sampling the unitary group.



## Arithmetic restrictions that do not simplify complex MOC

For each fixed dimension, full complex MOC is equivalent to its restriction
to Gaussian-rational spectra, Gaussian-integer spectra, globally primitive
Gaussian-integer spectral tuples, or Gaussian-prime spectra. The relative
unitary may additionally be required to have exactly Gaussian-rational
entries. Normal pairs with Gaussian-integer matrix entries also retain
the full problem. Prime **matrix entries** are not included in the prime
equivalence.

The prime reduction uses Tao's proved constellation theorem. For integer
spectra apply it to {a_i,−b_j}, obtaining x+r{a_i,−b_j} consisting of primes.
Then a'_i=x+r a_i and b'_j=r b_j−x are Gaussian primes and

\[
d'=r^n d,\qquad z'_\pi=r^n z_\pi.
\]

Density and denominator clearing finish the equivalence. Repeated spectral
values are allowed. This argument uses variable dilations, not unproved
fixed-gap twin-prime assertions.

The displayed constellation is checked by exhaustive integer trial
division of eight norms and exact checks of all sixteen sums and twenty-four
vertices. Its original spectra are

\[
a=(-3+3i,i,2+2i,1-6i),\qquad b=(2-3i,-2-3i,0,6i),
\]

with r=10 and x=−1680−1031i. The transformation preserves the shape of
{a_i,−b_j} up to translation and scaling; it does not establish MOC for
arbitrary mixing at the prime spectra.



Real prime, twin-prime, composite, or superabundant **eigenvalues** give
Hermitian matrices and are covered by the established Hermitian theorem.
Real prime **entries** need not give Hermitian matrices. A global gcd=1
condition on Gaussian spectra is equivalent to the full conjecture;
pairwise coprimality is a different condition and is not settled here.

Changing only a numeral radix changes no values. Changing the value field
to Q(β,i), or to Q(β) for nonreal β, retains a dense complex spectral set
and therefore the full difficulty. For any real-closed ordered field R,
fixed-dimensional MOC over R(i) has exactly the same truth value as over C,
by first-order transfer. This statement also applies to real-closed
infinitesimal fields; it is not a transfer theorem for p-adic fields.

See arithmetic/arithmetic_reductions.md and
arithmetic/representation_invariance.md for proofs, including how to
transport the adjoint under a nonorthonormal change of basis.

## Nested decomposition with a controlled residual

If both matrices reduce along the same orthogonal decomposition and each
leaf determinant has convex weights p_(leaf,π), the global weights are
the products of the leaf weights. This is an exact construction. Independent
decompositions of A and B cannot be combined in this manner.

A strict planar interior margin extends a proved block example to a
neighborhood of fully coupled normal pairs. Let a reference pair (A_0,B_0)
have a disk of radius r about det(A_0+B_0) contained in its permutation
hull. Put

\[
M_0=\|A_0\|_{op}+\|B_0\|_{op},\quad
\eta=\|A-A_0\|_F+\|B-B_0\|_F.
\]

For normal A and B, the sufficient condition is

\[
2n(M_0+\eta)^{n-1}\eta\le r.
\]

The proof matches normal spectra by the overlap/Birkhoff argument, bounds
each vertex movement by L=n(M_0+η)^(n−1)η, and bounds the determinant
movement by the same L. Every support inequality then retains margin r−2L.

An explicit reference is

\[
A_0=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,2+i,3+i),\quad
B_0=(H\oplus H)\operatorname{diag}(1,2,-1-i,-2)(H\oplus H)^*,
\quad H=2^{-1/2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Its determinant is (7+21i)/4. Four legitimate product vertices are
3+3i, 6i, 4+4i, and 8i. Their hull contains a disk of radius 1/√2 about
the determinant. Consequently **every normal pair** with

\[
\|A-A_0\|_F\le1/5000,\qquad \|B-B_0\|_F\le1/5000
\]

satisfies det(A+B) in its own permutation hull. This includes irreducibly
coupled pairs with perturbed spectra. It is a fixed-pair neighborhood
theorem, not a claim that the same spectra are settled for every U.
Appending scalar blocks with nonzero sums gives such open classes in
all dimensions at least four. The theorem does not provide a global
50% measure bound for generic complex spectra and eigenbases.
