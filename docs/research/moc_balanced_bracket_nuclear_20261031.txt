Balanced compact spinors give an extremal rank-two bracket matrix
31 October 2026

This exact consequence of compact minimax balance is not a proof of MOC.
It retains the actual spinor factorization rather than replacing it with
generic stable-polynomial data.

Let S=[u_1,...,u_n] and T=[v_1,...,v_n] be 2-by-n matrices with unit
columns, and let J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]. The actual bracket matrix is

  C=S^T J T,       C_ij=[u_i,v_j].

Write A=SS*, B=TT*. Both have trace n. At a hypothetical negative compact
minimum the proved moment equation is A+B=nI2. The elementary 2-by-2
identity J B J*=tr(B)I2-B^T then gives

  C C*=S^T A^T bar(S).                                      (1)

Put R=bar(S). Since RR*=A^T, the right side of (1) equals
R*(RR*)R. Its nonzero eigenvalues equal those of (RR*)^2. Hence, including
rank-one degeneracies, the nonzero singular values of C are precisely the
two eigenvalues of A. In particular

  rank C<=2,    ||C||_*=tr A=n.                            (2)

Here ||.||_* is nuclear norm. This is equality in the general bound

  ||S^T J T||_* <=||S^T||_F ||J T||_F=n.

The balanced factorization is therefore an extremal rank-two Schur
multiplier factorization. The inequality |C_ij|<=1 continues to hold.

Conversely, equality in that nuclear bound forces balance for unit-column
S,T. Indeed the nonzero singular values of C have the same sum as those
of the 2-by-2 matrix A^(T/2) (J B J*)^(1/2), whose trace norm is the
matrix fidelity between the positive matrices A^T and J B J*, both of
trace n. Equality in the Hilbert--Schmidt Cauchy--Schwarz characterization
of fidelity requires these two positive matrices to coincide, including
singular cases. Thus A^T=J B J*=nI-B^T, i.e. A+B=nI. The forward
implication (1)--(2) does not require importing this converse.

Concrete examples showing what (2) does and does not force

1. All row spinors are e1 and all column spinors e2. Then A=diag(n,0),
   C is the all-ones matrix, its sole nonzero singular value is n, and
   every matching product equals d=1. Thus nuclear norm n does not force
   spectral degeneracy that is fatal to the determinant.

2. For n=4, take row spinors e1,e1,e2,e2 and column spinors e2,e2,-e1,-e1.
   Then A=2I and C is block diagonal with two all-ones 2-by-2 blocks.
   Its singular values are 2,2,0,0. Matching products can be zero or one;
   the determinant inclusion here follows from the already proved
   two-projective-point boundary theorem.

3. For n=4 take row spinors e1,e2,(3/5,4/5),(3/5,4i/5) and define
   v_j=-J bar(u_j). Each pair u_j,v_j is orthonormal, so balance holds.
   The actual C is Hermitian positive semidefinite with diagonal1.
   A has eigenvalues 2+sqrt(337)/25 and 2-sqrt(337)/25, so those are
   exactly the two nonzero singular values of C. Neither equals2.
   Thus balance does not force the row or column frame individually
   tight, nor does it force C to be a scaled partial isometry.

Remaining full-target calculation

At a negative compact minimum, (2) must be imposed together with all
active matching contacts, the single common multiplier law, the tensor
singular-vector equations, and unitary maximizer stationarity. No theorem
has been established here comparing det(C Hadamard U)/det U with the
matching-product hull for all rank-two nuclear-extremal C. That comparison
cannot be assumed merely from (2). It is a possible new concrete input
to the coupled mixed-Hessian calculation.
