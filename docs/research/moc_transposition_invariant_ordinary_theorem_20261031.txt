Transposition-invariant nonnegative matching costs are ordinary
31 October 2026

THEOREM. If a pointwise nonnegative g in V14 is invariant under a row
transposition or a column transposition, then g belongs to the ordinary
matching cone. Therefore it is matching-SOS. In particular every actual
four-point support with a repeated eigenvalue in either spectrum is
ordinary, for every supporting phase and all other eigenvalues.

Proof. Use the proved complete82-ray decomposition of the nonnegative
matching cone. Average the decomposition with the transposition. The
average of an ordinary ray is a nonnegative ordinary combination.
Every mixed ray is moved to a DISTINCT mixed ray. The exact cancellation
certificates give

                       f_i+f_j=sum_k c_k r_k,
                               c_k>=0,

with coefficients exactly ONE on both mixed rays. Thus its average is
one half of that ordinary sum. The average of the original cost is g,
proving the theorem. No unequal-weight cancellation assumption is used.

The verifier replays the existing equal-pair identities on all1128
distinct mixed pairs, then checks all twelve row/column transpositions.
For each action it verifies the24 mixed two-cycles and their exact
ordinary averages. It also checks that ordinary rays remain ordinary.

There is an independent pencil explanation for the absence of a fixed
mixed ray. An odd row/column transposition acts on the universal inverse
pencil by

                   M(tau*g)=-R^T M(g)R,

where R is a symmetric orthogonal reflection with positive eigenspace
dimension6 and negative eigenspace dimension3. If tau*g=g, its matrix
is off-diagonal with respect to this6+3 splitting, so rank M(g)<=6.
Every mixed census ray has pencil rank9, hence none can be fixed. The
code checks this covariance on a complete14-feature basis for every
transposition, rather than inferring it from an unproved rank parity.

Reproduction:

Output:

The proof uses the previously certified82-ray completeness theorem and
the exact equal-pair cancellation identities. The new averaging and
covariance checks use rational arithmetic and no optimizer. This closes
a uniform symmetry family; it does not prove unrestricted MOC4.
