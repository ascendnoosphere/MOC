THEOREM. For arbitrary complex a0,a1,a2,a3 and eta, let

       g_pi=h-Re(eta*prod_i(a_i-a_pi(i))).

If all24 g_pi are nonnegative, then g is a nonnegative linear combination
of ordinary singleton and two-subset matching indicators. In particular
g is matching-SOS, since each such indicator squares to itself. This
proves MOC4 for the entire B=-A spectral family using the common-minor
nonnegativity identity. No restriction on phase or multiplicities is
needed. This is a restricted family theorem, not unrestricted MOC4.

1. A four-dimensional polyhedral relaxation

Put x=(a0-a1)(a2-a3), z=(a0-a3)(a1-a2). The third Pluecker product is
x+z. A product associated with a permutation with a fixed point is0.
The nine derangements have the six possible nonzero product formulas

                    x², z², (x+z)², -xz, x(x+z), z(x+z).

Let A=Re(eta*x²), B=Re(eta*xz), C=Re(eta*z²). Thus the24 scores are
drawn from

                0,A,C,A+2B+C,-B,A+B,B+C.

The nonnegative support costs form a cone in(h,A,B,C), defined by
h being at least each of these seven scores. We prove the stronger
statement that EVERY point in this polyhedral cone is ordinary. The
additional actual-source condition AC-B²<=0 is unnecessary.

The cone has exactly the following seven extreme rays. The second
column gives an ordinary decomposition of the associated24-value cost;

2. Why this family has actual pencil rank at most5

Use the universal source factorization. Write A0=L^T diag(a)L and
u=L^T diag(a)e0, with e0=1/2(1,1,1,1)^T. For a real matrix variable H,
put S=(H+H^T)/2 and K=(H-H^T)/2. Let axial([S,A0]) be the vector w
whose cross-product matrix is the skew matrix[S,A0]. Then

 Q_z(H)=vec(H)^T M(z)vec(H)
       =(u^T w)^2+u^T adj([K,A0])u.                         (1)

The checker verifies(1) coefficientwise in ALL four arbitrary complex
spectra and all nine H entries. There is no symmetric/skew mixed term.
The first summand is one complex square on the six-dimensional symmetric
sector; its real part after any phase has rank at most2. The second
is a quadratic on the three-dimensional real skew sector, so its real
part has rank at most3. Therefore

                       rank_R M(g)<=5

for every source and every phase, including degeneracies.

Generically the complex source column4-plane contains the entire real
skew3-space. One can see this from the source coefficient map
X -> A0 X-X A0 on symmetric matrices with Xu=0. Its domain has dimension3.
If X commutes with A0 and Xu=0, it annihilates u,A0u,A0²u. These span C3
whenever the a_i are distinct, as their determinant is a nonzero scalar
multiple of the Vandermonde product. Thus the map is injective and its
image is the whole skew3-space. This explains the three common complex
column directions underlying the generic real rank5, without a parity
assumption.
