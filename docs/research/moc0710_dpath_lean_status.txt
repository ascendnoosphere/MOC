The principal theorem d_path_from_admissible_start proves the missing strict
feasibility assertion for the canonical c=0 slice. Its hypotheses are
  a >= 0, b >= 0, a != b, d0 > 0, e0 >= 0,
  P(a,b,0,d0,e0) = 0, Q(d0,e0) < 0.
For every d between d0 and 1, the selected cap root reconstructs
  e(d) = cLift(a,b,capRoot(a,b,d),0),
and Lean proves S(a,b,d)>0, e(d)>0, P(a,b,0,d,e(d))=0, and Q(d,e(d))<0.
The theorem covers both directions d0<=1 and d0>=1, including d=1.

continuous_admissible_d_path proves continuity of the explicit path
  t |-> (d(t),e(d(t))), d(t)=(1-t)d0+t, t in [0,1].
admissible_d_path_start and admissible_d_path_end prove its exact endpoints.
d_path_coordinate_between places every parameter value in the interval used

from the initial admissibility assumptions, rather than assuming it.

The key new algebraic step is S_pos_of_A_neg. For ab<1, put r=ab-1<0 and
use A(r)=-ab*d*(a-b)^2<=0 together with
  A(r)*x-r*A(x) = (r-x)*(q*r*x+p*S).
If x>0 and A(x)<0, the assumption S<=0 makes the two sides have incompatible
signs. For d>1, comparison at x=p*(d-1) then proves
  Q(d,e(d))<0 iff K(a,b,d)<0.
Monotonicity of K transports this condition down to d=1. For d<=1,
the earlier S monotonicity lemma and positivity give the other branch.
