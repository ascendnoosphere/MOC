Drury's exceptional facet as a real-positive, non-SOS map
31 October 2026

The source is S.W. Drury, The external vertices conjecture in case n=4,
Electronic Journal of Linear Algebra 11 (2004),180--191, especially the
last expression in Section 5 for the exceptional ordinary-cone facet:
 https://journals.uwyo.edu/index.php/ela/article/download/263/263/263
The paper proves an external-halfspace result. Its normalized functional
does not furnish the missing bound for internal supporting halfspaces.

The following is a new exact calculation with that facet, not an extension
of its external-vertices theorem. Write
 u(f)=(f(2134)+f(3214)+f(1432)+f(4321)-f(1234))/3.
Use the real standard representation rho of S4 on the zero-sum subspace
of R4, in the orthonormal Hadamard basis
 (1,1,-1,-1)/2, (1,-1,1,-1)/2, (1,-1,-1,1)/2.
Define Phi_u(X)=sum_pi u_pi rho(pi) X rho(pi)^T.
On a real pure input y=(x,y,z), its matrix is

 (1/3) [ x^2+2z^2,  -xz,         -xy;
         -xz,        2y^2+z^2,   -yz;
         -xy,        -yz,         2x^2+y^2 ].

This matrix is positive semidefinite for every real x,y,z. Each of its
seven principal minors is a polynomial with nonnegative coefficients in
even monomials. In particular its determinant is
 4(x^4 y^2+x^2 y^2 z^2+x^2 z^4+y^4 z^2)/27.
Thus Phi_u is positive on real symmetric PSD matrices, by decomposing
an arbitrary such input into real pure states.

This explains why a simple family of new squares cannot remove the facet.
For real vectors p,q, the function h(pi)=p^T rho(pi)q has its square in
the 14-dimensional matching space: it is a matrix coefficient of Sym^2
rho=trivial directsum standard directsum[22]. But
 u(h^2)=p^T Phi_u(qq^T)p>=0.
Consequently no square from this rank-one standard-coefficient family
can separate the exceptional facet.

There is also an exact obstruction to treating the displayed real
biquadratic as a sum of squares of bilinear forms. After the input
permutation (u1,u2,u3)=(x,z,y), and dropping the positive factor 1/3, it is

 sum_i X_i^2 u_i^2
 +2(X1^2 u2^2+X2^2 u3^2+X3^2 u1^2)
 -2 sum_{i<j} X_i X_j u_i u_j.

The three anticyclic squared monomials X1^2 u3^2, X2^2 u1^2,
X3^2 u2^2 have coefficient 0. In any bilinear SOS, the corresponding
bilinear monomials must therefore be absent from every summand. The
mixed coefficients -2 X_i X_j u_i u_j can then arise only from the
three diagonal monomials X_i u_i. Their forced Gram principal block is
 [1,-1,-1; -1,1,-1; -1,-1,1],
which has negative Rayleigh quotient on (1,1,1). This contradicts PSD.
The real-positive biquadratic therefore is not bilinear SOS. This is the
same structural type as a Choi--Lam biquadratic obstruction; no claim
of equivalence of complex positive maps is used.

Indeed the complex-linear extension defined by the same five orthogonal
conjugations is not asserted positive. Its Choi matrix has exact
Rayleigh value -5/3 on vec(I3). That observation alone would not prove
the intrinsic bilinear-SOS obstruction, because extensions away from
real symmetric inputs can differ; the missing-monomial argument above
does prove it directly.

This identifies a concrete obstruction to an elementary rank-one-standard
SOS proof. A universal matching SOS construction must exploit more general
standard coefficients and their coupling to [22], or another mechanism.
No universal actual-support SOS containment theorem is established here.
