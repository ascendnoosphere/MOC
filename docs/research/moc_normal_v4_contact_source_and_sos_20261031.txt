1. A ten-coordinate face and its exact ordinary cone

Identify the four labels with F2^2, and its three nontrivial characters
with 1,2,3. Every permutation is affine: pi(x)=Lx+t. Its linear part
permutes the three nontrivial characters, giving sigma in S3. The signed
standard representation has entries rho31(pi)_(k,L^T k)=(-1)^(k.t).

Vanishing on all four translations removes the three diagonal standard
coefficients and relates the scalar to the trace of the [22] block.
Write the six remaining standard coefficients as a_ij, i!=j. The five
nonidentity quotient baselines are b0,b1,b2,c+,c-, with

                    c++c-=b0+b1+b2=:B.

The three b's correspond to quotient transpositions fixing axes0,1,2,
respectively. The c+ cycle is (0,1,2)->(1,2,0). The resulting 24x10
evaluation table spans exactly the contact-null matching space.

Pointwise nonnegativity is equivalent to three rectangle inequalities

 b0>=|a12|+|a21|, b1>=|a20|+|a02|, b2>=|a01|+|a10|,

and two tetrahedron families

 c+ +eps0*a01+eps1*a12+eps2*a20>=0,
 c- +eps0*a10+eps1*a21+eps2*a02>=0,
                    eps0 eps1 eps2=1.

The ordinary contact rays are exactly
 [17,18,19,20,22,24,25,27,28,29,32,33].
Each is a plus or minus coordinate ray a_ij with the appropriate
baseline allocation. Consequently the ordinary cone is described by
the same three rectangle inequalities and the STRONGER two bounds

        c+>=|a01|+|a12|+|a20|,
        c->=|a10|+|a21|+|a02|.                         (O)

For completeness, ordinary coefficients may be grouped into six total
weights u_ij>=|a_ij|. They must satisfy
 b0=u12+u21, b1=u20+u02, b2=u01+u10,
 c+=u01+u12+u20.
The possible sum in the last equation is the full interval
 [|a01|+|a12|+|a20|, B-|a10|-|a21|-|a02|].
Thus (O) and the rectangle inequalities are necessary and sufficient,
including every zero or equality case. Splitting u_ij into the two
nonnegative weights (u_ij+a_ij)/2 and (u_ij-a_ij)/2 constructs the
ordinary-ray certificate.

Equivalently, in either four-element three-cycle coset, each gap must be
at most the sum of the other three. For the c+ coset [3,11,12,20], one
of these ordinary facets is

                 L(g)=(g11+g12+g20-g3)/2>=0.           (2)

The eight mixed rays of this face are
 [35,40,51,56,58,65,74,81]. They account for the tetrahedral sectors
permitted by pointwise positivity but excluded by (O).

2. Compact restricted cubic and a checked secant line

Put
 M+=[[c+-b0,a01,a20],[a01,c+-b1,a12],[a20,a12,c+-b2]],

 F=2(det M+ +det M- +b0*b1*b2),

There is no uniform sign of F on this face: F(r35)=1, while
F(r17+r20+r22)=-1. Thus a sign or coefficient-positivity shortcut for

On the symmetric line g(t)=r35+t sum(all twelve ordinary contact rays),

Hence the only nonnegative secant point on this line is t=1/2, and it
is ordinary because (O) holds exactly when t>=1/3. This is a line
calculation, not a theorem for the full ten-dimensional face.

3. A rational actual-source chart from three linear equations

On the generic finite configuration chart, use the common PSL2 action
on the eight spinor labels to normalize a0=0,a1=1,a2=i. The action
multiplies every permutation product by the same nonzero complex
factor, absorbed into eta; it preserves all phased real scores.

For arbitrary complex b0,...,b3 put
 U_pi=product_(i=0,1,2)(a_i+b_pi(i)),
 V_pi=U_pi*b_pi(3).
Then z_pi=U_pi*a3+V_pi. Set w=eta*a3. The four contact scores agree
exactly when the three differences impose

 Re((U_k-U_0)w+(V_k-V_0)eta)=0, k=7,16,23.            (4)

This is a REAL LINEAR 3x4 system in
 (Re w,Im w,Re eta,Im eta), with rows
 (Re deltaU,-Im deltaU,Re deltaV,-Im deltaV).
At rank three its signed maximal cofactors give the unique projective
solution. If eta!=0 then a3=w/eta. Rational real and imaginary parts
of b therefore give rational real and imaginary parts of a3 and eta.
All twenty supporting inequalities can then be checked exactly.

This is more useful than numerically solving three quartic contact
equations in the original spectral variables. It gives an eight-real-
parameter rational chart, plus common real score scale. Rank-drop and
infinite-label strata require separate treatment in a uniform theorem;
they are not declared covered merely by the generic chart.

7. A full vector Gram criterion retaining all cross modules

Here is a uniform necessary-and-sufficient C0 criterion for an arbitrary
cost on this face. It is a finite PSD criterion, not yet a proof that the
actual source always satisfies it.

Order the three undirected axes so that
 p=(a01,a12,a20), q=(a10,a21,a02), beta=(b2,b0,b1).
There are matching features d,f_i,x_i,y_i, i=0,1,2, with values

 transposition coset i:
       d=0, f=e_i, x_i=eps, y_i=delta, all other x,y zero;
 plus three-cycle coset:
       d=1, f=(1,1,1), y=0, x=s, product(s_i)=1;
 minus three-cycle coset:
       d=-1, f=0, x=0, y=s, product(s_i)=1;
 contacts: all ten features zero.

These ten features form a basis of the contact-null V. Concretely x,y
are the respective offdiagonal standard coefficient functions, d is
the c+ chart column, and f_i is the corresponding beta column plus d.
The twelve ordinary rays are (f_i+/-x_i)/2 and
(f_i-d+/-y_i)/2, which also verifies the ordinary criterion in Section 1.

The cost g belongs to FULL C0 if and only if there are ten vectors

                 D,F0,F1,F2,X0,X1,X2,Y0,Y1,Y2

in a real inner-product space, of dimension at most ten, satisfying
the following twenty real scalar equations. Put G=D+F0+F1+F2.
For each i and the complementary pair {j,k}:

 beta_i=||F_i||^2+||X_i||^2+||Y_i||^2,
 p_i=2<F_i,X_i>, q_i=2<F_i,Y_i>, <X_i,Y_i>=0;

 c+=||G||^2+sum_i ||X_i||^2,
 p_i=2<G,X_i>+2<X_j,X_k>;

 c-=||D||^2+sum_i ||Y_i||^2,
 q_i=-2<D,Y_i>+2<Y_j,Y_k>.                            (G)

Proof: an SOS Gram supplies these vectors as the ten column factors.
On a transposition coset the squared norm is
||F_i+eps X_i+delta Y_i||^2. Comparing its four independent Boolean
characters gives the first twelve equations. On a three-cycle coset
use s_j s_k=s_i and compare the constant and three characters in
||G+sum s_i X_i||^2 or ||-D+sum s_i Y_i||^2. This gives the remaining
eight equations. Conversely those same comparisons check the gap at
all twenty noncontacts; contacts vanish identically. Factoring the
resulting ten-vector PSD Gram gives a full matching SOS. No other
point or Fourier equation is missing.

Criterion (G) retains cross terms among the four trivial features and
the six nontrivial-character features. In particular it is not a
block-diagonal or ordinary-ray ansatz. It exposes the remaining uniform
task as constructing these vectors from the rational actual source in
Section 3, or proving existence without choosing a restricted Gram.

This establishes a genuine distinction between the normal V4 case and
