Four bright roots and a four-dimensional dark normality problem
31 October2026

This note proves a universal reduction, not the desired universal
partial-transpose theorem. The remaining dark commutator vanishes at
the exact Q59/R3701 examples but has not been proved to vanish for
all real orthogonal mixers.

Let beta0,...,beta3 be distinct. For real coefficients c_pi define
rho=sum c_pi |v_pi><v_pi|, v_pi=tensor_i(1,beta_pi(i)). Assume rho>=0,
rankrho=8, and the leading first-qubit0 block A is invertible. Write
 rho=[[A,B],[B^*,D]].
Rank and positivity imply D=B^*A^{-1}B. Therefore rho^PT_first>=0
with rank8 iff
 D=B A^{-1}B^*,
equivalently K=A^{-1/2}B A^{-1/2} is normal.

Choose a square Gram factor rho=[R0;R1][R0;R1]^*, with R0 invertible.
Then A=R0R0^*, B=R0R1^*, and normality of K is equivalent to normality
of Z=R0^{-1}R1. In fact K is unitarily conjugate to Z^* by
U=A^{-1/2}R0. For the determinant family the universal Clifford factor
provides R0,R1 explicitly as rows indexed I without the first qubit,
and I with that qubit inserted.

Four known, orthogonal, reducing eigenvectors

For each j put qj=(-conjugate(beta_j),1) and uj=qj^tensor3. In the
three remaining qubits, uj^*v_rest is nonzero only when the omitted
label is j: otherwise one factor is orthogonal to beta_j. Hence,
term by term in the permutation expansion,
 B^*uj=beta_j Auj,
 uj^*Auk=0 for j!=k.
The structural product kernel qj^tensor4 gives
 R1^*uj=conjugate(beta_j)R0^*uj.
Consequently wj=R0^*uj satisfies BOTH
 Z wj=beta_j wj,
 Z^*wj=conjugate(beta_j)wj.
These vectors are nonzero and orthogonal. For actual determinant
amplitudes their squared norms are explicitly
 |O_0j|^2 product_{k!=j}|beta_k-beta_j|^2.
Thus their four-dimensional span is reducing and already normal,
with the four given beta eigenvalues. No full-PPT assumption was
used in this argument.

Fixed dark basis and remaining equation

The uj span Sym^3(C^2), independent of their particular distinct roots.
Let L be any fixed8x4 real basis of its orthogonal complement. One
choice consists of coefficient vectors(1,-1,0),(1,1,-2) on each of
the Hamming-weight1 and Hamming-weight2 sectors. Define
 X=B^*A^{-1}=R1R0^{-1}.
The identities above imply that X preserves rangeL. Set
 Td=(L^*L)^{-1}L^*X L,
 G=L^*A^{-1}L>0.
The transfer Z restricted to its dark complement is represented by
Td in the basis R0^{-1}L, with Gram metric G. Therefore the complete
single-qubit-PPT question is exactly the4x4 commutator identity
 [Td, G^{-1}Td^*G]=0.                                    (*)

The dimension4 is naturally spin1/2 tensor a multiplicity2 space of
the remaining three qubits. This is a concrete two-qubit-sized
algebraic target, not yet an identification with a known positive map.

Exact data and rejected shortcuts

moc_qft_transfer_normality.py exports R0,R1,Z,L,Td,G and the hidden
quartic at Q59 and R3701, beta=(0,1,i,2+3i). It verifies every displayed
bright identity, the norm formula, dark invariance, and (*) exactly.
At both points charpolyZ factors as product_j(t-beta_j) times a
nontrivial quartic. The beta values occur once each, not twice.

At Q59 the hidden roots are approximately
 .686349+3.431510i, 1.528342+.587383i,
 .204800+.325282i, .989854-.044458i.
The extra roots are not constrained to the convex hull of beta.
Changing the lower3x3 row rotation while fixing the first row of O
changes their sum, so they do not depend only on that first row.
Transfer matrices for different beta do not commute, so a fixed
beta-independent unitary diagonalization does not explain normality.

After common Mobius normalization beta=(0,1,-1,w), the real part of
rho is unchanged by any partial transpose; all changes lie in its
imaginary antisymmetric part. However conjugating any chosen subset
of the eight original Clifford columns fails to produce the partial
transpose at a generic example (all256 patterns were checked).
A nontrivial gluing or a different factorization is required.

The simpler conditional decomposition with pi(0)=j also fails:
at Q59 its three-qubit conditional moment matrices can be indefinite
of rank5. Single-cut separability, when it holds, can use factors
invisible to the fully symmetric three-qubit sector, and need not
use only the four original beta spinors.
