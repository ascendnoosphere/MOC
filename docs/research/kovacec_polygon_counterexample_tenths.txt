SIMPLIFIED EXACT COUNTEREXAMPLE TO KOVACEČ CONJECTURE 7

This is the same type of polygon-visibility counterexample as the independently
verified thousandths example, using all inputs rounded to tenths. It does
not disprove the Marcus–de Oliveira convex-hull conjecture. The primary
conjecture is the polygon statement in A. Kovacec, CMUC preprint 26-11 (2026),
https://www.mat.uc.pt/preprints/ps/p2611.pdf.

Let the four spectra be alpha/10 and beta/10, where
 alpha=(6-11i,-15-6i,6-12i,-9-5i),
 beta=(-14-15i,21-26i,-11+5i,21+9i).
Let K=(R+iS)/10, U=(I+K)(I-K)^-1, with
 R=[[0,-2,-4,3],[2,0,10,0],[4,-10,0,11],[-3,0,-11,0]],
 S=[[6,-15,-2,-2],[-15,-8,-12,-3],[-2,-12,-2,-2],[-2,-3,-2,-3]].
R is skew-symmetric and S symmetric, hence K*=-K and U is unitary. Put
 A=diag(alpha/10), B=U diag(beta/10) U*.
The exact determinant, in the coordinates scaled by 10^4, is
  10^4 det(A+B)=(4797443083792160-887096955783680 i)/12323449781.

The standalone verifier code/research/verify_kovacec_polygon_tenths.py
reconstructs all 24 permutation products and 72 transposition segments,
computes all intersections rationally, traverses the planar half-edges,
checks a simple 14-vertex outer polygon and its strict kernel point 32+15i,
and proves the ray from the determinant in direction -1-i misses all 72
segments using strict exact tests. It checks that the determinant lies
strictly inside the convex hull of permutation points 0,1,5, so the example
specifically contradicts the stronger polygon claim. The original
thousandths certificate remains unchanged.
