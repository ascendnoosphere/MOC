1. What the geometry actually accomplished

Consider every nonnegative five-parameter gap

  g=f34+a r17+b r20+p r18+q r23+r r28.

The support gap is normalized to mixed coefficient 1. The three known weighted
minor certificates combine with exact capacity

  C(p,q,r)=(p+q+r)/2 if no budget exceeds the sum of the others,
           sqrt(m(p+q+r-m)) otherwise, m=max(p,q,r).

The new theorem proves that every actual spectral gap of this form has C>=1.
Consequently the support inequality holds for every complex unitary U, with
all five coefficients independent and with zero coefficients allowed.

The calculation is a factorization of the secant obstruction. Put

 A=(1-r)[pq+r(p+q)-2r-1],
 B=(pq-1)(r^2-r+1)+r(p+q-2),
 C0=(r+1)[pq(1-2r)+r(p+q)-1],
 D=ab A+(a+b)B+C0.

The full projected nonic is just

  N=-(a+1)(b+1)(p+1)(q+1)D.

Exact computation from the original cubic proves

  det H_F(g-(F(g)/Q(g))1)=-4N^2/Q(g)^2.

This identity was not fitted to sample points. The verifier reconstructs the
entire six-variable Hessian determinant over the rationals: 2,776 coefficients
are retained, and the full cleared polynomial identity is checked.

In three of the four insufficient-capacity sectors, elementary inequalities
force A,B,C0<0 or A,B<=0,C0<0, making D=0 impossible. The only remaining sector
is r>1 and r(p+q)<1. Here A>0,B<0,C0<0, and D=0 has a single positive branch:

  a=t+s, b=t+k/s, s>0,
  t=-B/A>0, k=t^2-C0/A>t^2.

This branch is connected and stays in Q>0. The proof explicitly handles the
possible Q=0 locus before dividing; it cannot contain an actual point.

The HMSV theorem identifies the cubic/Hessian-zero locus with the complex
secant cone over M8. The previously audited rank theorem gives rank H<=12 on
that cone. Conversely, the exact order-two determinant zero along a transverse
b variation forces rank H>=12. Thus the rank is exactly12 along the entire
connected branch, so its real inertia cannot change. At

  (a,b,p,q,r)=(2,17/3,0,0,2)

exact arithmetic gives inertia(5,7,2). Actual complex spectral real parts must
instead have inertia(6,6,2). Therefore the whole branch is forbidden for actual
spectra. This closes the last sector without subdividing it into more faces.

Three exact checks illustrate the separation:

  (2,17/3,0,0,2):           D=0, rank 12, inertia(5,7,2), capacity0;
  (2,167/29,1/10,1/10,2):   D=0, rank 12, inertia(5,7,2), capacity sqrt(2/5);
  (1/3,2/5,1,1,1/20):       D=0, rank 12, inertia(6,6,2), capacity41/40.

The third is a neutral complex-secant candidate; no finite spectral realization
is asserted merely from its neutral inertia. This distinction is essential.
The argument imports neither a universal hyperbolicity direction nor a universal
split-real decomposition; both shortcuts had already been refuted.

The factorization also explains the targeted optimizer's p=q=1 pattern:
A=B=C0=0 identically on that slice, for every a,b,r. The secant equation
therefore vanishes without any tuning of a or b. For 0<=r<=2 its triangle
capacity is exactly 1+r/2, matching the observed 1.025 when r was constrained
to 0.05. This explains that numerical attractor; it does not assert that all
secant points on the slice are actual finite spectra.

2. Raw data exposed another missing geometric regime

I computed the rank-two real moment chart for the n4 raw spectral records,
using the outward normal nearest their recorded determinant. The chamber is
the sign of det W, where W spans the one-dimensional kernel of

  grad F(K(u,v))=B(u^2,uv,v^2), K a basis of ker H_x.

Among 3,984 cases passing a hull-shape guard, 2,619 passed the numerical Hessian
rank guard: 2,164 had definite W, 371 indefinite W, and 84 were near-degenerate.
The remaining 1,365 failed the rank guard and were not assigned a chamber. These
are floating-point descriptors, not exact classifications of every raw record;
maximum moment reconstruction error among retained rows was 4.13e-9.

All 88 targeted nonordinary examples from the previous campaign were definite.
That looked like a useful invariant restriction, but an additional search
around the adversarial optimizer spectra refuted it on its 15th perturbation.
Rounding the spectra to small Gaussian integers then gave an exact example:

 a=(-3-i,4i,-11-i,13-2i),
 b=(2-11i,5-3i,-4+i,1+6i), eta=1.

All 24 matching products are distinct. Both spectra are nonconcyclic, with
circle determinants -15864 and -7170. The support height is 4872, with only
permutation 12 in contact. Its canonical mixed coefficient and budgets are

  lambda=1250, (p,q,r)=(5950,3570,11222).

The exact ordinary-cone dual evaluates to -1250, so this is outside
the ordinary-minor cone. Its rank 12 Hessian has a rank-two moment map, and

 det W=-7750312937188011581413904646852132730836293702645801079 /
        1911403934178585407043727749335594184830957252492726416 <0.

The affine moment line has exactly two rank-one endpoints, both positive
semidefinite and with positive parameter. It has no negative-semidefinite
point, so the actual real spectral vector cannot be decomposed as the midpoint
of two real critical points. Thus this omitted chamber is present even among
actual nonordinary gaps, not just among ordinary easy examples.

It remains a safe MOC support direction: its weighted capacity squared is
106833440, exceeding lambda^2 by 105270940. It is not a counterexample to MOC.
The example rules out a tempting new simplification suggested by all 88 prior
mixed samples, and supplies an exact test for future certificate strategies.

3. A local equality calculation that did not prove the global inequality

At the archived exact weighted equality seed, the capacity defect
J=q(p+r)-lambda^2 has exact nonnegative KKT multipliers on the active spectral
support constraints. The seed has 11 contacts, not 9; the generic numerical
optimizer attractor had 9. Nine multipliers are positive and two are zero.
On the resulting eight-dimensional critical tangent space, the entire
Lagrangian Hessian is exactly zero. Consequently a second-order local test at
this seed provides no strict curvature estimate for the desired global capacity
inequality. This was checked rationally after scaling imaginary coordinates by
sqrt(23); it is not a numerical small-eigenvalue observation.

The computation is code/research/moc_moonshot_ag_local.py and local_boundary.json.
It explains why optimizing around the equality seed repeatedly sticks to a
very flat boundary. The orbit 51 proof avoids this flat local test by using the connected real
secant branch described above.
