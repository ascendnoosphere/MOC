M03: TWO SPECTRAL CIRCLES UNDER MÖBIUS TRANSFORMS

Status: already known, not a new MOC4 closure. Drury's 2006 paper
Essentially Hermitian Matrices Revisited, Theorem 1.2 and Corollary 1.3,
proves MOC in every dimension for normal A,B whose respective spectra lie
on arbitrary circles; the proof explicitly discusses equal, disjoint,
tangent and intersecting circles. Primary source:
https://journals.uwyo.edu/index.php/ela/article/download/403/403/403#page=1
The following derivation records the maps and limiting cases and extends
circle terminology to generalized circles (lines included). It is a
reconstruction of known mathematics, not a priority claim.

Use the difference form of MOC: det(A-B) lies in the convex hull of
z_pi=product_i(a_i-b_pi(i)). This is the plus form after replacing B by -B.
For f(z)=(az+b)/(cz+d), Delta=ad-bc !=0, and no pole on either spectrum,
the noncommuting matrix identity is
 (cA+dI)(f(A)-f(B))(cB+dI)=Delta(A-B).
Taking determinants gives
 det(f(A)-f(B))=K det(A-B),
 K=Delta^n/[det(cA+dI)det(cB+dI)].
Each transformed matching product is precisely K z_pi, independent of pi.
Consequently the full convex-hull statement, and not merely individual
support inequalities, holds before f exactly when it holds after f.

Normalize two finite circles to C1: |z|=r, C2: |z-L|=R, L>0, r,R>0,
by translation and rotation. For disjoint circles, including a strictly
nested pair, L>r+R or L<|r-R|. Let
 T=(r^2+L^2-R^2)/L,
 p,q=(T±sqrt(T^2-4r^2))/2.
The discriminant factors as
 T^2-4r^2 = [L^2-(r+R)^2][L^2-(r-R)^2]/L^2 >0.
Moreover pq=r^2 and (p-L)(q-L)=R^2. The map f(z)=(z-p)/(z-q)
therefore sends the circles to |f|^2=p/q and
|f|^2=(p-L)/(q-L), respectively. Both ratios are positive; the pole q is
on neither circle because otherwise p=q. For concentric input circles,
the identity after translation already works. On the images, both matrices
are real-radius multiples of unitaries with the same center, which cancels
in the difference. The established scalar-unitary MOC theorem applies:
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379506004757

For two distinct circles meeting at two points p,q, f(z)=(z-q)/(z-p)
sends the circles through p and q to two distinct lines through 0. Their
image spectra correspond to a Hermitian matrix and a complex scalar multiple
of another Hermitian matrix after a common rotation. If the line angle is
nonparallel, Drury's essentially Hermitian theorem applies; if the lines
coincide, the Hermitian case applies. Distinct transverse circles in fact
produce distinct lines. For two tangent distinct circles with common point
p, f(z)=1/(z-p) sends them to two parallel lines; the known shifted-Hermitian
polynomial theorem gives MOC. For a common circle, send any point p on it
to infinity and use the single-line Hermitian case. If the original circles
are generalized circles including lines, first use any Möbius map whose pole
is not on either generalized circle or on the spectra, making the lines finite circles;
apply the same classification, retaining the point at infinity as a point of
the Riemann sphere. A singleton spectrum is scalar and is an immediate
permutation-point case.

If a needed pole coincides with one or more eigenvalues, move those entries
slightly along their own circles or lines while keeping each matrix's
spectral eigenvectors fixed. The perturbed spectra avoid the pole and the
proved case applies. As they return, determinants and all finitely many
matching products converge. Convex-hull membership is closed: choose weights
in the compact simplex and extract a convergent subsequence. This handles
shared eigenvalues, repeated eigenvalues and tangency at a spectral point;
no limiting division by a zero denominator is made.

code/research/verify_mobius_circle_identities.py checks the cleared
noncommuting 2x2 matrix identity, the discriminant factorization, and the
general Apollonius-circle algebra exactly. The matrix proof for arbitrary n
follows directly by expanding the products and does not rely on the 2x2
check. Its fresh execution passed. This result gives a useful known spectral
stratum but does not cover generic four-point spectra: four arbitrary complex
points need not lie on one circle or line.
