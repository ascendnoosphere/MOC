1. Intrinsic cone and closedness.

Let V be the real 14-dimensional space of matching functions on S4, and
let v_pi be any spanning evaluation frame. Write
 C0={g: g_pi=v_pi^T T v_pi for some real T>=0}.
This cone is closed. Indeed, if g_k converges and T_k are positive Gram
matrices, then sum_pi g_k(pi)=tr(T_k A), A=sum_pi v_pi v_pi^T>0.
Thus tr(T_k) is bounded by the bounded sums divided by lambda_min(A).
A convergent subsequence of positive matrices gives a Gram for the limit.
Every ordinary indicator r_(I,J) is its own square and is in C0.

2. Precise algebraic input needed.

Let X={N9=0} in projective V_C. The required input is that its projective
dual Y has dimension 8 and that a generic real point x of X has a smooth
dual normal n in Y. Biduality then identifies the closure of the Gauss
fiber over n with P(Ann(T_n cone(Y))), a projective linear P4. In
particular the WHOLE P4, including its singular and nonactual points,
is contained in X. A real n gives a real tangent-annihilator; no real
balanced-matrix parametrization of n is necessary for this assertion.

Our numerical balanced-S chart is only one real chart. Its derivative
kernel computes this five-dimensional affine cone when its projected
derivative has rank9. It does not by itself prove that every real normal
has a real balanced-S representative. The abstract tangent-annihilator
statement avoids that potential real-form gap.

3. Why constants lie in the fiber and why adding epsilon is legitimate.

N9 is constant-translation invariant. Hence its gradient is unchanged by
x -> x+s e, where e is the all-one matching function. At a regular point
this entire affine line has the same Gauss normal; its projective closure
contains [e]. Therefore e belongs to the five-dimensional fiber cone.
Adding epsilon e preserves regularity. It does NOT regularize a singular
point, so a generic perturbation must precede this step when necessary.

4. The compact polytope argument.

Take a regular nonnegative actual cost g, add epsilon e, and divide by
its positive coordinate sum. In its real five-dimensional Gauss-fiber
cone G consider K={u in G: u_pi>=0, sum_pi u_pi=1}. This is a compact
polytope in the ordinary 24-simplex. It has relative dimension4 because
e/24 belongs to G and is strictly positive. Every vertex of K has at
least four distinct zero coordinates: otherwise fewer than four active
coordinate functionals cannot cut a point out of its four-dimensional
affine hull. Every vertex is still on N9, since the full projective fiber
closure is on X. No future-lift assumption is needed for the vertices.

Consequently, if every nonnegative N9 cost in each of the 29 nonordinary
four-contact faces belongs to C0, all vertices of K belong to C0. The five
remaining four-contact orbits are ordinary faces and need no nonic
argument. Convexity gives g+epsilon e in C0, and closedness gives g in C0.
Vertices with more than four contacts cause no gap: choose any four of
their contacts and apply the corresponding entire face assertion.
The contact census is code/research/moc_four_contact_orbits.py and
