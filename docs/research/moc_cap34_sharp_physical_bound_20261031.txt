Main result

For every real O in O4, put
 F(O)=O22^2+O13^2+det O[01,02]^2-det O[02,12]^2,
with zero-based indices. This is the canonical mixed cost f34. Its exact
minimum is the unique real root m of

                   4m^3-12m^2+14m+1=0,

namely m=-0.06744224886812297.... The independently stored strict rational
global matching-SOS dual has f34 expectation -0.068563242. Thus the SOS
dual profile body strictly contains the physical convex profile body.
This refutes equality of those bodies. It is not an actual spectral
support gap or a counterexample to MOC4 or universal actual-gap SOS.

1. Exact reduction from SO4 to two probability vectors

Row sign changes preserve this squared-minor cost, so detO=-1 reduces
to detO=1. Every SO4 matrix has the quaternion form O=L(p)R(q), with
unit p,q in R4. The left and right imaginary quaternion generators span
so4 (rank6 checked exactly). Hence this compact subgroup image is both
open and closed in connected SO4, proving surjectivity.

Let A=Rot(p), B=Rot(q) be the usual SO3 quaternion rotations. Direct
polynomial expansion gives, when p and q have unit norm,

 O22^2+O13^2
  =1/2+[-A01 B10+A02 B20+A11 B11-A12 B21+A20 B02]/2,
 det O[01,02]=(A01+B10)/2,
 det O[02,12]=(A12-B21)/2.

The two unwanted cross terms cancel, giving

 F=1/2+(A01^2-A12^2+B10^2-B21^2)/4
       +(A02 B20+A11 B11+A20 B02)/2.                 (1)

These are checked as homogeneous polynomial identities before imposing
unit norms; the script records the required norm factors explicitly.

Put C=B^T and let J reverse the three coordinate axes. Both
R=-AJ and S=-CJ belong to SO3, independently. Write R=Rot(u), S=Rot(v)
with unit quaternions u,v. Elementary quaternion identities give

 A01^2-A12^2=-16 u0u1u2u3,
 C01^2-C12^2=-16 v0v1v2v3,
 (A02,A11,A20)=-diag R,
 diagR dot diagS=4 sum_i u_i^2 v_i^2-1.

Consequently

 F=2 sum_i u_i^2 v_i^2-4(product_i u_i+product_i v_i). (2)

The two products can independently be chosen positive for any specified
squared coordinates. Therefore minimizing F is exactly minimizing

 f(w,t)=2 w dot t-4(G(w)+G(t)),
 G(w)=sqrt(w0w1w2w3), w,t in the probability simplex Delta3. (3)

There is no relaxation in this reduction: the two SO3 rotations and
their quaternion squared coordinates are independent and cover the
indicated domains.

2. A negative minimum cannot occur in the simplex interior

The function is continuous on the compact product of simplices, so it
has a global minimum. Suppose both w and t are strictly positive and
it is a stationary point. With Lagrange multipliers lambda,mu,

 2t_i-2G(w)/w_i=lambda,
 2w_i-2G(t)/t_i=mu.                                  (4)

Multiplying by the respective coordinates and summing gives
lambda+mu=2f. Subtracting the multiplied equations pointwise gives

 lambda w_i-mu t_i=2(G(t)-G(w)).                       (5)

If lambda=0, then w_i t_i=G(w) for all i. Unless mu=0, the second
equation forces t uniform and then w uniform, which also forces mu=0.
Thus lambda=0 implies mu=0 and f=0. The same holds with lambda,mu
interchanged. Such a point cannot be a negative minimum.

Assume now lambda mu!=0. Substitute the affine expression for t_i
from(5) into the first equation(4). Every w_i solves the same quadratic
with nonzero leading coefficient. Hence w has at most two distinct
coordinate values; the t_i have the same multiplicity partition.

If w is uniform, (3) gives f=1/4-4G(t)>=0 by AM-GM. If there are two
values, their multiplicities are either2+2 or1+3, after relabeling.

For2+2, write
 w=(a,a,b,b), t=(c,c,d,d), a+b=c+d=1/2.
Then exactly
 f=4(ac+bd-ab-cd)=4(a+c-1/2)^2>=0.                    (6)

For1+3 write
 w=(r^2,1,1,1)/(r^2+3),
 t=(s^2,1,1,1)/(s^2+3), r,s>0.
The restricted objective is

 f(r,s)=2(r^2s^2+3)/[(r^2+3)(s^2+3)]
           -4r/(r^2+3)^2-4s/(s^2+3)^2.              (7)

Its two derivative numerators, after positive denominators and factor12
are removed, are

 P=r^3s^2-r^3+r^2s^2+3r^2+3rs^2-3r-s^2-3,
 Q=r^2s^3+r^2s^2+3r^2s-r^2-s^3+3s^2-3s-3.

The exact resultant identity is

 Res_s(P,Q)=-16(r-1)^6(r+1)^3(r^2+3)^2.               (8)

Since r>0, a stationary point must have r=1. Then P(1,s)=4(s^2-1)
forces s=1, giving f=0. Thus no strictly interior stationary point has
negative value. This exhausts the multiplier degeneracies as well as
all multiplicity cases.

3. Boundary reduction to a vertex

If a boundary w has exactly three positive coordinates, introduce epsilon
in the missing coordinate and multiply the other three by1-epsilon.
The dot-product change is O(epsilon), whereas the negative term
-4G(w) gains a strictly negative multiple of sqrt(epsilon). This
strictly lowers f for sufficiently small epsilon, independently of t.
Thus such w cannot occur at a minimum. The same argument applies to t.

If a boundary vector w has at most two positive coordinates, G(w)=0.
For fixed t the remaining dependence on w is linear. Replacing w by
one of its supported vertices cannot increase f. Therefore a negative
global minimum has a representative with w a vertex. The objective
does have negative values, for instance the explicit matrix below at
r=1/3. Hence this reduction applies to the global minimum.

Take w=e0. For fixed a=t0, AM-GM maximizes t1t2t3 at
t1=t2=t3=(1-a)/3. Therefore the minimum reduces exactly to

 min_(0<=a<=1) [2a-4sqrt(a((1-a)/3)^3)].              (9)

Set a=r^2/(r^2+3), r>=0, including a=1 by the limit r=infinity. Then

 f_vertex(r)=2r(r^3+3r-2)/(r^2+3)^2,
 f_vertex'(r)=12(r^3+r^2+3r-1)/(r^2+3)^3.             (10)

The cubic is strictly increasing on r>=0 and has a unique positive
root r*=0.295597742522085.... This is the unique minimum of(10).
Elimination of r* gives the polynomial stated at the beginning. Its
derivative in m is12(m-1)^2+2>0, so there is one real root.

The sharp value is attained by the explicit orthogonal matrix

 O(r)=1/sqrt(r^2+3)
       [ -r,-1,-1, 1;
          1,-1, 1, r;
          1, 1,-r, 1;
          1,-r,-1,-1 ].

The verifier checks O(r)O(r)^T=I, detO(r)=1, and its mixed cost equals
(10), without approximating r*.

5. Consequence for the ongoing global proof

One cannot prove universal actual-gap membership by identifying every
normalized matching-moment PSD point with a physical convex mixture.
The explicit dual above defeats that identification. It remains possible
that all actual spectral support gaps are nonnegative on this strictly
larger dual body; the extra spectral linkage can matter. Nothing in this
note constructs an actual negative gap. The sharp mixed-facet separation
instead supplies a concrete nonphysical dual target for that search.

6. General variational interface and the limit of this proof shape

There is a useful exact common form for every real matching-cost profile.
With the independent SO3 factors A and C=B^T from Section1, it is

 F_g(A,C)=c+sum_ij M_ij A_ij C_ij
             +sum_ij N_ij(A_ij^2+C_ij^2).             (11)

One may uniquely require all row and column sums of N to vanish. This
leaves1+9+4=14 real coefficients, matching V14. Here is a direct proof of
the representation. Singleton squares have a constant plus bilinear
entrywise A,C expansion, as in Section1. Every two-minor is one half
of a signed A entry plus or minus the corresponding C entry, by the
self-dual/anti-self-dual exterior-square formula. Squaring yields one
entry of A^2+C^2 plus one entrywise A,C product. These ordinary rays
span all matching costs. Adding row/column potentials to N contributes
only a constant, so centering N loses nothing. The cross terms are
independent; averaging over C removes them. On centered N, the remaining
quadratic functional is independent because squared SO3 permutations
span the dimension4 three-by-three Birkhoff affine space. Thus(11)
indeed has the stated unique parameter count.

The exact stationary equations are

 A^T(2N entrywise A+M entrywise C) is symmetric,
 C^T(2N entrywise C+M entrywise A) is symmetric.        (12)

For actual normalized cofactor directions, M,N and the score constant
depend linearly on the four real coefficients of the complex two-cofactor
functional; the gap constant also adds the maximum of the24 scores.
The fixed beta data supply the spectral linkage. Equations(11)--(12)
give a smaller global comparison problem than an unconstrained quartic
in sixteen real quaternion coordinates.

The special mixed facet admits an additional axis reversal making the
self-quartic exactly -4product(u) and the cross term exactly
2sum u_i^2v_i^2. That extra symmetry is what makes the same scalar
quadratic govern all KKT coordinates in Section2. General coefficients
in(11) retain mixed signed quaternion monomials; the two-value KKT
classification has not been established for actual spectral directions.
In particular this argument does not assert that all nonpermutation
stationary points are saddles. Known actual Walsh families with strict
nonpermutation local extrema prevent that shortcut. A global comparison
of their critical values, or a constructive spectral Gram identity,
is still required.
