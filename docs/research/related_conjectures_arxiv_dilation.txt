RODTES' NORMAL-DILATION CLASS AND INDEPENDENT RELATIVE COUPLINGS

Source: K. Rodtes, A Class of Normal Dilation Matrices Affirming the Marcus-
de Oliveira Conjecture, arXiv:2006.14846v1 (2020),
https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.14846. The source proves the aligned pair
(N(X,s),N(Y,t)) is in MOC, using a common Hadamard block basis. It does not
assert that independent relative unitary conjugations are covered.

For n=2 write
 N(X,s)=[[X,(X-sI)*],[(X-sI)*,X]],
 H=(1/sqrt(2))[[I,I],[-I,I]].
Direct block multiplication gives
 H* N(X,s) H = [X-X*+conj(s) I] direct-sum
                [X+X*-conj(s) I].
The first block has spectrum on a vertical line and the second on a
horizontal line. Rodtes' proof applies a known MOC result to corresponding
blocks, then multiplies the two convex combinations. A common unitary change
of basis does not alter the conclusion.

A modest independent-coupling extension follows directly. In the H basis,
if the relative unitary W is block diagonal diag(U,V), the conjugated second
dilation preserves the same two sectors, and the original blockwise proof
continues to apply. If n=2, a block-antidiagonal W also gives two independent
2x2 normal pairs after sector exchange; the known dimension-2 MOC theorem and
Rodtes' direct-sum lemma settle that case. Neither claim covers a general W.

The general W cannot be handled merely by finding another common reducing
block decomposition, even in dimension four. Set
 X=diag(1,(3+i)/2), Y=diag(2,(5+2i)/2), s=t=0.
In the H basis their two spectra are respectively
 A=diag(0,i,2,3), B=diag(0,2i,4,5).
Let W be the normalized four-point Fourier matrix W_jk=i^(jk)/2, with
j,k=0,1,2,3, and set C=W B W*. The exact checker verifies every off-diagonal
entry of C is nonzero. Since A has simple spectrum, any A-invariant complex
subspace is spanned by a subset of its eigenvectors. No nonempty proper
coordinate subset is invariant for C. Thus A,C have no common nontrivial
invariant, and in particular reducing, subspace. They correspond to two
independently coupled normal dilations in the original basis. This proves an
obstruction to the paper's common-block strategy; it is not a counterexample
to MOC or to any extension by a different proof method.

There is also a spectral obstruction to encoding arbitrary MOC4 instances
as this class, even if an arbitrary common scalar shift, a nonzero complex
scaling, and unitary similarity are allowed. The four eigenvalues must split
into two pairs whose differences are perpendicular as real plane vectors:
one pair comes from the first block, the other from the second. A common
complex scale rotates and scales both differences, preserving perpendicularity.
For the spectrum (0,1,2i,1+3i), the three pair partitions have chord dot
products 1,6,5. None is zero. Consequently no shifted/scaled dilation of
the stated type has this spectrum. This excludes the direct spectral
embedding; it does not rule out an indirect reduction using additional
variables, limiting arguments or a different construction.

code/research/verify_dilation_relative_obstruction.py checks the two block
identities, the Fourier unitary, all 12 off-diagonal nonzero conditions and
all three chord products exactly. Its fresh execution passed. The independent
coupling MOC question itself remains open here.
