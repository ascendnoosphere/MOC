THEOREM. For every actual x,

                 [t^2] det(H_x+tE) <= 0.                       (1)

Proof first when Q(x) is nonzero. If rank(H_x)<=11, the determinant
vanishes to order at least3 at t=0, so the coefficient is zero.
Otherwise set K=ker(H_x), of dimension2. In an orthonormal decomposition
K-perpendicular plus K, write H_x=diag(A,0), with A invertible12x12.
The quadratic determinant coefficient is

                  det(A) det(E restricted to K).              (2)

Neutral inertia implies det(A)>0. For v in K,
v^T E x=v^T H_x e=0, so K is contained in x-perpendicular for E.
Modulo the radical of E, this orthogonal complement has inertia (1,2)
if Q(x)>0, and (2,1) if Q(x)<0. Thus K cannot be definite with the
same sign as Q(x), because that sign has index only1 in the complement.
It cannot be definite with the opposite sign either, since y is in K
and Q(y)=Q(x). A real symmetric form on a two-dimensional space which
is not definite has determinant at most zero. Formula (2) proves (1).

For Q(x)=0, approximate the underlying real and imaginary spectral
parameters by ones with Q(x) nonzero. Their existence is justified by
the nonzero polynomial Q composed with the actual spectral map; the
verifier supplies an exact nonzero anchor. The complement of the zero
set of a nonzero real polynomial is dense. Coefficients of the
determinant pencil are polynomial in x, so (1) passes to the limit.
