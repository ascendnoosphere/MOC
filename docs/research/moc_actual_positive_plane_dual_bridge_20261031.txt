3. Positive-plane lemma

Fix a dual y with y(f)=-1 and beta_j=y(r_j). Let R be a real9-by5 matrix
such that

 C=R^T M(f)R>0,
 X_j=R^T M(r_j)R+beta_j C>=0, j in A.                       (2)

Then the following POINTWISE POSITIVE MATRIX IDENTITY holds for all g:

 R^T M(g)R+y(g)C =sum_j g(pi_j) X_j.                        (3)

Indeed substitute(1); its f coefficient cancels because y(f)=-1.
For g>=0 the right side is PSD. If y(g)<0, then

                R^T M(g)R =sum_j g(pi_j)X_j-y(g)C>0,

so M(g) has five positive directions. This is impossible for actual g.
Thus y(g)>=0 for all actual g>=0. If all X_j are PD, equality y(g)=0
also excludes every nonzero actual cost: either some nu_j>0 gives a
positive compression or all nu_j=0 gives g=t f, whose nonzero
nonnegative multiples have y(g)<0.

This is an actual-aware matrix certificate. It allows N9 past roots
with five positive directions and does not revive the false all-N9
