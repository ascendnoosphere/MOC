Genericity and chart closure in the conditional twenty-nine-face reduction
31 October 2026

Status and scope

The genericity and component inputs of the leaf-polytope reduction can be
proved from the existing HMSV inputs and the recorded exact actual witness.
The twenty-nine all-nonnegative face containments are still unproved.
Nothing below proves one of those containments or unrestricted MOC4.

The essential extra point is normality of N8'. A rank-nine chart on its
height section alone does not prove that the ambient ninefold is smooth,
nor does an inclusion of projective duals prove equality. The transverse
chart and the conormal argument below address these two gaps explicitly.

1. Fixed inputs and dimensions

Work over C until Section 7. Let V be the fourteen-dimensional matching
space, e its constant-one vector, and let S=Sec(M8) in P(V), of dimension
eleven. Let N=N8' in P(V*), of dimension nine. The preceding project note
moc_projective_dual_and_markov_stationarity_20261031.txt establishes

                         S*=N.                         (1)

This uses the audited HMSV polar/secant theorem and its kernel-line
argument; it is an input here, not a new consequence of the chart alone.
Let J=Join([e],S), with its reduced structure. It is irreducible. Its
generic nonic chart has dimension twelve, as explained in Section 5.

Write hats for affine cones. The SL4 invariant profile morphism

 f: Mat4 x Mat4 -> hat(N)

is the common-minor profile of the eight columns [R|C], and is surjective
on complex points. The invariant quotient construction and the audited
HMSV affine-cone surjectivity supply this input. Its height is

              <f(R,C),e>=det(R)det(C).                 (2)

The singleton coordinates are the polynomial invariants

 P_ij=det(R_i<-C_j)det(C_j<-R_i)
     =[(adj(R)C) hadamard (adj(C)R)^T]_ij.             (3)

Each row and column of P sums to (2). Normals are functionals on V; a
metric-dependent orthogonal projection of a 24-vector is unnecessary.
The class [c(T)] means restriction to V, where

              c_pi(T)=sgn(pi) product_i T_i,pi(i).

For R invertible and T=R^-1 C, all common-minor invariants, including
every grade, are det(R)^2 times [c(T)]. The complementary-minor signs
were checked in moc_n8_leibniz_chart_audit_20261031.txt and its verifier.
Exchanging R,C inverts permutation labels; equivalently this replaces
c(T) by c(T^T) in the fixed profile coordinates.

Set hat(Y)=hat(N) intersect e-perp, with its reduced structure. Its
projectivization will be Y. The conclusions below are

       hat(Y) irreducible, dim hat(Y)=9, dim Y=8,
       J*=Y, and J=Y*.                                 (4)

2. The two height components have the same image closure

In the affine 32-dimensional source, (2) has precisely the two irreducible
divisors D_R={det R=0} and D_C={det C=0}. Each is irreducible because
the determinant hypersurface is irreducible. In D_C the subset det R!=0
is dense, and its profile image is exactly

             A={ [c(T)] : det T=0 },

allowing the harmless nonzero scalar det(R)^2. Every scalar is already
available from homogeneous scaling over C. The dense subset of D_R
where C is invertible gives profiles [c(T^T)] with det T=0. Transposition
is a bijection of the determinant hypersurface, so these image sets have
the same closure. No assumption that inversion acts trivially on an
individual profile is being made.

For any polynomial morphism f and dense subset U of a variety D,
f(D) is contained in closure(f(U)): the preimage of the latter closed
set is closed and contains U. Thus the both-dependent points, including
all rank drops, lie in the same image closure. Surjectivity of f and (2)
give hat(Y)=f(D_R union D_C), set-theoretically. Since hat(Y) is closed,

                         hat(Y)=closure(A).

It is therefore irreducible. This proof handles both-dependent points by
closure, rather than assuming that one group is independent everywhere.

3. The balanced rational chart is an actual open isomorphism

Let B={S:S1=0,1^T S=0}; it is a nine-dimensional vector space. Put
tau(S)=adj(S)_00. On B, adj(S)=tau(S)11^T whenever rank S=3; the same
polynomial identity holds everywhere on B. Define

          Phi(S)=[c(S)]/tau(S),  tau(S)!=0.             (5)

By the singleton cofactor identity, singleton(Phi(S))=S. Also height
Phi(S)=0. Hence Phi is an immersion and its singleton extraction is a
regular left inverse on this open set.

The converse is needed for an open-chart assertion. Take n in hat(Y)
and let S=singleton(n), with tau(S)!=0. Lift n to [R|C]. If both groups
are dependent, their singleton matrix has rank at most one: for ranks
three, factor both adjugates in (3); if one rank is at most two, P=0.
Thus both-dependent lifts are excluded by rank S=3. One group is
independent, and in the corresponding chart n=lambda[c(T)], det T=0.
Since singleton(n) has rank three, rank T=3 and every entry of its left
and right nullvectors is nonzero. Write adj(T)=u v^T. Then

     S=lambda diag(v) T diag(u),
     adj(S)=kappa 11^T,
     [c(S)]=kappa n,
     kappa=lambda^3 product_i u_i product_i v_i=tau(S)!=0.

The transposed independent-group chart gives the same conclusion.
Consequently

 hat(Y) intersect {tau(singleton(n))!=0}
       =Phi({S in B:tau(S)!=0}).                       (6)

Both directions in (6) are regular maps. This is a rational graph, not
merely a parametrized subvariety. It is smooth, irreducible, and of
dimension nine. Its nonemptiness follows, for example, from
S=11^T-4I, whose principal cofactor is -16. Thus it is dense in hat(Y).

4. Normality and a transverse ten-dimensional chart of hat(N)

Here normality has a concrete use. The invariant ring R(N8) is normal:
it is an invariant subring of a polynomial ring under SL4 and the column
torus imposing equal multidegrees. An invariant subring A^G of a normal
domain A is integrally closed in its fraction field: an element integral
over A^G is integral over A, hence belongs to A, and remains G-invariant.
The equal-multidegree ring is precisely the corresponding torus-invariant
subring; its nonnegative grading introduces no additional localization.

HMSV arXiv:1103.5255v1, Proposition 8.2, identifies R(N8') with the
Gale-invariant subring of R(N8). Applying the same normality argument
again proves that hat(N) is normal. This imports the actual invariant-
ring identification, not just a set-theoretic assertion about an image.
The cached primary text is docs/references/m8_hmsv/1103.5255v1/
8pts-032711.tex, Proposition labelled anotherbrowncow; its published
number in the cached text is 8.2.

Only normality away from the cone vertex is needed. There is also a
weaker way to obtain precisely that input: the projective Gale quotient
of normal N8 is normal, and the degree-one-generated homogeneous ring
of N8' makes its punctured affine cone locally a G_m bundle over N8'.
Indeed, on a degree-one principal open, the graded localization is
its degree-zero ring with an invertible degree-one variable adjoined.
Thus projective normality in the sense of a normal embedded homogeneous
coordinate ring is not an additional assumption of this proof.

For S in B with tau!=0 and t complex, consider the holomorphic map

       Psi(S,t)=[c(S+t11^T)]/tau(S) into hat(N).         (7)

The rank-one determinant formula gives the exact identity

         det(S+t11^T)=16t tau(S),
         height(Psi(S,t))=16t.                         (8)

At t=0 the singleton matrix is S. Choose nine linear singleton coordinates
that identify B with C^9, and append the height coordinate. The derivative
of these ten coordinates composed with Psi has block form

                    [ I9  * ]
                    [  0 16 ].

It is invertible. The complex inverse-function theorem therefore gives
a smooth ten-dimensional analytic graph germ inside hat(N), through
every Phi(S) in (6). A normal complex algebraic variety is analytically
normal and hence analytically irreducible at each point. A closed analytic
subgerm of equal dimension inside an irreducible analytic germ is the
whole germ. Therefore hat(N) itself is smooth at Phi(S). Moreover its
height differential is nonzero there, by (8).

This last irreducibility step matters: an immersion into a singular
variety alone would not exclude another local branch. Normality does.
We obtain a dense open subset Y0 of Y where N is smooth and Y is a
transverse hyperplane section of N.

5. The exact actual witness supplies the projection-critical check

The existing exact files give an actual gap g0 with Q(g0)!=0, rank
X(g0)=12, and a nonzero two-by-two Schur leading coefficient, where

 Q(g)=D_e F(g),
 X(g)=Q(g)H_g-F(g)H_e,
 x(g)=g-F(g)e/Q(g).

The global square identity is

                det X(g)=-4 Q(g)^12 N9(g)^2.           (9)

With a nonsingular principal twelve-block A and its rational kernel
basis K, the reference derivative compression R=K^T(D_r X)K has
det R!=0 at g0. The Schur determinant expansion is

       det X(g0+sr)=det(A)det(R)s^2+O(s^3).

Comparing with (9), since Q!=0, proves D_r N9(g0)!=0. Thus g0 is a
smooth point of the reduced hypersurface J in the Q!=0 chart; this is
stronger than merely observing rank twelve.

For clarity, on Q!=0 there is a genuine algebraic product chart

        J intersect {Q!=0}  <->  (S intersect {Q!=0}) x A1,
        g <-> (x(g),F(g)/Q(g)),
        (x,t) |-> x+te.                                (10)

Indeed F(e)=grad F(e)=0 makes F(x+te)=F(x)+tQ(x), and Q is translation
invariant. The audited set equality {F=det H=0}=S and (9) prove (10)
set-theoretically with reduced structures. Possible factors supported
only at Q=0 are irrelevant to this open isomorphism. Consequently
x0=x(g0) is smooth in S. Furthermore

                  D_e F(x0)=Q(g0)!=0,

so e does not belong to the affine tangent space of S at x0.

The exact Gauss profile n0 at this same point has been checked on all

6. Conormal dominance and the full dual equality

By (1) and projective biduality, the conormal variety of S is the flip
of that of N. Over n in Y0 its fiber is the full projective three-space

             P(B_n), B_n=(T_hat_n hat(N))^perp,

and P(B_n) is contained in S. Since height is transverse on N at n,
e is not in B_n. Also

 W_n=(T_hat_n hat(Y))^perp=Ce+B_n, dim W_n=5.           (11)

The restricted conormal incidence over Y0 is a projective-three-space
bundle over an irreducible eightfold, hence irreducible of dimension
eleven. It is not enough to count these dimensions: its projection to S
could conceivably have been trapped in the projection-critical locus.

At the exact pair (x0,n0), S is smooth and the conormal bundle over S
has projective-line fibers. The condition n(e)=0 cuts that fiber
transversely: e is not in T_hat_x0 S, so this nonzero linear functional
on the two-dimensional conormal space has one simple projective zero.
The restricted incidence therefore projects submersively to S near
(x0,n0). Its image contains an analytic open subset of S and is Zariski
dense. This is the specific dominance input supplied by the witness.

It follows that the closure of the union of P(B_n), n in Y0, is S.
Equation (11) now gives

 Y*=closure(union_(n in Y0) P(W_n))=Join([e],S)=J.

Biduality gives J*=Y. This proves (4), rather than inferring equality
from the weaker inclusion J* subset N intersect e-perp.

For every real smooth n in Y, the full real five-space

                     W=(T_hat_n hat(Y))^perp

contains e and is contained in the affine cone over J. This follows
from J=Y* and the full conormal fiber at a smooth point. In particular it
holds throughout the rational chart (6); no continuation of an actual
real spectral lift along W is needed.

7. Real actual genericity and continuity of the supporting height

Use all finite spectra and nonzero phases:

        D=C^4 x C^4 x C*, regarded as an open subset of R^18.

For p=(a,b,eta), set

 z_pi(p)=eta product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)),
 r_pi(p)=-Re z_pi(p),
 h(p)=max_pi Re z_pi(p),  g(p)=r(p)+h(p)e.

The raw vector r is polynomial in the eighteen real coordinates. The
height h is continuous as a finite maximum. It need not be differentiable
or analytic. All actual r and g lie in J by the product-M8/secant bridge.

The needed good conditions can be encoded by finitely many nonzero real
polynomial tests on r: Q!=0; the fixed principal twelve-minor of X!=0;
the cleared Schur reference coefficient!=0; and
tau(singleton(grad N9(r)))!=0. The last polynomial is well defined even
if N9 has additional factors at Q=0; on this chart its nonzero gradient
is the Gauss normal of the relevant reduced component. The exact witness
above makes every test nonzero. Their product composed with r(p) is
therefore a nonzero real polynomial, since it is nonzero at that same
actual parameter point.

A nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on a nonempty Euclidean open
subset of R^18. Its complement is dense in D. Thus every actual parameter
point is a limit of good actual parameter points. No assertion about
density in each fixed contact stratum is used or required.

The tests may be applied to r instead of g: Q and X are invariant under
translation by e, and the projected nonic N9 and its gradient are also
translation invariant. This avoids differentiating the finite maximum.
For a sequence p_k -> p of good points, continuity gives g(p_k)->g(p).
Each good g(p_k) has a nonzero real Gauss normal in the smooth chart (6),
and hence belongs to a real five-dimensional W satisfying (11).

Allowing arbitrary nonzero eta loses nothing: multiplication by a
positive scalar preserves all nonzero homogeneous tests and the cone
conclusions. A unit phase may therefore be imposed afterward. Repeated
eigenvalues, zero pair sums, and supporting-permutation ties are covered
by this same continuity argument. Eta=0 gives the zero gap directly.

8. Exact consequence for the twenty-nine-face reduction

The dense actual leaf input required in Section 3 of
moc_twenty_nine_face_global_reduction_20261031.txt is established, subject
only to the explicitly recorded geometric inputs (1), the audited source
identification, and the exact witness replays. The global relevant dual
has no second height component; both-dependent SL4 charts are included
by closure. The leaf spaces are real and contain e, and are contained
in the relevant component of {N9=0}.

Consequently the twenty-nine full face containments stated there would
imply all actual support gaps belong to C0, including degenerate limits.
Those containments remain a separate, substantial obligation. In
particular the proof does not replace them by future-only statements,
generic face points, or a lower-dimensional budget ansatz.

10. Zariski density of actual projective Gauss normals

There is a stronger density statement than avoidance of the particular
chart tests in Section 7: actual projective Gauss normals are Zariski
dense in Y. This is useful for avoiding any finite collection of proper
closed subsets of Y, after their properness has been independently proved.

First, the complex product map

 (a,b,eta) |-> eta (product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)))_pi

is dominant onto the affine M8 cone. Finite coordinates for the eight
P1 labels form a dense open configuration chart. Individual rescalings
of its eight spinor representatives multiply every matching product by
the same scalar; eta supplies that scalar. Thus this is a dominance
statement about the actual critical source, not a dimension count.

Complexify the eighteen real source coordinates used in Section 7.
The two lists (a,b,eta) and (conjugate(a),conjugate(b),conjugate(eta))
become independent complex lists: the change from complexified real
and imaginary parts to those two lists is invertible linear. Consequently
the complexification of the raw map r=-Re z is the sum of two independent
dominant M8-cone product maps, divided by -2. By definition of the secant
cone, it is dominant onto the affine S cone. Restricting to nonzero eta
on both lists removes only proper closed subsets of the source.

The real source is Zariski dense in this complexification: a complex
polynomial vanishing on a nonempty real open subset of R18 is zero.
Therefore actual raw real profiles are Zariski dense in the complex
secant cone. This assertion concerns complex Zariski closure; it does
not assert that every real secant point has a conjugate source lift.

Section 6 proves that the rational Gauss map of J, restricted to S on
its generic Q!=0 smooth chart, is dominant onto Y. Indeed the restricted
conormal incidence projects dominantly to S and is a P3 bundle over Y0;
at a generic S point the height condition selects its unique conormal.
Composing this dominant rational map with the dominant complexified raw
source proves the asserted Zariski density of actual Gauss normals.
The supporting height does not change a normal because J is invariant
under translation by e.

Accordingly, if D1,...,Dk are homogeneous polynomial tests on Y, none
identically zero there, their nonvanishing holds on a dense set of
actual source parameters where the Gauss map is defined. Clear the
rational denominators after composition; dominance guarantees that
their product is not the zero source polynomial. The real polynomial
argument and support-height continuity from Section 7 finish the
density step. This does not certify properness of a proposed exceptional
set; each Di must first be shown nonzero on Y itself.
