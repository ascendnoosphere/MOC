A four-dimensional cofactor-hull reformulation of unrestricted MOC4
31 October2026

This is an exact global reformulation, not a proof of the remaining hull
inclusion. No historical priority is claimed. It gives a smaller adversarial
search and a possible target for a uniform common-weight formula.

1. Fixed-data affine lemma

Let u_p,v_p be finitely many complex pairs and d1,d0 two complex numbers.
The following statements are equivalent:

 (A) For every x in C,
     d1*x+d0 belongs to conv_R{u_p*x+v_p}.

 (B) (d1,d0) belongs to conv_R{(u_p,v_p)} in C^2=R^4.

 (C) There are weights lambda_p>=0, sum lambda=1, with
     sum lambda_p*u_p=d1 and sum lambda_p*v_p=d0.

Thus a common positive law independent of x follows from all complex x,
even though a law at a single x can depend on x.

Proof. B and C are the definition of finite convex hull. C implies A by
applying the complex-linear map(s,t)->s*x+t. For the converse, first let
|x| tend to infinity in A and divide all complex values by x. Continuity
of a finite convex hull implies d1 belongs to conv_R{u_p}.

Every real-linear functional on C^2 has the form
 (s,t)->Re(mu*s+eta*t), mu,eta in C.
If eta!=0, choose x=mu/eta in A and use the planar support direction eta.
This proves
 Re(mu*d1+eta*d0)<=max_p Re(mu*u_p+eta*v_p).
If eta=0 the same inequality follows from the slope hull just obtained.
Finite convex hulls are closed; separation now proves B.

The slope-limit step can also be proved by choosing a convergent subsequence
of the probability vectors in the compact finite simplex. No unbounded
spectral case is discarded.

2. Homogeneous normalization of the actual four-by-four problem

For real orthogonal O define
 c_p=sign(p) product_i O_i,p(i)/det(O), sum c_p=1.
Its actual determinant identity is
 det(diag(a)+O diag(b) O^T)=sum_p c_p product_i(a_i+b_p(i)).

Write each factor as the ordinary bilinear pairing between a row spinor
 (a_i,1) and a column spinor (1,b_j)^T. Simultaneously applying T^{-1}
to all row spinors and T to all column spinors preserves every pairing.
Rescaling individual spinors multiplies every permutation product by
the same nonzero constant, independent of the permutation. It multiplies
their c-weighted mean by that same constant, preserving hull inclusion.

For three distinct row projective points choose T and row scalings to
normalize the rows to
 (0,1), (1,1), (1,0), (x,1).
Generically every transformed column has first coordinate nonzero and
can be rescaled to(1,beta_j). The normalized products are exactly

 z_p(x)=beta_p(0)*(1+beta_p(1))*(x+beta_p(3))
       =u_p*x+v_p,
 u_p=beta_p(0)*(1+beta_p(1)),
 v_p=u_p*beta_p(3).

The row with spinor(1,0) contributes1, which is the homogeneous meaning
of one normalized a-value at infinity. The linear determinant functional
is defined there by the same polynomial c-expansion, not by assigning
an infinite value to a matrix entry.

3. Exact unrestricted target

Set d1=sum c_p*u_p and d0=sum c_p*v_p. Unrestricted MOC4 is equivalent
to the assertion that, for EVERY real orthogonal O4 and EVERY complex
four-vector beta,

 (d1,d0) in conv_R{(u_p,v_p):p in S4}.                 (H4)

Necessity follows by the affine lemma and homogeneous continuation from
finite spectra. For sufficiency, H4 supplies a positive common law for
all x. Normalize an arbitrary generic eight-spinor configuration as above,
apply H4, and undo the common nonzero scaling. Degenerate configurations
follow by density and continuity of the finite support inequalities.
Finally the already established four-dimensional common-minor-profile
convexification transfers all real-O support bounds to all complex U4.

This reduction does not assume a positive common law for all minors.
Its weights may depend on O and beta. They match only the two complex
cofactor/intercept quantities, while being independent of x.

4. Computation and witness extraction

The24 points are stored in real coordinates
 (Re u_p, Im u_p, Re v_p, Im v_p).
A four-dimensional hull facet normal n corresponds to
 mu=n0-i*n1 and eta=n2-i*n3.
If it separates the mean pair and eta!=0, x=mu/eta recovers an actual
support violation of the normalized spectral problem. A facet with eta=0
gives a slope violation; an arbitrarily small nonzero eta preserves a
strict separating gap and produces a finite x. One can then use another
common projective change avoiding all eight poles to return to finite
spectra.

For an actual O this would be a genuine MOC violation only after exact
verification of the strict hull separation and orthogonality. For a
relaxed SOS-dual functional c it only refutes the stronger SOS route;
it must not be called a MOC counterexample.

The cofactor hull lets an adversary optimize only the eight real beta
parameters. It maximizes over all x and all planar support directions
by finite hull-facet enumeration. The current implementation is
code/research/moc_adversarial_cofactor_dual.py, owned by the positive-
representation lane. Numerical facet decisions are discovery only.

5. Principal-minor form and exact bookkeeping audit

Put B=O diag(beta) O^T. The two mean coordinates have the simple forms
 d1=B00+det B[{0,1},{0,1}],
 d0=det B[{0,3},{0,3}]+det B[{0,1,3},{0,1,3}].
Equivalently d1*x+d0 is det(B[{0,1,3}]+diag(0,1,x)). These follow by
determinant multilinearity and the common-minor expansion.

A tempting spectrum-independent strengthening is false: matching ALL
subset marginals for the diamond
 {0} subset {0,1},{0,3} subset {0,1,3}
already matches the full14-dimensional common-minor profile space.
The exact evaluation table has rank14. Such a universal profile law
is the previously refuted common-minor conjecture. H4 only needs two
complex combinations for one beta, and permits beta-dependent weights.

code/research/moc_cofactor_hull_exact_audit.py verifies these formulas,
the rank14 distinction, and one exact common law at Q59 with
 beta=(1+i,2-i,-1+2i,3+i).
The five lexicographic permutation indices are3,4,10,11,14; their exact
positive weights have common denominator82106347 and numerators
 16847013,31110878,22542104,8487334,3119018.
The two coefficient identities prove the law works for every x in
this fixed example, without sampling x. This example audits the reduction
and is not evidence of uniform completion. Raw data:
 build/moc_cofactor_hull/exact_audit.json.

The finite affine-hull and common-weight equivalence has also been
accepted in Lean: code/lean/MocJointAffineHull.lean. Its finite-only
support theorem derives the slope inequality by taking a sufficiently
large real x algebraically, so no infinity assumption is necessary.
Projective spectral normalization and the original unitary bridge are
separate mathematical inputs, not hidden axioms of that module.

The missing proof remains exactly the uniform inclusion H4. Nothing in
the affine lemma establishes it.
