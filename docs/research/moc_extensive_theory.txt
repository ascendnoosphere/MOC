MOC: A RANDOM-ENSEMBLE THEOREM AND TESTED DETERMINISTIC CERTIFICATES
31 October 2026

The main result is a high-probability all-dimensional theorem for independent
complex Gaussian spectra and Haar mixing. It follows from an explicit
three-permutation triangle and the exact Haar second moment. This is a
theorem about a specified random ensemble, not a proof of unrestricted MOC.
The accompanying deterministic tests also refute a natural attempt to turn
compound purity into a universal hull-contraction bound.

1. Gaussian spectra: MOC holds with exponentially high probability

Let a_1,...,a_n,b_1,...,b_n be independent standard complex Gaussian variables
with density pi^-1 exp(-|z|^2), and let U be an independent Haar unitary.
Write d=det(D_a+U D_b U*) and P=conv{product_i(a_i+b_pi(i))}. For n>=4,

 P(d not in P) <= (1-p0)^floor(n/4) + exp(-n/400)
                 + (8/3)(n+2)(n+3) exp(-n/50),                   (1)
 p0 = exp(-5)/200^16 > 0.

The right side can be replaced by its minimum with 1. In particular, there
are positive absolute C,c such that the failure probability is at most
C exp(-c n). The explicit p0 is deliberately conservative: approximately
1.0281e-39. Formula (1) is therefore an asymptotic theorem, not a useful
numerical guarantee at n=1000. No novelty claim is made without a broader
literature search. The construction was proposed by the root researcher;
the argument and constants below were checked independently in this task.

Step A: a deterministic four-index triangle.

Let z0=product_i(a_i+b_i), with no zero factors. On four indices, define

 q1=((a1+b2)(a2+b1))/((a1+b1)(a2+b2)),
 q2=((a3+b4)(a4+b3))/((a3+b3)(a4+b4)).

Three permitted matching products are z0, z0*q1, and z0*q1*q2. They come
from the identity, one transposition, and two disjoint transpositions.
Let omega=(-1+i sqrt(3))/2. If |q1-omega|,|q2-omega|<1/10, these three
products contain the closed disk centered at zero of radius |z0|/4.

To prove this, normalize by z0. The vertices 1,q1,q1q2 differ from
1,omega,omega^2 by at most 0,1/10,21/100, respectively. The equilateral
triangle has support at least 1/2 in every unit direction. Perturbing its
vertices by at most 21/100 changes every support value by at most 21/100.
The new triangle therefore has support at least 29/100 in every direction,
and contains the disk of that radius. We use the smaller radius 1/4.
This support argument also proves that zero remains inside the triangle;
it does not assume that conclusion from the angular appearance alone.

Step B: independent blocks supply such a triangle.

The four complex centers

 (a1,a2,b1,b2)=(0,1,1,t), t=(-2+i sqrt(3))/7,

satisfy q1=2t/(1+t)=omega, with |t|=1/sqrt(7) and |1+t|=2/sqrt(7).
Use the same centers for the second pair. Suppose each of the eight
variables lies in a complex disk of radius r=1/200 around its center.
Each pair sum then changes by at most 2r. Writing q=N/D, the product
error bounds and |N0|=|D0| give

 |q-omega| <= (9r+8r^2)/(2/3-4r-4r^2)
            =1356/19397 < 1/10.                                  (2)

The coarse constants use 1/sqrt(7)<1/2 and 2/sqrt(7)>2/3. The denominator
in (2) is strictly positive. Thus every point in these eight disks is a
good block; no zero denominator occurs there.

A standard complex Gaussian lands in a disk of radius r about c with
probability at least r^2 exp(-(|c|+r)^2). The sum of the eight exponent
bounds is at most

 2*(r^2+2*(1+r)^2+(1/2+r)^2)=22751/5000 < 5.

Hence a given four-index block is good with probability at least p0.
Disjoint four-index blocks involve disjoint spectral variables and are
independent, so the probability that none is good is at most the first
term of (1). The chosen good block may depend on the observed spectra.

Step C: the common matching product is exponentially large.

The independent variables X_i=log|a_i+b_i| have

 E X_i=(log 2-gamma)/2 > 0,
 E exp(-s X_i)=2^(-s/2) Gamma(1-s/2), 0<s<2.

This follows because |a_i+b_i|^2/2 has exponential density exp(-x).
Take s=1/10. Chernoff's inequality gives

 P(|z0|<exp(n/100))
 <= [exp(1/1000)*2^(-1/20)*Gamma(19/20)]^n
 <= exp(-n/400).                                                  (3)

The last numerical inequality has a rational verification. For x=1/20,
the classical series log Gamma(1-x)=gamma*x+sum_{k>=2}zeta(k)x^k/k gives

 1/1000-x log 2+log Gamma(1-x)
 < 1/1000-x*(693/1000)+x*(578/1000)
       +(1645/1000)*x^2/(2*(1-x))
 = -393/152000 < -1/400.

The inequalities log 2>0.693, gamma<0.578, zeta(2)<1.645 are themselves
checked with finite rational sums. Use four positive terms of
2*atanh(1/3) for log 2; gamma<H_1024-10 log 2; and the integral-test upper
bound sum_{j=1}^{100}j^-2+1/100 for zeta(2). For k>=2, zeta(k)<=zeta(2),
and sum_{k>=2}x^k/k<=x^2/(2(1-x)). The gamma series follows also from
NIST DLMF 5.7.3 by expanding -log(1-x): https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.7.E3 .

Step D: Haar mixing keeps the determinant below that disk.

The independently checked moment identity in moc_campaign_theory_20261031.txt
gives E|d|^2=(n+2)(n+3)/6. Markov's inequality implies

 P(|d|>(1/4) exp(n/100))
 <= (8/3)(n+2)(n+3) exp(-n/50).                                   (4)

If no event on the right of (1) occurs, a good block exists, its three
matching products surround a disk of radius at least exp(n/100)/4, and
d belongs to that disk. This proves (1). The good-block event, baseline
product, and determinant are correlated. The proof uses a union bound
and requires no independence among these three events. Independence is
used only between disjoint spectral blocks, between the baseline factors,
and between U and the spectra in the moment theorem.

The finite rational/algebraic constants passed
code/research/verify_moc_gaussian_triangle_constants.py. Its exploratory
simulation found the broader event that the triangle (1,q1,q1q2) contains
the disk of radius 1/4 in 4441 of 200000 independent blocks (0.022205).
This frequency is not used as a rigorous probability bound. Improving
the explicit p0 needs a larger analytically controlled region.

Independent audit: docs/research/moc_gaussian_triangle_independent_audit_20261031.txt
reports PASS after replaying the constant verifier, checking the geometric
and probability arguments, and reading the DLMF gamma expansion. The
theorem is a written mathematical proof with exact finite constant checks;
it has not been formalized in Lean.

General distribution criterion. Let all spectral entries be iid from a
centered complex law with E|a|^2=1. Assume E log|a+b|=m>0 for independent
draws a,b, E|a+b|^-s0<infinity for some s0>0, and positive probability
that a four-index block has both swap ratios within 1/10 of omega.
Then, for independent Haar U, MOC fails with probability at most C exp(-c n)
for constants C,c>0 depending on this law. Indeed, choose 0<alpha<m.
The negative moment and finite variance justify differentiation at zero
of E exp(-s(log|a+b|-alpha)); its derivative is alpha-m<0. A small
positive s therefore gives an exponentially decreasing Chernoff bound.
The same exact second moment (n+2)(n+3)/6 and the independent-block
argument finish the proof. Finite variance bounds the positive logarithmic
tail, and the assumed negative moment bounds its negative tail.

This includes the uniform distribution on the disk of radius sqrt(2).
It is centered with variance one. Rotational averaging gives
E log|a+b|=E log max(|a|,|b|)=log(sqrt(2))-1/4>0: the maximum radius
has density r^3 on [0,sqrt(2)]. The sum density is bounded, so its negative
moments of orders 0<s<2 are finite by a planar radial integral. The eight
target disks above lie inside the radius-sqrt(2) disk, so their joint
probability is exactly (r^2/2)^8>0. This proves a second all-dimensional
random-ensemble corollary; no explicit Chernoff constants are claimed for
it here.

For the worst-case conjecture none of the probability statements rules
out a rare or structured violating input. The deterministic four-index
disk certificate is the part that can be tested on an individual instance.

2. An affine covariance certificate for actual matching products

For any m selected complex matching products, identify C with R^2 and set
 mu=(1/m)sum_j z_j, x_j=z_j-mu, Sigma=(1/m)sum_j x_j x_j^T.
Assume Sigma is positive definite. For y=d-mu define

 w_j=(1/m)*(1+x_j^T Sigma^-1 y).                                  (5)

Then sum_j w_j=1 and sum_j w_j z_j=d exactly. Consequently all w_j>=0
is a sufficient MOC certificate. This holds for any subset of matching
products and any n, so enumeration of n! products is unnecessary.
Proof: sum_j x_j=0, while (1/m)sum_j x_j x_j^T=Sigma.

Put R^2=y^T Sigma^-1 y and L=max_j x_j^T Sigma^-1 x_j. Cauchy-Schwarz
proves that R^2 L<=1 is a simpler sufficient certificate. Also L<=m-1:
the whitened rows have sum zero and covariance identity, and the diagonal
of their projection matrix is bounded by that of the mean-zero projection.
Thus R^2<=1/(m-1) suffices without computing L. These conditions are
affine invariant. They are sufficient only; negative canonical weights
do not refute MOC or exclude another nonnegative representation.

The complete-hull portion of the combined 20127-record campaign contains
9364 records. Reconstructing every matching product in those records and
requiring major RMS axis>1e-12 and axis ratio>=1e-6 leaves 6711 provisional
numerical analyses. Among them, 3205 have nonnegative weights (5), 2010
pass R^2 L<=1, and 574 pass R^2<=1/(m-1). These are float64 diagnostics,
not interval certificates. Very small absolute minor axes remain a risk;
the integration agent applies a stronger conditioning filter by record ID.
Do not present these raw counts as certified inclusions or as independent
new experimental draws. They reanalyze existing campaign records.

3. Compound purity does not determine a universal hull contraction

Let S(U)=sum_{k,I,J}|det U[I,J]|^4 and let mu be the full permutation
centroid. A plausible strengthening was

 d in mu+sqrt(kappa)*(P-mu),
 kappa=(S(U)-(n+1))/(2^n-(n+1)).                                  (6)

At n=2 this is exact: S(U)=3+(2|u11|^2-1)^2 and the determinant lies on
the matching segment at the corresponding signed displacement. At a
permutation mixer kappa=1. Nevertheless (6) is false at n=4.

Take U to be the normalized Fourier4 matrix and
 a=(2-i,-1-2i,2-2i,i), b=(-1-2i,2,2i,1+i).
Exact arithmetic gives

 S(U)=21/4, kappa=1/44,
 d=9/4-99i/4, mu=73/6-129i/4.

For q=-77-34i define support_q(z)=Re(conj(q)z). The largest matching
support is 2040, and

 support_q(d-mu)=6103/12,
 max_pi support_q(z_pi-mu)=5641/3.

Their ratio 6103/22564 is approximately 0.270475, larger than
sqrt(1/44), approximately 0.150756. The exact squared violation is
(6103/12)^2-(1/44)*(5641/3)^2=282429175/1584>0.

This is not a counterexample to MOC. In fact d is the convex combination
of the products indexed by (0,1,2,3), (0,1,3,2), and (0,3,2,1), with
strictly positive respective weights 9/80,1/4,51/80. The exact verifier
checks unitarity, all minors, all 24 matching products, the separating
support calculation, and the positive triangle identity:
code/research/verify_moc_purity_contraction_counterexample.py.

The same exact example passes the affine covariance certificate: its
minimum weight (5) is 4738357/522067008>0 and R^2 L equals
98765249659741/118295989948816<1. The verifier checks all 24 weights and
their exact determinant reconstruction as well as the three-point witness.

The provisional numerical analysis also finds 1636 failures of (6) among
4520 n=3/4 tests. The exact rational example already settles falsity; those
floating counts only describe how often the stronger claim fails in this
sample. The lesson is specific: the scalar sum S(U) forgets which minor
coordinates carry the spectral cost. It controls a spectral ensemble
second moment but cannot impose the proposed deterministic contraction.

4. Consequences for the unrestricted proof search

The random theorem upgrades the observed high-dimensional interior
behavior to a proved statement for one ensemble. It identifies a small
explicit certificate: three products differing on only four indices.
The affine covariance construction gives another all-dimensional
sufficient certificate that can be checked on selected products and
upgraded to exact or interval arithmetic.

Neither mechanism covers arbitrary fixed spectra and U. A full proof
would need a reason that every input admits suitable nonnegative
weights or another certificate, including thin hulls, highly structured
spectra, and noncommuting boundary maximizers. The exact counterexample
above rules out one tempting replacement of that obligation by a single
purity number. It does not rule out spectral-direction-dependent
compound inequalities. This note proves no new universal MOC case
beyond its explicitly stated sufficient conditions and random ensemble.
