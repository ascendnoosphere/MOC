Compact spinor normalization of unrestricted MOC
31 October 2026

This is an exact all-dimensional reduction and a necessary-condition theorem
for a hypothetical counterexample. It does not establish the conjecture.
Its purpose is to remove noncompact spectral/infinity issues before drawing
conclusions from global spectral minimax. No contact-count inference is made.

1. A compact parameter space capturing the entire unrestricted problem

For i,j=1,...,n take unit vectors u_i=(x_i,y_i), v_j=(s_j,t_j) in C^2.
Put [u,v]=x t-y s and let U be an n-by-n unitary. Define

 p_pi=product_i [u_i,v_pi(i)],
 M_ij=[u_i,v_j] U_ij,
 d=det(M)/det(U).

The denominator is nonzero and has modulus1. All these functions are smooth
on the compact manifold (S^3)^(2n) times U(n). Put

                  h=max_pi Re(p_pi-d).                 (1)

The unrestricted complex MOC(n) holds if and only if h>=0 everywhere on this
compact manifold. Consequently, if MOC(n) fails, h has a negative attained
global minimum m.

The global minimum is always<=0: take U=I and all row spinors equal, so
all p_pi coincide with d and h=0. Therefore the full conjecture is exactly
the assertion that this compact global minimum is0, in every dimension.

Proof of exact scope.
If all bottom coordinates y_i,t_j are nonzero, let
 a_i=x_i/y_i, b_j=-s_j/t_j, L=product_i y_i product_j t_j.
Then M=diag(y)(D_a U+U D_b)diag(t), so

 d=L det(D_a+U D_b U*),
 p_pi=L product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)).                         (2)

Every finite spectral tuple is represented by normalizing spinors(a_i,1)
and(-b_j,1). Any desired common phase of all p_pi,d can be obtained by
multiplying one unit spinor by that phase. Thus every bad support direction
for finite spectra produces h<0 at a unit-spinor tuple.

Conversely, for any finite collection of nonzero spinors, a common S in SU(2)
can make every bottom coordinate nonzero. Choose the bottom row outside the
finite union of their orthogonal complex lines, then complete it to SU(2).
Common S preserves all brackets because detS=1, and preserves all unit norms.
It therefore preserves p_pi and d exactly. Formula(2) now applies with L!=0.
If h<0, divide byL and transform the support multiplier byL; this is an
ordinary unrestricted finite spectral counterexample. No appeal to an
unproved rectangular statement or to a value at an infinite eigenvalue is used.

2. Determinant contraction

Let J=[[0,1],[-1,0]]. Define two isometries from C^n to C^n tensor C^2:
 A has column_i equal to the vector bar(u_i) in block_i, zero elsewhere;
 B has column_j equal to J v_j in block_j, zero elsewhere.
Their columns have disjoint supports and norm1. Direct multiplication gives

                    M=A* (U tensor I2) B.

Thus M is a contraction. Its singular values are<=1, giving |d|<=1.
Also |p_pi|<=1 since every bracket of unit spinors has modulus<=1. In
particular(1) is a uniformly bounded continuous function. This norm bound
alone is not a permutation-hull inclusion.

3. Necessary simultaneous spectral criticality at a negative global minimum

Let A be the nonempty set of active permutations at a global minimum m<0.
There exist real lambda_pi>=0, supported on A and summing to1, such that
the differential, restricted to the full tangent space, of

             Re P,   P=sum_pi lambda_pi p_pi-d,

vanishes. This follows from elementary finite-dimensional separation: if
zero were outside the convex hull of the active restricted gradients, a
tangent vector would strictly decrease all active functions, contradicting
local minimality. Inactive functions remain below them in a neighborhood.
The same lambda are used for every spinor and every U tangent direction.

Hold these lambda fixed only to state first-order criticality. Since P is
holomorphic and degree1 separately in each of its2n spinor labels w_k,
sphere-tangent vanishing gives real numbers tau_k with

           partial P/partial w_k=tau_k bar(w_k).

Euler's identity for degree1 in the kth spinor gives P=tau_k, since||w_k||=1.
All tau_k therefore coincide and P is real. Its real value is the weighted
average of the active gaps, namely m. Thus

 P=m,
 partial P/partial x_k=m bar(x_k),
 partial P/partial y_k=m bar(y_k),                       (3)

for all2n labels. Spinor phase tangents are included; they are essential to
the conclusion that P is real. No differentiability of the maximum itself
is presumed.

A scalar Caratheodory bound for a fixed complex output does not limit these
lambda to three nonzero entries: the same law must cancel all spectral
gradients. The earlier three-permutation theorem applies only to one fixed
complex-eigenvalue affine fiber, not to this joint criticality system.

4. Moment balance

All bipartite perfect-matching products are invariant under commonSL(2,C)
action on the2n spinors. The determinant expression d has the same invariance
entrywise, so P does too. Differentiating in any traceless complex2-by-2
matrix H and using(3) yields

     0=sum_k (partial P/partial w_k) dot H w_k
      =m tr(H sum_k w_k w_k*).

Because m!=0 and this holds for all traceless H,

                  sum_k w_k w_k*=n I2.                 (4)

Entrywise, the diagonal-generator identity gives equal sums of the squared
coordinate moduli; their total is2n, so each sum is n. Off-diagonal generators
give sum x_k bar(y_k)=0. All multiplicities and zero brackets are allowed.
This is a concrete real-algebraic restriction on the actual spectral labels.

Along the normalized commonSL(2,C) orbit, every p_pi,d is multiplied by
R(S)=1/product_k||S w_k||, for detS=1. A negative global minimum requires
product_k||S w_k||>=1. Equation(4) is its infinitesimal form. Conversely,
convexity of sum_k log||exp(tH)w_k|| for Hermitian tracelessH, together with
(4), proves the global product inequality along every such orbit. This
balance controls the common projective orbit; it does not prove MOC.

5. Boundary multiplicities

Equation(4) excludes any projective point with multiplicity>n, since its
projector alone would have eigenvalue>n. If a point has multiplicity exactlyn,
all othern labels must lie in its orthogonal projective point: evaluate(4)
on the first unit vector and use nonnegativity of every remaining overlap.

For such a two-point configuration, MOC holds directly. Up to unit phases
and a commonSU2, every spinor is e1 or e2. Suppose r of the row labels aree1.
Then exactlyr column labels aree2. Reorder rows with their e1 group first,
and columns with their e2 group first. All nonzero permutation products equal
one common complex number p of modulus1. The masked matrix M is block
diagonal, with blocks U11 and -U22, times the unit row/column phases.
Jacobi's complementary-minor identity for a unitary gives

                    d=p |det U11|^2.

The coefficient lies in[0,1]. For0<r<n, some permutation products are0, so
d lies in their segment[0,p]. For r=0 or n every product equals p and d=p.
Thus a negative global minimum cannot have projective multiplicity>=n.
For n=4 the previously proved zero-pair and double-eigenvalue cases exclude
all repeated labels, but that stronger exclusion is an imported result.

6. Correct coupled second-variation obligation

One may NOT infer that the Hessian of ReP is positive semidefinite on all
product-sphere directions. P is a supporting weighted average below a
nonsmooth maximum, and its first-order criticality does not make it a local
minimum. A necessary second-order test must respect the active functions.

Here is a polynomial pencil for such a test. Let K be traceless skew-Hermitian
and vary U(t)=exp(tK)U. For spinor tangent vectors z_k with w_k* z_k=0, use
w_k(t)=(w_k+t z_k)/sqrt(1+t^2||z_k||^2). Let C_ij=[u_i,v_j], let C_z denote
its first spinor variation, and write

 M=C Hadamard U,
 N=C Hadamard(KU), R=C Hadamard(K^2U), M_z=C_z Hadamard U.

With Ddet_M and D2det_M denoting first and second polarized determinant
differentials, all valid also for singularM,

 D_K d = Ddet_M[N]/detU,
 D_K^2 d=(D2det_M[N,N]+Ddet_M[R])/detU,
 D_z D_K d=(D2det_M[M_z,N]+Ddet_M[C_z Hadamard(KU)])/detU. (5)

U-stationarity gives Re D_Kd=0 for everyK. If
 Re D_z(p_pi-d)=0 for every active pi,
then each active gap has second derivative

 Re[D_z^2 p_pi-D_z^2 d-2D_zD_K d-D_K^2 d]
                  -m sum_k||z_k||^2.                  (6)

At least one active value in(6) must be nonnegative for every admissible(z,K).
Finding(z,K) that makes all of them negative would exclude a hypothetical
negative global minimum. For a one-label spinor tangent, multiaffineness
makes D_z^2p_pi=D_z^2d=0, but generally the simultaneous active first-order
conditions leave no such nonzero tangent. This restriction cannot be dropped.

Remaining global theorem.
The compact formulation and equations(3)--(6) are exact. Excluding m<0 using
these equations remains unproved. Moment balance by itself is not an
inertia certificate or a rank bound; generic distinct configurations can
satisfy it. The equations provide a globally attained, normalized target
for a coupled spectral/unitary calculation, without assuming away infinity.
