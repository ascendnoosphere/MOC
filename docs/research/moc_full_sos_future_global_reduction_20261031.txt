1. The full cone and strict separating witnesses

Let B be a full-column-rank real24x14 matching frame and define
 C0={diag(B X B^T): X>=0}.
This cone is closed. Indeed sum_pi g_pi=tr(X B^T B) bounds tr X whenever
g is bounded and X>=0; B^T B is positive definite. A bounded sequence of
costs therefore has bounded Gram proposals and a convergent PSD subsequence.

For a zero-contact set C let F=B E, where the columns of E span the kernel
of B[C,:]. A nonnegative g vanishing on C is in C0 if and only if
 g=diag(F S F^T), S>=0.
Every square in any SOS must vanish at each zero of g, proving necessity.
Sufficiency is immediate. This is an exact full-cone reduction, retaining
all14 matching features before the unavoidable contact directions are removed.

If g is outside this cone, closed-cone separation gives a functional y with
 F^T diag(y) F>=0 and y dot g<0. A small addition of a strictly positive
moment functional preserves the negative pairing and makes the contact
moment positive definite. Contact weights can be set to zero, since they
do not affect this contact moment, g, or the mixed ray f34.

For a canonical nonordinary cap
 g=t f34+sum_{j in Z} beta_j r_j, t>0,beta_j>=0,
all ordinary-ray evaluations at a PSD moment are nonnegative. Hence a
negative pairing necessarily has y dot f34<0. Positive scaling gives the
EXACT homogeneous search gauge y dot f34=-1; no arbitrary negativity cut
or trace normalization is needed.

A strict contact-moment witness also has a full14 PSD completion. Add
sufficient positive point mass at each contact. In the decomposition into
contact-null and contact-evaluation directions, its Schur complement is
positive once the added contact block is large enough. This preserves
y dot g and y dot f34. Thus any robust negative hit here really separates
the entire C0 cone. It would not alone refute MOC; a physical negative
orthogonal profile would still be needed.

Hence generic unique-contact configurations are the relevant discovery
target; a proof of absence would still have to justify density/charts and
