Exact positive and signed common-minor laws on the toric Walsh path
31 October 2026

This is an exact classification for the specified one-parameter mixers, not
an unrestricted MOC proof. It supplies a quantitative obstruction encountered
by the positive diagonal flag-torus escape developed in the parent lane.
No priority claim is made relative to general fourteen-term identities.

Put d=p^2+1 and, for p>0, define

                [p^2, p,    p,    1   ]
  Q(p) = 1/d *  [1,  -p,   p,   -p^2 ] .
                [p,   1,  -p^2, -p   ]
                [p,  -p^2,-1,    p   ]

Its numerator N satisfies NN^T=d^2 I, so Q(p) is orthogonal. At p=1 it is
the normalized Walsh matrix. It arises by Gram-Schmidt from
diag(p,p^-1,1,1)W, with the indicated eigenvector ordering.

Permutations use zero-based lexicographic indices in S4; their one-based
word forms are stored in the JSON artifact. Define numerator weights

    indices 0,3,11,16:          p^2(p^2-1)^2 each;
    indices 1,6,9,14,17,22:    2p^4 each;
    index 5:                   p^2(p^6+3p^2-2);
    index 18:                  1+2p^2-p^4;
    every other index:         zero.

These twelve weights sum identically to d^4. For every equal-size pair of
subsets I,J of {0,1,2,3}, including empty and full subsets, they satisfy

  |det Q(p)[I,J]|^2
      = sum_pi [weight_pi(p)/d^4] 1_{pi(I)=J}.             (1)

All 70 identities hold in Q(p), verified by exact symbolic arithmetic.
Consequently, whenever these weights are positive, the complete determinant
polynomial for arbitrary complex spectra is a convex combination of the
matching products. This is a genuine arbitrary-spectra mixer theorem.

For p>=1, every displayed numerator except index18 is nonnegative, and
index18 is nonnegative exactly when p<=sqrt(1+sqrt(2)). Thus the formulas
give a positive law on that entire interval.

An exact dual proves that this interval cannot be enlarged by choosing a
different spectrum-independent common-minor law. Let m_ij=|Q_ij|^2 and
m_(I,J)=|det Q[I,J]|^2, with zero-based indices. Define

 L = 1-m_00-(1/2)m_01-m_02-(1/2)m_12-(1/2)m_20-(1/2)m_22
       +(1/2)m_(01,02)+(1/2)m_(02,01)+(1/2)m_(02,02).

For a permutation matrix the 24 values of L all lie in {0,1/2,1}. Therefore
L>=0 for every nonnegative common-minor law. Direct substitution gives

      L(Q(p))=(1+2p^2-p^4)/(p^2+1)^4.                    (2)

For p>sqrt(1+sqrt(2)), (2) is negative, so no such positive common law
exists. This is an exact method obstruction; it is not a spectral MOC
counterexample.

More quantitatively, let nu=sum max(-w_pi,0) be the negative mass of any
real signed common-minor law. Since every permutation score lies in [0,1],
equation (2) gives nu>=max(0,-L(Q(p))). Our explicit law has just the one
potential negative index18, so it attains this bound for every p>=1:

  min nu = max(0,p^4-2p^2-1)/(p^2+1)^4,       p>=1.       (3)

The reciprocal identity

 Q(1/p)=diag(1,-1,-1,1) Q(p) J_reverse

is exact. Row phases and column permutations preserve existence and least
negative mass of common-minor laws. Thus a positive law exists for p>0
exactly on

  sqrt(sqrt(2)-1) <= p <= sqrt(1+sqrt(2)).                (4)

For 0<p<=1 the minimum negative mass is

  p^4 max(0,1-2p^2-p^4)/(p^2+1)^4.                       (5)

At p=2 (and p=1/2), the minimum is exactly 7/625. The maximum over p>0 of
the minimum negative mass equals (5sqrt(5)-11)/16, attained at
p=sqrt(2+sqrt(5)) and its reciprocal. Differentiating (3) with q=p^2 gives
-2(q^2-4q-1)/(q+1)^5, proving this maximum. The endpoint limits p=0 and
p=infinity are monomial mixers, so their negative masses return to zero.

Implication for the topological route

The special Walsh spectral support has an explicit quartic ascent all the
way along this toric degeneration toward an exposed permutation. Equations
(3)-(5) show that part of that same path leaves the cone of common positive
minor laws. Thus a proof based on toric ascent must use the actual spectral
cost or otherwise handle signed minor identities; common spectrum-independent
positivity cannot cover the whole escape. This is a computable distinction
between two proposed mechanisms, not merely an absent proof.
