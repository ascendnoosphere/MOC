Finite-time unitary heat flow: an explicit law and two exact obstructions
31 October 2026

This attack does not prove unrestricted MOC. It gives an exact positive
permutation representation for a finite-time heat average of every complex
4 by 4 instance, and shows exactly why two natural ways of removing that
averaging fail. The finite-time result is uniform in the spectra and mixer.
No assertion of historical novelty is made.

The strongest bound obtained below is t >= 0.1075604517..., with the
exact threshold defined by 2r^3+3r^2=3 and t=-log(r)/2. It uses the
repository's previously verified 82-ray cone theorem. A simpler explicit
formula, proved directly from unitary minor identities, works at
t >= log(3)/4. Both statements concern heat averages, not the original
determinant at time zero.

The useful formula is particularly short. For U in U(4) and pi in S4, set

  S1_pi = sum_i |U[i,pi(i)]|^2,
  S2_pi = sum_{|I|=2} |det U[I,pi(I)]|^2.

For arbitrary a,b in C^4 let z_pi=product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)), and let
f(U)=det(diag(a)+U diag(b) U*). Then

  f(U) = sum_pi [(S1_pi+2 S2_pi-2)/24] z_pi.                 (1)

These real weights sum to one but can have either sign. Under the heat
normalization specified below, the time-t expectation has weights

  w_pi(t) = [1-3r^2 + (3r^2-2r^3)S1_pi + 2r^3 S2_pi]/24,
  r=exp(-2t).                                               (2)

Every weight in (2) is nonnegative when t >= log(3)/4. Consequently

  E[f(V_t U)] belongs to conv{z_pi}

for every complex a,b and U in dimension four at all those times. V_t is
unitary Brownian motion started at the identity. This is a finite-time
quadrature formula, not just the familiar Haar limit. It does not imply
the corresponding statement at t=0.

1. General proof of (1), including all coefficient identities

Write p_ij=|U_ij|^2 and m_IJ=|det U[I,J]|^2 for two-element subsets. Both
matrices p and m are doubly stochastic. Complementary minors have equal
squared modulus. We also use the one-particle contraction identity

  sum_{J containing j} m_IJ = sum_{i in I} p_ij.             (3)

One elementary proof of (3) applies Cauchy--Binet to the two orthonormal
rows indexed by I. For a fixed j, summing
|u_j v_l-u_l v_j|^2 over l cancels the cross term by orthogonality and
leaves |u_j|^2+|v_j|^2. This proof applies to complex U.

There are six permutations taking a fixed i to a fixed j. Summing S1 over
them gives

  6p_ij + 2 sum_{l!=i,k!=j} p_lk = 4+8p_ij.

For S2, the three subsets containing i and their complements contribute
equally. Each allowed target two-subset has two preimages among these
six permutations. Therefore, using (3),

  sum_{pi(i)=j} S2_pi
    = 4 sum_{I containing i,J containing j} m_IJ
    = 4(1+2p_ij) = 4+8p_ij.

It follows that the sum of the signed weights in (1) over pi(i)=j is p_ij.

Now fix two-subsets I,J. Put m=m_IJ and s=sum_{i in I,j in J}p_ij.
Exactly four permutations take I onto J. The total entry mass of the
complementary block also equals s, and hence

  sum_{pi(I)=J} S1_pi = 4s.                                (4)

Put q=m_{I,J^c}. The two-row contraction identity gives s=1+m-q.
In the S2 sum, the terms indexed by I and I^c contribute 8m. The other
four subsets cross the I/I^c partition. Summing their cross-target terms
and using the column sums of m gives 2+2m+2q. Thus

  sum_{pi(I)=J} S2_pi = 2+10m+2q = 4+12m-2s.              (5)

Combining (4) and (5) in (1) recovers m exactly. Complementation gives
the coefficients for three-subsets. Finally, each p_ij is counted six
times in sum_pi S1_pi and each m_IJ four times in sum_pi S2_pi, so

  sum_pi S1_pi = sum_pi S2_pi = 24.

This proves normalization and the empty/full-subset coefficients.
Expansion of the determinant by Cauchy--Binet now proves (1) for every
complex a,b. The argument is a proof on arbitrary unitary minors; it is
not an inference from a finite set of tested matrices.

There is a second exact computational check of this general argument.
The verifier uses 53 formal coordinates: a constant, 16 entry squares,
and 36 two-minor squares. It imposes only the doubly stochastic,
complement, and one-particle contraction identities just proved above.
It verifies over Q that every one of the 70 reconstruction residuals
lies in the span of those linear constraints. This certificate applies
to all profiles satisfying the identities, not only to sampled mixers.

2. The Brownian generator is a finite exclusion generator on this module

For i<j define real tangent fields from

  A_ij=E_ij-E_ji,   B_ij=i(E_ij+E_ji),   D_i=i E_ii.

Use the bi-invariant Brownian generator

  L = (1/4) sum_{i<j}(A_ij^2+B_ij^2) + (1/2)sum_i D_i^2,

where a tangent symbol denotes its left directional derivative. Diagonal
fields act trivially on f and on squared minors. If precisely one of i,j
lies in I, a rotated minor is c u + s exp(i phi) v, up to a harmless
sign. Adding the real and imaginary second derivatives cancels the
interference terms. Consequently

  L |det U[I,J]|^2
    = sum_{i in I,j not in I}
       (|det U[I-i+j,J]|^2 - |det U[I,J]|^2).                (6)

If both or neither row lies in I the contribution is zero. Formula (6)
is the continuous-time simple exclusion process on k-subsets of the
complete graph, with rate one on each exchange.

For f itself this becomes the exact identity

  Lf(U) = sum_{i<j} [f(P_ij U)-f(U)],                      (7)

where P_ij swaps rows i,j. Thus unitary heat expectation and the finite
random-transposition semigroup agree on the determinant module. To see
the semigroup conclusion, apply (7) simultaneously to the 24 functions
f(PU); the transposition sum is central and closes the same finite
linear ODE. Uniqueness of its solution gives the claimed equality.

Equations (6) and (7) hold in every dimension. In dimensions two and
three the only nonconstant subset type has rate n, so the heat mean is
exactly mu+exp(-nt)(f(U)-mu). Radial contraction is therefore true there
in every norm. Dimension four introduces a second decay rate, and the
exact example below proves that this new rate already defeats radial
contraction. This is a concrete distinction between the first unresolved
dimension and the dimensions below it.

For n=4, the subset modules contain just the S4 types (4), (3,1), (2,2),
with generator eigenvalues 0,-4,-6. There is also an elementary finite
certificate: the exact 24 by 24 integer transposition generator Q obeys

  Q(Q+4I)(Q+6I) v_IJ = 0

for every one of the 70 subset-indicator vectors v_IJ(pi)=1_{pi(I)=J}.
The included verifier checks these exact matrix products. Since every
row-permutation orbit of f is a linear combination of these vectors,
the certificate applies to arbitrary spectral coefficients and arbitrary U.

Define

  mu = (1/24) sum_pi z_pi,
  X  = sum_pi [(S1_pi-1)/8] z_pi,
  Y  = sum_pi [(S2_pi-S1_pi)/12] z_pi.

Then f=mu+X+Y and

  E[f(V_t U)] = mu + exp(-4t)X + exp(-6t)Y.                (8)

Equivalently, X=3(f-mu)+Lf/2 and Y=-2(f-mu)-Lf/2. Equations
(1), (6), and the finite module calculation prove (2). For r in [0,1],
the coefficients 3r^2-2r^3 and 2r^3 are nonnegative; 1-3r^2 is
nonnegative at the stated time. This proves the positive quadrature law.

The threshold is sharp for nonnegativity of this particular formula:
take U=I and pi a four-cycle. Then S1_pi=S2_pi=0, so this weight is
(1-3r^2)/24. This does not prove the threshold is sharp for the existence
of other positive weights; at U=I the original determinant already is
one matching product.

2a. Earlier positive endpoint laws from the complete minor cone

The existing verified cone theorem gives 82 rays for the cone C82 of
nonnegative permutation functions in the 14-dimensional common-minor
space: 34 ordinary squared-minor rays and 48 mixed rays. Inside C82,
membership in the ordinary cone C34 is characterized by the 48 capacity
duals W. This is an existing exact theorem, not a new numerical premise.

Let T_r be the transposition heat operator, with eigenvalues 1,r^2,r^3.
The exclusion generator makes T_r send each ordinary ray into the
ordinary cone. All 48 mixed rays are related by row and column
permutations, with which T_r commutes. It is therefore enough to check
the canonical mixed ray f34. Exact arithmetic gives only eight distinct
capacity values W(T_r f34):

  (3+2r^3+r^2)/2,     (3+2r^3-3r^2)/2,
  (3+r^2)/2,          (3+r^3)/2,
  (3-3r^2-2r^3)/2,   (3+r^2-2r^3)/2,
  (3-r^2)/2,          (3-r^3)/2.

For 0<=r<=1 all except the fifth are nonnegative automatically. Thus

  2r^3+3r^2 <= 3  ==>  T_r(C82) is contained in C34.       (9)

The reverse implication for this cone inclusion follows from the fifth
dual, which becomes negative beyond the unique root r* in (0,1).
Numerically r*=0.8064439323587976 and t*=-log(r*)/2 is approximately
0.1075604517686944. The exact condition is the polynomial inequality,
not a rounded time comparison.

Every ordinary-minor cost is nonnegative at every unitary U. By (9),
the heat-evolved profile at U is therefore nonnegative on every
nonnegative permutation function in the common-minor space. Finite
convex duality then places that normalized profile in the convex hull
of the 24 permutation profiles. This supplies a positive permutation
law depending only on U and t, simultaneously valid for every a,b,
for every t>=t*. Equivalently, applying (9) directly to each actual
spectral support deficit proves the heat-averaged MOC statement.

This threshold is sharp for the stated C82-to-C34 inclusion. It is not
asserted sharp for positivity on the smaller set of actual unitary
profiles, which can satisfy inequalities beyond the ordinary cone.

There is a concrete exact client of the improvement. For the rational
orthogonal U in section 3 and r=4/5, the simple heat weights (2) still
have minimum -547/5043000. The cone method nevertheless gives a
different positive law on 14 permutations. The verifier obtains its
support numerically, solves the resulting linear system over Q, and
checks all weights nonnegative, their sum exactly one, and all 70
heat-evolved compound marginals exactly. All 14 rational weights and
permutations are in cone_heat_certificate.json. Thus this is an actual
positive reconstruction beyond the range of the simple formula.

3. Exact failure of any spectrum-independent unsmoothed positive law

Take the dense rational orthogonal matrix

  U=(1/41) [ -32  20 -16   1
             -16 -31  -8 -20
              20   8 -31 -16
              -1  16  20 -32 ].

For one-based indices define

  g(U)=|U41|^2+|U14|^2+|det U[12,13]|^2-|det U[13,34]|^2.

For every permutation matrix, g is nonnegative. Indeed, when the last
indicator is one, pi({1,3})={3,4}. If pi(1)=4 the U14 term is one.
Otherwise pi(1)=3, pi(3)=4: either pi(4)=1, activating U41, or pi(2)=1,
activating the 12-to-13 minor. All 24 values are in {0,1,2}.

At the displayed orthogonal U the four terms are respectively

  1/1681, 1/1681, 0, 49/1681,

so g(U)=-47/1681. A nonnegative probability law on permutations cannot
have these single- and double-subset marginals. If one law depending
only on U reproduced f(U) for every a,b, comparison of the independent
spectral monomials would require exactly those marginals. Such a law
therefore does not exist. Any successful endpoint construction at time
zero must depend on the spectra, or retain more than these common
nonnegative permutation weights.

This is not a counterexample to MOC: the latter permits the probability
law to change with a,b. The four-term g is a nonnegative permutation
cost, not an asserted actual spectral support deficit.

4. Exact failure of radial heat contraction, even at the centroid

For the same U use

  a=(0, 1+i, -1+2i, (326021765-20679132i)/33163481),
  b=(1, 2i, -2+i, 2-i).

Both spectra have distinct entries and are nonconcyclic (their cross
ratios have nonzero imaginary parts, checked exactly). Direct rational
calculation gives

  f(U)=mu=(-2548340420-1503468600i)/33163481,
  X=(52570843221-8465947107i)/8576586394 != 0,
  Y=-X.

Thus the initial determinant is exactly the permutation centroid, but

  E[f(V_t U)]-mu = X(exp(-4t)-exp(-6t)) != 0 for t>0.

Its maximum distance from the centroid is 4|X|/27, attained at
t=log(3/2)/2. This disproves a universal contraction of distance to
the centroid under forward unitary heat, in every norm on C. It also
disproves the claim that the heat curve always lies in the initial
determinant-to-centroid segment. An uncorrected Brownian determinant is
not a martingale. Formally compensating the two modes by exp(4t) and
exp(6t) produces martingales, but their amplification provides no bounded
endpoint law and does not recover the missing convex-hull statement.

5. Raw-data checks and three independently computed instances

The verifier streams all 20,127 immutable source records to establish
coverage and selects all 5,006 small-complete n=4 rows for the new heat
analysis. It reconstructs their original mixers from the saved seeds,
including 4,167 noncontrol rows. No original record is edited. Optimizer
rows and large-dimensional rows are counted but are not claimed to have
received this four-dimensional calculation.

The largest determinant reconstruction residual, divided by the largest
matching-product modulus after common spectral rescaling, was
2.32e-14. Three rational/algebraic instances additionally reconstruct all
70 compound coefficients exactly: the dense Walsh example, the complex
Fourier example, and a rational Cayley mixer with the known nonordinary
spectra. These tests supplement the general proof above.

In the Walsh example a=(1,-1,-3i,3i), b=(-3,3,-i,i),

  mu=-86/3, X=0, Y=-43/3-24i,

so its heat curve has only the fast rate. The complex Fourier matrix
also has X=0, as follows from its flat squared-entry matrix and (3).
The generic rational Cayley example has both modes. Numerical fields
for every replayed record, stratified counts, and all exact example
values are saved alongside this note.

Among 4,154 noncontrol rows retained by the elementary nontrivial-hull
RMS guard, 64 have a positive initial derivative of squared distance
from the centroid. In 59 rows the sampled heat curve moves at least
one percent farther from the centroid than its initial distance. The
largest recorded ratio is 11.34 in a Haar anisotropic row. These are
descriptive finite-sample counts; the exact example above carries the
mathematical conclusion without a conditioning caveat. The output also
repeats the calculation after affine covariance whitening under the
same minor-axis guards used in the extensive campaign report.

That stricter guard retains 3,442 noncontrol rows. Of them, 24 have a
positive initial derivative of whitened squared radius, and 20 grow
more than one percent in whitened radius at some sampled time. The
largest whitened growth factor is 2.596. Thus the observed failure of
radial contraction survives removal of the flat-hull artifact, although
its prevalence and maximum size are substantially reduced.

Every original n=4 mixer has a negative coefficient in the particular
canonical signed law (1), within the 1e-10 numerical threshold. This
does not mean no positive law exists for those instances. Even U=I has
negative canonical coefficients while its obvious point-mass law is
positive. It is therefore a diagnostic of this choice of representation,
not an empirical argument against MOC.

6. Relation to finite free probability and the remaining mathematical gap

The known Haar expectation of a characteristic polynomial is a finite
free convolution; Marcus, Spielman and Srivastava establish this for
complex normal matrices in Theorem 1.2 of their paper. Their real-root
bounds require real-rooted input polynomials. Applying those root
bounds directly to unrestricted complex spectra would discard the
hypothesis that makes them work. The calculation here keeps the
finite-time minor module and gives an explicit signed/positive endpoint
law instead of importing a real-rooted bound outside its domain.

Primary references consulted:
  Marcus, Spielman, Srivastava, Finite free convolutions of polynomials:
    https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.00350
    https://doi.org/10.1007/s00440-021-01105-w
  Marcus, Polynomial convolutions and (finite) free probability:
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.07054

The surviving opening is a spectra-dependent cancellation of the negative
weights in (1), using algebraic relations of actual matching products.
The explicit negative marginal example shows why a universal Markov
coupling of all compound minors cannot do this. The centroid example
shows why forward heat does not supply a monotone path back to t=0.
Neither failure rules out a carefully spectra-dependent stopping or
transport construction, but no such construction is proved here. The
unrestricted four-dimensional conjecture remains unresolved.
