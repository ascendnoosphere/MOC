Four-contact actual facets have irreducible noncircular gap curves
31 October 2026

Result. For fixed first three complex beta values, the four-contact gap
determinants have complex bidegree at most(2,2), but they do not generally
factor into lines and circles. Exact canonical-cap examples contain
absolutely irreducible cubic and quartic gaps. A quartic is certified
to be active on the actual supporting chamber, so it cannot be discarded
as a redundant inequality to recover a circle-only arrangement.
This is a geometry result, not a universal MOC or spectral-SOS proof.

Exact checker:

Complete48 polynomial gaps, their irreducible rational factors, normals,
actual finite spectral witnesses, and the active-boundary certificate:
 build/moc_cofactor_facet_factorization/exact.json

1. General degree structure

Use the additive projective chart a=(0,1,infinity,x). Put
 u_pi=beta_pi0(1+beta_pi1), v_pi=u_pi beta_pi3,
 W_pi=(Reu,Imu,Rev,Imv).
With beta=(b0,b1,b2,w), each complex coordinate is affine holomorphic
in w. For four contacts S=(s0,s1,s2,s3), let D have rows W_sj-W_s0,
j=1,2,3. Its last-row cofactor normal n has degree at most3, and

 g_pi=det[D;W_pi-W_s0]=n dot(W_pi-W_s0)

has total degree at most4. After the invertible complex column change
to(u,bar u,v,bar v), its determinant has degree at most2 in w and at
most2 in barw separately. Thus the natural polynomial class is real
bidegree(2,2), rather than arbitrary quartics. This permits irreducible
quartics; it does not imply products of bidegree(1,1) circles.

The checker computes both each cofactor-normal evaluation and the
literal24-term four-by-four determinant, then checks them identically.

2. A particularly small noncircular factor

Fix b0=-2-i/2,b1=-1/2-i,b2=-1+i/2 and contacts
 S=(1,12,15,17), using lexicographic permutation indices0,...,23.
At w=3i/2 this is an actual supporting facet: exactly these four gaps
vanish, all20 others are strictly positive, and the canonical nonordinary
facet functional has pairing-25/48. Every contact lies among the zeros
of f34. The derived free value and support multiplier are

 x=253/221-264i/221, eta=95/8-45i/8.

Writing w=s+it, the exact gap at permutation7 is

 g7=-25/256 [4s^2-24st+8s-28t^2-24t+19].             (1)

Its quadratic part has determinant-256 and its homogenized three-by-three
conic matrix has determinant-3840. Hence it is a nonsingular irreducible
hyperbola over C, not a circle or a product of lines. The gcd of all
nonzero gaps is1, so there is no global common factor to cancel.

This example has16 cubic gaps and4 quadratic gaps, besides its four
identically zero contact gaps. The complete factor list contains six
lines, four circles, one noncircular conic, and ten rationally irreducible
cubics. Several cubic absolute irreducibility certificates are included.

3. An active absolutely irreducible quartic

Now fix b0=-2-i/2,b1=-1/2-i,b2=-3/4+i/2 and contacts
 S=(1,12,20,22). At w=7i/4 this again is strictly supporting with exactly
the four indicated contacts and negative canonical-cap pairing
-426965/393216. All contacts are zeros of f34.

The gap at permutation6 is g6=5P/4096, where

 P=512s^4+1476s^3+1024s^2t^2-1136s^2t+635s^2
   +1476st^2-4170st-144s+512t^4-1136t^3+2440t^2-3232t.

For a geometric complex variable z=s+it and an independent variable
v=s-it, the polynomial is quadratic in z. Its discriminant is

 i[(1296672-1146180i)v^4+(5665668-1380336i)v^3
   +(7401024+4527329i)v^2+(2352560+7090080i)v
   -232704+2606272i].

The exact polynomial gcd of this quartic with its derivative is1. The
three coefficients of the quadratic in z also have gcd1 in C[v].
Therefore the discriminant is not a square in C(v), so the quadratic
is irreducible there and the primitive bivariate P is absolutely
irreducible. Equivalently its function field is Y^2=D(v) with a
squarefree quartic D; the nonsingular projective normalization has
genus1. In particular it cannot be replaced birationally by one circle.

This curve actually occurs at a supporting-chamber boundary. On t=7/4,

 g6=5[2048s^4+5904s^3+7132s^2-11685s+2121]/16384.

There is exactly one root in
 I=(21518/100000,21520/100000).
The checker proves this by opposite endpoint signs and strictly negative
Bernstein coefficients of the derivative on I. On the entire closed
interval it also proves, by exact positive Bernstein coefficients:

 - all19 other noncontact gaps remain strictly positive;
 - the canonical-cap facet pairing stays strictly negative;
 - one cofactor-normal component stays nonzero.

At the root, the old four contacts and permutation6 therefore form a
supporting five-contact face, while every other gap is positive. Since
partial_s g6 is nonzero, the implicit function theorem supplies a regular
arc of this quartic as the boundary of the actual S-supporting chamber.
Thus the noncircle curve is not merely a discarded potential boundary.

This second example has15 quartic and5 cubic gaps besides its four
contact zeros. The common gcd is again1. Every complete factorization
is retained in the JSON record.

4. Actual finite spectra and consequences

Both base points are checked directly against the24 projective matching
products and their exact support height. The code also applies the common
map T(z)=1/(z-2) to the eight points (a_i,-b_j), obtaining finite spectra

 a'=(-1/2,-1,0,1/(x-2)), b'_j=1/(b_j+2).

Their products differ by the common factor
 kappa=1/[2(x-2) product_j(b_j+2)], and eta'=eta/kappa
preserves all gaps. All24 product identities are verified exactly.
Thus no unrealized formal/nonic profile is being used here.

A finite algebraic-curve arrangement in the free w-plane remains valid:
its defining polynomials have degree at most4 and bidegree at most(2,2).
The examples rule out a general replacement by an arrangement of only
lines and circles, and a direct one-quadratic S-lemma does not cover the
resulting chambers. They do not exclude more elaborate rational Gram,
quartic SOS, or semialgebraic methods for the actual spectral inequality.
