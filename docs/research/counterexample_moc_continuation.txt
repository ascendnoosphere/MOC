Continuation from the polygon counterexample toward the original MOC hull

This is a fixed-unitary, one-direction partial calculation, not a proof
of MOC4. The rational spectra alpha,beta and unitary U are those of the
audited counterexample in related_conjecture_polygon.txt. The determinant
lies outside the stronger polygon but strictly inside the original hull.

Interpolate the spectra by

  alpha_i(t)=t alpha_i,
  beta_i(t)=1+t(beta_i-1),       0<=t<=1,

while keeping U fixed. At t=0 the determinant and all 24 matching
products equal 1; at t=1 this is the exact polygon counterexample.
The outward normal of the closest original convex-hull edge at t=1 is
proportional to

  n=(-10359999855417,-19088640036014),

the edge with zero-based permutation indices 16 and 7. For matching
permutation 15, the projected gap relative to the unitary determinant
is exactly

  t^2(a t^2+b t+c),

with a>0, 2a+b<0, and a+b+c>0. Consequently the quadratic is
strictly positive throughout [0,1], and this particular matching
permutation beats the unitary determinant in this direction for every
0<t<=1. The first-order term is zero and the second-order term c is
strictly positive. These facts, the exact hull-edge normal, and unitary
Cayley identity are recomputed from the original certificate by
code/research/verify_counterexample_moc_path.py. Other support
directions along this path are not covered by that certificate.

The leading behavior has a general explanation. For arbitrary complex
diagonal A=diag(a_i), B=diag(b_j), fixed unitary U, and spectral path
tA and Id+tB, the determinant matrices are Id+tC with
C=A+UBU*. Their first elementary symmetric coefficients all agree.
For matching permutation pi, the second-order coefficient difference
(matching product minus unitary determinant) is

  sum_ij |U_ij|^2 a_i b_j - sum_i a_i b_pi(i).

For any real support functional on the complex plane, the first term
is a convex combination of the assignment sums by the Birkhoff
theorem, because (|U_ij|^2) is doubly stochastic. Therefore at least
one matching permutation has a nonnegative projected second-order gap.
If all entries of U are nonzero and those projected assignment sums
are not all equal, the largest gap is strictly positive. This explains
why a deformation from scalar spectra cannot first cross the original
hull at second order in a nondegenerate direction; it does not control
the higher-order terms or all directions uniformly.

An additional heuristic fixed-U search optimized spectra and direction,
normalizing the projected gap by the diameter of the 24 matching
products. It made 41,780 differential-evolution evaluations and found
a smallest normalized gap about 0.00399497, with no negative value.
This is not a global bound or an exclusion proof. The run is in
build/moc_continuation/search_fixed_u.py and its numerical result in
build/moc_continuation/fixed_u_search.npz.
