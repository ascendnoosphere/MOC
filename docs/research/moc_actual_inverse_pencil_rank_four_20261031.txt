Every actual complex spectral profile has pencil rank at most4
31 October 2026

THEOREM. Let z_pi=prod_i(a_i+b_pi(i)), with arbitrary finite complex
a_i,b_j, and let M be the universal real-coefficient inverse quadratic
pencil on V14. Then

                        rank_C M(z)<=4.                    (1)

Consequently every actual nonnegative support cost

               g=h e-Re(eta z), h real, eta complex,

satisfies

                    n_+(M(g))<=4, n_-(M(g))<=4.             (2)

This is a global necessary condition, including all degeneracies and
all supporting phases. It is not merely an observed generic inertia.
At rank8 it gives inertia(4+,4-,1zero). No converse is claimed.

1. Direct factorization

Expanding the product defining z by subsets of rows expresses z as a
linear combination of the set-image indicators r_IJ, so z belongs to
the complexified matching space V14 and M(z) is well defined.

Set e0=(1,1,1,1)^T/2 and use the real tetrahedral orthonormal L from the
universal-pencil construction. E=[e0,L] is orthogonal. Write

 A=diag(a_i), B=diag(b_j),
 A0=L^T A L, B0=L^T B L,
 u=L^T A e0, v=L^T B e0.

For invertible K put S=L K L^T and H=K^(-T). Leibniz expansion gives

 q_z(K)=sum_pi sgn(pi) z_pi prod_i S_i,pi(i)
       =det(A S+S B).

Conjugating by E puts the latter matrix into the block form

                [ 0       u^T K       ]
                [ K v     A0 K+K B0   ].

Its determinant is unchanged, because det(E)^2=1. Multiplying on both
sides by diag(1,K^(-1)) therefore gives

 q_z(K)/(det K)^2
   =det[ 0    u^T                     ]
       [ v    K^(-1)A0+B0 K^(-1)      ]
   =-u^T adj(T(H)) v,

                 T(H)=H^T A0+B0 H^T.                       (3)

The universal-pencil identity identifies the left side with
hvec^T M(z)hvec, hvec=vec(H). Both sides are quadratic polynomials in
the nine independent H entries. Equality on invertible H proves it
identically, so no division or singular-H issue remains.

If u=0 or v=0 the quadratic is zero and(1) is immediate. Otherwise choose
constant invertible complex P,Q such that

                          P v=e3, u^T Q=e3^T.

The adjugate transformation rule gives

 e3^T adj(P T Q)e3=det(P)det(Q) u^T adj(T)v.

The left side is the top-left2-by2 determinant of P T(H) Q. Thus the
quadratic in(3) is a nonzero scalar multiple of

                         l1(H)l4(H)-l2(H)l3(H),

where each l_i is a complex linear form in H. Its symmetric coefficient
matrix has image in the span of those four coefficient vectors. This
proves rank_C M(z)<=4 for all a,b.

2. From complex rank to real inertia

Any complex symmetric matrix of rank r has a factorization C C^T with
C having r columns. Absorb the phase eta into C using a complex square
root. Write C=X+iY with X,Y real. Then

                       Re(C C^T)=X X^T-Y Y^T.

Its positive inertia index is at most rank X<=r, and its negative index
is at most rank Y<=r. Since M is real linear and M(e)=0,

                     M(g)=-Re(eta M(z)),

which proves(2). Changing the sign interchanges the two indices, so it
does not affect their common bound. The argument does not assume that
M(z) has rank4 or that its real and imaginary column spans are disjoint.

The bound is closed under limits. It therefore also excludes an alleged
actual cost or limit of actual costs whenever a rational compression
already has five strictly positive or five strictly negative directions.
