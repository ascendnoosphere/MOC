Adversarial assessment of the current MOC recovery chain
2026-10-30

The central weakness is the missing implication from an actual spectral gap
to a sufficient unitary-support certificate. The cubic, determinant, neutral
Hessian, compound and pencil-sign conditions are necessary relaxations; the
ordinary-minor and weighted-minor inequalities are sufficient certificates.
Passing the former does not prove the latter. In particular, the exact
uniform orbit7 candidate passes all 35 critical-direction pencil signs but
has no real gradient lift. That example shows the tests do not characterize
realizability; without an exact negative-unitary witness it does not refute
the possibility that the larger 35-test relaxation is support-positive.
Conversely, an actual spectral gap is known to lie outside the ordinary cone, so ordinary membership cannot be imposed as a
universal intermediate claim. A proposed stronger linear triangle inequality
also fails for an explicitly realized spectral gap. These are failures of
particular bridges, not MOC counterexamples.

The precise unresolved obligation in the present reduction is: for every
actual gap obtained from the fixed-unitary, fixed-support, strict-violation
transfer, after reduction to a canonical mixed cap, prove that its cost is
nonnegative for the original unitary. A universal weighted-triangle capacity
bound on actual gaps would suffice, but is a stronger, currently unproved
statement; its failure would not by itself disprove MOC. The current
finite-dimensional cone decomposition reduces mixed supports but does not
supply this missing positivity.

Eight-contact closures remove the specific eight-contact configurations and
any higher-contact subcases they contain. They do not close the six- or
seven-contact interior left by the existing transfer. Positivity on boundary
strata does not extend automatically to interior spectral points. Raising the
contact threshold requires an additional theorem preserving actual spectral
realizability and strict violation; the existing fixed-complex-fiber argument
can stop at six, as witnessed by an exact local example. Dimension counting
alone cannot justify further contact escalation.

Keep the quantifiers distinct. The secant and PSD moment tests enlarge or
reformulate necessary lift conditions; they do not automatically reconstruct
finite spectral data or prove support positivity. Full spectral reconstruction
is not required if support positivity can be proved on a suitable relaxation. A generic Zariski-open
perturbation of a strict counterexample is legitimate only with the stated
fixed-unitary/support transfer and preservation of positive violation.
Rank-12 arguments, nonzero denominators and positive-height branches cannot
be applied silently to higher-corank or zero-height points; each needs its
explicit continuity, alternative chart or separate Drury argument. Numerical
searches and individual certificates guide the next lemma but cannot establish
an implication over all actual gaps.
