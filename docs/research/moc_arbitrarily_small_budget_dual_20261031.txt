Constructive full matching-SOS duals with arbitrarily small ray budgets
31 October 2026

For each j in {0,7,10,13,18,23,28} and every real epsilon>0, there is a
24-row real dual y such that

  y.f34=-1,  y.rj=epsilon,
  B^T diag(y) B is positive definite,

where B is any basis of the full14-dimensional matching-function space.
For rational epsilon the construction gives rational y. This proves that
positive lower bounds such as beta_singleton>=1 or beta_pair>=2/3 cannot
hold for every normalized full-PD dual. Those useful sufficient thresholds
must be checked for the particular dual under study.

This is a statement about the matching-SOS relaxation and its duals. It is
neither a MOC counterexample nor a claim about actual spectral realizability.

1. Four explicit row weights

Write f=f34 and r=rj. For every listed j the joint permutation-value set
contains the four pairs

  (f,r)=(1,0), (1,1), (2,1), (0,1).

Choose one row of each type, with respective weights

                   -1, -2, 1, 1+epsilon.

All other weights initially vanish. Direct arithmetic gives

  y0.f=-1, y0.r=epsilon,
  y0.f^2=1, y0.(fr)=0, y0.r^2=epsilon.

Thus the moment restricted to the two functions (f,r) is exactly

                         diag(1,epsilon)>0.

The verifier chooses the following zero-based lexicographic permutation
rows (in the order of the four pairs displayed above):

  j=0:  [10,0,5,1]
  j=7:  [0,4,5,17]
  j=10: [2,0,5,6]
  j=13: [0,3,5,22]
  j=18: [0,4,5,18]
  j=23: [2,0,5,14]
  j=28: [0,3,5,9]

These row labels are replayed directly from the retained integer ray table;
the formulas, rather than the labels, are the essential construction.

2. Why the full14-dimensional completion exists

Let Z be the rows where f=r=0. Exact ranks give rank B[Z,:]=12, and its
nullspace is exactly the coefficient span of f and r. Choose12 independent
contact rows C. Add two noncontact rows of types (1,0) and (0,1); the
resulting14-row evaluation matrix is invertible.

Interpolation supplies12 additional matching functions D1,...,D12 that
vanish at those two noncontact rows and evaluate as the standard basis on
C. Consequently F=(f,r,D1,...,D12) is a basis of the matching space and

                       F[C,:]=[0_(12x2) I12].

In this basis write the initial moment as

  H0(epsilon) = [ diag(1,epsilon)  W(epsilon) ]
                [ W(epsilon)^T    D(epsilon) ],

with W=W0+epsilon W1 and D=D0+epsilon D1. Adding mass t at each of the12
chosen contact rows adds exactly diag(0,0,t I12), preserving y.f and y.r.

Here is an explicit mass valid for EVERY epsilon>0. Let

  d_k=sum_ab |(Dk)_ab|,
  a_k=sum_b (Wk)_(1b)^2,
  b_k=sum_b (Wk)_(2b)^2,             k=0,1,

and put

  t=1+d0+epsilon d1+2a0+2epsilon^2 a1+2b0/epsilon+2epsilon b1.

All constants are nonnegative rationals. For any vector x,

  x^T D x >= -(d0+epsilon d1)||x||^2,

because the spectral norm of a matrix is bounded by the sum of the absolute
values of its entries. Also (u+v)^2<=2u^2+2v^2 gives

  ||W_(1,:)||^2 <=2a0+2epsilon^2 a1,
  ||W_(2,:)||^2/epsilon <=2b0/epsilon+2epsilon b1.

Therefore the Schur complement satisfies

  D+tI-W^T diag(1,1/epsilon) W >= I.

Its upper-left block diag(1,epsilon) is positive definite, so the completed
full14 moment is positive definite. Positive definiteness is preserved by
the invertible change from F to any other matching basis. This proves the
all-epsilon claim; it is not inferred from finite numerical tests.

For the concrete interpolation choices in the verifier the mass simplifies
considerably: t=173+20/epsilon for j=0, t=217+20/epsilon for j=10,23,
and t=109+20/epsilon for j=7,13,18,28. Thus the construction has an explicit
linear growth in reciprocal epsilon, with no optimization step.

3. Consequence for the full SOS cone

Let C0 consist of matching functions represented by B_p^T X B_p, X>=0.
For every such function g, the constructed y satisfies

  y.g=tr(X B^T diag(y) B)>=0.

For g=f+lambda r and any lambda>0, choosing 0<epsilon<1/lambda gives

                    y.g=-1+lambda epsilon<0.

Thus f+lambda r is outside the FULL matching-SOS cone for every lambda>0
and every listed j. The argument uses the full14-feature cone, not a smaller
feature selection or factor-width relaxation. It does not assert that these
functions arise as actual spectral support deficits.
