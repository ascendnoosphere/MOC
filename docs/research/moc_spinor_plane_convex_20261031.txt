SPINOR PLANE CONVEX COMBINATION AND AFFINE WEYL EXPANSION
31 October 2026. This is not a proof of unrestricted MOC.

Checker: code/research/moc_spinor_plane_convex.py
The quartic is q(C)=[2 tr((C C^T)^2)-(tr C C^T)^2]/16, transpose not conjugate.
For traceless spinor coordinates the identity
  q(diag x + D)=q(diag x)+q(D)-||x||^2 ||D||_F^2/8 + landscape(D)
is the free 9-entry expansion already used in
  code/research/moc_global_bell_pair_quadratic.py.
On D=R diag(y) S^T with R,S real orthogonal it becomes
  landscape = sum_ij alpha_ij (R_ij^2+S_ij^2) + beta_ij R_ij S_ij,
  alpha_ij=x_i^2 y_j^2/4,
  beta_ij=[x_i y_j (x_i^2+y_j^2-(||x||^2+||y||^2)/2) - xhat_i yhat_j]/2.
The 24 Weyl values q(diag(x+eps y_sigma)), product of signs +1, are the
24 permutation products of the spinor 4-spectra.

1. COORDINATE-PLANE CONVEX COMBINATION

Let R and S be rotations by angles a and b in the coordinate 01-plane.
Then, for every complex x,y,
  q(diag x + R diag(y) S^T)
    = w++ q(id, signs +++)
    + w-- q(id, signs --+)
    + v++ q(swap 01, signs +++)
    + v-- q(swap 01, signs --+),
with
  w++=(cos a+cos b)^2/4,   w--=(cos a-cos b)^2/4,
  v++=(sin a+sin b)^2/4,   v--=(sin a-sin b)^2/4.
The four weights are nonnegative and sum to 1. The same identities with the
rotated coordinate plane give the other two axis-parallel cases. Conjugation
by a signed permutation matrix moves the identity among the 24 Weyl values,
so the same holds after a common monomial change of frame.

This is the complex-spectral MOC inequality for that mixer family, because a
convex combination lies in the convex hull. It is not the general mixer.

2. AFFINE EXPANSION FOR EVERY REAL ORTHOGONAL PAIR

The 24 Weyl quadratics span a 14-dimensional space of degree-4 polynomials.
Every linear relation among them is orthogonal to the landscape as soon as
R and S have orthonormal rows and columns. The checker reduces each
left-kernel obstruction to those row and column square-sum identities.
The right kernel changes weights without changing the value, and every
kernel vector sums to 0. At R=S=I the landscape equals the identity Weyl
quadratic, so every weight solution sums to 1.

Thus for every real orthogonal R,S and every complex x,y there exist real
weights, depending only on R,S, summing to 1, such that q equals the
corresponding affine combination of the 24 Weyl values. Some of those
weights are negative for a generic pair: nonnegativity can fail for every
choice in the coset. An explicit rational witness is the Cayley pair
K=(1,1,1) and K=(2,-1,3), whose weight polytope is empty in the nonnegative
orthant. Negative universal weights are therefore essential there. They do
not by themselves produce a point outside the convex hull, and they do not
prove the hull membership either.

3. RELATIVE DIAGONAL SIGNS

If S=R E with E diagonal, det E=1, then
  landscape = sum_ij (2 alpha_ij + E_jj beta_ij) R_ij^2.
R∘R is doubly stochastic, so this is at most its maximum on permutation
matrices. Each of the six permutations occurs as R∘R for some R in SO(3)
whose relative signs reproduce E along that permutation, and that value is
a Weyl value. This is the classical Birkhoff bound for this subfamily.

4. WHAT REMAINS

The unrestricted inequality is the case of arbitrary R,S in SO(3), or by
the realification in algebra_unitary_realification.md, arbitrary U in U(4).
Section 2 writes every such value as an affine combination. The missing step
is a nonnegative choice of weights, or another certificate that the negative
part cannot exit the hull. The plane family in Section 1 is the case where
the nonnegative choice is explicit.
