SHORT CYCLE-CAPACITY THEOREM FOR A MARKOV CONORMAL
31 October 2026

This is a uniform one-contact theorem. It does not prove unrestricted MOC
or exclude stationary minima with several active contacts.

Let S be a real4-by-4 matrix with S_ij>=0 for i!=j and
S_ii=-sum_(j!=i)S_ij. Balance of column sums is NOT required. Define

  n_pi=-sign(pi) product_i S_i,pi(i).

Use zero-based lexicographic permutation indices. Let g be a nonnegative
matching function, with g0=0. The relevant matching identities follow from
vanishing3-by-3 minors of a rank-two pairing matrix, and also hold for all
actual support costs h-Re(eta product_i(a_i+b_pi(i))).

The three double-transposition amplitudes are D7,D16,D23. Every D is a
nonnegative product of four off-diagonal rates and has coefficient -D in n.
All other nonidentity amplitudes in n are positive: six transpositions,
eight3-cycles (with one fixed diagonal outdegree), and six4-cycles.

The transposition partners are

  D7: T6,T1; D16: T14,T5; D23: T21,T2.

Each partner satisfies T>=D. For a transposition(ab) fixing c,d,

  T-D = S_ab S_ba (d_c d_d-S_cd S_dc)>=0,
  d_c=sum_(k!=c)S_ck.

Indeed d_c=S_cd+R_c and d_d=S_dc+R_d with R_c,R_d>=0, so the
parenthesis is S_cd R_d+S_dc R_c+R_cR_d.

Group the six4-cycle amplitudes into inverse pairs

  Q0=F9+F18, Q1=F10+F13, Q2=F17+F22.

The three disjoint ordinary-ray scores are precisely

  b0=Q0-D7-D23 = n(r26),
  b1=Q1-D7-D16 = n(r31),
  b2=Q2-D16-D23 = n(r21).

Assume b0,b1,b2>=0. These are the only extra scalar inequalities needed.

CAPACITY FROM TWELVE SIX-TERM MATCHING IDENTITIES

The following are exact sums of three matching values on each side:

   [6,9,10]=[7,8,11]       [1,9,13]=[7,3,15]
   [1,18,10]=[7,4,20]      [6,18,13]=[7,12,19]
   [5,10,17]=[16,4,11]     [14,10,22]=[16,8,20]
   [14,13,17]=[16,12,15]   [5,13,22]=[16,3,19]
   [21,9,17]=[23,11,15]    [2,9,22]=[23,3,8]
   [2,18,17]=[23,4,12]    [21,18,22]=[23,19,20].

For example, all six permutations in the first identity send row0 to
column1. After removing that common factor, it is the determinant expansion
of the remaining rank-two3-by-3 pairing matrix.

Set m_i=min(g at the two endpoints of Q_i). Nonnegativity of g and these
twelve identities imply

  g7  <=g6+g1 +m0+m1,
  g16 <=g14+g5+m1+m2,
  g23 <=g21+g2+m0+m2.

In fact each choice of one endpoint from each relevant Q pair permits
dropping one of the two partner transpositions. The displayed weaker
inequalities suffice. Let s_D be their nonnegative surpluses.

EXPLICIT NONNEGATIVE DECOMPOSITION

Writing parent(T) for its double and including(i,D) for the incidence
shown above, the exact identity is

 n.g = sum_T g_T (T-D_parent(T))
       +sum_i m_i b_i
       +sum_3cycle g_C C
       +sum_i sum_(F in Q_i) (g_F-m_i)F
       +sum_D s_D D.

Every term on the right is nonnegative. Therefore n.g>=0 for EVERY
nonnegative matching g with g0=0. The proof does not require the completeness
of the82-ray census or any spectral reconstruction.

If all off-diagonal S entries are strictly positive and all three b_i>0,
then n.g>0 for every nonzero such g. Equality would first force all six
transposition values g_T=0, because T-D>0. It forces all eight3-cycle
values to vanish, all m_i=0, and then all six4-cycle values to vanish.
The capacity inequalities force all double-transposition values to vanish.
Together with g0=0 this gives g=0.
