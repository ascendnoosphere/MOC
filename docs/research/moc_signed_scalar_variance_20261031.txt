Signed scalar variance: the real-orthogonal theorem and its complex limit
31 October 2026

Main results

For every real orthogonal O and every complex matrix M of rank at most two,

  det(O) det(O Hadamard |M|^2) >= |det(O Hadamard M)|^2.             (1)

Here |M|^2 is entrywise modulus squared. The left side is real. For arbitrary
complex unitary U, the corresponding universally valid conclusion is

  |det(U Hadamard |M|^2)| >= |det(U Hadamard M)|^2.                  (2)

Replacing the modulus in (2) by
Re[det(U Hadamard |M|^2)/det U] is false, even when the additive multiplier
comes from two Hermitian matrices. An explicit rational counterexample is
given below. Neither (1) nor (2) proves MOC or positivity of a full signed
moment matrix.

1. Additive rank-two multiplier and signed moment identities

First let M_ij=a_i+b_j. Put
  A=diag(a), B=U diag(b) U*, C=A+B,
  N=AA*+AB*+A*B+BB*.
Direct multiplication, using normality of A and B, gives

  (U Hadamard M)U* = C,
  (U Hadamard |M|^2)U* = N.                                      (3)

Consequently
  det(U Hadamard M)/det U = det C,
  det(U Hadamard |M|^2)/det U = det N.

For c_pi=Re[sgn(pi) product_i U_i,pi(i)/det U] and
z_pi=product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)), determinant expansion gives

  sum_pi c_pi=1,
  sum_pi c_pi z_pi=det C,
  sum_pi c_pi |z_pi|^2=Re det N.                                  (4)

The middle identity uses the known real-amplitude minor identity. For the
last identity no such bridge is needed: |z_pi|^2 is exactly the product of
the real factors |a_i+b_pi(i)|^2 in determinant expansion.
For real O, the amplitudes themselves are real and det N is real, so the
signed scalar variance is exactly det N-|det C|^2.

2. Elementary all-dimensional proof for real O

For every complex unitary U,

  H=(N+N*)/2 = (CC*+C*C)/2 >=0.                                  (5)

If U=O is real, N is entrywise real: its i,k entry is
sum_j O_ij O_kj |a_i+b_j|^2. Thus N=H+S, where H is real symmetric
positive semidefinite and S is real skew symmetric.

Assume first H>0. The real skew matrix H^(-1/2) S H^(-1/2) has nonzero
eigenvalues in pairs +it_j,-it_j. Therefore

  det N = det H product_j(1+t_j^2) >= det H.                      (6)

To compare det H with |det C|^2 when C is invertible, apply the following
elementary inequality to P=CC* and Q=C*C:

  det((P+Q)/2) >= sqrt(det P det Q),  P,Q>0.                       (7)

To prove (7), let lambda_j>0 be the eigenvalues of P^(-1/2) Q P^(-1/2).
Then (7) is the product of the scalar inequalities
(1+lambda_j)/2 >= sqrt(lambda_j). Since det P=det Q=|det C|^2,
(7) gives det H>=|det C|^2.

If C is singular this latter inequality is immediate from H>=0. If H is
singular, replace N by N+epsilon I and H by H+epsilon I in (6), and take
epsilon down to zero. This regularizes only the matrix inequality; no
unproved perturbation of normal pairs is used.

Combining (6)-(7) proves

  sum_pi c_pi |z_pi|^2 >= |sum_pi c_pi z_pi|^2                      (8)

for arbitrary dimension, arbitrary complex spectra, and real orthogonal O.
The argument is a direct application of the classical Ostrowski-Taussky
determinant mechanism, with a second scalar-AM-GM determinant comparison.

For arbitrary complex U, write N=H+S with S skew Hermitian. Its whitened
eigenvalues are i lambda_j with lambda_j real, without a paired spectrum.
Consequently

  |det N|=det H product_j sqrt(1+lambda_j^2) >= det H >= |det C|^2,

with the same limiting argument. This proves (2) for additive M, but gives
no lower bound on Re det N.

3. Extension from additive multipliers to arbitrary rank at most two

Write M_ij=u_i v_j+s_i t_j. On the dense locus where all u_i,t_j are
nonzero, set a_i=s_i/u_i and b_j=v_j/t_j. Then

  M_ij=u_i t_j(a_i+b_j).

If alpha=(product_i u_i)(product_j t_j), the Hadamard determinant in M
is multiplied by alpha under this row/column scaling, while the determinant
in |M|^2 is multiplied by |alpha|^2. Thus (1)-(2) follow from the additive
case. Perturbing the factors u,s,v,t and taking limits handles all zero
entries and rank-one cases without leaving the rank-at-most-two locus.

4. Exact failure of the complex-unitary real-part extension

The root supplied the following construction, independently checked here.
Let
  C=diag(1,2,3,4), D=C^(-1),
  A_ii=0, A_ij=i t/(D_ii-D_jj) for i!=j,
  B=C-A,
where t is real. Both A and B are Hermitian, so they are normal. Since

  N=A^2+2AB+B^2=C^2+[A,C],

we obtain the exact congruence

  D N D = I+[D,A] = I+i t(J4-I4).

The latter matrix has eigenvalues 1+3it and 1-it (multiplicity three).
Therefore

  det N/|det C|^2 = (1+3it)(1-it)^3
                   =1+6t^2-3t^4-8it^3.                          (9)

At t=2, explicitly

  A = [[0,4i,3i,8i/3],[-4i,0,12i,8i],
       [-3i,-12i,0,24i],[-8i/3,-8i,-24i,0]],
  B=diag(1,2,3,4)-A,

and
  |det C|^2=576,
  det N=-13248-36864i,
  Re det N-|det C|^2=-13824.

Simultaneously change basis to diagonalize A and then write B by its spectral
unitary. These operations preserve C's determinant and N's determinant.
Thus (9) is an actual complex-relative-unitary counterexample to the naive
real-part signed-variance claim (4). The spectra can even be chosen real,
because the displayed A and B are Hermitian. It does not violate MOC:
Hermitian pairs already satisfy that conjecture.

The distinction matters for a proposed real-to-complex bridge. A positive
representation preserving the determinant for rank-two additive multipliers
does not automatically preserve its nonlinear squared product moment;
the latter uses the generally higher-rank multiplier |M|^2.

Realification demonstrates the same distinction directly. Embed a complex
unitary U into the real orthogonal matrix R(U) of order2n, and repeat each
spectral entry a_i,b_j twice. In complex coordinates this decomposes as
U direct-sum conjugate(U). The ordinary determinant minor formula gives
det C_conjugate(U)=det C_U, since squared minor moduli are unchanged. The
real multiplier |M|^2 instead gives
det N_conjugate(U)=conjugate(det N_U). Therefore the real-orthogonal theorem
in order2n yields only

  |det N_U|^2 >= |det C_U|^4,

which is the modulus theorem(2). It cannot recover a bound on Re det N_U.

5. Primary-source audit

The elementary accretive determinant mechanism is classical. A primary paper
giving precisely the real-skew eigenvalue-pairing proof is F. M. Dannan and
J. K. Merikoski, A Further Improvement of the Ostrowski-Taussky Inequality
for Real Matrices, Applied Mathematics E-Notes22(2022),331-334:
https://www.math.nthu.edu.tw/~amen/2022/AMEN-210209.pdf
See its Lemma1 and Corollary2. The argument above includes a complete proof
of the weaker fact needed here and assumes no novelty for that matrix bound.

The following primary publisher abstracts were checked:
  Drury, A bound for the determinant of certain Hadamard products and for
  the determinant of the sum of two normal matrices, LAA199(1994),329-338,
  DOI10.1016/0024-3795(94)90356-5,
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379594903565

  Drury, The determinantal conjecture and Hadamard type inequalities,
  LAA246(1996),279-297, DOI10.1016/0024-3795(94)00361-0,
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0024379594003610

The first abstract describes an upper bound for a unitary Hadamard-product
determinant, while the second describes a four-dimensional geometric
reduction using Hadamard inequalities. Both full texts returned403 in this
audit. Their abstracts do not establish whether (1) was explicitly stated
there. No historical priority or absence claim is made.
