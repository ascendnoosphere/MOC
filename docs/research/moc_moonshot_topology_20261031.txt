Topology, attainable determinant regions, and positive fixed-point weights
31 October 2026

This lane produced two positive results. First, an integer winding test on
permutation products certifies whole open regions of actual determinant values
in every dimension. Second, an explicit positive law proves MOC for arbitrary
complex spectra on a complete one-parameter family of dense 4 by 4 mixers,
including the entire Walsh-to-permutation cubic-ascent arc. Neither result is
an unrestricted MOC proof. The same investigation rules out a direct Hamiltonian
torus moment-map interpretation of the unrestricted determinant support.

The primary source for the graph idea is Kovacec's April 2026 preprint,
https://www.mat.uc.pt/preprints/ps/p2611.pdf. Its Givens-segment calculation
motivates the construction below. Its proposed outer polygon is not used:
the repository already contains an independently audited counterexample to
that stronger outer-bound conjecture in related_conjecture_polygon.txt.
No historical novelty claim is attached to the results here.

1. A positive topological theorem in every dimension

Fix arbitrary a,b in C^n, with repeated values allowed, and write

  z_pi = product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)),
  Delta = { det(diag(a)+U diag(b) U*) : U unitary }.

Let Gamma be the abstract graph with vertex set S_n and an edge whenever two
permutations differ by swapping two positions. Map each vertex pi to z_pi and
each edge to the straight segment between its endpoint values. Different edges
may cross, overlap, or collapse; they remain different edges of Gamma.

THEOREM. Let C be a closed walk in Gamma. If q is not on its image and the
winding number wind(z(C),q) is nonzero, then q belongs to Delta. Consequently,
if rho is the distance from q to that polygonal walk, the open disk B(q,rho)
is contained in Delta.

Proof. A graph edge swapping positions i,j lifts to a Givens path on the
normal orbit O_b={U diag(b) U*}. On that path the determinant is exactly

  cos(theta)^2 z_pi + sin(theta)^2 z_pi',  0<=theta<=pi/2.

The endpoints in O_b are the relevant diagonal matrices. Thus a closed
abstract graph walk lifts to an actual closed loop gamma in O_b, with exactly
the prescribed polygonal determinant image.

The normal orbit is simply connected. To see this with multiplicities m_j,
use O_b=U(n)/(U(m_1) x ... x U(m_r)). The stabilizer is connected, and its map
on fundamental groups is (k_1,...,k_r) -> sum k_j from Z^r onto Z. The homotopy
sequence of this homogeneous bundle therefore gives pi_1(O_b)=0. Equivalently,
the orbit is a partial complex flag manifold.

Hence gamma extends to a continuous map from the disk into O_b. If q were
absent from Delta, composing that disk with B -> det(diag(a)+B)-q would give
a disk in C\{0} whose boundary has nonzero winding around zero. This is
impossible: a boundary that extends to such a disk has winding zero. Thus q
is attained. Winding is constant on B(q,rho), proving the disk statement.

This proof uses only normality of B; a must be diagonal for the Givens edge
formula. It handles scalar B and all collapsed-edge cases without genericity.
It proves existence of a realizing U, not an explicit inverse algorithm.

The cohomological object has a concrete role. For q off every graph edge,
the map Gamma -> C\{q} induces a class theta_q in H^1(Gamma;Z), represented by
integer ray crossings. If q is omitted from Delta, this class factors through
H^1(O_b;Z)=0 and must vanish. Therefore theta_q != 0 is a finite, computable
certificate of attainability. This is not a change of vocabulary for MOC:
it supplies an inner bound, whereas MOC asks for an outer bound.

It is essential to use closed walks in the ABSTRACT Cayley graph. A planar
face boundary may switch from one Givens edge to an unrelated edge at a
crossing. Those intersection points need not have matching lifts in O_b.
Simply filling every bounded face of the planarized drawing is not justified
by this proof. Nor does a simply connected source force its continuous image
to be simply connected.

2. An exact finite algorithm and three computed instances

Choose a spanning tree rooted at the identity permutation. For a point q not
on an edge, give each oriented edge the integer signed crossing of the
positive horizontal ray from q. Accumulate those integers from the root along
the tree. For an edge i->j, its crossing plus potential(i)-potential(j) is the
winding number of its fundamental cycle. Some closed walk has nonzero winding
if and only if one of these residuals is nonzero. This takes O(E+V) operations
after constructing products and graph, with no optimization over unitaries.

The cycle ranks E-V+1 are 4,49,481,4681 for n=3,4,5,6. The corresponding graph
sizes are (V,E)=(6,9),(24,72),(120,600),(720,5400).

The exact checker uses rational cross products and rational squared distances
to segments. It verifies the following examples, with zero-based lexicographic
permutation indices.

  n=2: a=(1+2i,3-i), b=(-2+i,4+2i). The entire segment joining -10+20i
  and 5+4i is attained by a Givens rotation.

  n=3: a=(0,1,2i), b=(i,2,3-i). The graph cycle [0,1,3,2,0] has values
  [-3+9i,-6+10i,6+24i,4+8i,-3+9i]. Its winding about q=7/3+15i is -1.
  Its minimum squared distance to q is 3721/585. Therefore the ENTIRE open
  disk of radius sqrt(3721/585), approximately 2.52204, about q is attained.

  n=4 Walsh spectra: a=(1,-1,-3i,3i), b=(-3,3,-i,i). The graph cycle
  [0,2,3,9,8,6,0] has values [-64,48i,24,-48i,-96,-256,-64]. Its winding
  about q=-43-24i is -1, and its minimum squared distance is 5. Thus the
  entire open disk of radius sqrt(5) about the dense Walsh determinant is
  attained. This is an open-region result, beyond verifying one hull witness.

A further rational n=4 example, all coordinates and exact distances included
in exact_inner_regions.json, certifies a radius 31/65 disk about -2-i.

3. What the raw corpus says about this inner region

The script read all 20,127 immutable source records and selected all 8,324
small-complete records in dimensions 3 through 6 for the graph computation.
Of those, 6,918 are labelled noncontrols. The unchanged source contains the
other dimensions and producer types; their graph criterion was not evaluated
in this lane. The enriched analysis elsewhere covers those producers.

For the geometric statistics, impose the existing covariance guards: minor
RMS axis >1e-7 and minor/major axis >1e-4 after the producer's common scaling.
Also require distance >1e-9 from every Cayley-graph segment, because a winding
test next to an edge is numerically ill-conditioned. This extra guard removes
many near-permutation boundary cases. These are numerical safeguards, not
interval certificates.

  dimension     guarded noncontrols      nonzero cycle
      3                 528                    526
      4               3,178                  3,100
      5                 965                    919
      6                 533                    496
  total               5,204                  5,041

At n=4, the split is 2,512/2,585 Haar records and 588/593 near-permutation
records. All 78 guarded failures of the inner-region criterion are clustered
spectra. All guarded Gaussian, disk, anisotropic, and near-cancellation n=4
records pass. At n=5 and n=6, every guarded failure is also clustered. At n=3,
the two failures are Gaussian records small_n03_00012 and small_n03_00420.

Failure here means only that this particular inner-bound theorem is silent.
Every recorded determinant is, by construction, an attained value, so these
failures demonstrate that the inner bound does not describe the entire range.
They are not MOC violations. The calculation is useful because it separates
the broad interior supported by explicit topological certificates from the
parts of the range requiring additional geometry. It independently identifies
the clustered family as a source of behavior hidden by generic Gaussian draws.

4. A positive common-minor law on a full four-dimensional mixer family

Set

        [ 0  1  1  1 ]
  K  =  [-1  0  1 -1 ],          V(t)=(tI+K)/sqrt(t^2+3).
        [-1 -1  0  1 ]
        [-1  1 -1  0 ]

K^T=-K and K^2=-3I, so V(t) is orthogonal. Use one-based word notation for
permutations. Divide the following weights by D=(t^2+3)^2:

  1234                              t^4+4t-3
  2143,3412,4321                     2 each
  1342,2431,3124,4213                2t each
  all six transpositions            (t-1)^2 each
  every other permutation           0.

The weights sum identically to D. For every equal-cardinality pair I,J,

  |det V(t)[I,J]|^2
       = sum_pi weight_pi(t)/D * 1_{pi(I)=J}.                 (1)

The checker verifies all 70 identities symbolically in Q[t], including
cardinalities 0 through 4, after clearing positive powers of t^2+3. This is
a polynomial identity certificate, not a check at finitely many t values.

If t>=0 and t^4+4t-3>=0, all displayed weights are nonnegative. In particular,
this holds for every t>=1. Expanding det(diag(a)+V diag(b)V*) by principal
minors and Cauchy-Binet, then applying (1), proves

  det(diag(a)+V(t)diag(b)V(t)^T) = sum_pi weight_pi(t)/D z_pi

for EVERY complex pair of spectra a,b. Thus MOC holds on this entire dense
mixer family. Diagonal phases, row/column permutations, and transpose preserve
the conclusion with corresponding relabeling. The identity endpoint t=infinity
is included by continuity. Exact parameter instances t=1,2,3 are supplied.

There is a useful positive-coefficient derivation. Put x=t-1. The numerator
vector of minor probabilities is a degree-four polynomial in x. Its five
coefficient vectors have positive permutation laws of total masses

  16,16,12,4,1,

whose sum with weights 1,x,x^2,x^3,x^4 is (x^2+2x+4)^2. The x^2 coefficient
law is particularly simple: mass 6 on the identity and mass 1 on each of the
six transpositions. Summing these coefficient laws gives the fourteen weights
above. This is a concrete positive fixed-point representation; it does not
rely on a signed cohomological localization formula.

The relationship to the Walsh escape path must include its gauge. Let W be
the normalized real Walsh matrix in moc_pair_maximum_moonshot.txt, and set
P=((I+K)/2)W, a signed permutation. Then, whenever c^2+3s^2=1,

  (cI+sK)W = (xI-yK)P,
  x=(c+3s)/2,  y=(c-s)/2,  x^2+3y^2=1.

On the complete ascent arc c>=s>=0, y>=0 and x>=y. For y>0 the middle
matrix is V(x/y)^T, with x/y>=1. At y=0 it is the identity. Consequently
the ENTIRE dense Walsh-to-signed-permutation escape arc satisfies MOC for all
complex spectra, not only the special spectra used to find the cubic saddle.

Relationship to earlier repository work: Round038 of the standalone volume
allows arbitrary quaternion amplitudes but assumes a spectral cross-ratio
identity that kills its chiral term. The present formula allows arbitrary
spectra and retains chirality, while restricting the three off-diagonal
amplitudes to equal magnitude and imposing the stated t-domain. These are
different restricted slices; neither is a proof for all quaternion mixers.
The general fourteen-term representation literature and archived cone results
may subsume aspects of this formula. It is retained as a newly explicit,
independently replayable certificate in this lane, without a priority claim.

5. Why the direct moment-map shortcut cannot handle the unrestricted case

For the Walsh spectra above, take F(U)=Re(i det(diag(a)+U diag(b) U*)).
The existing exact certificate and accepted Lean determinant identity give

  F(W)=24,
  dF(W)=0,
  Hess_W F is negative semidefinite,
  F((cI+sK)W)=24+384cs^3,  c^2+3s^2=1.

Along exp(tK), the first two derivatives vanish and the third is 2304>0.
Therefore W is not a local maximum. The Hessian statement descends from U(4)
to the normal orbit because the quotient map is a submersion and F is constant
on its fibers.

THEOREM. This F is not a component of a Hamiltonian compact-torus moment map
on its normal orbit for ANY symplectic form and ANY smooth torus action.

Proof. A component of such a moment map is Morse-Bott. Locally at a critical
point it is constant on the critical submanifold and a nondegenerate quadratic
form in the normal directions. If its Hessian is negative semidefinite, all
normal quadratic weights are negative, and the point is a local maximum.
The Walsh cubic ascent contradicts that conclusion. The same argument applies
after any smooth scalar reparameterization psi(F) with psi'(24) nonzero,
with local maximum/minimum interchanged when the derivative is negative.

This does NOT say F cannot be a Hamiltonian function: every smooth function
defines a Hamiltonian vector field once a symplectic form is fixed. What fails
is the periodic/compact-torus action structure needed for moment-map convexity
and exact Duistermaat-Heckman localization. Changing the symplectic form cannot
remove this particular obstruction. The Bott-Morse theorem for compact
Hamiltonian torus actions in twisted generalized complex geometry gives the
same obstruction to that proposed extension.

Primary references checked:
  Atiyah and Bott, The moment map and equivariant cohomology (1984),
  https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~mmovshev/MAT570Spring2008/BOOKS/atiyahbott_moment.pdf
  explains the equivariantly closed extension requirement and the resulting
  localization formula. Arbitrary torus invariance does not supply it.

  Nitta, Convexity properties of generalized moment maps, arXiv:0901.0361v1,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.0361v1, proves the Bott-Morse property in the
  generalized complex setting. This more elaborate geometry does not bypass
  the exact cubic-saddle test.

  Mulero-Martinez, A variational framework for determinantal inequalities of
  normal matrices: Successes and obstructions (2026),
  https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2026.03.019. Its publisher abstract explicitly
  retains the unrestricted problem and notes nonmonomial support maximizers.
  The full publisher page returned 403 in this lane. No stronger claim about
  its proof or whether SOME monomial maximizer always exists is inferred.

For comparison, n=2 determinant support is an affine function of the usual
two-sphere height and admits the elementary two-fixed-point positive law.
For n=3, complementary minors turn the determinant into a linear function of
the doubly stochastic matrix |U_ij|^2, so a Birkhoff law suffices. That linear
function need not be a component of one fixed standard torus moment map;
the positive representation is the relevant property. At n=4 the middle
compound introduces the obstruction, but Section4 shows that positivity can
still be proved on nontrivial dense families without moment-map convexity.

6. What survives as a route toward the full theorem

The productive route is positive, spectra-dependent endpoint representations,
or a certified global inequality, rather than trying to relabel F as a moment
map. The coefficient-law construction above supplies a concrete model: find
a finite set of positive permutation laws whose polynomial interpolation
reproduces the actual middle-minor contribution. For arbitrary unitary mixers,
spectrum-independent common laws are known to fail. Any global extension must
use relations among the actual z_pi or allow spectrum-dependent weights.

The winding construction supplies inner regions and exact inverse-existence
certificates. It cannot by itself exclude determinant values outside the
permutation hull: every polygonal Cayley cycle already lies inside that hull,
so its winding is zero about every exterior point. An additional analytic
inequality is required for an outer bound. This is the precise remaining gap,
not a hidden claim that the topological theorem proves unrestricted MOC.

No Lean theorem for the Lie-group/topological bridge is claimed. The exact
Walsh determinant identity is already Lean-checked in MocPairDeterminant.lean.
The new code/lean/MocTopologyWeights.lean compiles with Lean4.19.0 and proves
eight scalar lemmas: positivity and total mass of the fourteen weights, their
positive-coefficient expansions, and the resulting normalized support bound.
Its final theorem depends only on propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound;
there are no sorrys or custom axioms. The matrix/minor bridge is explicitly
outside that Lean module. The all70 common-minor identities and winding
certificates are verified separately using exact rational/SymPy algebra.
