PHASE QUOTIENTS, GRAPH CYCLES, AND EXACT ELLIPSE CERTIFICATES
17 October 2026. Derived in the categorical follow-up; no priority claim.

1. GAUGE QUOTIENT OF A BLOCK COMPOSITION.
Let A and B be unitary and block diagonal on coordinate partitions P and Q.
Let G be their bipartite incidence multigraph: one edge per coordinate,
joining its P-block and Q-block. Put D(theta)=diag(exp(i theta_e)).

Adding alpha_p+beta_q to each edge phase theta_e leaves the complete
squared-minor kernel R(A D(theta) B) unchanged. Indeed the added P-constant
diagonal matrix commutes with A, and the Q-constant diagonal commutes
with B; the new product differs only by exterior row/column phases.
These phases disappear from every squared minor.

Fix a spanning forest of G. By successively choosing vertex phases, all
edge phases on that forest can be set to zero. Exactly

 beta_1(G)=|E|-|V|+#connected_components

chord phases remain. Equivalently these gauge-invariant phase combinations
are alternating cycle sums. Reversing the sign of Q-vertex phases turns
the gauge map into the usual graph coboundary. This gives a concrete
first-cohomology interpretation, with circle-valued phases; it does not
assert a categorical positivity axiom.

For a forest beta_1=0, no phase parameter remains. More strongly, the
occupancy-uniqueness theorem proves R(AB)=R(A)R(B), hence positive
composition whenever A and B have common permutation laws.

2. A SINGLE CYCLE HAS AN EXACT ELLIPSE ORBIT.
Suppose G is connected and has beta_1=1; trees attached to the unique
cycle and parallel-edge two-cycles are allowed. The gauge above leaves
one diagonal coordinate phase z=exp(i theta), on a selected chord e0.
For fixed equal-size I,J, Cauchy--Binet gives

 det(A D(z) B)[I,J] = alpha_IJ + z beta_IJ.

This expression is affine in z because each intermediate coordinate
subset either contains e0 or does not. In fact its nonzero intermediate
subsets are at most two: the difference of two equal-degree edge subsets
vanishes on every tree and alternates around the sole cycle.

Consequently every squared minor has Fourier frequencies only -1,0,1.
The full determinant, which is linear in these squared minors, has form

 d(theta)=d0+p cos(theta)+q sin(theta),    d0,p,q complex.

Its image is an ellipse, allowing a line segment or a point as a
Degenerate ellipse. This statement is uniform in all complex spectra,
all block sizes, and all actual block unitaries satisfying this graph
hypothesis. The center is the phase average, equal to evaluation on
R(A)R(B). This follows directly by integrating the cross term.

More generally an arbitrary block-incidence graph yields a finite
Laurent polynomial on beta_1 phase variables, with each frequency vector
in {-1,0,1}^{beta_1}. Each residual chord appears at most once in a minor.
Several frequencies may coincide after cycle constraints; beta_1 bounds
the number of independent phase variables, not the Fourier rank of every
particular choice of matrices.

3. A COMPLETE FINITE CERTIFICATE FOR THE ENTIRE PHASE CIRCLE.
For fixed spectra a,b, let z_pi=product_i(a_i+b_pi(i)). Assume their convex
hull is two-dimensional, and list its vertices v_1,...,v_m counterclockwise.
Let e_j=v_(j+1)-v_j and [e,w]=Im(conj(e)w). Then the entire phase orbit
lies in the permutation hull if and only if, for every polygon edge,

 h_j=[e_j,d0-v_j] >= sqrt(c_j^2+s_j^2),
 c_j=[e_j,p],   s_j=[e_j,q].

Proof: the oriented edge gap is h_j+c_j cos(theta)+s_j sin(theta).
Its minimum over all theta is h_j-sqrt(c_j^2+s_j^2). All edge gaps are
nonnegative exactly when every point lies in the closed convex polygon.
For rational complex data the certificate uses only rational comparisons:

 h_j >= 0,    h_j^2 >= c_j^2+s_j^2.

No phase sampling or numerical minimizer is needed. A degenerate hull
must be checked in its affine span instead of using polygon-edge normals.

An averaged positive law proves h_j>=0, but does NOT give the second
inequality. That precise missing quadratic comparison is where coherent
interference enters; it has not been proved universally for all spectra.
The ellipse criterion closes a fixed phase family when the finite exact
inequalities pass. It does not by itself prove the unrestricted conjecture.

4. EXACT SIX-DIMENSIONAL PHASE FAMILY.
The standard-library script category_phase_ellipse.py constructs rational
complex A and B with partitions

 P={{1,2,5},{3,4},{6}},  Q={{1,4,6},{2,3},{5}}.

The graph has six edges, six vertices, is connected, and has one cycle.
Use Householder H(v)=I-2vv^T/(v^Tv), H=H(1,2,2), K=H(1,2,3), and

 C=H diag(1,i,1) K,
 E=K diag((3+4i)/5,1,1) H,
 R(c,s)=[[c,s],[-s,c]].

A acts by C on {1,2,5}, R(3/5,4/5) on {3,4}, and 1 on {6}.
B acts by E on {1,4,6}, R(5/13,12/13) on {2,3}, and 1 on {5}.
Put W(theta)=A diag(exp(i theta),1,1,1,1,1) B and

 a=(1+i,2-i,-1+2i,3+i,-2-i,2+3i),
 b=(-2+i,1-2i,3+3i,-1+i,-2-2i,2-i).

The exact checker verifies A*A=B*B=I and constructs all 720 permutation
products. Their hull has 12 vertices. The ellipse is nondegenerate:

 d0= -32338565434976/36975700125
       +i 378821018453552/332781301125,
 p=  -10600933376/2465046675 - i 140968603648/7395140025,
 q=  -56102912/13042575 - i 977174528/117383175.

All twelve inequalities h_j>0 and h_j^2>c_j^2+s_j^2 hold as exact rational
comparisons. Therefore MOC holds for these fixed spectra and for EVERY
real theta, not just tested values. The smallest ratio h_j/sqrt(c_j^2+s_j^2)
is approximately204.73, reported only as a descriptive numerical value;
the proof uses the exact squared inequalities stored in the certificate.

The program obtains d0,p,q from four roots of unity, justified by the
proved Fourier-degree bound, and also checks an independent rational
unit-circle point z=(3+4i)/5. It writes all matrices, spectra, hull vertices,
and twelve rational edge checks to category_phase_ellipse_certificate.json.

5. WHAT IS ACTUALLY ADVANCED.
The graph first-cohomology quotient exactly counts phase variables left
by block gauge symmetry. Acyclicity supplies a proved positive composition
class in every dimension; one cycle supplies an exact finite certificate
for a whole continuous phase family. Neither is an assumption that all
positive equivariant kernels are unitary kernels, nor an inference from
phase averages to pointwise bounds.
