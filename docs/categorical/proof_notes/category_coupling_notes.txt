2. The unlabelled rank-two problem, and a new necessary implication.
Let P,W be orthogonal projections with PW=0, rankW=2, Q=P+W. An unlabelled interval law consists of X subset Y, |X|=rankP, |Y|=rankP+2, such that the random set obtained by independently adding each point ofY minusX with probabilityt has law DPP(P+tW), for everyt in[0,1].
This implies the stronger difference marginal law Y minusX ~ DPP(W). Proof: for distincti,j, the coefficient of t^2 in the probability that both are in the interpolated set equals Pr({i,j} subset Y minusX). The same coefficient in det((P+tW)[{i,j}]) is det(W[{i,j}]). Since Y minusX has size2, these give its entire law. Thus ordinary nested endpoint coupling is insufficient: a rank-two difference law is also required.
This links the problem to Lyons' disjoint-union coupling Question10.1, but the interval condition has additional mixed higher-order constraints and does not follow merely from its three marginal laws.

4. Exact five-dimensional midpoint sector in the(2,2,2) problem.
Write intervalweights w_{X,Y}, |X|=2, |Y|=4, X subset Y; there are90. The three interval layers are p_X, r_Z, q_Y, with masses1,2,1. Write d_D for the law of D=Y minusX, and c_C=q_{C^c} for C=Y^c. On every deterministic interval, and hence every law,

An explicit basis is given by the five noncrossing pairings

with

Each h_M has integer entries0,+1,-1. For any fixedpair, summing h_M over triples containing it cancels; direct elimination verifies independence. These five harmonic moments of r are therefore exactly the additional data beyond the three color marginals.
The interval incidence matrix has rationalrank43. The three colorpair marginal matrix has rationalrank38. Indeed the observable vanishes on configurations of sizes2 and4, and on size3 equals h_M. This provides five directly measurable cubic observables capturing precisely what the midpoint lift must preserve.

5. Disintegration criterion and a proved positive subclass.
Let lambda(X,Z,Y) be any nested three-level chainlaw with endpoint marginals p,q and normalized middle marginal r/2. Define its diamond involution
 F(X,Z,Y)=(X, X union(Y minusZ), Y).
A uniform-middle intervallaw exists exactly when such a chainlaw can be chosen invariant underF. In one direction take lambda=w_{X,Y}/2 for each of the two middle sets. In the other set w_{X,Y}=sum_Zlambda(X,Z,Y). Ordinary Markov disintegration supplies some lambda but does not impose F-invariance. Averaging lambda with its F-image changes the middle marginal unless that additional condition already holds. Thus symmetrization is not a proof.
Positive theorem: suppose the coordinate groundset decomposes E=E1 disjointunion E2, P=P1 directsum P2, and W=W1 directsum W2 with rankW1=rankW2=1 and P_iW_i=0. Then the full unlabelled intervallaw exists. Couple X_i~DPP(P_i) subset Y_i~DPP(P_i+W_i) using rank-one stochastic domination, independently across the coordinate blocks. Set X=X1 unionX2 and Y=Y1 unionY2. Each difference Y_i minusX_i is one point. Conditional independent Bernoulli(t) inclusion of these two points gives, independently in each block,
 (1-t)DPP(P_i)+tDPP(P_i+W_i)=DPP(P_i+tW_i),
because the determinant distribution is affine in a rank-one eigenvalue. Block independence gives DPP(P+tW). This is a complete coupling construction for a genuine rank-two-increment class in every dimension; it does not cover indecomposable rank-two increments.
More generally the same proof handles any number of coordinate blocks with one rank-one increment per block. This is the precise monoidal closure available; replacing coordinate-direct-sum independence by arbitrary orthogonal Hilbert subspaces is the unresolved step, not a categorical identity.
