Let P(U) have coordinates p_{I,J}=|det U[I,J]|^2 for all equal-cardinality
subsets I,J of {1,...,n}. There are sum_k binom(n,k)^2=binom(2n,n)
coordinates, including p_empty,empty=p_full,full=1. Define
 S_n=P(U(n)) (the actual Euclidean image),
 X_n=the complex Zariski closure of S_n in the complex coordinate space.

RESULTS.
 * S_n is compact semialgebraic and already Euclidean closed.
 * For every n>=2, S_n is NOT a real algebraic set.
 * dim_R S_n=dim_C X_n=(n-1)^2 for every n>=1.
 * X_2 is an affine line and X_3 is an affine4-space; both have degree1.
 * The proposed factorial-times-factorization-count degree formula fails
   already at n2, independently of how an integer factorization count is
   defined. No general degree formula for n>=4 is established here.
These are geometry corrections, not a proof of MOC.

1. Correct phase count

All squared minors are unchanged under U->LUR with diagonal unitary L,R.
The acting group T^n x T^n has dimension2n. At a unitary whose entries are
all nonzero, LUR=U implies L=lambda I, R=lambda^-1 I; its stabilizer has
dimension1. The effective orbit therefore has dimension2n-1, giving
 n^2-(2n-1)=(n-1)^2.
An equivalent calculation works in SU(n): its dimension is n^2-1 and the
left/right determinant-one diagonal group has dimension2n-2 with finite
generic stabilizer, again leaving (n-1)^2.

T^{n-1} x T^{n-1} has dimension2n-2, not (n-1)^2. Also n^2-(n-1)^2=2n-1,
not n^2-1. Neither version of the proposed dimension count is valid.
Exceptional zero patterns do not increase the image dimension: a nonzero
maximal-rank differential minor is nonzero on an open set, which meets
the dense all-nonzero-entry locus. The complexification argument below
also supplies a direct global algebraic dimension bound.

2. An elementary explicit all-n Jacobian proof of equality

Let 1 denote the column of n ones and set
 alpha=(-1+i)/n, U=I+alpha 11^T.
It is unitary because alpha+bar(alpha)+n|alpha|^2=0, and all its entries
are nonzero. Consider just the singleton projection mu(U)=(|U_ij|^2).
Its tangent variation along a skew-Hermitian K is
 dmu_U(KU)_ij=2 Re((KU)_ij bar(U_ij)).

A real matrix T annihilates this derivative for every K if and only if
 M=(T elementwise-multiplied by U) U*
is Hermitian: this is real Hilbert-Schmidt orthogonality to all
skew-Hermitian matrices. Put d_i=T_ii and s_i=sum_j T_ij. For i!=j,
 M_ij=alpha T_ij+bar(alpha)d_i+|alpha|^2s_i.
Hermiticity of its imaginary parts gives
 T_ij+T_ji=d_i+d_j.
Hermiticity of the real parts then gives
 T_ij=d_j+(s_i-s_j)/n.
Thus every cokernel element has the row-plus-column form
 T_ij=a_i+b_j, where a_i=s_i/n and b_j=d_j-s_j/n.
The diagonal entries of M are automatically real, so no omitted equation
is needed. Conversely every row-plus-column matrix annihilates the
derivative, because row and column sums of mu are fixed. This cokernel
has dimension2n-1, proving
 rank(dmu_U)=n^2-(2n-1)=(n-1)^2.
Since the complete profile contains the singleton coordinates, its rank
is at least this value; phase invariance supplies the matching upper bound.

The literature agent independently checked this elementary derivation,
including the diagonal equations and the converse.

This conclusion agrees with the primary reference Alexander V. Karabegov,
“A mapping from the unitary to doubly stochastic matrices and symbols on a
finite set,” arXiv:0806.2357 (2008), https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.2357.
Example2 and Theorem5.1 give the same explicit unitary family and generic
submersion. Those statements and the explicit witness were checked in the
primary PDF. The direct cokernel derivation above is included so the
present dimension conclusion does not depend on importing its proof.

3. Correct complexification and a useful rational parametrization

For G in GL_n(C), define
 Phi(G)_{I,J}=det G[I,J] det(G^-1)[J,I].
On U(n), (G^-1)[J,I]=U*[J,I], so Phi(U)=P(U). Equivalently, Jacobi's
complementary-minor identity gives
 Phi(G)_{I,J}=(-1)^{sum I+sum J}
             det G[I,J] det G[I^c,J^c]/det G.

The compact real form U(n) is Zariski dense in GL_n(C). One elementary
justification is to compose a holomorphic function near I with the
exponential of a real basis of skew-Hermitian matrices: that basis spans
all complex matrices over C, and vanishing on the real parameter slice
forces vanishing in a complex neighborhood. Thus X_n is precisely the
Zariski closure of Phi(GL_n(C)). The same left/right complex diagonal
torus invariance has generic orbit dimension2n-1, proving
 dim_C X_n<=n^2-(2n-1).
The real Jacobian witness proves the reverse bound. It also proves
that the real image has that same dimension.

On the open set where the first row and column of G are nonzero, left/right
diagonal scaling uniquely normalizes that row and column to all ones.
The remaining (n-1)^2 coordinates can be taken as
 q_ij=G_ij G_11/(G_i1 G_1j),  i,j>=2.
Substituting this normalized matrix in Phi gives an explicit rational
parametrization of a dense part of X_n. This correctly identifies the
parameter dimension; it does not supply a claimed degree formula or imply
that every real point of X_n is a unitary profile.

4. The exact n2 image and degree

Every2x2 singleton block is
 [[t,1-t],[1-t,t]], 0<=t<=1,
and both endpoint-rank coordinates equal1. Every such t is realized by
 [[sqrt(t),sqrt(1-t)],[-sqrt(1-t),sqrt(t)]].
Thus S_2 is an affine line segment of real dimension1, not a
three-dimensional Birkhoff polytope. Its real Zariski closure is the whole
real line, its complex Zariski closure is the corresponding complex line,
and its projective closure has degree1.

5. The exact n3 closure, degree, and actual membership condition

For3x3 unitaries, Jacobi's identity says
 |det U[I,J]|^2=|U_{I^c,J^c}|^2  (|I|=|J|=2).
Therefore the full profile is the affine embedding
 B -> (1,B,complementary copy of B,1)
of the3x3 unistochastic singleton matrix B. The real bistochastic affine
space is given by row sums=column sums=1 and has dimension4. The explicit
Jacobian witness in Section2 proves the unistochastic image contains a
relative open subset of this affine space. Hence X_3 is exactly that
complex affine4-space, embedded linearly, and deg X_3=1.

There is also an elementary complete membership test for the Euclidean
image. A3x3 nonnegative bistochastic B is unistochastic if and only if,
putting a=B_11 B_21, b=B_12 B_22, c=B_13 B_23,
 2(ab+bc+ca)-a^2-b^2-c^2>=0.                                (A)
Proof: orthogonality of the first two unitary rows asks that three complex
numbers of magnitudes sqrt(a),sqrt(b),sqrt(c) sum to zero. This is exactly
the (possibly degenerate) triangle condition, equivalent to(A). If it
holds, choose phases yielding the first two orthonormal rows and complete
them to an orthonormal basis of C^3. Column norms then force the third
row's squared moduli to be1-B_1j-B_2j=B_3j. Conversely every unitary gives
such a triangle. This includes zero and degenerate cases.

For example
 B=(1/2)[[1,1,0],[1,0,1],[0,1,1]]
is bistochastic but not unistochastic: its first two rows overlap in
exactly one nonzero column and cannot be orthogonal. Equivalently(A)
has value -1/16. This point belongs to the affine Zariski closure but not
the Euclidean image. The nonlinear membership restriction is an
inequality; it does not make the Zariski closure have degree greater than1.

6. Why the Euclidean image is not algebraic for any n>=2

Embed the2x2 family above as U2(t) direct-sum I_{n-2}. Every squared minor
is0,1,t,or1-t, so the full profiles trace a segment in an affine line L.
For t outside[0,1], the corresponding line point has a negative singleton
coordinate and is not a profile. Consequently S_n intersect L is exactly
the closed nontrivial segment0<=t<=1. If S_n were a real algebraic set,
its intersection with L would be an algebraic subset of a real line.
Every polynomial vanishing on a nontrivial interval vanishes on the
whole line, a contradiction. Thus ordinary closure is never the claimed
real algebraic variety. The image is compact and closed because U(n) is
compact, and semialgebraic by polynomial-image quantifier elimination.


8. Linear profile constraints and exact checks

For each rank k, m=binom(n,k), the compound matrix wedge^k U is unitary.
Consequently its squared-minor block has each row sum and column sum1.
Among these2m equations exactly2m-1 are independent. Their total-sum
consequence is sum_{I,J}p_{I,J}=m, not1 unless m=1; it is redundant.
Complementarity p_{I,J}=p_{I^c,J^c} adds cross-rank identifications.
These are valid constraints, not a complete profile membership theorem.

profile_geometry_verify.py is standalone, uses exact Gaussian fractions,
and verifies singleton Jacobian ranks for n2 through8. The universal
all-n result is established by the proof in Section2, not inferred from
these finite checks. For n3 the first four independent singleton outputs
and four explicit skew-Hermitian tangent directions give a4x4 minor
with determinant -64/729. It checks all n3 complementary minors at that
witness and the explicit non-unistochastic bistochastic matrix above.
All checks passed. No MOC positivity conclusion is inferred from them.
