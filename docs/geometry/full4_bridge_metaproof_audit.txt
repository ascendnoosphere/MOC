1. THE FULL PROFILE CONVEXIFICATION IS VALID, WITH A FOUR-POINT BOUND.

Write p(U)_{I,J}=|det U[I,J]|^2 for every pair of equal-size subsets.
For each U in U(4), choose any row r. Column phases make that row a real
unit vector v without changing any p(U) coordinate or U D_b U*.
If x_i is another row transposed, let E_i=Re(x_i x_i*) on v-perp.
These three effects are real symmetric positive semidefinite, have trace
one and rank at most two, and sum to the identity of real dimension 3.

The real effect-triple set
 C={ (E_1,E_2,E_3): E_i>=0, tr(E_i)=1, sum E_i=I_3 }
has exactly ordered orthogonal rank-one frames as its extreme points.
Indeed, if all ranks are >=2, the supported symmetric perturbation spaces
have total dimension >=9, while the sum and trace conditions impose at
most 8 independent constraints. There is a nonzero supported perturbation
of both signs, contradicting extremality. If one E_i is rank one, it is
a projection; the two other effects are supported on its orthogonal
complement, sum to I_2, and are simultaneously diagonalizable. Their
eigenvalues are theta,1-theta and 1-theta,theta, so the triple is a mixture
of two ordered orthogonal frames. At an extreme point theta is 0 or 1.

For the actual triples above there is the stronger cardinality bound 4.
If one effect has rank one, use the two-frame formula. Otherwise all have
rank two. Along a nonzero supported trace-preserving perturbation, the
maximal PSD interval is bounded in both directions and contains 0 in its
interior. At each endpoint at least one effect loses rank, because an
endpoint with all effects positive definite on their fixed supports could
be extended. Trace one excludes rank zero. Each endpoint is consequently
a mixture of at most two frames, hence the original triple of at most 4.

Insert the fixed v as row r of each resulting real orthogonal matrix O_s.
The same weights preserve all minors, for these separate reasons:
 - Singletons are the diagonal entries of E_i.
 - A 2-minor in rows i,r and columns j,k is
   (v_k e_j-v_j e_k)^T E_i (v_k e_j-v_j e_k), hence linear in E_i.
 - Every remaining 2-minor has complementary row set containing r, and
   unitary complementary minors have equal squared modulus.
 - 3-minors are complementary to singletons, and ranks 0 and 4 are 1.
Changing the sign of one nonanchor row makes det(O_s)=1 when necessary,
without changing any profile or the anchor. Thus

 p(U)=sum_{s=1}^q lambda_s p(O_s),
 q<=4, lambda_s>=0, sum lambda_s=1, O_s in SO(4), row_r(O_s)=v.

No claim concerning real dimension 4 effect triples or n>=5 is used.
The rank-perturbation dimension count is specifically for three effects
on a real three-dimensional space. All effects and perturbations here
are REAL symmetric; the proof does not silently import a complex POVM
extreme-point assertion.

The determinant expansion
 det(D_a+U D_b U*)=sum_{|I|=|J|} a_{I^c} b_J p(U)_{I,J}
then gives, with the SAME spectrum-independent weights and O_s,
 det(D_a+U D_b U*)=sum_s lambda_s det(D_a+O_s D_b O_s^T)
for every complex a,b. Therefore conv p(U(4))=conv p(SO(4)), including
their anchored fibers.

Useful strengthened formulation: for any fixed a,b,eta, the maximum
of Re(eta det(D_a+U D_b U*)) over U(4) equals the maximum over SO(4).
If U is a global maximizer, each positive-weight O_s in its decomposition
is also a global maximizer, and may retain any chosen phase-adjusted
anchor row of U. The same equality holds when the anchor row is prescribed.
This proves existence of an orthogonal maximizer, not that every maximizer
is orthogonal, monomial, or commuting.
