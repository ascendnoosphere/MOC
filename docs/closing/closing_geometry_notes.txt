2. THE EXACT NORMAL-DEFECT STATIONARITY AND CURVATURE SYSTEM

Complete V to a unitary frame and write the actual normal matrix in blocks:

 A_tilde = [[C,v],[u*,a]],       C=V* A V,
 E=C+D, R=E^{-1}, kappa=eta det E.

Here u,v belong to C^(n-1), a is scalar, and E is assumed invertible. Normality gives

 [C,C*]=u u* - v v*,                  ||u||=||v||,
 C u + conjugate(a) v = C* v + a u.                    (2.1)

These constraints distinguish an actual normal compression from an arbitrary nonnormal matrix.

For an arbitrary external frame direction z in C^(n-1), conjugate A_tilde by exp(tK), where

 K=[[0,z],[-z*,0]].

The compressed block derivatives are

 C' = z u* + v z*,
 C''=2 a z z* - z z* C - C z z*.                       (2.2)

Put
 alpha=u*Rz, beta=z*Rv, gamma=u*Rv, rho=z*Rz.

The exact support curvature is

 H_ext(z)=Re kappa{
     -2||z||^2 + z*(DR+RD)z
     +2(a-gamma)rho +2 alpha beta }.                   (2.3)

Proof: the usual second determinant derivative is kappa times
 tr(RC'')+(tr(RC'))^2-tr((RC')^2).
Here tr(RC')=alpha+beta and tr((RC')^2)=alpha^2+beta^2+2 gamma rho. Also CR=I-DR and RC=I-RD, giving tr(RC'')=-2||z||^2+z*(DR+RD)z+2a rho. Substitution proves (2.3).

Stationarity in all external directions is equivalent to

 kappa R v + conjugate(kappa) R* u =0.                 (2.4)

Indeed Re{kappa(alpha+beta)} is the real pairing of z with the vector on the left of (2.4). If q=kappa Rv, then

 v=E q/kappa,   u=-E* q/conjugate(kappa),
 ||E q||=||E* q||.                                    (2.5)

The last equality follows from normality's ||u||=||v||. Internal frame stationarity separately says kappa[C,R] is Hermitian. A global maximum must satisfy H_ext(z)<=0 for every z, together with the entire internal/external mixed Hessian conditions.

The standalone exact checker closing_geometry_verify.py verifies the normal defect and first/second derivative identities over Gaussian rationals on a dense four-dimensional normal example, using a full real external basis and its pairwise sums (21 checks).

Let omega=exp(2 pi i/3), A=diag(1,omega,omega^2), and let V have columns

 (1,omega,omega^2)/sqrt(3),
 (1,omega^2,omega)/sqrt(3).

Then C=V*A V=[[0,0],[1,0]]. This compression is not a convex combination of 2 by 2 normal matrices whose spectra are drawn from {1,omega,omega^2}, even allowing repetition.

4. THE SAME EXAMPLE HAS A CONSTRUCTIVE D-DEPENDENT REPAIR

The failure above is not treated as an obstruction to the candidate. It admits an explicit repair for each fixed eigenbasis of D.

Write D=W diag(d_1,d_2) W*, replace V by VW, and put
 p_i=|(VW)_(i,1)|^2.
Then sum p_i=1 and 0<=p_i<=2/3, while the other column has squared entries 2/3-p_i. There are nonnegative weights w_ij, i!=j, satisfying

 sum_(j!=i) w_ij=p_i,        w_ij+w_ji=1/3.             (4.1)

To see this, put
 w_01=t, w_02=p_0-t,
 w_10=1/3-t, w_12=p_1-1/3+t,
 w_20=1/3-p_0+t, w_21=2/3-p_1-t.
The admissible interval is

 max(0, 1/3-p_1, p_0-1/3)
 <=t<=min(p_0, 1/3, 2/3-p_1).

Every lower bound is at most every upper bound exactly by 0<=p_i<=2/3 and sum p_i=1, so the interval is nonempty. These weights give, for arbitrary complex d_1,d_2,

 det(C+D)=sum_(i!=j) w_ij (omega^i+d_1)(omega^j+d_2).   (4.2)

The d_2 coefficient is the first diagonal entry of W*CW; the d_1 coefficient is its second diagonal entry. The constant term is zero because det C=0 and each unordered pair has weight1/3, while 1+omega+omega^2=0. The d_1 d_2 coefficient is one. Thus (4.2) is an exact polynomial identity, not a numerical fit.

For example, W=[[3/5,-4/5],[4/5,3/5]] gives p=(49,13,13)/75. In the order (01,02,10,12,20,21), weights (49,49,1,25,1,25)/150 work.

This verifies concretely that a determinant-specific replacement can survive a complete failure of matrix-level normal replacement. It does not extend the transport proof to general n: higher minors create additional compatibility requirements. No unproved higher-dimensional coupling is claimed.
