CLOSING ROUND: FUNCTIONAL-CALCULUS WEIGHTS IN THE ACTUAL DETERMINANT HESSIAN

Status: the formulas below are proved algebraic identities. They do not prove unrestricted Marcus--de Oliveira. The remaining problem is a sign/flat-transport assertion for the actual inverse-C data, described in Section 6. No floating-point search is used in a proved assertion.

1. Setup and exact complex second variation

Absorb the witnessing unitary into B. Let A and B be normal, C=A+B invertible, R=C^{-1}, kappa=eta det C with |eta|=1, and D=kappa[B,R]=kappa[R,A]. At a critical point of F(U)=Re eta det(A+UBU*), D is Hermitian. In both A and B eigenbases its diagonal is zero. For a complex matrix X, extend the orbit holomorphically as f(G)=eta det(A+GBG^{-1}), and write q(X) for the second derivative along G=exp(tX). Then
 q(X)=(tr XD)^2/kappa+kappa tr([X,B]R[X,A]R).                 (1)
The feasible support Hessian is H(K,K)=Re q(K) for K*=-K.

The intended closing statement is ascent OR an exact flat transport to a commuting maximizer. A nonzero D does not force ascent at every support maximizer: noncommuting n=2 constant-support examples already show this. Nothing below replaces that necessary alternative by the false assertion that every maximizer commutes.

2. A universal paired-direction identity

Work in a B eigenbasis. Let X=w e_j* with w_j=0. Then X^2=0 and XBX=0. Thus
 exp(tX)Bexp(-tX)=B+t[X,B],
and [X,B] has at most one nonzero column. The determinant is affine in the complex parameter t, so q(X)=0. The adjoint X* has the corresponding one-row property, so q(X*)=0 too. By polarization, putting K=X-X* and L=i(X+X*),
 H(K,K)=H(L,L),   H(K,L)=0.                                (2)
This identity does not require normality of A or stationarity. It also applies to X=DP_j at a stationary point, where P_j=e_je_j* and D_jj=0.

If H is nonpositive and one of these common diagonal values is zero, every Hessian mixed term with K or L must vanish. This follows from the elementary quadratic-form fact that a zero vector of a nonpositive form belongs to its kernel. Such vanishing can detect escapes when the actual data force a nonzero mixed term. However, the common diagonal values are not universally zero.

3. Exact weighted-projector trace

Continue in the B eigenbasis and assume D*=D. Put
 E=[D,B], a_j=(D^2)_jj, r_j=R_jj,
 K_j^+=i(DP_j+P_jD), K_j^-=DP_j-P_jD.
Here a_j>=0. Summing the exact second variations gives
 sum_j(H(K_j^+,K_j^+)+H(K_j^-,K_j^-))
  =-4 Re(sum_j a_j^2/kappa)
   -2 Re kappa{tr(E[R,D])+sum_j r_j[D,E]_jj
                             -2 sum_j r_j(ERE)_jj}.         (3)
The notation Re kappa{...} means Re(kappa times the entire brace).

Derivation: use [DP_j,B]=EP_j and [P_jD,B]=P_jE. The plus/minus sum cancels the pure X-X and X*-X* terms, leaving the two mixed terms. Also
 R[X,A]R=[R,X]-R[X,B]R.
Their linear commutator contribution sums to
 tr(E[R,D])+sum_j r_j[D,E]_jj,
and their remaining contribution sums to -2 sum_j r_j(ERE)_jj. The scalar square terms sum to -4 sum_j a_j^2/kappa.

There is no universal trace-zero identity here. An exact family in Section 5 has a strictly positive value of (3). Formula (3) retains the actual inverse-C mixed terms and does not turn them into a sum of nonnegative quantities.

4. A determinant-ratio identity for all functional-calculus weights

Let P=diag(p_j) commute with B, and put
 W=R(AD+DB)=D+R[D,B]=D+RE.
For the holomorphic similarity pencil G(t)=I+tDP, direct multiplication yields
 A G(t)+G(t)B=C+t(AD+DB)P.
Therefore, wherever G(t) is invertible,
 f(G(t))=kappa det(I+tWP)/det(I+tDP).                       (4)
This equality is an identity of rational functions, so does not depend on a choice of small real parameter.

The commutator relation implies
 kappa R E=D^2-kappa[B,RD].                                 (5)
Since the commutator with diagonal B has zero diagonal and D_jj=0,
 kappa W_jj=(D^2)_jj=a_j.
Consequently the first derivative of (4) is
 f'(0)=sum_j a_j p_j.                                      (6)
In particular, positive real p_j give a nonnegative real derivative, strictly positive when the weights see a nonzero column of D. This is a nonunitary complex-orbit direction. The pencil G(t)=I+tDP does not generally lie in the unitary group, so (6) is NOT a support ascent.

For the exponential path with X=DP, the exact second derivative is
 q(DP)=(sum_j a_j p_j)^2/kappa
       -kappa tr(WPWP)+kappa tr(DPDP)+tr(DPDPD).             (7)
Indeed the determinant-ratio second derivative has the first three terms, because tr(DP)=0; replacing I+tX by exp(tX) adds the first derivative applied to X^2, namely tr(X^2D).

Equation (7) makes the unresolved interference explicit. Its off-diagonal coefficients contain W_jk W_kj, not |W_jk|^2. Neither the Hermiticity of D nor normality of A and B has here been shown to impose the required sign on their combination with the last two terms. The diagonal coefficients of q(DP) vanish, consistently with Section 2.

For simple spectrum B, every diagonal P is a polynomial p(B), by finite interpolation. For repeated eigenvalues, polynomial weights must agree on equal eigenspaces; choosing individual rank-one eigenprojectors then enlarges the family beyond functional calculus. This distinction matters when interpreting computational probes.

5. Exact actual spectral tests

5.1. Known N077 saddle from the supplied volume.
In its B eigenbasis, with b=(-3,-1,-2,0), the eight directions ordered
 (K_0^+,K_0^-,K_1^+,K_1^-,K_2^+,K_2^-,K_3^+,K_3^-)
have all diagonal Hessians zero. Every minus-direction row is zero, and the plus-direction block is
 [[0,-28,0,-28],[-28,0,-28,0],[0,-28,0,-28],[-28,0,-28,0]].
It has a positive eigenvalue 56. Taking p=(1,-1,1,-1) gives
 H(i(DP+PD),i(DP+PD))=224.
These weights come from
 p(t)=(2/3)t^3+3t^2+(7/3)t-1.
The point has support7 below matching ceiling8. This calculation is an exact escape of that known saddle, not a new restricted MOC theorem and not a global-maximizer counterexample.

5.2. A broad exact stationary family.
Let n=2m, let S be a positive real diagonal matrix, and let J be a real symmetric orthogonal involution with m eigenvalues +1 and m eigenvalues -1. Define
 B=S^2, A=-i S J S.
Then A is skew-Hermitian, B is Hermitian, and
 C=S(I-iJ)S,
 R=(1/2)S^{-1}(I+iJ)S^{-1},
 det C=2^m det B>0.
Consequently D=(det C)[B,R] is Hermitian, so this is an exact stationary pair for real determinant support. The statement is stationarity, not maximality.

For n=4, S=diag(1,2,3,4), let
 U=(1/3)[[2,0,-1,-2],[2,1,0,2],[1,-2,2,0],[0,-2,-2,1]],
 J=U diag(1,1,-1,-1)U^T.
The exact checker finds det C=2304 and weighted trace (3)
 25438453760000/729 > 0.
This certifies that actual normality and support stationarity do not force the weighted trace to vanish. It also exhibits a feasible ascent in this particular family member, as a positive trace forces a positive diagonal summand.

5.3. Scope of verification and exploration.
closing_variational_verify.py is a standalone standard-library checker using exact Gaussian fractions. It checks the complete polynomial numerator identity in (4), the exponential second derivative (7), the paired nilpotent identity (2), and (3) on explicit stationary pairs. It checks the full N077 weighted Hessian and its curvature224 direction, and an actual noncommuting n=2 flat support. These finite calculations verify the displayed examples and independently test the algebra. They do not prove a universal positivity statement.
