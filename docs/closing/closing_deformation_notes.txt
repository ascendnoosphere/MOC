CONSTRUCTIVE SPECTRAL DEFORMATION AND INDEPENDENT CLOSING-ROUND AUDIT
2026-10-17. Literature/deformation agent.

Scope. This note contains exact deformation lemmas and an independent audit
of the algebraic/variational agents' new compact-curve argument. The latter
does remove escape to infinity in the complementary-pair support problem.
It does not yet prove unrestricted MOC: its terminal high-contact spectral
strata remain to be controlled. No abstract countermodel below is presented
as an actual normal-matrix counterexample.

1. One-eigenvalue deformation is exactly a coefficient-polytope problem.

Fix U, b, and a_1,...,a_(n-1), and let a_n=x. Write

 d(x)=det(D_a+U D_b U*)=p x+q,
 z_pi(x)=product_i(a_i+b_pi(i))=u_pi x+v_pi.

PROPOSITION 1. MOC holds for this fixed family for every complex x if and
only if

 (p,q) belongs to conv{(u_pi,v_pi):pi in S_n} in C^2=R^4.

Equivalently, there is one nonnegative permutation law, independent of x,
which reproduces both determinant coefficients.

Proof. Coefficient inclusion immediately gives the evaluated inclusion.
Conversely, every real linear functional on C^2 has the form
 Re(lambda p+mu q). If mu!=0, evaluate MOC at x=lambda/mu in direction mu.
The missing functionals mu=0 follow by continuity in (lambda,mu), or by
letting x tend to infinity. Real separation then gives coefficient inclusion.

This removes a quantifier but leaves a genuine four-real-dimensional
polytope membership obligation. Lower-dimensional MOC does not automatically
prove it: p is a determinant involving a compression of the normal matrix
U D_b U*, and that compression need not be normal.

For fixed support multiplier eta, a strict violation in x is the region

 Re[eta((p-u_pi)x+(q-v_pi))]>0 for every pi.

It is an open intersection of half-planes. If A_pi in R^2 denotes its slope
and c_pi its intercept, the maximum possible support margin has the exact
linear-programming dual

 sup_x min_pi(A_pi dot x+c_pi)
 = min{sum_pi lambda_pi c_pi:
       lambda>=0, sum lambda=1, sum lambda A_pi=0},

when the primal has a finite optimum. An optimal dual law can be chosen
with at most three nonzero weights. A bounded violating fiber is therefore
compatible with affine dependence; escape to infinity is not automatic.


2. Fixed supporting-edge transport and actual spectral KKT equations.

Let d,z_pi be holomorphic along a spectral family. Fix two labels 0,1 with
rho=z_1-z_0 nonzero. Put

 H=(d-z_0)/rho, R_pi=(z_pi-z_0)/rho.

The edge is oriented so Im R_pi>=0. Its determinant support inequality is
Im H>=0. On a disk where this edge persists, boundary validity propagates
to the interior by the harmonic minimum principle. Hence a violating
component cannot be relatively compact with nonnegative boundary while
rho stays nonzero and this edge remains supporting. It must meet a new
support tie, a denominator zero, or the domain boundary. A new tie can
occur at fully distinct finite spectra and is not a spectral collision.

At a regular constrained local minimum of Im H, with active constraints
Im R_pi=0, the KKT multipliers satisfy

 DH=sum_pi lambda_pi D R_pi, lambda_pi>=0.

Write L=H-sum lambda_pi R_pi and define
 nu_1=L, nu_0=1-sum lambda_pi-L, nu_pi=lambda_pi otherwise.
Then, at that point,

 sum nu_pi=1, d=sum nu_pi z_pi, Dd=sum nu_pi D z_pi.

The last equation includes derivatives in every actual complex spectral
coordinate. For M=D_a+U D_b U*, d!=0,

 partial_(a_i) d=d(M^-1)_(ii),
 partial_(b_j) d=d(U* M^-1 U)_(jj),
 partial_(a_i) z_pi=z_pi/(a_i+b_pi(i)),
 partial_(b_j) z_pi=z_pi/(a_(pi^-1(j))+b_j),

with the polynomial extension at zero factors. The two coefficients nu_0,
nu_1 have opposite nonzero imaginary parts when Im H<0. Thus these exact
actual-determinant stationarity equations do not themselves yield a
nonnegative permutation law. KKT is used here only under a suitable
constraint qualification; no universal qualification is asserted.

3. Actual projective compactification and the initially suspected gap.

Write a_i=s_i/t_i and b_j=u_j/v_j. On X=(CP^1)^(2n), define

 Z_pi=product_i(s_i v_pi(i)+t_i u_pi(i)),
 F_U=det(((s_i v_j+t_i u_j)U_ij)_(ij))/det U.

Every Z_pi and F_U is a section of the same multidegree-(1,...,1) line
bundle. For even n and two perfect matchings, the loop product Pspec is
also such a section: it is a product of within-A and within-B brackets,
each spectral coordinate occurring once. Consequently Z_pi/Pspec and
F_U/Pspec are global rational functions, with possible poles only on
paired spectral-collision divisors.

Continuity of the original inequality at Pspec=0 by itself does not
control normalized 0/0 limits. HOWEVER, section4 below supplies a bounded
subharmonic extension which bypasses these limits completely. The initial
leading-jet objection is therefore NOT a gap in that stronger argument.

4. New compact-curve transfer: independent audit and all-even-n extension.

Assume the established matching-cycle notation: n=2r>=4, two local matching
layers, gate outcomes pi_s for s in {0,1}^n, complementary bits bar s,
and the nonzero complex loop product P=Pspec. For a fixed multiplier eta,

 h=max_pi Re(eta z_pi), delta_pi=h-Re(eta z_pi)>=0,
 B=2 sum_{one representative of {s,bar s}}
                 sqrt(delta_pi_s delta_pi_bar_s),
 T=2|Re(eta P)|.

The proposed complementary-pair bound is B>=T. It is stronger than the
capacity condition needed for these unitary circuits. The following is a
theorem about where a failure of this proposed bound must occur.

THEOREM 2 (finite high-contact transfer).
If B<T for any actual spectra in even dimension n>=4, there are actual
finite spectra, with the same complex P and the same eta, for which B<T
and at least 2n-2 permutation LABELS attain the maximum h. In dimension4
the threshold is six labels. Labels may have coincident complex products;
the statement counts labels, not distinct vertices.

Inputs from the supplied research volume, not new assumptions:
The affine 2n-point invariant cone has pure complex dimension 2n-2. Its
coordinates can be taken as the permutation bracket products. Every
nonzero cone point is representable by finite complex spectra up to a
common complex scalar, which can be absorbed into spectral scaling. See
moc_volume.txt, finite-chart lemma around lines9887-9915. P is a linear
combination of these coordinates, by the bracket/Plucker relations.

Proof, one transfer step. Suppose the current violating point has k<=2n-3
active labels A, and choose pi_0 in A. On the invariant cone impose the
complex affine equations

 P=P(current),
 z_pi-z_pi_0=(z_pi-z_pi_0)(current), pi in A\{pi_0}.

There are k equations. The local complex dimension is at least 2n-2-k>=1,
so this fiber contains a nonconstant irreducible algebraic curve through
the point. Take its smooth compact normalization S; the finitely many
points outside the affine curve form the puncture set E. Pullback of all
coordinate functions is meromorphic on S and holomorphic on S\E.

On S\E define the harmonic functions
 delta_pi=Re[eta(z_pi_0-z_pi)].
For active labels these are identically0. Let Omega be the connected
component containing the current point of the open set where every
inactive delta_pi is strictly positive. On Omega, pi_0 remains supporting.

For positive harmonic u,v,

 Laplacian sqrt(uv)
 =-(sqrt(uv)/4)|grad log u-grad log v|^2 <=0.

Any identically-zero factor contributes the identically-zero function.
Thus B is superharmonic and u=T-B is subharmonic on Omega. Moreover u<=T.
Assume for contradiction that u<=0 at every finite boundary point of Omega.
Define v=max(u,0) on Omega and v=0 outside Omega, initially on S\E.
The finite-boundary assumption and continuity make this standard gluing of
subharmonic functions subharmonic across the boundary. It obeys 0<=v<=T.
Every isolated puncture is removable for a bounded-above subharmonic
function. Therefore v extends subharmonically over compact S and is
constant. Since it was positive at the starting point, it is a positive
constant. It follows that Omega=S\E: any finite complement or boundary
would have v=0.

Consequently every delta_pi is nonnegative everywhere on S\E. A
meromorphic function whose real part is nonnegative there cannot have a
pole: its leading pole term has both signs on every punctured disk. Its
real part is then harmonic on compact S and constant. Applying this to
eta(z_pi_0-z_pi), every complex difference z_pi-z_pi_0 is constant.
The coordinate vector of the curve therefore has the form m+c(q)1.

Such a nonconstant common-translation curve is impossible when P!=0.
Here is an elementary proof avoiding the skew-cubic machinery. Pair the A
labels according to their matching and biject these pairs with the B
matching pairs. For pair l let q_l^0 and q_l^1 be the two local matching
products. Their difference is, up to sign, the product of the associated
A gap and B gap. P!=0 makes every q_l^0-q_l^1 nonzero. Choose any two
pairs, and in every remaining pair choose a nonzero one of q_l^0,q_l^1;
this is possible because their difference is nonzero. The resulting four
full matching products satisfy

 z00 z11=z10 z01,
 D=z00-z10-z01+z11
   =(q_1^0-q_1^1)(q_2^0-q_2^1) product_(l>=3) q_l !=0.

Replacing all four products by z_ab+c changes the quadratic identity's
left-minus-right side by cD. Thus no nonconstant common translation can
remain in the actual invariant cone. This contradicts the nonconstant
curve.

There is therefore a finite boundary point with u>0. At this point all
old active labels remain active and at least one new label is active.
P and eta are unchanged, and the invariant-cone finite-chart lemma gives
actual finite spectra. Repeat until k>=2n-2. The number of labels strictly
increases at each step, so this process terminates. QED.

The proof does not need a chosen meromorphic barrier, explicit infinity
asymptotics, or a global minimum attained in an affine chart. The bounded
positive part is the key additional fact. It also covers singular fibers
by passing to normalization of an irreducible curve branch through the
starting point.

5. Upgrade from the complementary bound to the actual cycle capacity.

For the same outcome deficits define

 C(delta)=inf_(x in R^n) sum_s delta_pi_s exp((2s-1) dot x).

This function is nonnegative, monotone, homogeneous and concave. It is
continuous even on the boundary of the nonnegative orthant. Upper
semicontinuity follows because it is an infimum of continuous linear
functions. For lower semicontinuity, if delta^(j)->delta and epsilon>0,
then eventually delta^(j)>=(1-epsilon)delta coordinatewise: this is true
on positive coordinates by convergence and automatic on zero coordinates.
Monotonicity and homogeneity give liminf C(delta^(j))>=C(delta). Its value
is always finite, being at most the sum of the coordinates.

If the deficits are harmonic on a complex curve, concavity and their mean
value property imply C(delta(center))>=mean C(delta); continuity makes
C(delta) superharmonic. Thus the entire proof of Theorem2 remains valid
with B replaced by C. The capacity condition C>=T is equivalent to the
support inequality for every bias and coherent phase of the fixed
matching-cycle circuit. Therefore a genuine violation within these
circuits, and not merely a failure of the stronger AM-GM bound, transfers
to the same high-contact locus.

6. Further upgrade: unrestricted fixed-unitary MOC has the same transfer.

The parent agent then supplied a stronger deformation, independently
verified here. It applies to every fixed U, not just matching cycles, and
needs no capacity function or subharmonic gluing at all.

THEOREM 3 (unrestricted high-contact localization).
If MOC fails in dimension n>=4, then, for the same witnessing unitary U,
there are finite spectra and a support multiplier eta giving a strict
violation for which at least 2n-2 permutation labels attain the maximum
matching support value. For n4 the threshold is six active labels.

Proof. Fix a strict violation. For fixed U the determinant is the
holomorphic linear functional of matching coordinates

 d_U(z)=sum_pi c_pi z_pi,
 c_pi=sgn(pi) product_i U_(i,pi(i))/det U,
 sum_pi c_pi=1.

The c_pi may be complex; no positivity or reality is required here.
Because strict violation is open in the spectra, first perturb the
spectra, preserving U and eta, to ensure that a chosen disjoint-swap
quartet has

 z00 z11=z10 z01,
 Gamma=z00-z10-z01+z11 !=0.

Such Gamma is a nonzero spectral polynomial for every n>=4: it is the
product of two nonzero A gaps, two nonzero B gaps, and the remaining
matching factors. Its zero set has empty interior. Fix this quartet
throughout the argument.

Suppose there are k<=2n-3 active labels, and choose pi_0 among them.
Impose on the actual invariant cone the k complex linear equations

 d_U(z)-z_pi_0=P_0:=d_U(current)-z_pi_0(current),
 z_pi-z_pi_0=(z_pi-z_pi_0)(current), pi active, pi!=pi_0.

The fiber has local dimension at least 2n-2-k>=1 and contains a
nonconstant irreducible algebraic curve through the current point.
Crucially Re(eta P_0)>0, so every feasible point of the curve is still
a strict MOC violation with the same positive support gap.

Normalize and compactify the curve to a connected compact Riemann
surface S. Remove the finitely many points where an affine coordinate
has a pole, and also the finitely many zeros of Gamma. Gamma is not
identically zero because it is nonzero at the starting point. Denote
the remaining connected punctured surface by S'. Removing finitely many
points from a connected Riemann surface preserves connectedness.

On S' all inactive deficits
 delta_pi=Re[eta(z_pi_0-z_pi)]
are harmonic. Let Omega be the strict-feasible connected component
containing the starting point. If Omega has a boundary point in S',
that point adds at least one active label. All deficits there are
nonnegative, Gamma remains nonzero, and Re(eta P_0)>0 preserves the
violation. The finite-chart lemma returns actual finite spectra.

If Omega has no boundary in S', it is nonempty, open and closed there,
so Omega=S'. Every deficit is then the nonnegative real part of a
meromorphic function on S away from finitely many points. The pole-sign
argument in Theorem2 shows that all those functions, hence all complex
matching differences, are constant. Thus z=m+c(q)1 on this curve. The
quartet equation changes by c Gamma(m), with Gamma(m)!=0. Therefore c
is constant, contradicting the nonconstant curve in coordinate space.

The finite new-tie alternative must occur. Because the zeros of Gamma
were removed, the new point retains Gamma!=0, so the same argument can
be repeated without imposing any additional equation. Active labels
increase strictly until there are at least 2n-2. QED.

This proof also explains why fixing Gamma is unnecessary: its finitely
many zeros on each curve can be deleted as extra punctures. Fixing it
would waste one complex dimension and give only the weaker threshold
2n-3. It also explains why neither a normal-compression induction nor
an analytic-continuation argument for inequalities is being used.

The statement is a restriction on possible counterexamples, not an
assertion that the terminal strata contain none. The proof is geometric
enough to work for any fixed holomorphic linear functional d_U with
coefficient sum1; unitary-specific positivity must enter when proving
the terminal high-contact inequalities.
